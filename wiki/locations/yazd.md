# Yazd

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Overview

Yazd is a southern Iranian provincial, commercial, and agricultural centre. In 1868 its population was estimated at 40,000 (line 33467). One of the towns visited by [[vahid|Vahid]]: his proclamation of the Báb's message there led to unrest (MBBR 106–8; line 33473).

## Bahá'í History

Yazd later became a major centre of Bahá'í activity, including the conversion of Zoroastrians (line 33476). It was also the site of a considerable number of martyrdoms, including the "Seven Martyrs of Yazd" (1891) and the infamous 1903 massacre (MBBR 301–5, 357–8, 385–98; lines 33479–33482).

A significant number of Yazdi Zoroastrians became Bahá'ís from the 1880s onwards, coming to play an important role in the local Zoroastrian lay council (lines 33777–33781). Bahá'ís of Zoroastrian background from Yazd made a major contribution to the subsequent development of the Bahá'í Faith in India (lines 33783–33786).
