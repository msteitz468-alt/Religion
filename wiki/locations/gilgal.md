# Gilgal

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Gilgal is the first Israelite encampment west of the Jordan in [[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] — the
base from which the conquest narratives operate — and later a recurring cult and assembly site
associated with the making and the unmaking of [[saul|Saul]]'s kingship. The text locates it "on
the east border of Jericho" (Josh 4:19). It is a place of firsts and of endings: the twelve
memorial stones, the mass circumcision, the first Passover in the land, the cessation of the
manna; then the renewal of the kingdom, and the two Gilgal scenes in which Saul loses it.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants

- **Josh 4:19-24**: Israel crosses the Jordan and camps at Gilgal; [[joshua|Joshua]] erects the
  twelve stones taken from the riverbed, "that all the peoples of the earth may know that the hand
  of the LORD is mighty," and so that later generations will ask what they mean.
- **Josh 5:2-12**: the men born in the wilderness are circumcised with flint knives; the reproach
  of Egypt is "rolled away" — the text ties the name to *gālal* — Passover is kept, the people eat
  the produce of the land, and the manna ceases.
- **Josh 9–10**: the Gibeonite envoys come to Joshua "at the camp at Gilgal" with their worn sacks
  and mouldy bread; from Gilgal Joshua marches all night to relieve [[gibeon|Gibeon]].
- **Josh 14:6**: [[caleb|Caleb]] makes his Hebron claim at Gilgal; land is distributed by lot
  there before the later shift to [[shiloh|Shiloh]] (14:1-2).
- **Judg 2:1**: the angel of the LORD goes up from Gilgal to Bochim to rebuke Israel for making
  covenants with the inhabitants of the land.
- **1 Sam 7:16**: Gilgal is one of the circuit stops of [[samuel|Samuel]]'s judgeship (with Bethel
  and Mizpah).
- **1 Sam 11:14-15**: after the Ammonite victory the kingdom is renewed at Gilgal and Saul is made
  king "before the LORD."
- **1 Sam 13:8-14**: Saul, waiting seven days at Gilgal for Samuel while the people scatter,
  offers the burnt offering himself; Samuel tells him his kingdom will not continue.
- **1 Sam 15**: after the Amalekite campaign Samuel confronts Saul, declares obedience better than
  sacrifice, and hacks Agag in pieces "before the LORD at Gilgal."
- **2 Sam 19:15, 40**: Judah comes to Gilgal to bring [[david|David]] back across the Jordan after
  Absalom's revolt.
- **Amos 4:4; 5:5; Hos 4:15; 9:15; 12:11**: Gilgal denounced by the prophets as a site of
  illegitimate or corrupt worship — "do not enter into Gilgal."

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The wiki has ingested no archaeological source on Gilgal, and none of the ingested biblical
commentaries reports excavation results for it. **No site has been securely identified**, so there
is no stratigraphy, no ceramic sequence, and no epigraphic material to report. Claims sometimes
made in popular literature about "Joshua's Gilgal" rest on candidate identifications that are
themselves disputed; this page records none of them as established. Where a source is later
ingested that treats the survey and excavation of Jordan-Valley candidate sites, this section
should be filled from it.

## Modern Identification and Geography

Unresolved. The biblical data place Gilgal in the plain between the Jordan and [[jericho|Jericho]]
(Josh 4:19), and Deut 11:30 associates "the Gilgal" with the region opposite Gerizim and Ebal — a
notice difficult to reconcile with the Jordan-Valley location and itself part of the dispute.
Several sites in the Jericho plain have been proposed as the conquest-era Gilgal; the wiki names
none as the identification because the collection contains no source adjudicating it. The Hebrew
Bible also uses the name of at least one other location — the Gilgal of the Elijah/Elisha
narratives (2 Kgs 2:1; 4:38) appears to lie in the hill country, since Elijah and Elisha go *down*
from it to Bethel — and the multiplicity of Gilgals is one reason the geography stays contested.
This is recorded as an open question, not adjudicated.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Gilgal is the covenant threshold of the land. [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] (NICOT Joshua), as
recorded on [[book-of-joshua|the Joshua page]], frames the circumcision and Passover of Josh
5:1-12 as **covenant-related rites even though the word "covenant" does not appear in the
pericope** (reading them against Gen 17 and Exod 12), and reads the ending of the desert period —
Egypt's shame "rolled away," the manna ceasing, the produce of Canaan eaten — as the transition
from wilderness sustenance to the gift of the land. On the memorials of Josh 4, Woudstra's stated
account of the book's occasion is recorded as covenantal: to keep alive the memory of YHWH's acts
so that future generations trust and obey.

The Samuel material gives Gilgal a second, darker valence: the place where kingship is renewed is
the place where it is twice pronounced forfeit. The prophetic denunciations in Amos and Hosea
complete the arc, treating a foundational site as a byword for corrupted cult.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

- **Twelve stones and the crossing memorial** (Josh 4) — [[joshua|Joshua]].
- **Circumcision and first Passover in the land** (Josh 5) — see [[book-of-joshua|Joshua]].
- **Base of operations** for the Gibeon relief march and the southern campaign (Josh 9–10) — see
  [[gibeon|Gibeon]].
- **Saul's rejection scenes** (1 Sam 13; 15) and the hacking of Agag — [[saul|Saul]],
  [[samuel|Samuel]], [[amalekites|Amalekites]].
- **Controversy — location.** Open; candidates disputed; multiple biblical Gilgals. Not adjudicated.
- **Controversy — conquest historiography.** [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] reports and contests
  Noth's traditio-historical account, in which Josh 2–9 comprises etiological legends originally
  **Benjaminite/Gilgal** in transmission and later nationalized; the wiki records Woudstra's report
  of Kaiser's critiques (on the Gilgal transmission site, and on how a fictional all-Israel
  conquest could arise if Judg 1 was known) as recorded on [[book-of-joshua|Joshua]]. Positions,
  not verdicts.
