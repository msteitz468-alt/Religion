# Haifa

A city on the slopes of Mount Carmel in northern Israel. For Bahá'ís, Haifa is the seat of the Bahá'í World Centre and the location of the Shrine of the Báb.

## Bahá'í Historical Significance

'Abdu'l-Bahá moved across the bay from [[akka|'Akká]] to Haifa in 1910; "thenceforth [it] was to remain the headquarters of the Faith" (line 1220). The Báb's remains were interred on Mount Carmel on 21 March 1909, giving Haifa additional spiritual importance for Bahá'ís (lines 1222–1225).

## The Shrine of the Báb and Mount Carmel

Construction of the Shrine of the Báb on Mount Carmel was directed by 'Abdu'l-Bahá (line 1209–1210 from the 'Abdu'l-Bahá entry). The remains of the Báb and his companion, after being kept hidden in various locations in Iran, were transferred to the Holy Land in 1899 and interred in the completed Shrine on 21 March 1909 (lines 4792–4795 from the Báb entry).

## The Arc Buildings

The Universal House of Justice and related institutions are located on an "Arc" traced along the flank of Mount Carmel above the grave of Bahíyyih Khánum. Buildings along the Arc include (lines 3559–3641):

- The International Archives Building (first to be constructed, 1957)
- The Seat of the Universal House of Justice (completed 1982, occupied 1983) — a large building in classical style with Corinthian columns and a low dome, designed by architect Husayn Amanat
- The Seat of the International Teaching Centre and Centre for the Study of the Texts (announced 1987, under construction from 1992)
- A projected International Bahá'í Library

The completion of the Arc buildings is regarded by Bahá'ís as linked to the onset of the Lesser Peace (line 3582–3583).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Bahá'u'lláh's Tablet of Carmel directed that God would "sail His Ark" upon the mountain — a prophecy Shoghi Effendi interpreted as foretelling the establishment of the Universal House of Justice on Mount Carmel (lines 3562–3568).

## From Smith, Concise Encyclopedia (2026-06-28)

### The Shrine of the Báb — Detailed History

The site for the Shrine was indicated by Bahá'u'lláh to 'Abdu'l-Bahá in 1891 during a visit to Mount Carmel (line 29015). In 1898 'Abdu'l-Bahá gave instructions for the Báb's remains to be transported from their concealment in Tehran; the remains arrived secretly in 'Akká on 31 January 1899 — fifty lunar years after the Báb's execution (lines 29017–29022). A marble sarcophagus was received from the Bahá'ís of Burma to serve as the eventual resting place (lines 29025–29028). The building was completed by 1907; in March 1909 'Abdu'l-Bahá placed the Báb's remains inside the sarcophagus in a vault beneath the central room (lines 29031–29044). When 'Abdu'l-Bahá died in 1921 he was buried beneath another room, giving the building increased religious significance (lines 29041–29044).

The original Shrine consisted of a six-room masonry structure on the northern slope of Mount Carmel, above the German Templer colony (lines 29045–29051). In 1928–30 Shoghi Effendi had three additional rooms constructed to the south. In 1942 Shoghi Effendi commissioned William Sutherland Maxwell (his father-in-law) to design a superstructure (lines 29066–29072); the final form was approved in 1944. Construction proceeded in stages from 1947: a colonnaded arcade (completed May 1950); an octagon with minaret-like pinnacles; an 11-metre-high clerestory with eighteen lancet windows; and finally a dome covered in gilded tiles topped with a lantern and finial. Completed September 1953 at a total cost of three-quarters of a million dollars; much of the work was done in Italy (lines 29086–29107).

The Shrine is illuminated at night (line 29107). 'Abdu'l-Bahá named the five doors of the original Shrine; Shoghi Effendi named doors of the extra rooms after the donor, the architect W.S. Maxwell, Ugo Giachery (Italian liaison), and Leroy Ioas (who oversaw final work; lines 29109–29135).

Shoghi Effendi described the Shrine as a "focal center of Divine illumination and power", and the Báb's sacred remains as the spiritual centre of a series of concentric circles ultimately embracing the entire planet (lines 29155–29164).

### Terraces

In 1987 the Universal House of Justice initiated a project involving reconstruction of nine terraces leading up from the foot of the mountain to the Shrine, and construction of nine further terraces above the Shrine, plus extensions to the gardens. The project's architect is Fariborz Sahba, also designer of the Indian Bahá'í temple (lines 29136–29154).

### German Temple Society

The first and largest German Templer colony was founded in Haifa in 1868–9 under David Hardegg. Both Bahá'u'lláh and 'Abdu'l-Bahá had friendly contacts with the Templars. The colony was dispersed by the British in the late 1930s. The street along which their houses were built leads to the stairway up to the Shrine of the Báb (lines 30706–30721).
