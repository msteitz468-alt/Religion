# Ashdod

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Ashdod**, in the southern coastal plain, was one of the five **[[philistines|Philistine]]
pentapolis** cities ([[ashdod|Ashdod]], [[ashkelon|Ashkelon]], [[ekron|Ekron]], [[gaza|Gaza]],
Gath). The Philistines **destroyed the Late Bronze [[canaanites|Canaanite]] city** (and the
Egyptian fortress at nearby Tel Mor) and **built a much larger city over its ashes** c. 1175
BCE, marked by locally-made **Mycenaean IIIC pottery** (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Canaanite → Philistine**: over the burned Canaanite town the Philistines raised a major
  pentapolis city; after c. 980 BCE, as Israel expanded, Ashdod **grew five-fold to a
  metropolis of ~40 ha (100 acres)** even as inland Philistine cities shrank.
- **1 Samuel 5–6**: the captured **ark of the covenant** is brought into the **temple of
  Dagon** at Ashdod — Dagon's statue falls and breaks before it, and plague (tumors/mice)
  strikes the city, the most famous Ashdod episode.
- **Prophets & Chronicles**: Uzziah breaks down its wall (2 Chron. 26); oracles in Amos 1/3,
  Jer. 25, Zeph. 2, Zech. 9. **Isaiah 20**: Isaiah walks naked and barefoot as a sign
  against Ashdod when **Sargon II's** general takes it (711 BCE).
- **Assyrian period**: Ashdod's anti-Assyrian revolt is crushed by **Sargon II (711 BCE)**,
  who makes it a provincial seat (see [[assyrians]]).
- **Nehemiah 13.23–24**: the post-exilic alarm that children spoke "the language of
  **Ashdod**" and not Judahite — a marker of intermarriage.
- **Acts 8.40**: as Hellenistic-Roman **Azotus**, where Philip is found after baptizing the
  Ethiopian eunuch.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavations exposed the Philistine refoundation (Myc IIIC pottery, the "Ashdoda" seated
mother-goddess figurine), later Iron II growth, and **Assyrian-period** remains including
fragments of a **Sargon II victory stela** confirming the 711 conquest. A potter's
workshop and weaving installations (Aegean-style spool weights) attest the immigrants'
culture.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tel Ashdod**, ~6 km southeast of the modern Israeli port city of Ashdod; the ancient
harbor lay seaward at **Tel Mor / Ashdod-Yam**.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Ashdod is the stage for the **ark's triumph over Dagon** (1 Sam. 5), a key episode asserting
Yahweh's supremacy over the Philistine gods on their own ground, and a standing member of the
prophetic oracles against Philistia. Its mixed "speech of Ashdod" becomes a Nehemian symbol
of assimilation.

## Key Events and Controversies

The ark and the fall of Dagon; Sargon II's conquest (711) and Isaiah's sign-act; the
Nehemiah intermarriage crisis; Azotus in Acts. Related: [[philistines]], [[ashkelon]],
[[ekron]], [[gaza]], [[sea-peoples]], [[canaanites]], [[assyrians]], [[jerusalem]].
