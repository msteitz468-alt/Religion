# Edirne

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 11403–11430.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Ancient city in what is now European Turkey, about 200 kilometres northwest of Istanbul. Europeans historically called it by its old Roman name, Adrianople (line 11405–11407). The city served as the Ottoman capital before the conquest of Constantinople.

## Significance in Bahá'í History

Bahá'u'lláh and his companions were exiled to Edirne by the Ottoman authorities in 1863, arriving on 12 December. They remained until 12 August 1868 (lines 11408–11413).

During this period:
- The definitive break between [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] and [[subh-i-azal|Subh-i-Azal]] occurred (line 11413–11415)
- Bahá'u'lláh began the proclamations of his claims (line 11415–11416)

In his writings Bahá'u'lláh often referred to Edirne as 'the Land of Mystery' (*ard-i-sirr*), because *sirr* has the same numerical value as Edirne according to the [[abjad|Abjad]] system (lines 11417–11420).

## Prophetic Reference

The city suffered greatly during the Russian occupation of 1878 and much territory to the north was lost — events regarded by Bahá'ís as fulfillment of Bahá'u'lláh's prophecies in the *Súratu'l-Ra'ís* (see Tablets of Ra'ís) (lines 11421–11426).

## Modern Identification and Pilgrimage

One of the houses occupied by Bahá'u'lláh during his stay — the house of Riḍá Beg — survives and has become a place of pilgrimage. The other houses no longer exist (lines 11427–11430).
