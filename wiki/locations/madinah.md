# Madinah (Medina)

**Madinah** (Arabic: المدينة المنورة, "the Luminous City") is the second holiest city in Islam
after Mecca, and the city to which the Prophet Muhammad emigrated in 622 CE — the **Hijra** that
marks year one of the Islamic calendar. Known as **Yathrib** before the Prophet's arrival, it was
renamed *Madinat al-Nabi* ("City of the Prophet").

## The City's Sanctity (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 30)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 3, Book 30 (Nos. 91–114). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Madinah as Sacred Sanctuary (Haram)

The Prophet declared Madinah a sanctuary (*haram*) with territorial boundaries:

**No. 91** (Anas): "The Prophet said, 'Medina is a sanctuary from that place to that. Its trees
should not be cut and no heresy should be innovated nor any sin should be committed in it, and
whoever innovates in it a heresy or commits sins (bad deeds), then he will incur the curse of
Allah, the angels, and all the people.'"

**No. 93** (Abu Huraira): "The Prophet said, 'I have made Medina a sanctuary between its two
(*Harrat*) mountains.'" [Harrat = volcanic lava fields; Madinah is bounded by two black lava plains —
Harrat Rahat to the south and Harrat al-Wabarah to the west.]

**No. 94** (written document from the Prophet, narrated by Ali): "Medina is a sanctuary from
the 'Air Mountain to such and such a place, and whoever innovates in it a heresy or commits a
sin, or gives shelter to such an innovator in it will incur the curse of Allah, the angels, and
all the people, none of his compulsory or optional good deeds of worship will be accepted."

The sanctuary prohibitions cover: cutting trees, hunting game animals (Nos. 97–98), introducing
religious innovations (*bid'a*), and committing sins.

### Names and Identity

**No. 108** (Abu Humaid): The Prophet called the city by the names *Taybah* and *Tabah* —
both meaning something like "the Pure" or "the Good." The pre-Islamic name Yathrib (never used
in a positive context in the hadith) was effectively suppressed.

### Eschatological Role: Madinah and the Dajjal

**Nos. 103–107** (Abu Huraira, Abu Said al-Khudri, others): Madinah will be protected from the
Dajjal (the Antichrist figure): "There will be no town which Ad-Dajjal will not enter except
Mecca and Medina, and there will be no entrance (road) of Mecca or Medina but the angels will be
standing in rows guarding them against him" (No. 105). Earthquakes will shake the city, driving
out the hypocrites (*munafiqun*) but leaving the faithful: "Medina will remain as it is... till it
is left undisturbed and stray dogs and wolves will visit it" (No. 109, part of end-time sequence).

### Plague and Protective Merit

**No. 101** (Abu Huraira): "There is no disease that Allah has created, except that He also has
created its treatment" — and Madinah is specifically associated with protection against plague:
"Plague and Ad-Dajjal will not be able to enter Medina" (No. 106, Umar's authority).

### Migration to Madinah as Eschatological Act

**No. 113** (Ibn Umar): A time will come when believers are urged to return to Mecca and
Madinah: "A time will come when the best property of a Muslim will be sheep which he will take on
the top of mountains and the places of rainfall, running away with his religion from the afflictions."

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Madinah is the city where Islamic law and community were definitively established. The **Madinan
suras** of the Quran — longer, legislative, communal texts — contrast with the Meccan suras (shorter,
eschatological, monotheistic appeals). The Prophet's mosque (*Masjid al-Nabawi*) contains his tomb
and is the second most important pilgrimage site in Islam. Visiting it is *sunnah* but not obligatory
(*wajib*) for Hajj.

The **Ansar** ("helpers") were the Medinan Muslims who supported the Meccan emigrants (*Muhajirun*) —
their merit as a community is extensively praised in Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 58.
