# Samaria

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Samaria (Hebrew **Shomron**) was the capital of the **northern kingdom of Israel**,
founded by **Omri** on a defensible hill in the central highlands of **Ephraim**. After
Assyria destroyed it in 720 BCE it became the seat of the imperial province of
**Samerina**, and the region's name — **Samaria** — came to denote the whole central
hill country between Galilee and Judah. Herod later rebuilt the city as **Sebaste**.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Israelite capital (c. 880–720 BCE)**: the royal city of Omri, Ahab, Jezebel, and
  their successors; the target of **Amos, Hosea, and Micah's** social and cultic
  critique. The **Samaria ostraca** (8th c.) record tax receipts of oil and wine and
  reveal intact clan districts (see [[israelites]]).
- **Fall and resettlement (720 BCE)**: **Shalmaneser V** besieged it; **Sargon II**
  took it, deported ~27,290 Israelites (the "**ten lost tribes**"), and brought in
  foreigners — organizing **Samerina** under an Assyrian governor (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7;
  see [[assyrians]]).
- **The 2 Kings 17 portrait**: settlers attacked by lions until taught Yahweh-worship
  at Bethel, yet keeping their own gods (the charge of **syncretism**). Coogan corrects
  this: the imported cults vanish by the late 7th c., and the population becomes a
  community of **Yahweh worshipers** (see [[samaritans]]).
- **Persian period**: a populous, prosperous province under the **Sanballat** governors,
  in marked contrast to impoverished [[jerusalem|Yehud]]; the **Wadi ed-Daliyeh
  (Samaria) papyri** (4th c.) show a largely **Yahwistic** ruling elite (Leith, Ch. 8).

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The Omride **palace and acropolis**, the **Samaria ostraca**, Assyrian provincial
remains and "Assyrian palace ware," and the **Wadi ed-Daliyeh** legal papyri (found in
a cave with the remains of refugees, c. 335 BCE) all document the city's long arc.
Assyrian annals record Sargon's conquest and resettlement.

## Modern Identification and Geography

The ancient royal city is the hill of **Sebastia (Sabastiyah)** in the northern West
Bank, ~11 km northwest of **Nablus**; the wider **region of Samaria** is the central
highlands. Excavated remains include the Israelite palace and Herod's Sebaste.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Samaria embodies the fate of the **northern kingdom** — apostasy, judgment, and exile
in the Deuteronomic scheme — and stands as the object lesson that frightened Judah into
reform (Hezekiah, Josiah). It is also the cradle of the **[[samaritans|Samaritan]]**
community and of the Persian-period struggle over **"who is Israel."**

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

Omri's founding; the Elijah–Jezebel Baal conflict; the fall of 720 and the **"ten lost
tribes"**; the disputed nature of post-720 worship (foreign syncretism vs. continuous
Yahwism — see [[samaritans]]); the Samaria–Jerusalem rivalry of the restoration. In the
NT, "Samaria/Samaritan" carries the charge of the old schism (John 4; Luke 10).
