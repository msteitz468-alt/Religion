# Chihriq

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 8966–8997.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

A fortress in northwestern Iran (Azerbaiján province), in the region of Urúmiyyih, near the Turkish and Iraqi borders (lines 8969–8971).

## Significance in Bábí History

[[the-bab|The Báb]] was imprisoned at Chihriq for two years prior to his execution, from April 1848 until June 1850 (lines 8972–8974). He was transferred there from [[maku|Mákú]].

The Báb called Chihriq 'the Grievous Mountain' (*Jabal-i-Shadíd*), to differentiate it from Mákú (the 'Open Mountain'), although the pattern of imprisonment was similar: initial strictness eventually giving way to comparative freedom as the warden, Yaḥyá Khán, became devoted to his prisoner (lines 8975–8983).

## Events During the Báb's Imprisonment (lines 8983–8997)

Even more than at Mákú, the Báb received large numbers of visitors. Notable incidents include:
- The conversion of [[dayyan|Dayyán]] (line 8986)
- Popular enthusiasm greeting the Báb's arrival in Urúmiyyih, including pilgrims taking the water of the public bath after he had used it (assuming it had acquired holiness) (lines 8987–8992)
- The despatch of the Báb's 'Sermon of Wrath' (*Khuṭbi-yi-Qahriyyih*) to Ḥájí Mírzá Áqásí following the Tabriz trial (July 1848) (lines 8993–8996)
