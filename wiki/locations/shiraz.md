# Shíráz

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Shíráz (1868 population estimated at 25,000) is the capital of the southern Iranian province of Fárs. It is the birthplace of [[the-bab|the Báb]] and the site of his initial declaration of mission (lines 28635–28639).

## Significance in Babi-Bahá'í History

Shíráz is a city of primary importance in the Bahá'í tradition as the site where the Báb declared his mission on 22–23 May 1844 in his own house, which he later identified as the new "House of God" in succession to the Ka'ba at Mecca, making it a place of [[pilgrimage|pilgrimage]] for his followers (lines 28647–28651).

[[quddus|Quddús]] met the Báb in Shíráz, where he was chosen as the prophet's companion on the Mecca pilgrimage, and where he and others were savagely punished for creating religious disorder on their return (lines 25955–25959). [[mulla-sadiq-khurasani|Mullá Ṣádiq Khurásání]] (Muqaddas) also became a leader of congregational prayers in Shíráz and publicly added the Báb's name to the call to prayer (June 1845), which occasioned uproar and his arrest (lines 27518–27526).

Shíráz was also the birthplace of [[tahirih|Táhirih]] (line 25909).

## The House of the Báb

The Báb's house, where he first declared his mission in 1844, was later identified by him as the new "House of God" in succession to the Ka'ba at Mecca, and made a place of pilgrimage for his followers. Bahá'u'lláh named it as one of two sacred houses and as a place of pilgrimage. It came into the possession of the Bahá'ís and was restored at 'Abdu'l-Bahá's instructions in 1903, becoming a major centre of pilgrimage for Bahá'ís in Iran. It also became a focus for anti-Bahá'í zealots:

- Damaged in an arson attack in 1942
- Almost destroyed by an anti-Bahá'í mob in 1955
- Finally thoroughly demolished by Islamic revolutionaries in September 1979

A road and public square were later built over the site (lines 28647–28665).

## Historical Inhabitants

An ancient Iranian city, historically important as a centre of Persian culture, poetry (Hafiz and Sa'di were natives), and learning. Home to a significant population in the Qajar period (19th century).

## Modern Identification

Capital of Fárs province, Iran. Major Iranian city. The site of the Báb's house has been built over and is no longer accessible.
