# Mount Satrunjaya

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Satrunjaya — "the hill which conquers enemies" — lies outside the small market town of **Palitana** in the **Saurashtra region of Gujarat**, western India. It is one of the five holy mountains of Svetambara Jainism and "unquestionably" one of the two most famous Jain pilgrimage sites (the other being [[shravana-belgola]] for the Digambaras). The hill carries an alternative name, **Pundarika** ("White Lotus"), derived from the legend of Rsabha's grandson (ch. 7).

The hill is situated in Bhavnagar district, Gujarat state, and the town of Palitana at its foot is the primary access point for pilgrims.

## Primary Textual Appearances

Satrunjaya "has the distinction of being mentioned several times in the Svetambara scriptural canon, albeit in the later parts, which would suggest some sort of familiarity with this most famous of *tirthas* by the fifth century" (ch. 7). The most important early account is the **Saravali**, one of the "mixed" texts of the canon, probably dating from about the eleventh century and "reflecting a time when the hill was beginning to gain major prominence as a holy spot" (ch. 7).

According to the Saravali:
- Rsabha's grandson **Pundarika** travelled to Satrunjaya after being told by the fordmaker that he would gain final release there, giving the hill its alternative name.
- Pundarika's father **Bharata** built a temple there.
- **Rsabha himself** supposedly visited the *tirtha*, ensuring it be regarded as "the first of all holy places."
- Bharata attained deliverance there, as did heroes incorporated into Jain tradition — Rama, Sita, and the five Pandava brothers (ch. 7).

The Saravali also states that "spiritual attainments are gained effortlessly on Satrunjaya and that merely installing one image there will lead to rebirth in heaven," and that "pilgrimage to Satrunjaya is the equivalent of pilgrimage to all other holy places and that religious giving there is particularly excellent" (ch. 7).

The thirteenth/fourteenth-century Kharatara monk **Jinaprabha Siri** wrote the most important Svetambara guide to holy places, the *Vividhatirthakalpa* ("Description of Various Holy Places"), which provides an account of Satrunjaya at a time when it had "fully entered into historical reality" — the temples having been sacked by the Muslims in 1311. Jinaprabha describes wild animals on the hill giving up carnivorous ways and fasting "under the influence of the atmosphere of non-violence which prevailed on the hill" (*VTK* 42; ch. 7).

## Archaeological and Historical Data

The first clear historical evidence for a lay Jain presence on the hill is "an image of Pundarika and a preaching monk set up by a merchant in honour of his guru and dated **1006 CE**" (ch. 7). A slightly later image dated 1075 depicts a prosperous layman. Original wooden temple structures were replaced by stone ones in the twelfth century.

The site was **sacked by the Muslims in 1311**. Renovation took place within two years under the sponsorship of Samara Sah, but temples on the summit "remained relatively limited in number for over two centuries" (ch. 7).

The decisive expansion came in **1593** when Hiravijaya Suri, head of the Tapa Gaccha, organised a massive pilgrimage to the hill and probably consecrated the temple to Rsabha dedicated by the merchant Tejpal Soni. This "gave the impetus to the remarkable proliferation of shrines and temples which has characterised Satrunjaya to this day" (ch. 7).

In **1656** the Muslim governor of Gujarat gave custody of the village of Palitana at the foot of Satrunjaya to the great merchant **Santidas Jhaveri**, effective leader of the Svetambara community, and subsequently exemption from all taxes was granted. Around **1730** the site came under the control of the **Anandji Kalyanji** trust, which still manages a variety of Svetambara temples and holy places. Disputes with the maharajas of Bhavnagar — who gained control over Palitana after the Mughal empire's collapse — over taxes complicated matters prior to Indian independence (ch. 7).

## Modern Identification and Geography

Satrunjaya is the hill immediately outside Palitana town, **Bhavnagar district, Gujarat, India**. Palitana is accessible by road from Bhavnagar city (roughly 50 km). The hill is the dominant feature of the landscape. Today, with "nearly one thousand shrines, some admittedly little bigger in size than a British mailbox," it "provides remarkable testimony both to Jain architectural style and to the commitment of the laity to gain merit through the glorification of their religion" (ch. 7). The site remains an active pilgrimage destination. (Precise coordinates not provided in source; Palitana is at approximately 21.52°N 71.82°E.)

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

For Svetambaras, Satrunjaya "exists outside the dimensions of normal chronology. It has supposedly been renovated sixteen times by devotees in the course of this world era and in the future nineteen fordmakers will preach there" (ch. 7). "A near supernatural atmosphere is associated with this holy mountain" (ch. 7).

The *tirtha* is particularly associated with the fordmaker **Rsabha** and his descendant Pundarika. Its tutelary goddess is **Cakresvari**, described as "the helper of all women who fast and the attendant divinity of Rsabha to whom the main temple on the mountain is dedicated" (ch. 7).

Satrunjaya is the most auspicious location for the **Aksayatrtiya** ("Undying Third") festival observance — the ceremonial completion of fasting and recounting of the story of Rsabha's fastbreaking; "the most suitable spot for this to take place is regarded by Svetambaras as being Mount Satrunjaya in Gujarat" (ch. 7). Those who have completed a series of fasts over the course of a year receive sugarcane there in commemoration of the first act of religious giving.

Most Svetambara temples contain painted representations of Satrunjaya (alongside other great holy places) "which can serve as ancillary focuses of devotion after homage has been paid to the main temple image" (ch. 7), so that the ideal of pilgrimage remains before lay Jains even in their local worship.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

- **Legendary founding**: associated with Rsabha, Pundarika, and Bharata of the Universal History.
- **1006 CE**: first datable image on the hill.
- **1311**: Muslim sack of the temples; prompt rebuilding by Samara Sah.
- **1593**: Hiravijaya Suri's great pilgrimage and temple consecration — the turning point in the site's expansion.
- **1656**: Custody of Palitana granted to Santidas Jhaveri.
- **c. 1730**: Management transferred to Anandji Kalyanji trust.
- **Jinaprabha Siri's *VTK*** (13th/14th c.): the primary medieval guide to Satrunjaya and other Svetambara holy places.
