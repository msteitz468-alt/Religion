# Sinai / Horeb

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Sinai** (called **Horeb** in the Deuteronomic and Elohist strands), **"the mountain of
God,"** is the **wilderness peak of revelation** — not a city but the desert site where, in
the biblical narrative, Yahweh descends to make the covenant and give the law. Its **actual
location is unknown and much disputed** (Redmount, Coogan Ch. 2): the geography of the Exodus
itinerary cannot be securely mapped, and no archaeological trace of the wilderness wandering
has been found.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Exodus 3 / Horeb**: while shepherding the flock of **Jethro**, priest of **[[midianites|
  Midian]]**, **[[moses|Moses]]** encounters the **burning bush** at "Horeb, the mountain of
  God," and learns the divine name **YHWH** — a setting that anchors the **Midianite/Kenite
  hypothesis** that Yahwism originated in the desert southeast of Israel (Redmount/Stager,
  Coogan Chs. 2–3; cf. Exod. 3, Judg. 5, Deut. 33's "Yahweh came from Sinai").
- **Exodus 19–34**: the **theophany** (thunder, smoke, trumpet), the **Decalogue and covenant
  law**, the **golden calf** apostasy, and the renewal of the tablets — the constitutive
  events of Israel's identity.
- **Numbers / Deuteronomy 1–5**: Israel departs "Horeb" after the covenant; Deuteronomy
  recapitulates the lawgiving "out of the midst of the fire."
- **1 Kings 19**: **Elijah**, fleeing Jezebel, journeys to **Horeb** and meets Yahweh not in
  wind, earthquake, or fire but in "a still small voice" — a deliberate echo of the Mosaic
  theophany.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

There is **no extrabiblical or archaeological evidence** for the Exodus, the wilderness
itinerary, or the mountain itself (Redmount, Coogan Ch. 2). The **traditional identification**
with **Jebel Musa** in the southern Sinai Peninsula is a **Byzantine Christian** localization
(St. Catherine's Monastery, 6th c. CE) with no demonstrable link to any second-millennium
event. **Rival proposals** place the mountain elsewhere in Sinai, in **Midian / NW Arabia**
(e.g. Jebel al-Lawz — favored by those stressing the Midianite connection), or in the
**Negeb** (Har Karkom).

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Unidentified and contested.** The long-standing pilgrimage site is **Jebel Musa** in the
southern Sinai, Egypt, home to **St. Catherine's Monastery**; alternative reconstructions
point to Midian/Arabia or the Negeb. The uncertainty is itself a major scholarly and
devotional crux.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Sinai/Horeb is the **axis of biblical revelation**: the place of the divine **Name**, the
**Torah**, and the **covenant** that constitutes Israel as Yahweh's people. In Christianity
**Paul allegorizes "Mount Sinai in Arabia"** as the covenant of law/slavery over against the
freedom of the gospel (Gal. 4.24–25); in Islam, **Tur Sina / Tur** is the blessed mountain
where God spoke to **Musa** (Qur'an 19, 28, 95). It is thus a shared sacred geography across
all three Abrahamic traditions.

## Key Events and Controversies

The burning bush and the revelation of the divine Name; the covenant and lawgiving; the
golden calf; Elijah's "still small voice"; the **unresolved location** (Jebel Musa vs.
Midian/Arabia vs. Negeb) and the **Midianite/Kenite origin of Yahwism**. Related:
[[midianites]], [[israelites]], [[moses]], [[exodus]], [[jerusalem]], [[shechem]].
