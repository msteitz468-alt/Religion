# Dan

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Dan** (modern **Tel Dan / Tell el-Qadi**), at the foot of **Mount Hermon** beside the great
spring that feeds the **Jordan**, was the **northernmost Israelite town** — the proverbial
limit "from Dan to Beersheba." Originally the Canaanite city of **Laish (Leshem)**, it was
seized by the migrating **tribe of Dan** and became, under Jeroboam I, one of the two royal
sanctuaries of the northern kingdom (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Canaanite Laish**: an old city named in the **Mari** and Egyptian texts.
- **Judges 18**: the **Danite migration** — having failed to hold their southern coastal
  allotment, the tribe of Dan marches north, conquers peaceful **Laish**, and renames it
  **Dan**, installing a (Mosaide) priesthood and a graven image. The destruction of **Tel
  Dan Stratum VI A (c. 1175–1150 BCE)** may reflect this conquest — though, Stager notes,
  it is too late to sync with the Late Bronze destructions of [[bethel|Bethel]] and
  [[hazor|Hazor]] (Coogan Ch. 3).
- **1 Kings 12.28–30**: **Jeroboam I** sets up a **golden bull-calf** at Dan (and
  [[bethel|Bethel]]) — Frank Cross suggests Dan housed priests of the **Moses line** as a
  deliberate compromise (Coogan Ch. 3).
- **Amos 8.14**: those who "swear by… the way of Dan" are condemned — the prophetic critique
  of the northern cult.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

**Avraham Biran's** excavations recovered Dan's massive Bronze Age mud-brick **arched gate**
and, crucially, a large **cultic platform (bamah)** in ashlar masonry — about 19 × 9 m, with
an altar, plastered pool, and oil press — dated to **Jeroboam's era** and destroyed in the
early ninth century (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). Above all, in **1993–94** three joined fragments
of a basalt **victory stela in Aramaic — the Tel Dan stela — came to light**, set up by an
**Aramean king (probably Hazael of Damascus)** celebrating his killing of a "king of Israel"
(Jehoram) and a king of the **"house of David"** (Ahaziah) c. 842 BCE. It is the **earliest
extrabiblical attestation of the Davidic dynasty**; charges that it is a forgery are
"untenable" (Cogan/Stager, Coogan Chs. 3, 7). Its shattered reuse as building stone attests
Israel's later **recovery of Dan from the Arameans** (see [[arameans]]).

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tel Dan (Tell el-Qadi)**, in the **Hula Valley** of far northern Israel at the foot of
**Mount Hermon**, beside the largest source-spring of the **Jordan**; today a nature reserve
and archaeological park.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Dan marks the **northern boundary of the land** and, with Bethel, the **counter-Jerusalem
cult** of the northern kingdom — "the sin of Jeroboam" in Deuteronomistic eyes. Its stela has
made Dan, ironically, one of the strongest archaeological witnesses **for** the historical
**house of David** that its own kings opposed.

## Key Events and Controversies

The Danite migration and conquest of Laish; Jeroboam's calf and the Dan bamah; the **Tel Dan
stela** and the "house of David" historicity debate; Aramean (Hazael) vs. Israelite control.
