# The Indus Valley Civilization (Harappa)

The **Indus Valley Civilization** (IVC, or **Harappan** Civilization) was a Bronze Age urban civilization
of the Indus River plain (c. 2300–1500 BCE) — older than the oldest Hindu texts (the **[[vedas|Vedas]]**),
and the wiki's deepest stratum of South Asian prehistory. It is known to the wiki through
[[wendy-doniger|Doniger's]] *[[doniger-the-hindus|The Hindus]]* (Chs. 3–4), whose central question is
**what, if anything, of the IVC survives in later Hinduism** — and whose central caution is against
reading Hinduism back into it.

## Geography and Material Culture

A "stunningly uniform" civilization spanning over 1,000 sites and ~750,000 sq mi, with two great cities
**400 miles apart — Harappa** (on the Ravi, in the Punjab) and **Mohenjo-Daro** ("Mound of the Dead," in
Sindh) — and the port of **Lothal** in the Gujarat delta. Doniger stresses its hallmarks: grid-planned
streets, standardized kiln- and sun-fired **bricks** of uniform proportion, advanced **drainage/sewers**
"superior to those in parts of the world today," a great granary, weights and measures, trade with Crete,
Sumer, Mesopotamia, and Elam (Iran) — but **no iron** (weapons of copper/bronze), and the earliest known
**cubical dice** (significant given gambling's later role in Indian myth). The uniformity suggests strong
central control — read by some as "urbanity and well-being," by others as "obsessive," even totalitarian.

## The Seals, the Script, and "Is Indus Religion a Myth?"

The IVC left **~2,000+ inscribed seals** (≈400 graphemes) in an **undeciphered script** — too short and
context-poor to crack; possibly not a language at all, perhaps mere marks of ownership "like a bar code."
"The civilization of the Indus is not silent, but we are deaf." Doniger's recurring methodological warning
(her "siren song of hindsight"): scholars persistently read **later Hindu meaning into Indus images** —
the "Great Bath" as a ritual tank, a "College of Priests," female figurines as **mother goddesses**, small
conical stones as **Śiva lingas** — when the evidence equally permits that the IVC had *no* state religion
at all ("Could they have been more like protoatheists than protoyogis?"). She notes the telling absence of
**cow figurines** (though they had cows) — the Indus did *not* use the cow as a cultural symbol, unlike
later Hinduism.

## The "Pashupati" Seal (Seal 420) — the proto-Śiva debate

The most consequential disputed object: a tiny (~1-inch) seal of a horned, seated, possibly ithyphallic
figure surrounded by animals. **John Marshall** (1931) identified it as a **prototype of Śiva**
as *Paśupati* ("Lord of Beasts") — on five grounds (yogic posture; horned headdress; multiple faces; the
ring of animals + tiger-skin; the apparent erection) — which would push a key element of Hinduism back
*before* the Vedas. Doniger records the identification **and her skepticism**, per the
[[wendy-doniger|hindsight]] caution: she lists **ten** rival identifications scholars have proposed
(a goddess; the buffalo-demon Mahiṣa; Indra; Rudra; Agni; a chief; a "seated bull"; the antelope-horned
sage Ṛśyaśṛṅga; a bull cult; or merely a yogic posture) — recording the dispute **without adjudicating**.
*(The wiki has no Śiva figure page yet — a flagged gap; this seal debate is the proto-Śiva
question.)*

## Relation to Later Hinduism (and to the Vedic People)

Doniger's measured conclusion (Ch. 4): the resemblances between some IVC motifs — the **fig (pipal) tree**,
the horned god, lingas, voluptuous women, the "animal style" later seen on Aśokan pillars — and later
Hinduism are "too stunning to ignore," so **forms** may have survived; but "the mistake is in assuming that
the function follows the form." Whether the IVC *is* a source of Hinduism is bound up with the
[[aryan-migration-question|origins-of-the-Vedic-people controversy]]: the IVC's apparent **lack of the
horse** (central to the Vedas, absent from the Harappan seals) is a key argument that the Indus people were
**not** the Indo-European-speaking Vedic people. Her synthesis: later Hinduism is a **fusion / bricolage**
of Vedic words and Indus (and other non-Vedic) images — the "non-Veda" as a partly-uncredited *fons et
origo*.

## Modern Identification and Controversy

The cities lie in modern **Pakistan** (Harappa, Punjab; Mohenjo-Daro, Sindh), with sites also in
northwestern **India** (e.g. Lothal, Gujarat). First noticed when 19th-c. British engineers carted off
Harappan bricks as railway ballast; excavated seriously after 1917 (Mohenjo-Daro) under Marshall. A
Buddhist **stupa** sits over a possibly-religious structure at Mohenjo-Daro (unexcavated). The
"**Sarasvati Valley**" / "Indus-Sarasvati" relabeling is itself politically charged (linked to claims of
Vedic indigeneity — see the [[aryan-migration-question|origins controversy]]).
