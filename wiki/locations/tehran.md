# Tehran

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Tehran became the Iranian capital during the Qájár period. In 1868 its population was estimated at 85,000 (line 30610). It was the birthplace of [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] and [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] (also of Bahíyyih Khánum and Mírzá Mihdí; lines 30612–30616).

Bahá'u'lláh eulogized the city as "the Dayspring of His [God's] light" and the "source of joy of all mankind", predicting that power within it would soon pass to the people and that it would eventually be ruled by a just king who would protect the Bahá'ís (KA 53–4; lines 30617–30624).

## Babi and Bahá'í History

Tehran became an early centre of Babi activity; Bahá'u'lláh himself was one of the earliest converts there (1844; lines 30625–30630). The first persecution of the Babis in the city was in 1850 (the "Seven Martyrs of Tehran"). The bloody aftermath of the botched assassination attempt on [[nasiru-d-din-shah|Násiru'd-Dín Sháh]] (1852) saw large numbers of Babis executed, including Táhirih; it was at this time that Bahá'u'lláh was arrested and placed in the [[siyah-chal|Síyáh-Chál]] dungeon (lines 30631–30649).

A Bahá'í community emerged in the 1860s and grew rapidly, augmented by conversions and movement of Bahá'ís from other parts of Iran. Persecution in Tehran was relatively limited in part because family connections between Bahá'ís and government officials enabled early warnings, and in part because the government wished to avoid embarrassment before the powerful foreign embassies in the capital (lines 30650–30671). Major exceptions included the martyrdoms of Badí (1869), 'Alí-Muhammad Varqá and his son Rúhu'lláh (1896), and imprisonment of groups of Bahá'ís in 1882–3 and 1891 (lines 30672–30676).

Tehran became the centre of national Bahá'í administration: all four Hands of the Cause appointed by Bahá'u'lláh (1887–c.1889) were resident there; the Tehran Spiritual Assembly (1899) served as the central administrative body for the whole country. The city was also the locale of the well-known Tarbíyat Bahá'í schools (1898, 1910; lines 30677–30687).

## House of Bahá'u'lláh

Bahá'u'lláh was born in a large early 19th-century house complex belonging to his father, Mírzá 'Abbás (Mírzá Buzurg Núrí), located in the Pí-Minár (minaret's foot) area of the old Shimrán Gate district, a neighbourhood where many Qájár courtiers and notables lived (lines 30688–30695). After his father's fall from office (late 1830s) the house was sold. It is regarded by Bahá'ís as a sacred site; it was located, purchased, and restored by them during the 1940s. It was confiscated by government authorities after the Islamic Revolution (1979; lines 30695–30703).

The [[siyah-chal|Síyáh-Chál]] ("Black Pit") underground dungeon where Bahá'u'lláh was imprisoned is the second holiest Bahá'í site in Iran (after the House of the Báb in Shiraz; line 29374). It was in Bahá'í ownership from 1954 until the Islamic Revolution of 1979 (line 29376).
