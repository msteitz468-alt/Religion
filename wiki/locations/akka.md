# 'Akká

An ancient port city on the Mediterranean coast of what is now northern Israel. For Bahá'ís, 'Akká is the site of Bahá'u'lláh's prolonged imprisonment and the location of several historically significant buildings.

## Bahá'í Historical Significance

Bahá'u'lláh arrived in 'Akká under Ottoman banishment in 1868 and remained the effective prisoner of the state until 1877, when he moved outside the city walls. 'Abdu'l-Bahá continued to live in the city under the government's order of banishment, "increasingly gaining acceptance as a local notable" and regularly attending the local mosque (lines 1100–1108, from the 'Abdu'l-Bahá entry).

### Key Buildings

**The House of 'Abdu'lláh Páshá:** The building used as the governorate of 'Abdu'lláh Páshá (governor of Akka 1819–31) was later rented by 'Abdu'l-Bahá. The larger (seaward-facing) part of what is now termed the House of 'Abbúd was occupied by Bahá'u'lláh (lines 967–974, 1640–1644).

**The House of 'Abbúd:** 'Abbúd was a Christian merchant of Akka and owner of the seaward-facing part of what is now termed the House of 'Abbúd (lines 967–974).

**Bahjí (mansion):** The original (1821) structure of the mansion of Bahjí was built by 'Abdu'lláh Páshá (lines 1646–1648). It later became Bahá'u'lláh's residence and is now the holiest site in the Bahá'í world.

### The Ridván Garden at the Na'mayn River

An island garden rented by 'Abdu'l-Bahá in 1875 and visited by Bahá'u'lláh from 1877 onwards. He loved its beauty and named it Ridván (Paradise) after the original Ridván garden near Baghdad, and "Our Verdant Isle." It was the site of Bahá'u'lláh's vision of Trustworthiness. Iranian Bahá'ís brought seedlings to grow in it. Two other Bahá'í gardens (Firdaws and Ashraf) are nearby (lines 2652–2662).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Bahá'u'lláh's imprisonment in 'Akká, prophesied in Bahá'í interpretation to fulfil biblical references to the "Most Great Prison," was central to his ministry. Despite incarceration, he continued to compose major works including the Kitáb-i-Aqdas (completed c.1873, while still in the city, line 3381).

## Modern Identification

The city of 'Akká (Acre) is now part of Israel, situated on the northern shore of Haifa Bay across from [[haifa|Haifa]]. The Bahá'í World Centre is headquartered in Haifa, with the shrine of Bahá'u'lláh (at Bahjí, just north of 'Akká) as its holiest pilgrimage site.
