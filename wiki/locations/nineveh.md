# Nineveh

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Nineveh, on the east bank of the **Tigris**, became the great **imperial capital of the
Neo-Assyrian Empire** when **Sennacherib** vastly expanded it (his "palace without
rival"). Its state archives "number in the tens of thousands" of tablets (Cogan, Coogan
Ch. 7). It was **sacked and destroyed in 612 BCE** by the Medes and Babylonians,
effectively ending Assyria.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Assyrian capital (late 8th–7th c. BCE)**: seat of **Sennacherib, Esarhaddon, and
  Ashurbanipal**. From Sennacherib's palace come the **[[lachish|Lachish reliefs]]**,
  depicting his 701 siege of the Judahite city — among our richest sources for the
  Assyrian wars on [[jerusalem|Judah]]. See [[assyrians]].
- **Jonah**: Nineveh as the "great city" to which the reluctant prophet is sent; its
  repentance and Yahweh's mercy.
- **Nahum & Zephaniah 2**: oracles exulting over Nineveh's coming fall — "your shepherds
  are asleep, O king of Assyria… there is no assuaging your hurt" (Nahum 3).
- **612 BCE**: the Medes and Babylonians overran and burned it; Berossus reports the
  last king burning himself in his palace (see [[babylonians]]).

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

19th-century excavations (Layard, Rassam) uncovered the Assyrian palaces, the
**Lachish reliefs**, and the **library of Ashurbanipal** — including the *Epic of
Gilgamesh* and the Flood account, foundational for comparative study of Genesis. The
royal annals (propaganda glorifying the kings' wars and piety) are a primary source for
the period.

## Modern Identification and Geography

The mounds of **Kuyunjik** and **Nebi Yunus** lie opposite — and now within — **Mosul,
Iraq**, on the Tigris. Parts of the site and its museum holdings were damaged in recent
conflict.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Nineveh is the **archetypal great pagan city** — object of Jonah's mission and Yahweh's
surprising mercy, yet doomed in Nahum for its cruelty. The two books together hold the
tension of judgment and compassion toward the Gentile oppressor.

## Key Events and Controversies

Sennacherib's expansion and the Lachish reliefs; Jonah's mission and Nineveh's
repentance; the fall of 612. Ashurbanipal's library and the Gilgamesh/Flood parallels
feed [[flood-universality|the flood debate]] and comparative Genesis study. Related:
[[assyrians]], [[babylon]], [[lachish]], [[jerusalem]], [[babylonians]], [[jonah]],
[[genesis]].
