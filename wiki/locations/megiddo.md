# Megiddo

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Megiddo guarded the strategic **Aruna (Wadi Ara) pass** where the coastal **Via Maris**
enters the **Jezreel Valley** — making it one of the most fought-over sites in the
ancient world, with **over twenty superimposed cities** spanning the Neolithic to the
Iron Age (Pitard/Stager, Coogan Chs. 1–3).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Egyptian (Late Bronze)**: **Thutmose III's** famous battle and seven-month siege
  (c. 1457 BCE), the showcase victory of the New Kingdom over a Canaanite coalition.
  The 12th-c. **Canaanite** city (Stratum VIIA) contrasts with the simpler highland
  villages — Coogan uses it to show that early Israel was a socioeconomic, not just
  ethnic, departure (see [[israelites]]).
- **Judges 5.19**: the Canaanite kings fight "at Taanach, by the **waters of Megiddo**"
  (the Deborah battle).
- **Israelite Iron Age**: a **Solomonic** chambered gate and (debated) chariot
  installations; an Omride royal/administrative city (1 Kings 9.15).
- **Assyrian**: provincial capital **Magiddu** after the conquest of the north (a known
  governor served as an Assyrian year-eponym; see [[assyrians]], [[samaria]]).
- **2 Kings 23.29**: **King Josiah is killed at Megiddo** by Pharaoh **Neco II** (609
  BCE) — a pivotal disaster for Judah (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7).

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

A deeply stratified tell, excavated since the early 20th century; Bronze Age temples and
palaces, Iron Age gates, a water system, and grain silos. Thutmose III's Karnak
inscription lists the towns taken in his Megiddo campaign; Assyrian records attest its
provincial role.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tel Megiddo**, Jezreel Valley, northern Israel — a UNESCO World Heritage Site
(biblical tels) overlooking the plain that bears the symbolic weight of "Armageddon."

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Megiddo's strategic plain — scene of so many battles and of Josiah's death — gave its
name to **Armageddon** (Greek *Har Magedōn*, "mount of Megiddo," Rev. 16.16), the site
of the final eschatological battle in Christian apocalyptic. Thus a Canaanite
chariot-fortress became, via prophecy, a byword for the end of history.

Thutmose III's battle; the Deborah war; the Solomonic-gate dating debate; Josiah's death
(609); "Armageddon." Related: [[hazor]], [[shechem]], [[jezreel|Jezreel]],
