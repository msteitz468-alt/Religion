# Mount Gerizim

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Mount Gerizim** (Arabic **Jabal at-Tur**), rising over [[shechem|Shechem]] opposite **Mount
Ebal**, is the **holy mountain of the [[samaritans|Samaritans]]** — the place they hold to be
the one site God chose for sacrificial worship, against the Judean claim for **[[jerusalem|
Jerusalem]]** (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8). It is a sacred mountain rather than a settlement, though
a temple and associated town stood on and below it.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Deuteronomy 11.29; 27.12; Joshua 8.33**: Gerizim is the **mountain of blessing** (Ebal,
  the mountain of curse) in the covenant ceremony — the textual root of its sanctity. The
  **Samaritan Pentateuch** reads Deut. 27.4 as **Gerizim** (not Ebal) and includes a
  Gerizim "tenth commandment," grounding the Samaritan cult in Torah itself.
- **Judges 9.7**: **Jotham** proclaims his fable of the trees from the top of Gerizim against
  Abimelech's Shechem kingship.
- **Persian / early Hellenistic period**: the **Samaritan temple** is built on Gerizim.
  Josephus dates it to the late fourth century (linking it to **Sanballat** the governor of
  Samaria and a marriage with the Jerusalem priestly family), and the excavators at **Tell
  er-Ras** claimed remains of this temple; archaeology now points to a sanctuary already in
  the **fifth century** (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8).
- **128 BCE**: the Hasmonean **John Hyrcanus destroys the Gerizim temple** (sacking
  [[shechem|Shechem]] in the process), and two decades later razes Shechem and Samaria — a
  decisive blow in the Jewish–Samaritan rupture.
- **John 4.20–21**: the Samaritan woman tells Jesus, "Our ancestors worshiped on **this
  mountain**, but you say that the place where people must worship is in Jerusalem" — Jesus
  answers that the hour comes when worship is "neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem."

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavations on the summit and at **Tell er-Ras** have uncovered a major **sacred precinct**
of the Persian-Hellenistic period — fortification walls, altars, thousands of dedicatory
inscriptions and bones — confirming a substantial **Samaritan temple-city** earlier and
larger than Josephus implies. A Roman-era **temple of Zeus** later occupied Tell er-Ras.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Mount Gerizim (Jabal at-Tur)**, ~880 m, immediately south of **Nablus / Tell Balata** in
the West Bank. A small **Samaritan community (Kiryat Luza)** still lives on its slope and
performs the **Passover sacrifice** on the mountain — one of the oldest continuously observed
rites in the world.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Gerizim is the **theological counter-Jerusalem**. For the **Samaritans** it is *the* place
God chose, ordained in their Torah; for **Judeans/Jews** the choice fell on Zion. The two
claims draw on the **same scriptural traditions** with opposite conclusions (Coogan Ch. 8),
making Gerizim the geographical crux of the entire Samaritan–Jewish schism — and, in **John
4**, the foil for Jesus' relativizing of all sacred-place rivalry.

## Key Events and Controversies

The Deuteronomic blessing ceremony; the Samaritan Pentateuch's Gerizim reading; the building
and dating of the temple (Persian 5th c. vs. Josephus' 4th-c. Sanballat account); John
Hyrcanus' destruction (128 BCE); the John 4 dialogue; the surviving Samaritan Passover.
