# Lahore

The historic capital of the Punjab and, under [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]] (1799–1849), the seat of the
Sikh kingdom. This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1**.

## In Sikh History (Khushwant Singh)

Singh notes that until the fifteenth century the Punjab had "only two important cities," Lahore — "the seat
of most governments" — and Multan (Vol. 1, ch. 1). Lahore is woven through the Sikh story: it was the city to
which [[guru-arjan|Guru Arjan]] was taken for execution under Jahangir (1606); the seat of the Mughal
governors Zakarya Khan and Mir Mannu whose persecutions filled the city's horse-market, renamed
**Shahidganj** ("place of martyrdom"); and the prize repeatedly fought over in the misl era. The Sufi
Mian Mir, who laid the foundation stone of the [[amritsar|Harimandir]], was of Lahore.

After [[ahmad-shah-abdali|Abdali]]'s eighth invasion (1766), three Sikh sardars partitioned the city —
Lehna Singh holding the centre and fort — and Singh stresses that the Muslim citizens came to prefer Sikh
rule, petitioning the Afghan governor to be handed to Lehna Singh; he calls this "the most decisive factor in
the success of Ranjit Singh thirty-four years later" (ch. 8).

## Ranjit Singh's Capital

Ranjit Singh captured Lahore on **7 July 1799** at the invitation of its oppressed citizens, his first public
act being to pay homage at the Badshahi mosque and the mosque of Wazir Khan (ch. 11). He was crowned
**Maharajah of the Punjab** here on 12 April 1801, and held court in the Mughal Musamman Burj. Lahore remained
the capital of the **[[sikhism|Sarkar Khalsa]]** throughout his reign; he died in the city on 27 June 1839,
"exactly forty years after he had entered Lahore as a conqueror" (ch. 18).
