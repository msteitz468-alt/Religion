# Mari

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Mari** (modern **Tell Hariri**) lay on the **middle Euphrates** in eastern Syria, a great
Bronze Age city commanding the river trade between Mesopotamia and the Levant. Though
**never mentioned in the Bible**, its archives are among the single richest sources for the
Syro-Mesopotamian world of the early second millennium — the broad cultural horizon of the
patriarchal narratives (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Late 3rd millennium**: one of only three Syrian cities (with [[ebla|Ebla]] and Tell
  Beidar) to yield early written documents.
- **19th–18th c. BCE (the archives)**: the sprawling **palace of Zimri-Lim** (over 260
  ground-floor rooms across ~2.5 ha) yielded **some 25,000 tablets** — about 2,000 of them
  **letters** giving "inside information, not royal propaganda." The city passed from
  **Yahdun-Lim**, into the empire of **Shamshi-Adad I** (who installed his lax son
  **Yasmah-Adad**), then back to **Zimri-Lim**.
- **c. 1760 BCE**: **Hammurapi of Babylon**, long Zimri-Lim's ally, turned on him and
  **destroyed Mari**; the region (Khana) later centered on **Terqa**.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavated from 1933 (André Parrot), Mari produced the palace, temples, and the vast archive
illuminating Amorite-era diplomacy, the pastoral **[[amorites|Hanean]]** tribes (Simalites
and Yaminites) along the Euphrates, and **prophecy** — letters reporting omens, dreams, and
oracular utterances to the king, often compared with later Israelite prophecy. The texts
also mention a **Hazor** (probably [[hazor|Canaanite Hazor]], though a Syrian namesake is
possible), the largest town then in Canaan.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tell Hariri**, on the Euphrates near the Syria–Iraq border in Deir ez-Zor Governorate; the
site and its museum holdings suffered damage and looting during the recent Syrian war.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Mari has no direct biblical role, but its archives are a cornerstone for reconstructing the
**Amorite cultural world** and for the comparative study of **ancient Near Eastern prophecy**,
treaty, kinship, and pastoral-nomad/state relations that frame the biblical patriarchal
traditions.

## Key Events and Controversies

The reigns of Shamshi-Adad I and Zimri-Lim; Hammurapi's destruction (1760 BCE); the
**Mari prophecy texts** and their relation to biblical prophecy; debates over the "two
Hazors." Related: [[ebla]], [[amorites]], [[babylon]], [[babylonians]], [[hazor]],
[[hammurapi|Hammurapi]].
