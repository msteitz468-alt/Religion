# Karbalá

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 19957–19979.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Karbalá is an Iraqi city containing the shrines of the Imám [[husayn-imam|Ḥusayn]] and his half-brother 'Abbás. It was the site of the Battle of Karbalá (10 Muharram 61 AH / 10 October 680 CE), in which Ḥusayn and his companions were killed (l. 19957–19964). The battle became the paradigm for sacrificial [[martyrdom|martyrdom]] for Shí'ís and also for the Bábís at Ṭabarsí (l. 19965–19967). The city became a leading centre of Shí'í scholarship (l. 19967–19968).

## Shaykhí and Bábí Significance

Karbalá was the residence of Sayyid [[kazim-rashti|Kázim Rashtí]], who made it the centre of Shaykhism (l. 19968–19969). Mullá 'Alí Basṭámí was sent there by the Báb to proclaim his advent (l. 19969–19971), and the Báb's followers were summoned to gather in the city to await the advent of the Mahdí (l. 19971–19974). [[tahirih|Táhirih]] made Karbalá her base until expelled by the Ottoman authorities (l. 19974–19976), and [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] retired there briefly in 1851–52 (l. 19977–19979).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

In Shí'í Islam, the Battle of Karbalá and the martyrdom of Ḥusayn is annually commemorated with great emotional fervour during the holy month of Muharram (l. 17560–17563). It is regarded as sacrificial martyrdom for the cause of truth against the forces of impiety, and continues to inspire Shí'ís to sacrificial action (l. 17556–17565). Ḥusayn is expected to return following the advent of the Mahdí (l. 17564–17566). Iraq has special significance in Shí'ísm as the burial place of six of the twelve [[imams|Imáms]] (l. 19318–19333); Karbalá holds the shrines of the Third Imám Ḥusayn and his half-brother.
