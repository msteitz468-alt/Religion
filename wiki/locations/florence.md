# Florence (Firenze)

The Tuscan city of [[dante-alighieri|Dante]]'s birth (1265) and exile (condemned
1302), and the omnipresent backdrop of the [[divine-comedy|*Commedia*]]. Though the
poem is set in the afterlife, Florence is its "real" subject: the souls Dante meets
are overwhelmingly Florentines, and the poem is in part a reckoning with his city's
corruption and faction.

## In the Divine Comedy

Florentine politics structure the [[inferno|Inferno]] from Ciacco's prophecy of the
Black–White [[guelphs-and-ghibellines|Guelf]] conflict (Canto 6) onward. Major
Florentine souls include [[farinata-degli-uberti|Farinata degli Uberti]] (Canto
10), [[brunetto-latini|Brunetto Latini]] (Canto 15), the Guelf nobles of Canto 16,
and the false counsellors and traitors of the lower circles. Kirkpatrick notes
Dante's legend of the city's origin: a Roman colony founded under Mars (its first
patron), later displaced by the Christian Saint John the Baptist — yet enough of
"Mars's temple" remained that the city's violent strain persisted (Inf. 13:142–50).
Fiesole, the hill-town above, was held to be the source of Florence's seditious
element, against the "sacred seed of Rome" (Inf. 15:76).

## Significance

Dante writes as an exile addressing — and indicting — his lost home; the poem is
offered partly "as a means to the restoration of his reputation among his fellow
Florentines" (Par. 25:1–9). The contrast between corrupt contemporary Florence and
an idealized austere Florence of the past becomes a sustained theme through
Paradiso (esp. 15–17).
