# Ebla

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Ebla** (modern **Tell Mardikh**), southwest of **Aleppo**, was one of the great cities of
**third-millennium northern Syria**. Around 2500 BCE a striking wave of urbanization swept
the region, and Ebla expanded to some **61 hectares (150 acres)**, taking charge of the
agricultural and trade resources the southern Mesopotamian cities had relied on (Pitard,
Coogan Ch. 1). **Not mentioned in the Bible**, Ebla is nonetheless central to understanding
early Syria.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Mid-3rd millennium (the archive)**: Ebla is **one of only three Syrian cities** (with
  [[mari|Mari]] and Tell Beidar) to yield written documents from this period. Its palace
  archive — many thousands of tablets in **Eblaite**, an early Semitic language — documents
  a far-flung commercial network and a sophisticated administration.
- **c. 23rd century BCE**: the city was **destroyed** — variously credited to the kings of
  **Akkad** (Sargon or Naram-Sin) or to **Mari**.
- **Middle Bronze Age**: Ebla **revived** as an Amorite-era center with monumental palaces
  and temples, before a final destruction c. 1600 BCE.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavated from 1964 (Paolo Matthiae), Ebla's **state archive** transformed the study of
third-millennium Syria, attesting an early Semitic urban civilization, extensive trade, and
a pantheon and onomasticon that triggered (often overstated) early debates about supposed
biblical parallels. Ebla shows that **Syria was an economic peer of southern Mesopotamia**,
not a mere conduit, well before the rise of Mari.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tell Mardikh**, Idlib Governorate, northwestern Syria, ~55 km southwest of Aleppo; the
site lies in a region heavily affected by the recent Syrian conflict.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Ebla has no biblical role, but its rediscovery reshaped the picture of the **pre-patriarchal
Near East** and is a caution about premature "biblical parallel" claims: early sensational
links between Ebla's texts and Genesis were largely retracted on closer reading.

## Key Events and Controversies

The mid-3rd-millennium urban explosion of north Syria; the Akkad-vs-Mari debate over Ebla's
destruction; the overheated 1970s "Ebla and the Bible" controversy. Related: [[mari]],
[[amorites]], [[ugarit]], [[byblos]].
