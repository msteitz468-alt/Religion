# Gold Base (Gilman Hot Springs / "Int Base")

**Gold Base** is the name given to the Church of Scientology's most senior administrative and operational complex, located in **Gilman Hot Springs, Riverside County, California**. It serves as the headquarters of the **Religious Technology Center (RTC)** and **Golden Era Productions**, and is effectively the nerve center of the entire [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] under [[david-miscavige|David Miscavige]].

## Geographical and Historical Overview

The site was formerly a resort and mineral-springs spa. The Church acquired it in stages beginning around 1978–1980, disguising the purchase behind the fictitious front name **"Scottish Highland Quietude Club"** (a designation that appeared on local signage and records for years).

The property sits in a dry valley outside Hemet, approximately 90 miles east of Los Angeles. Surrounding terrain is desert scrub. Modern setting: accessible via Highway 79 but remote enough to impede casual observation or escape.

## Physical Description and Security

Gold Base is one of the most heavily fortified private properties in the United States, according to multiple former residents. Security elements documented across sources:

- **Perimeter**: 3-inch inward-facing razor-wire spike fence (designed to keep people *in*, not out); motion detectors
- **Armed guards**: uniformed security personnel; guard shack at the main entrance
- **Surveillance**: drone surveillance, extensive closed-circuit camera system covering all buildings and roads
- **Highway 79**: runs directly past the main gate; proximity made the base semi-visible from the road but isolation was maintained by distance and security infrastructure
- **"Blow drill"**: supervised by security chief Gary "Jackson" Morehead — protocol for recapturing Sea Org members who escape ("blow"). Morehead told [[wright-going-clear|Wright]] that no one who escaped ever successfully returned; all were recovered. Headley provides the most detailed first-hand account (see below)
- **Riverside County Sheriff's**: Sergeant Joe Borja confirmed to Wright that the Church had an arrangement with the county; escapees who reached law enforcement were typically returned. Headley's own escape in January 2005 was a notable exception — Riverside County Sheriffs declined to return him

**Shore story**: Staff were instructed to tell anyone who asked that they worked at **"Golden Era Productions, a music and video production company"** — never mentioning Scientology. This cover was maintained for the general public and any external contacts.

## Major Facilities and Buildings

The following facilities are documented across ingested sources, with Marc Headley (*Blown for Good*, 2009) providing the most detailed building-level account:

- **Cine Castle**: main film production building; houses editing bays, screening rooms, and film production departments
- **A/V Manufacturing**: audio/video duplication and distribution facility; Headley's primary work site for most of his tenure
- **Building 50**: Miscavige's flagship office complex, completed approximately 2003; cost approximately **$70 million**; three workers died during its construction, including a woman named **Stacey**, who was electrocuted. Headley describes its scale as disproportionate to any practical function — a monument to Miscavige's personal authority
- **Bonnie View**: L. Ron Hubbard's personal mansion on the property; maintained after his death in 1986 and kept ready for his expected return; furnished seasonally per Miscavige's direction; Hubbard's Rolls-Royces and other vehicles remain on the property
- **Studio One**: Hubbard's personal music recording studio on the base
- **Golden Era Productions** complex: the Church's in-house media production facility, producing training films, promotional videos, and music recordings
- **The Golf Course**: recreational facility on property; reportedly used by Miscavige
- **The Villas**: staff housing blocks; communal residential buildings for Sea Org members stationed at the base
- **Aeration pond**: sewage treatment structure at the edge of the property; used as a punishment site — hundreds of staff were ordered to empty it by hand over multiple days when dried sediment needed removal (Headley was among those so assigned)
- **Eagle lookout**: an elevated guard post on the property; Headley describes an incident in which a guard named Kevin Posten aimed a rifle at Miscavige from this position

### The Highway Tunnel

Headley documents the construction of a **tunnel** underneath or adjacent to Highway 79, built to address highway noise that had become a problem in areas near Hubbard's former garden and residence areas. The project involved significant engineering challenges including repeated flooding. It represents one of many major infrastructure undertakings that Sea Org members at Gold Base were assigned to as part of their regular duties.

## Confinement Areas

- **The Hole**: a set of double-wide trailers on the base used to confine senior executives deemed out-of-ethics by Miscavige; up to 70 people confined at one time. See [[the-hole|The Hole]]
- **Happy Valley**: a secluded area on or adjacent to the Gold Base property used for confinement of specific individuals; [[annie-broeker|Annie Broeker/Tidman]] is documented by Headley as confined here or in a small house near "Old Gilman's" at the property edge

## The Blow Drill — Headley's Account

**Marc Headley** (*Blown for Good*) provides the most detailed first-hand account of how the Blow Drill functioned:

1. Security Alert called upon confirmation that a staff member has escaped
2. All exits secured; Security vehicles dispatch toward the escapee's last known location or likely route
3. Local law enforcement contacts alerted; the Church had arrangements with local agencies to return escapees
4. The escapee's **PC folder** (personal auditing files) reviewed for information about potential destinations — friends, relatives, places of significance mentioned in sessions
5. Family members still at the base used as a lever — the escapee's separation from family is emphasized

Headley triggered the Blow Drill on January 5, 2005, departing on a motorcycle. A Security SUV pursued him and ran him off the road. Riverside County Sheriffs arrived and declined to return him. He continued via U-Haul to Kansas City. His wife [[claire-headley|Claire Headley]] escaped separately via Greyhound bus, was intercepted in Las Vegas by RTC representative Greg Wilhere, refused to return, and joined Marc in Kansas City.

## Working Conditions

Multiple former residents (Marc Headley, Amy Scobee, Tom De Vocht, Claire Headley, Mariette Lindstein, Mike Rinder, Marty Rathbun, and others) describe:

- **Pay**: $13–$50 per week; further reduced through the Team Share Card system (zero cards = no pay, no food, no housing)
- **Food**: 75¢ per meal allotment; 15-minute meal breaks standard; speed-eating was an unofficial norm
- **Clothing**: two sets as a lifetime allotment
- **Hours**: 100+ hours per week common; no holidays observed; weeks ended Thursday per Hubbard's administrative calendar
- All personal phone calls monitored; limited access to outside media
- No cash, no access to personal bank accounts
- Sea Org members may not leave without escort

Against this: Miscavige's personal compound received food budgets of $3,000–$20,000 per week; his lifestyle at the base features Macallan Scotch, silk pajamas, sheepskin slippers, and a $150,000 custom stereo (per Wright's interviews with former staff; confirmed in material details by Headley).

## Hubbard Connection

Hubbard apparently never permanently lived at Gold Base but visited. A fully staffed mansion (Bonnie View) was maintained for his return after his death in 1986. His vehicles remain on the property. Hubbard's **"Advices"** — unsigned management directives issued during his period in hiding — were transmitted to Gold Base management; the unsigned format was used to maintain legal deniability regarding his continued management role (per [[atack-a-piece-of-blue-sky|Atack]]).

## Modern Status

Gold Base remains operational. Former Sea Org member John Brousseau and others confirm its continued use as Miscavige's base of operations. The Church has never publicly confirmed its function, routinely describing it as a "production facility."
