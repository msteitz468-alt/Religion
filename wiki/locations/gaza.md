# Gaza

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Gaza**, the **southernmost** of the five **[[philistines|Philistine]] pentapolis** cities,
sat astride the vital land route between **Egypt and the Levant** ("the way of the land of
the Philistines," Exod. 13.17). Long a Canaanite town and a key **Egyptian administrative
base** in the Late Bronze Age, it became, after c. 1175 BCE, a principal Philistine city
(Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). Because the ancient mound lies **beneath the living modern city**,
it is the least excavated of the pentapolis.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Canaanite/Egyptian → Philistine**: from an Egyptian-controlled Canaanite town to a
  Philistine stronghold; its warm coastal plain produced **heavier wines** (Stager, Coogan
  Ch. 3).
- **Genesis 10.19; Joshua 13**: Gaza marks the Canaanite border and is listed among the
  Philistine lords' cities.
- **Judges 16**: the climax of the **Samson** cycle — Samson carries off Gaza's city gates,
  is later blinded and set to grinding grain there, and pulls down the **temple of Dagon**
  on the Philistine lords.
- **Amos 1.6–7; Jeremiah 25, 47; Zephaniah 2.4; Zechariah 9.5**: prophetic oracles against
  Gaza and Philistia.
- **Assyrian/Babylonian**: a tributary and at times rebel city (Hezekiah's revolt context,
  2 Kings 18.8); later under Babylon and Persia.
- **Acts 8.26**: "the road that goes down from Jerusalem to **Gaza**… a desert road," where
  Philip meets the Ethiopian eunuch.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Gaza is named in **Egyptian** itineraries and **Assyrian** annals as a strategic node on the
coastal highway. Its continuous occupation to the present has buried most Philistine-era
remains under later Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, and Islamic Gaza. **Alexander the Great**
famously besieged and stormed it in 332 BCE.

## Modern Identification and Geography

The ancient city underlies **modern Gaza City (Ghazza)** in the **Gaza Strip**, on the
southern coastal plain near the Mediterranean — still on the historic Egypt–Levant corridor.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Gaza is forever the city of **Samson's** humiliation and revenge — the blinded judge bringing
down Dagon's house — and a fixed target of the **oracles against Philistia**. As the gateway
between Egypt and Canaan it carries the geography of Exodus and conquest.
