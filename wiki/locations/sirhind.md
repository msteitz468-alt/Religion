# Sirhind

The Mughal provincial seat that became "anathema to the Sikhs" as the place where the two younger sons of
[[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind Singh]] were executed — and, in consequence, the target of the most
emphatic acts of Sikh vengeance. This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1,
Chs. 5–8**.

## In Sikh History (Khushwant Singh)

Sirhind was the seat of the governor **Wazir Khan**, who in 1704 had Gobind Singh's sons Zorawar Singh and
Fateh Singh put to death — his adviser Sucha Nand urging that "the young ones of a snake are as poisonous"
(Singh, ch. 5, fn.32). [[banda-bahadur|Banda Bahadur]] stormed and destroyed the city at the **Battle of
Sirhind** (24 May 1710), killing Wazir Khan; it was "destroyed in detail; only a few mausolea were spared"
(ch. 6). It was sacked again in 1764, when "at the site of the execution of Guru Gobind's sons, a temple was
raised and named **Fatehgarh**, the fort of Victory" (ch. 8). The Afghan chronicler Nur Mohammed found it a
ruin: "The whole city lay in ruins. No man, not even a bird, was to be seen there except the owl."
