# Ekron

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Ekron (**Tel Miqne**), on the frontier between **Philistia** and **Judah**, was a small
[[canaanites|Canaanite]] town that the **[[philistines|Philistines]]** rebuilt c. 1175
BCE into a city **five times larger**, one of the best-excavated of the pentapolis and a
showcase of Philistine urban planning (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Canaanite → Philistine**: over the ashes of the ~4 ha Late Bronze town the
  Philistines built a ~20 ha city with massive mud-brick walls, abundant **Mycenaean
  IIIC pottery**, and a **palace-temple complex with an Aegean-style sunken hearth**,
  spool-weight weaving rooms, and cult objects paralleling Cyprus and the Jerusalem
  Temple.
- **The Bible**: one of the five Philistine lords' cities (Josh. 13). The captured ark
  reaches Ekron, spreading panic (1 Sam. 5–6). **2 Kings 1**: Ahaziah consults
  **"Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron"** (later "Beelzebul"). Prophetic oracles (Amos 1;
  Jer. 25; Zeph. 2).
- **Assyrian period (7th c.)**: under Assyrian vassalage Ekron became **the largest
  olive-oil production center in the country** — over a hundred oil installations
  (estimated 1,000+ tons annually) just inside the walls (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7). Its king
  **Padi** was deposed by Hezekiah's rebels and restored by Sennacherib (701). A royal
  **dedicatory inscription** (of Ikausa/Achish son of Padi) — the first of its kind from
  a Philistine city — was found here.
- **c. 604/603 BCE**: destroyed by Babylon (see [[babylonians]]).

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavations (Tel Miqne–Ekron) revealed the Philistine fortifications, the
palace-temple, the industrial olive-oil zone, and the **Ekron royal inscription** that
confirmed the site's identity and named a goddess and a line of kings. Ekron is a
key site for tracing Philistine **acculturation** and the Iron II economy.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tel Miqne**, inner coastal plain/Shephelah of central Israel, on the old
Philistia–Judah border.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Ekron embodies the Philistine "other" at Judah's doorstep, and its god **Baal-zebub /
Beelzebul** became, in later tradition, a name for the prince of demons (Mark 3.22 par.).
Its olive-oil economy illustrates the prosperity of the Assyrian-dominated 7th-century
Levant.
