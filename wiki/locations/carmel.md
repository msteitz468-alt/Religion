# Mount Carmel

**Scope of this page.** This page is primarily about **Mount Carmel**, the ridge in the north where
[[elijah|Elijah]] confronts the prophets of Baal in [[1-2-kings|1 Kings 18]]. The Hebrew Bible also
uses the name **Carmel** of a **town in the hill country of Judah**, south of [[hebron|Hebron]] —
the home of Nabal and Abigail in [[1-samuel|1 Sam 25]], and a place where [[saul|Saul]] sets up a
monument after the Amalekite campaign (1 Sam 15:12). That Judahite Carmel is a different place, and
is disambiguated below rather than treated at length here.

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Mount Carmel is not a peak but a wooded limestone **ridge** running northwest from the Samarian
highlands to the Mediterranean, ending in the promontory above the bay at Haifa, with the
[[jezreel|Jezreel]] Valley opening to its northeast. Its name means orchard or garden land, and the
biblical writers use it proverbially for lushness: to say Carmel withers is to say the land is
under judgment (Amos 1:2; Isa 33:9; Nah 1:4), and to say Carmel flourishes is to promise
restoration (Isa 35:2).

Its narrative importance is concentrated in a single episode. Under [[ahab|Ahab]] and the Sidonian
queen [[jezebel|Jezebel]], with the Baal cult established in [[samaria|Samaria]] and YHWH's
prophets hunted, Elijah summons all Israel and the prophets of Baal to Carmel for a public test of
which god answers by fire.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants

- **Josh 12:22; 19:26** — Carmel as a territorial marker in the northern allotments (Jokneam "in
  Carmel"; the border of Asher reaching Carmel).
- **1 Kgs 18:19-40 — the contest.** After three years of drought Elijah has Ahab gather all Israel
  to Mount Carmel with the four hundred fifty prophets of Baal and the four hundred prophets of
  Asherah. Elijah puts the question: "How long will you go limping between two different opinions?
  If YHWH is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him." The people answer nothing. Two bulls
  are prepared, no fire set under either. Baal's prophets call from morning until noon and limp
  around their altar; Elijah **mocks** them — "Cry aloud, for he is a god; either he is musing, or
  he has gone aside, or he is on a journey, or perhaps he is asleep and must be awakened" — and
  they cry louder and **cut themselves with swords and lances until the blood gushed out**, raving
  until the time of the evening offering. No voice, no answer, no attention. Elijah repairs the
  ruined altar of YHWH with **twelve stones** for the twelve tribes, digs a trench, lays the wood
  and the cut bull, and has four jars of water poured over it three times until the water runs
  round and fills the trench. He prays briefly; the fire of YHWH falls and consumes the burnt
  offering, the wood, the stones, the dust, and licks up the water in the trench. The people fall
  on their faces: "YHWH, he is God; YHWH, he is God."
- **1 Kgs 18:40 — the slaughter at the Kishon.** Elijah orders the prophets of Baal seized — "do
  not let one of them escape" — brings them down to the **Wadi Kishon**, and **slaughters them
  there**. The narrative does not soften this and neither does the wiki.
- **1 Kgs 18:41-46** — Elijah climbs to the top of Carmel, bows to the ground with his face between
  his knees, and sends his servant seven times to look toward the sea until a cloud the size of a
  man's hand rises; the sky goes black, the great rain comes, and Elijah runs before Ahab's chariot
  to the entrance of [[jezreel|Jezreel]].
- **1 Kgs 19:1-2** — Jezebel, told that Elijah has killed all the prophets with the sword, swears
  to make his life like one of theirs; Elijah flees toward Horeb.
- **2 Kgs 2:25; 4:25** — Carmel as a station of [[elisha|Elisha]]; the Shunammite woman rides to
  "the man of God at Mount Carmel."
- **Amos 1:2; 9:3; Isa 33:9; 35:2; Jer 46:18; 50:19; Nah 1:4; Song 7:5** — Carmel as the type of
  fertility, height, and hiding place, invoked in oracles of judgment and restoration.

**The Judahite Carmel (disambiguation):**

- **1 Sam 15:12** — Saul sets up a monument for himself at Carmel after the Amalekite campaign, then
  goes down to [[gilgal|Gilgal]] (see [[agag|Agag]]).
- **1 Sam 25** — Nabal, a very rich man of Maon whose business is in Carmel, insults David's
  messengers at sheepshearing; Abigail intercepts David's raiding column with provisions and turns
  him from the slaughter he has sworn; Nabal's heart dies within him and David takes Abigail as
  wife (as recorded on [[1-samuel|the 1 Samuel page]]).
- **Josh 15:55; 2 Sam 2:2; 3:3** — Carmel among the towns of the Judahite hill country; Abigail
  identified as "the Carmelite."

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The wiki has ingested **no archaeological source on Mount Carmel or on the Judahite Carmel**, and
none of the ingested biblical commentaries reports excavation results for either. No stratigraphy,
altar installation, or epigraphic find is reported on this page, because the collection contains no
source from which to report one.

Two things can be said honestly. First, the **ridge itself requires no identification** — it is a
continuously known geographical feature and no dispute attaches to it. Second, **the specific site
of the 1 Kgs 18 contest is not identified.** Traditional sites are shown on the southeastern end of
the ridge, above the Kishon, and their association is a matter of later devotional tradition rather
than of evidence the wiki holds; the page records none of them as established. Where a source is
later ingested treating survey or excavation on the ridge, this section should be filled from it.

**No excavation result is recorded here as confirming or refuting the Carmel contest.**

## Modern Identification and Geography

The **Carmel ridge** in northwestern Israel, running roughly 25 km northwest from the Samarian hills
to the Mediterranean and terminating in the headland above the bay at Haifa, is the biblical Mount
Carmel; the identification is secure on continuity of the name and on the fit with the biblical
data (a wooded height overlooking the sea, from whose summit a servant can watch the Mediterranean
horizon for cloud, 1 Kgs 18:43; adjacent to the Kishon, 18:40; bordering the Jezreel Valley and
within a chariot ride of Jezreel, 18:45-46).

The **Judahite Carmel** is generally located in the hill country south of [[hebron|Hebron]], near
Maon and Ziph, on the basis of the town lists of Josh 15 and the geography of the David narratives;
the wiki records that as the standard placement and holds no source arguing it in detail.

The Carmel ridge carries substantial post-biblical sacred use — Jewish, Christian (the Carmelite
order takes its name from it), Muslim (Jebel Mar Elyas, after Elijah), and Bahá'í, the Shrine of the
Báb and the Bahá'í world centre standing on its northern slope at Haifa (see [[the-bab|the Báb]]).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Carmel is where [[1-2-kings|Kings]] poses the question of exclusive loyalty in its sharpest public
form. [[iain-provan|Provan]] (NIBC), as recorded on [[elijah|the Elijah page]] and on
[[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]], reads the Elijah–Elisha block as **salvation within judgment** and Carmel
as the **public "who is God?"**: the twelve stones make the altar an image of all Israel; Elijah's
brief prayer stands against ecstatic Baal-prophecy; [[ahab|Ahab]] is shown to be as impotent as
Baal; and — Provan's point, recorded as his — **Jezebel and the Asherah prophets remain
under-fought**, so that the victory is real and incomplete at once. On this reading the drought,
the fire, and the rain together demonstrate that the living God, not Baal, controls rain,
fertility, life and death.

The slaughter at the Kishon is part of the same narrative and is not softened here. It is also the
immediate trigger of the reversal: Jezebel's threat sends the victor of Carmel into flight and
despair, and the Horeb theophany that follows — wind, earthquake, fire, and then the low whisper —
is set deliberately against Carmel's fire. The wiki records this juxtaposition as the text's own,
and records Provan's framing of it as his.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

- **The contest with the prophets of Baal** (1 Kgs 18:19-40) — [[elijah|Elijah]], [[ahab|Ahab]],
  [[jezebel|Jezebel]], [[phoenicians|Phoenicians]].
- **The slaughter of the four hundred fifty at the Wadi Kishon** (1 Kgs 18:40).
- **The rain and the run to Jezreel** (1 Kgs 18:41-46) — [[jezreel|Jezreel]].
- **Elisha's Carmel station** (2 Kgs 2:25; 4:25) — [[elisha|Elisha]].
- **Disambiguation — Carmel in Judah** (1 Sam 15:12; 25) — Nabal, Abigail, [[david|David]],
  [[saul|Saul]], [[agag|Agag]]. A different place; not to be conflated with the northern ridge.
- **Controversy — the site of the contest.** The ridge is certain; the specific location is not
  identified, and traditional sites rest on later devotional association. Not adjudicated.
- **Controversy — the historical core.** As recorded on [[elijah|Elijah]], the drought and contest
  narratives are widely held to stand on a historical prophet, and comparative material on
  rain-making contests and prophetic guilds is invoked in scholarship; the wiki records this as a
  position summarized on that page, not as a finding of its own.
