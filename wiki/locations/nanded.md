# Nanded

The town on the Godavari in the Deccan where [[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind Singh]] died and declared the
[[guru-granth-sahib|Granth]] the eternal Guru — and where he had earlier met the hermit who became
[[banda-bahadur|Banda Bahadur]]. This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1,
Chs. 5–6**.

## In Sikh History (Khushwant Singh)

Nanded was the site of the fifteen-year hermitage of Madho Das, whom Gobind Singh met in 1708 and renamed
Banda (Singh, ch. 6). Arriving with Bahadur Shah's southward march in September 1707, the Guru was stabbed
there by two Pathan assassins (Singh links them to Wazir Khan of [[sirhind|Sirhind]]); the wounds reopened,
and he died "an hour and a half after midnight on 7 October 1708" (ch. 5). On his deathbed he "assembled his
followers and told them the line of Gurus was to end with him and the Sikhs were thereafter to look upon the
Granth as the symbol of all the ten Gurus and their constant guide" — the [[guru-granth-sahib|Adi Granth]]
becoming **[[the-guru|the eternal Guru]]**.
