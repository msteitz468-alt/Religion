# Beersheba

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Beersheba** sits in the **northern Negev**, at the southern edge of the settled land — the
proverbial southern terminus of Israel's territory in the formula **"from [[dan|Dan]] to
Beersheba."** Its name is explained as **"well of seven" / "well of the oath"** (*be'er
sheva*), tying it to the patriarchal **well-and-treaty** etiologies. In the Iron Age it
became a fortified Judahite administrative town guarding the Beersheba Valley.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Genesis 21.22–34**: **[[abraham|Abraham]]** and **Abimelech** of [[gerar|Gerar]] swear an
  oath over a disputed well; Abraham plants a **tamarisk** and calls on Yahweh — a practice
  later Israel would prohibit (Stager notes such patriarchal customs, Coogan Ch. 1).
- **Genesis 26.23–33**: **[[isaac|Isaac]]** digs wells, makes a treaty with Abimelech, and
  names the place Shibah/Beersheba.
- **Genesis 46.1–5**: **Jacob** offers sacrifice at Beersheba on his way down to Egypt and
  receives a night vision.
- **1 Samuel 8.2**: Samuel's sons serve as judges at Beersheba — its role on the southern
  frontier.
- **Amos 5.5; 8.14**: Beersheba is condemned as a **pilgrimage shrine** ("do not cross over
  to Beersheba"; "as the way of Beersheba lives") — evidence of a popular cult there.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

**Tel Beersheba (Tell es-Seba)** is one of the best examples of a **planned Iron Age
Judahite town**: an oval fortified plan, a sophisticated water system, storehouses, and the
celebrated dismantled **four-horned altar** (its reused ashlars suggesting a cult
**suppressed** — often linked to Hezekiah's reform). The town was destroyed in the
**Assyrian** campaigns of the late eighth century. It is now a **UNESCO World Heritage**
"Biblical Tel."

## Modern Identification and Geography

The ancient mound is **Tel Beersheba**, ~5 km east of the modern Negev city of **Be'er
Sheva**, the largest city in southern Israel's Negev.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Beersheba marks the **southern boundary of the promised land** and is woven through the
**patriarchal narratives** as a place of wells, oaths, altars, and theophany — a site where
the ancestors' claim to the land's water and the God of the fathers are repeatedly affirmed.
Its later condemnation by Amos shows the tension between **patriarchal sanctity and
prophetic cult-critique**.

## Key Events and Controversies

The well-and-oath etiologies (Abraham, Isaac); Jacob's vision; the horned altar and the
question of a **suppressed cult** (Hezekiah's reform); Amos against the Beersheba pilgrimage;
the Assyrian destruction. Related: [[abraham]], [[isaac]], [[gerar]], [[dan]], [[israelites]],
[[jerusalem]], [[amos]].
