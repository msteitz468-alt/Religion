# Ramoth-gilead

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Ramoth-gilead is a Transjordanian highland town with two distinct roles in the Hebrew Bible. In
the allotment and law traditions it is a **city of refuge** and a **Levitical town** in the
territory of Gad, one of the three refuge cities east of the Jordan. In the narrative of
[[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]] it is a **contested frontier fortress** on the Israelite–Aramean border,
fought over repeatedly in the 9th century BCE — the place where [[ahab|Ahab]] dies from a chance
arrow, where his son Joram is wounded, and where a prophet's servant pours oil on
[[jehu|Jehu]]'s head and starts the coup that destroys the house of Omri.

Its name, "heights of Gilead," fits its function: a high place on the eastern plateau, valuable
enough that two Israelite kings die or are wounded trying to hold it.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants

**As city of refuge and Levitical town:**

- **[[deuteronomy|Deut 4:41-43]]** — Moses sets apart three cities east of the Jordan for the
  unintentional manslayer: Bezer for the Reubenites, **Ramoth in Gilead for the Gadites**, and
  Golan in Bashan for the Manassites.
- **[[book-of-joshua|Josh 20:8]]** — the same three named as cities of refuge beyond the Jordan
  east of Jericho.
- **Josh 21:38 // 1 Chr 6:80** — Ramoth in Gilead with its pasturelands given to the
  [[levites|Levites]] of the Merarite clans out of the tribe of Gad.

**As administrative seat:**

- **1 Kgs 4:13** — Ben-geber is Solomon's officer "in Ramoth-gilead," with the villages of Jair and
  the region of Argob in Bashan; one of the twelve districts provisioning the royal house.

**As contested border fortress:**

- **1 Kgs 22:1-40 // 2 Chr 18** — **Ahab's last campaign.** After three years of peace with Aram,
  Ahab says to his servants, "Do you know that Ramoth-gilead is ours, and we keep quiet and do not
  take it out of the hand of the king of Aram?" He enlists Jehoshaphat of Judah. Four hundred
  prophets promise success; Micaiah son of Imlah, summoned at Jehoshaphat's insistence, first
  parrots them and then reports Israel scattered on the mountains like sheep without a shepherd, and
  a lying spirit in the mouth of the prophets. Zedekiah son of Chenaanah strikes him on the cheek;
  Micaiah is jailed on bread and water. Ahab goes into battle **disguised** while Jehoshaphat wears
  his robes; a man draws his bow **at random** and strikes Ahab **between the scale armor and the
  breastplate**. Propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans, he dies at evening, the blood
  running into the bottom of the chariot; the army disperses. The chariot is washed at the pool of
  [[samaria|Samaria]] and the dogs lick up his blood.
- **2 Kgs 8:28-29 // 2 Chr 22:5-6** — **Joram wounded.** Joram son of Ahab, with Ahaziah of Judah,
  fights Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead; the Arameans wound Joram, and he returns to
  [[jezreel|Jezreel]] to be healed. Ahaziah goes down to visit him there.
- **2 Kgs 9:1-14** — **Jehu anointed at Ramoth-gilead.** [[elisha|Elisha]] sends a member of the
  prophetic guild with a flask of oil to Ramoth-gilead, where the army is holding the town against
  Hazael. He calls Jehu out from among the commanders into an inner chamber, pours the oil on his
  head with the charge to strike down the house of Ahab and avenge the blood of the prophets and of
  the servants of YHWH shed by [[jezebel|Jezebel]], and flees. Jehu's fellow officers press him;
  when he tells them, they spread their garments under him on the bare steps, blow the trumpet, and
  proclaim "Jehu is king." He conspires against Joram and rides for Jezreel.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The wiki has ingested **no archaeological source on Ramoth-gilead**, and none of the ingested
biblical commentaries reports excavation results for it. Because **no site has been securely
identified**, there is no stratigraphy, no ceramic sequence, and no epigraphic material to report
for the place under this name.

Nothing is recorded here from survey or excavation of any candidate tell, and **no excavation
result is recorded as confirming or refuting any biblical event at the site**. Where a source is
later ingested treating the survey and excavation of northern Jordanian candidate sites, this
section should be filled from it.

The broader Israelite–Aramean warfare of the period is attested outside the Bible in Aramaic royal
inscription material; the wiki holds no ingested source treating that evidence and therefore makes
no claim here about whether any extrabiblical text refers to the struggle over this particular
town.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Unresolved.** The biblical data are internally consistent and locate the town in the
Transjordanian uplands of Gilead: it lies east of the Jordan in Gadite territory (Deut 4:43; Josh
20:8), high enough for the name "heights," within a Solomonic district that also embraced Bashan
and the villages of Jair (1 Kgs 4:13), and on the frontier facing Damascus — close enough to Aram
to be lost to it and worth two Israelite royal campaigns to recover, and far enough from
[[jezreel|Jezreel]] that a wounded king withdraws there to convalesce and that
[[jehu|Jehu]]'s approach can be spotted by a watchman only at the last stage.

Several tells in northern Jordan have been proposed as candidates. **The wiki names none as the
identification**, because the collection contains no source adjudicating the question, and because
the shorter form "Ramah" (2 Kgs 8:29) and the common Hebrew element *rāmâ*/"height" multiply the
possibilities for confusion with other places bearing similar names. This is recorded as an open
question, not adjudicated.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Ramoth-gilead is where [[1-2-kings|Kings]] tests the prophetic word against royal ambition, twice.
In 1 Kgs 22 the question is not whether Ramoth-gilead should be taken but **whose word is true**:
four hundred prophets against one, and the narrative resolves it not by argument but by an arrow
shot at random that finds the one seam in a disguised king's armor. [[iain-provan|Provan]] (NIBC),
as recorded on [[1-2-kings|the Kings page]] and on [[ahab|Ahab]], names **"Micaiah's true word"**
among the load-bearing episodes of the Elijah–Elisha block, which he reads as **salvation within
judgment**, and treats **prophecy-and-fulfillment as the engine of the history**. Recorded as
Provan's position.

The second Ramoth-gilead scene inverts the first. The same frontier that killed Ahab is where the
prophetic guild anoints the man who will destroy Ahab's house — the oracle spoken in
[[jezreel|Naboth's vineyard]] handed on, in an inner chamber on the border, to a chariot commander.
Provan's recorded framing of [[jehu|Jehu]] as **incomplete** — executing the sentence on Omri's
house and keeping the calves of [[bethel|Bethel]] and [[dan|Dan]] — governs how the wiki reads what
begins here.

Alongside all of this the town's first identity persists: a **city of refuge**, a place established
by law so that a man who kills without intent may live. That a sanctuary town becomes the wiki's
clearest example of a disputed border killing-ground is a juxtaposition the text does not comment
on and neither does the wiki.
