# Síyáh-Chál

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Overview

Síyáh-Chál (Persian: "Black Pit") was the underground dungeon in Tehran in which [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] and other Babis were imprisoned following the assassination attempt on [[nasiru-d-din-shah|Násiru'd-Dín Sháh]] in 1852 (lines 29350–29356). It was a disused cistern reached along a narrow corridor and down three steep flights of steps; as many as one hundred and fifty prisoners might be held there simultaneously, in conditions of appalling discomfort (lines 29356–29361).

## Events

The Babis were chained together; many were taken away for execution, but they retained their spirit, chanting verses of scripture with such fervour that they could apparently be heard in the royal palace nearby (lines 29361–29366). Bahá'u'lláh was weighed down with the infamous chains "Qara-Guhar" (51 kg in weight) and "Sálásil" (lines 29367–29369).

It was here that Bahá'u'lláh experienced the initiatory visions that marked the beginning of his mission (ESW 21–2; lines 29369–29372). [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] described it as the second holiest Bahá'í site in Iran (MBW 80; line 29374). The Pit was later closed. It was in Bahá'í ownership from 1954 until the Islamic Revolution of 1979 (lines 29372–29376).

## Theological Significance

The Síyáh-Chál experience is central to Bahá'u'lláh's prophetic biography: the initiatory visions received there are understood as the moment when his mission began, analogous to the prophetic calls of earlier Manifestations of God.
