# Byblos

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Byblos (Semitic **Gubla/Gebal**, biblical **Gebal**; modern **Jbeil**, Lebanon) is one
of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and, from the third millennium
BCE, **Egypt's chief trade port** on the Levantine coast — the principal source of
**cedar** and other timber (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Bronze Age trade hub**: through the Early and Middle Bronze Ages Byblos was the
  pivot of Egyptian sea trade with the Levant.
- **Late Bronze Age (Amarna)**: its king **Rib-Addi** wrote or received **67 of the
  Amarna letters** — far more than any other ruler — endlessly begging the pharaoh for
  help against **Abdi-ashirta and Aziru of Amurru**, to no avail (Pitard). A vivid case
  study in the collapse of Egyptian vassal politics. See [[apiru-habiru]], [[amorites]].
- **The Bible**: the **"Giblites/Gebalites"** are skilled stoneworkers and shipwrights —
  builders for Solomon's Temple (1 Kings 5.18) and caulkers of Tyre's ships (Ezek. 27.9);
  Gebal marks a boundary of the land (Josh. 13.5).
- **The tale of Wen-Amun** (11th c.): an Egyptian envoy's troubled dealings with the
  prince of Byblos, illustrating the decline of Egyptian prestige.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavations exposed Bronze Age temples, royal tombs (with early alphabetic
inscriptions), and a long architectural sequence into Roman and Crusader times. Byblos
is closely tied to the **alphabet's** development and transmission (see
[[phoenicians]]). Its name underlies Greek *biblos* ("papyrus/book") and hence
"**Bible**."

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Jbeil, Lebanon**, ~37 km north of Beirut on the coast — a UNESCO World Heritage Site
with extensive multi-period remains.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Byblos is less a theological site than a **cultural keystone**: a Phoenician/Canaanite
port whose timber built the Jerusalem Temple, whose craftsmen the Bible praises, and
whose name (via "papyrus → book") is embedded in the word "Bible" itself.

## Key Events and Controversies

The Rib-Addi correspondence (Amarna); the Wen-Amun episode; Giblite craftsmanship for
Solomon and Tyre. Related: [[phoenicians]], [[canaanites]], [[ugarit]], [[amorites]],
[[sea-peoples]], [[1-2-kings]], [[ezekiel]].
