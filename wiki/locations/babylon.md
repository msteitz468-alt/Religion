# Babylon

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Babylon, on the **Euphrates** in southern Mesopotamia, was an ancient city that first
gained prominence under the Amorite king **Hammurapi** (18th c. BCE) and rose to its
greatest glory as the **imperial capital of the Neo-Babylonian Empire** under
**[[nebuchadnezzar|Nebuchadrezzar II]]** (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7; Leith, Ch. 8). Its very
name, *Bābilim*, "gate of the god," underlies the biblical pun "Babel."

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Genesis 11**: the Tower of **Babel** — the archetype of human pride and scattering.
- **Neo-Babylonian capital (6th c. BCE)**: the seat of **Nebuchadrezzar**, who deported
  Judah's elite here. **King Jehoiachin** and his sons appear on cuneiform **ration
  tablets** from Babylon (592 BCE); skilled Judean artisans are named in the records.
- **The Babylonian Exile (597/586–539 BCE)**: the main body of exiles was settled in
  ethnically organized, self-governing communities in the damaged borderlands
  (**Tel-abib**, etc.), keeping their identity intact (see [[babylonians]]). Jeremiah's
  letter (Jer. 29) tells them to "build houses… seek the welfare of the city." Psalm
  137 laments "by the rivers of Babylon."
- **539 BCE**: **Cyrus the Persian** takes Babylon without casualties; the **Cyrus
  Cylinder** is his propaganda foundation document; the exile ends (see [[persians]]).
- **Daniel**: the court tales set in Nebuchadnezzar's and Belshazzar's Babylon.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The **Babylonian Chronicle** (notably evenhanded), the **Jehoiachin ration tablets**,
the **Cyrus Cylinder**, and the **Verse Account of Nabonidus** illuminate the city and
its last kings (Nabonidus absent in Tema; Belshazzar holding the city). German
excavations (Koldewey) uncovered the Ishtar Gate, processional way, and palace
complexes.

## Modern Identification and Geography

The ruins lie near **Hillah** in **Babil Governorate, Iraq**, ~85 km south of Baghdad
on the Euphrates — extensively excavated, partly reconstructed in the late 20th
century, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Babylon is the great biblical symbol of **exile and of arrogant, idolatrous world
power**. It is judgment on Judah's covenant breach, yet also — paradoxically — the
fertile soil where exilic Judaism (Sabbath, prayer, scripture, the Aramaic shift) was
born. The prophetic oracles against Babylon (Isa. 13; 47; Jer. 50–51) proclaim its
doom and the exiles' return.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

The Tower of Babel; Nebuchadrezzar's deportations; Belshazzar's feast (Daniel 5);
Cyrus's bloodless conquest (539). "**Babylon the great**" becomes the cipher for
oppressive empire in apocalyptic — Daniel, then **Revelation 17–18**, and **1 Peter
5.13** where "Babylon" most likely means **Rome**. Related: [[babylonians]],
[[persians]], [[nebuchadnezzar]], [[jerusalem]], [[nineveh]], [[lamentations]],
[[ezra-nehemiah]].
