# Mákú

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 21264–21301.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview
Small town in north-western Iran (Azerbaijan) close to the Russian and Turkish borders. Rising above the town is an overhanging rock face at the base of which there is a fortress. The local population were Sunni Kurds (lines 21264–21278).

## Bábí Significance
The fortress was the site of [[the-bab|The Báb]]'s imprisonment for nine months (July 1847 – 9 April 1848). The imprisonment was initially severe, but the attitude of the governor, 'Alí Khán, later changed (reportedly as a result of a vision), and a flow of Bábí visitors was permitted. The local Sunni Kurdish population, initially hostile, came to regard the Báb as a holy man whose blessing they sought (lines 21268–21279).

The Báb referred to the place as the 'Open Mountain' (*Jabal-i-Bábit*). The town was the birthplace of Ḥájí Mírzá Áqásí, and was under his control — it was no doubt for this reason that it was initially chosen as the Báb's place of confinement. Russian security concerns and the governor's leniency prompted the Báb's subsequent transfer to [[chihriq|Chihríq]] (lines 21282–21290).

## Doctrinal Significance
The Mákú period is significant both as marking the end of any hopes that the Báb might gain the support of the Qájár regime, and in its doctrinal developments: the Báb's composition of the [[bayan|Bayán]] and the *Dalá'il-i-Sab'ih*, and his claim to be the [[qaim|Qá'im]] (lines 21291–21299).
