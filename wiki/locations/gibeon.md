# Gibeon

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Gibeon is a hill-country town northwest of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] whose inhabitants, alone among
the peoples of Canaan in the [[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] narrative, secure their survival by treaty
rather than by battle — and whose defection triggers the Amorite coalition, the hailstones, and
the long day. It reappears at three later hinges: the pool where twelve champions of Abner and
Joab kill one another, the blood-vengeance in which seven of [[saul|Saul]]'s descendants are
impaled, and the great high place where [[solomon|Solomon]] receives his dream.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants

- **Josh 9**: the Gibeonites, hearing what befell [[jericho|Jericho]] and Ai, come with worn-out
  sacks, patched sandals, and dry mouldy bread, claiming a distant homeland. The Israelite leaders
  "did not ask direction from the LORD" and make a covenant with them under oath. Three days later
  the fraud is discovered; the oath is honored, but they are made hewers of wood and drawers of
  water for the house of God. The text calls them Hivites (9:7).
- **Josh 10:1-27**: five [[amorites|Amorite]] kings — of Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth,
  [[lachish|Lachish]], and Eglon — attack Gibeon for defecting. Joshua marches all night from
  [[gilgal|Gilgal]]; the LORD throws the coalition into panic, and more die from the great
  hailstones than by the sword. Joshua calls sun and moon to stand still "over Gibeon" and over
  the Valley of Aijalon, and the day is prolonged, "as it is written in the Book of Jashar."
- **Josh 10:2**: Gibeon is described as "a great city, like one of the royal cities… and all its
  men were mighty."
- **Josh 11:19**: "there was not a city that made peace with the people of Israel except the
  Hivites, the inhabitants of Gibeon."
- **2 Sam 2:12-17**: at the pool of Gibeon twelve champions from Abner's side and twelve from
  Joab's kill one another simultaneously; the place is named Helkath-hazzurim, and a long battle
  follows in which Asahel is killed.
- **2 Sam 21:1-14**: a three-year famine is traced to Saul's slaughter of the Gibeonites, whom the
  text here calls a remnant of the [[amorites|Amorites]] spared under oath. The Gibeonites refuse
  silver and demand seven of Saul's male descendants; [[david|David]] hands them over and they are
  **impaled before YHWH** at Gibeah. Rizpah guards the exposed bodies on sackcloth from the barley
  harvest until the rain falls.
- **1 Kgs 3:4-15 // 2 Chr 1:3-13**: Gibeon is "the great high place," where Solomon offers a
  thousand burnt offerings and receives in a dream the offer that produces his request for a
  discerning heart. Chronicles adds that the tent of meeting and the bronze altar were there.
- **Neh 3:7; 7:25**: men of Gibeon among those of the restoration period.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The wiki has ingested no archaeological monograph on Gibeon; what follows is limited to the basis
of the identification itself. The excavations at **el-Jib** directed by **James B. Pritchard**
(1950s–60s) recovered jar handles bearing the inscription **gbʿn** ("Gibeon"), and these are the
principal evidence linking the site to the biblical name; a large rock-cut water system and
associated installations were also uncovered there. The inference is strong but not
self-evidently decisive — inscribed handles establish a connection to the name, and their
find-context and interpretation have been discussed in the literature. The wiki records the
identification as the leading one and flags that the collection contains no source arguing the
case in detail. **No excavation result is reported here as confirming or refuting any biblical
event at the site.**

[[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]]'s recorded position on Joshua's archaeology generally (see
[[jericho|Jericho]]) is that archaeology "does not answer all questions" and has a limited role in
adjudicating the narratives; that stance is his, not the wiki's verdict.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**el-Jib**, roughly 8–9 km northwest of Jerusalem in the Benjaminite hill country, is the
identification generally adopted, on the epigraphic basis described above. The biblical geography
is consistent with it: Gibeon lies within reach of a night march from the Jordan Valley, adjacent
to the Aijalon corridor (Josh 10:12), and is grouped in Josh 9:17 with Chephirah, Beeroth, and
Kiriath-jearim. The wiki does not treat the identification as beyond dispute and adopts no
position on the precise correlation of excavated strata with the narrative periods.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Gibeon is the case that proves the [[book-of-joshua|conquest]] narrative's own exception.
[[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]], as recorded on [[book-of-joshua|the Joshua page]], stresses the
narrator's explicit criticism — "they did not ask the LORD's counsel" (9:14) — while holding that
the oath sworn by the LORD is kept lest wrath fall for oath-breaking, the life-guarantee being the
narrative's chief concern. On his reading the Gibeonites, spared from *ḥerem* yet reduced to
temple servitude, live as a "monument" both of the required separateness and of Israel's fault in
not seeking counsel; and at 11:19-20, where the LORD hardens Canaanite hearts, Gibeon shows that
"the other way was open" — a presentation Woudstra explicitly refuses to eliminate or soften.

The long day of Josh 10 Woudstra frames as an extraordinary answer to prayer under a biblical
creation theology, declining positivist rationalization and allowing a rather literal reading.
Recorded as his position.

The Gibeonite oath then reaches forward: [[2-samuel|2 Sam 21]] treats Saul's breach of it as
blood-guilt on the land, resolved only by the deaths of seven of his descendants. Oath-keeping
across generations, and the cost of its violation, is the thread that binds Josh 9 to 2 Sam 21 —
a link [[book-of-joshua|the Joshua page]] already registers as an interpretive crux.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

- **The deceptive treaty** (Josh 9) — [[joshua|Joshua]], the Gibeonites, [[canaanites|Canaanites]].
- **The five kings, the hailstones, the long day** (Josh 10) — [[amorites|Amorites]],
  [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]], [[lachish|Lachish]], [[gilgal|Gilgal]].
- **The pool of Gibeon and the champions' combat** (2 Sam 2) — [[abner|Abner]], [[joab|Joab]].
- **The blood-vengeance and impalings** (2 Sam 21) — [[david|David]], [[saul|Saul]]; Rizpah's
  vigil.
- **Solomon's dream at the great high place** (1 Kgs 3 // 2 Chr 1) — [[solomon|Solomon]].
- **Controversy — the Gibeonites' ethnicity.** Josh 9:7 and 11:19 call them Hivites; 2 Sam 21:2
  calls them a remnant of the Amorites. Recorded as a tension in the sources, not harmonized here.
- **Controversy — the long day.** Literal astronomical reading (Woudstra's allowance), poetic
  citation from the Book of Jashar, and rationalizing explanations are competing positions; the
  wiki adjudicates none.
- **Controversy — site identification.** el-Jib is the leading candidate on epigraphic grounds; the
  wiki does not declare the matter closed.
