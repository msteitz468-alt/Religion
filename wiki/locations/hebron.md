# Hebron

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Hebron sits high in the southern hill country of Judah, roughly a day's journey south of
[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. The biblical text gives its older name as Kiriath-arba. It is the
patriarchal burial city, the town [[caleb|Caleb]] claims as his inheritance, [[david|David]]'s
first capital before Jerusalem, and the base from which [[absalom|Absalom]] launches his revolt —
four distinct layers of significance in the same place. It remains a major site of Jewish and
Muslim devotion, and one of the most contested locations in the modern region.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants

- **[[genesis|Genesis]] 13; 18**: [[abraham|Abraham]] settles by the oaks of Mamre near Hebron;
  the visitation and the promise of Isaac occur there.
- **[[genesis|Genesis]] 23 — Machpelah**: on [[sarah|Sarah]]'s death Abraham, "a stranger and a
  sojourner," negotiates with Ephron the Hittite before the assembly at the town gate and buys the
  cave of Machpelah and its field for four hundred shekels of silver — the one piece of the land
  he owns outright. Sarah is buried there.
- **[[genesis|Genesis]] 25; 35; 49–50**: Abraham, [[isaac|Isaac]] and [[rebekah|Rebekah]],
  [[leah|Leah]], and finally [[jacob|Jacob]] are buried in the same cave; Jacob's dying charge and
  the great funeral procession from Egypt make the tomb the family's fixed point in the land.
- **Numbers 13**: the spies reach Hebron and see the descendants of Anak there — the report that
  turns the people back.
- **[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] 10**: the king of Hebron joins the coalition of five
  [[amorites|Amorite]] kings against [[gibeon|Gibeon]]; the kings are struck down, hanged on trees
  until evening, and thrown into the cave at Makkedah. Hebron is then taken in the southern sweep
  and left with no survivor.
- **[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] 14–15**: [[caleb|Caleb]], now eighty-five, claims Hebron on the
  strength of the oath sworn him at Kadesh-barnea, asking for the hill country with its Anakim and
  fortified cities; he drives out the three sons of Anak.
- **[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] 20–21**: Hebron is designated a city of refuge for the unintentional
  manslayer and assigned among the towns of the [[levites|Levites]].
- **[[2-samuel|2 Samuel]] 2–5**: after [[saul|Saul]]'s death [[david|David]] goes up to Hebron at
  the LORD's direction and is anointed king over Judah there, reigning seven years and six months
  before the elders of Israel come to him and he takes [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. Hebron is also
  where [[abner|Abner]] is killed by [[joab|Joab]] at the gate, and where Ish-bosheth's murderers
  are executed.
- **[[2-samuel|2 Samuel]] 15**: [[absalom|Absalom]] asks leave to pay a vow in Hebron, sends
  messengers through the tribes, and has himself proclaimed king there — the old capital used
  against the new one.
- **[[1-2-chronicles|Chronicles]]** repeats the Hebron anointing and the levitical assignment in
  its genealogical and royal material.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The ancient settlement is associated with the tell within the modern city, and the traditional
Cave of Machpelah lies beneath a monumental rectangular enclosure of large ashlar masonry
conventionally dated to the Herodian period, later serving as both synagogue and mosque. This wiki
has ingested no excavation report or archaeological monograph on Hebron; no stratigraphic claim,
dating, or correlation with the biblical narrative is asserted here. Recorded as a gap.

Attributed to [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] ([[woudstra-joshua-nicot|NICOT Joshua]], as recorded on
[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]]): archaeology is useful for Joshua but site-identification is fragile,
and he treats Conquest-date archaeology as an inadequate guide — a caution that applies to the
southern-campaign towns, Hebron among them.

## Modern Identification and Geography

Hebron is the modern city of Hebron / **al-Khalil** in the southern West Bank, roughly 30 km south
of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]], at high elevation in the Judaean hill country. The enclosure over the
traditional burial cave functions today as both the Ibrahimi Mosque and a Jewish prayer site, and
the city is a focus of sustained modern political and religious conflict. The wiki records this as
context; no ingested source here supports analysis of the modern dispute.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Hebron carries two founding claims to the land, one patriarchal and one royal. Machpelah is the
only land Abraham purchases rather than receives by promise — a legally witnessed deed in the
middle of a narrative otherwise governed by promise, and the tomb that anchors the family to
Canaan through the Egyptian sojourn. Attributed to [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] (as recorded on
[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]]): the three graves at the end of Joshua function as a fulfilment symbol
set against Abraham's *purchased* tomb, and [[caleb|Caleb]]'s Hebron claim "paradigmatically shows
what faith can do with 'land that remains'" — Judah's allotment begins with Caleb's faith and ends
with failure at Jebus (Josh 15:63). Woudstra also holds that the OT land promise yields no modern
territorial claims — his position, recorded as such.

The royal layer adds a second beginning: the Davidic monarchy starts at Hebron and only afterwards
moves to Jerusalem, which is why [[absalom|Absalom]] can use Hebron as the natural site of a rival
legitimacy.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

The purchase of Machpelah and the patriarchal burials; the Anakim and the spies' report; the
execution of the king of Hebron and the town's destruction in the southern campaign;
[[caleb|Caleb]]'s inheritance; Hebron as levitical city and city of refuge; David's seven-and-a-
half-year reign and the murder of [[abner|Abner]] at the gate; [[absalom|Absalom]]'s revolt.
Open questions: the relation of Kiriath-arba, Mamre, and Hebron as named sites; the tension
between Josh 10's "no survivor" summary and Caleb's later expulsion of the Anakim from the same
town; the absence of ingested archaeological data.
