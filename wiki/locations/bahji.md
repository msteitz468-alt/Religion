# Bahjí

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from lines 7534–7637 unless noted.*

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Bahjí (Arabic: "Delight") is a mansion north of [[akka|'Akká]] occupied by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] from September 1879 until his death in 1892. The present structure was built by 'Údí Khammar, a Christian merchant, and was completed in 1870. It was here that Bahá'u'lláh wrote most of his later writings, and where he received the English orientalist [[edward-granville-browne|E.G. Browne]] (lines 7534–7544).

When Bahá'u'lláh died he was interred in a room in one of the surrounding buildings occupied by his daughter Furúghiyyih Khánum and her husband, Sayyid 'Alí Afnán. That room became the **Shrine of Bahá'u'lláh**, regarded by Bahá'ís as the most sacred place on earth and the *qiblah* toward which Bahá'ís turn in prayer (lines 7545–7553).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Many members of Bahá'u'lláh's family had lived at Bahjí, in the mansion itself or in surrounding buildings. After Bahá'u'lláh's passing a complex situation developed: 'Abdu'l-Bahá was the majority (two-thirds) owner of the mansion, but the actual occupants were his half-brothers, headed by Muḥammad-'Alí. Given the open animosity of this group toward 'Abdu'l-Bahá (see *covenant-breakers*), visits to the Shrine became fraught. 'Abdu'l-Bahá obtained a small "tea-house" nearby so that he could visit his father's Shrine in greater serenity (lines 7559–7580).

After 'Abdu'l-Bahá's death, Muḥammad-'Alí made legal claim to become custodian of the Shrine, having the keys seized (30 January 1922). The British authorities intervened, retaining the keys until February 1923, when they decided the case in [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]'s favor. The mansion fell into disrepair until Muḥammad-'Alí was finally forced to abandon it in November 1929; Shoghi Effendi immediately began restoration, furnishing it as a Bahá'í museum and place of pilgrimage (by 1932). Complete ownership was secured in 1957 (lines 7581–7599).

## The Gardens

With possession of the mansion, Shoghi Effendi began to beautify the land. In 1952 he acquired over 145,000 sq.m. and began an extensive project to surround the Shrine and mansion with ornamental gardens, personally supervising construction of the Haram-i-Aqdas (Most Holy Precincts or Sanctuary), comprising the north-western quadrant next to the Shrine itself. Houses adjoining the mansion previously occupied by Covenant-breakers were demolished (lines 7601–7632). Further land acquisitions have been made by the Universal House of Justice, completing the remaining three quadrants (lines 7633–7636).
