# Zanjan

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Overview

Zanjan is a city in north-western Iran on the road between Tehran and Tabriz. In 1868 its population was estimated at 20,000 (line 33661). A large Babi community of at least 3,000 emerged following the conversion of one of the town's religious leaders, Mullá Muhammad-'Alí Zanjání ([[hujjat|Hujjat]]). This was probably the largest concentration of Babis in Iran (lines 33664–33669).

## Babi Conflict

An altercation between some Babis and orthodox Shi'is led to a rapidly escalating dispute between Hujjat and the city governor, who finally ordered the city physically divided into Babi and orthodox sectors. Hostilities erupted on 13 May 1850. The Babis maintained a sustained resistance against large numbers of regular troops, several thousand of whom were killed (lines 33673–33682). Following the death of Hujjat (c. 29 December 1850), with their own numbers greatly reduced, the remaining Babis surrendered at the beginning of January 1851 and were massacred by the soldiery (lines 33683–33692).

The struggle is noted for the involvement of Babi women in the defence and, in at least one case, in the actual fighting. Much of the town remained in ruins until at least the late 1860s. A strong Bahá'í community later emerged (lines 33692–33697).
