# Bethel

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Bethel** ("house of God," formerly **Luz**) lay on the central highland watershed route,
on the border between **Ephraim and Benjamin**, ~17 km north of Jerusalem. A Canaanite town
that the excavators report was **destroyed in the thirteenth century BCE** — one of the few
sites whose archaeology has been read as fitting the conquest model (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3) —
it became a major Israelite **sanctuary** and, after the kingdom split, one of the two royal
shrines of the northern kingdom.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Genesis 28 & 35**: **Jacob's** dream of the ladder/stairway to heaven — he names the
  place **Bethel** and sets up a **standing stone (massebah)**, a practice later Israel would
  prohibit (Stager notes such patriarchal customs, Coogan Ch. 1).
- **Joshua 8 / Judges 1**: Bethel taken; "archaeology has confirmed its destruction in the
  thirteenth century" (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3) — alongside [[hazor|Hazor]], one of the rare
  matches to the conquest narrative.
- **Judges 20–21; 1 Samuel 7, 10**: Bethel as an early cultic and judicial center (the ark
  is there in Judg. 20; Samuel judges on a Bethel circuit).
- **1 Kings 12–13**: **Jeroboam I** installs a **golden bull-calf** at Bethel (and at
  [[dan|Dan]]) as the southern royal sanctuary of the breakaway northern kingdom — an
  "alternative to worship in Jerusalem." Stager notes (with Frank Cross) that this likely
  drew on **old El/Yahweh iconography**, "not idolatrous or even newly syncretistic" except
  from the Deuteronomistic and Chronicler's viewpoint (Coogan Ch. 3).
- **Amos 7.10–13**: Amos is expelled from Bethel, "the king's sanctuary… a temple of the
  kingdom," by the priest **Amaziah**; Amos and **Hosea** denounce its cult.
- **2 Kings 23.15**: **Josiah** pulls down and defiles the Bethel altar in his reform.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The excavators (Albright, Kelso at Beitin) reported a substantial Late Bronze town destroyed
in the thirteenth century — figuring prominently in the **conquest-historicity** debate, where
**only Bethel and [[hazor|Hazor]]** among securely identified Joshua sites show a fitting
destruction (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). At Bethel itself, **archaeology has yielded no remains of
Jeroboam's sanctuary** (in contrast to the platform found at [[dan|Dan]]). The Beitin
identification, though standard, remains debated.

## Modern Identification and Geography

Usually identified with **Beitin**, a village ~17 km north of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] near
Ramallah in the West Bank; some scholars argue for nearby el-Bira/Bireh.

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

Bethel is doubly charged: the **patriarchal "house of God"** where heaven touches earth
(Jacob's ladder), and the **rival royal sanctuary** whose calf-cult the Deuteronomistic
History brands as **"the sin of Jeroboam."** It thus embodies the north's claim to legitimate,
ancient worship and the south's polemic against it.

## Key Events and Controversies

Jacob's ladder and the massebah; Bethel in the conquest-historicity debate (Bethel and Hazor);
Jeroboam's calf and the Bethel-vs-Jerusalem polemic; Amos vs. Amaziah; Josiah's defilement.
