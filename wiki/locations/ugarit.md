# Ugarit

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Ugarit (modern **Ras Shamra**) was a wealthy, cosmopolitan **Late Bronze Age** trade
port on the north Syrian coast. Excavated continuously since 1929, "no other
Syro-Palestinian city from this period is so well known" (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1). It
steered a careful course between Egypt and Hatti before its **destruction c. 1185 BCE
by the [[sea-peoples|Sea Peoples]]**, after which it was never reoccupied.

## Primary Significance — the Ugaritic Texts

Ugarit appears in **no biblical narrative**, yet its tablet discoveries "**revolutionized
the study of the Bible**." The library of the high priest of Baal yielded the only
surviving collection of **Canaanite myth and epic**, written in a previously unknown
**alphabetic cuneiform** (Ugaritic, a West Semitic language close to Hebrew):

- **The Baal Cycle**: Baal vs. **Yamm** ("Sea"); the building of Baal's palace; Baal vs.
  **Mot** ("Death") — Baal dies and returns to life. Parallels the Babylonian *Enuma
  Elish* and the biblical Yahweh-vs.-Sea motif (Ps. 74.12–17).
- **The Kirta and Aqhat epics**: human-hero tales; **Aqhat** (the childless king Daniel
  granted an heir by El) is strikingly close to the [[abraham|Abraham and Sarah]] story.

## The Canaanite Pantheon and Israelite Religion

The texts reveal the [[canaanites|Canaanite]] pantheon: **El** (creator, father and king
of the gods, "the kind, the compassionate," dwelling on a mountain in a tent);
**Baal** (storm/fertility, de facto ruler); **Asherah** (El's consort); **Anat** (war
goddess). Coogan's volume shows how closely **El** matches the patriarchs' deity in
Genesis (El Shadday, El Elyon, El Olam; the name Yisra-**el**), and how the storm-god
imagery later attached to Yahweh resembles **Baal**, who becomes Yahweh's chief
first-millennium rival. See [[divine-name]], [[phoenicians]].

A caution Coogan repeats: Ugarit was **not identical** to Palestinian Canaan or to
Israel; drawing too many one-to-one parallels overreaches the evidence.

## Archaeological Data

A nearly one-hectare **palace** with ~90 rooms and six courtyards; temples of **Baal**
and (probably) **El/Dagan**; multiple archives (priestly library, royal archives in
Ugaritic and Akkadian, private libraries). Its final letters record the **Sea Peoples'**
onslaught — "the enemy ships are already here… they have set fire to my towns."

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tell Ras Shamra**, ~11 km north of **Latakia, Syria**, on the Mediterranean coast;
excavated since 1929 (originally by Claude Schaeffer), with about half the Late Bronze
city exposed.

## Significance and Controversies

Ugarit is the single most important extrabiblical source for reconstructing the
**Canaanite religious background of the Hebrew Bible** — the El/Yahweh question,
Hebrew poetry's parallelism and vocabulary, and the Baal polemic. Related:
[[canaanites]], [[phoenicians]], [[sea-peoples]], [[byblos]], [[divine-name]],
[[abraham]], [[psalms]].
