# Gezer

## Geographical and Historical Overview

**Gezer** (modern **Tel Gezer / Tell el-Jazari**) commanded the junction where the road from
the **coastal plain** climbs into the Judean hills toward Jerusalem — a position that made it
one of the most important **[[canaanites|Canaanite]] city-states** and a perennial military
prize (Pitard/Stager, Coogan Chs. 1, 3). It is among the most thoroughly excavated tells in
the country.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Late Bronze Age (Amarna)**: Gezer is among the most prominent Canaanite city-states
  (with [[hazor|Hazor]], [[shechem|Shechem]], [[megiddo|Megiddo]], and [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]).
  Its king **Milkilu** appears in the **Amarna letters** — accused by Jerusalem's
  **Abdi-Hepa** of conspiring with the **[[apiru-habiru|Apiru]]** against Egyptian control
  (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1). **Thutmose IV** had earlier deported captives from Gezer.
- **Merneptah Stela (c. 1209 BCE)**: **Gezer** is named (with Ashkelon and Yanoam) as a
  conquered **city-state** — written with the city determinative, in contrast to **"Israel,"**
  written as a *people* — a key datum in the emergence-of-Israel debate (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3).
- **Late Bronze → Iron**: destroyed at the end of the Late Bronze Age, then **rebuilt by
  Rameses III as an Egypto-Canaanite counterforce** against Philistine [[ekron|Ekron]] (Stager,
  Coogan Ch. 3).
- **Joshua 10/16, Judges 1**: Gezer's Canaanite king is defeated, but the Canaanites are
  **not driven out** — Israel "did not dispossess" them.
- **1 Kings 9.15–17**: a pharaoh captures and burns Gezer, gives it as **dowry** to his
  daughter married to **Solomon**, who **rebuilds it together with [[hazor|Hazor]] and
  [[megiddo|Megiddo]]** — the classic trio of "Solomonic" fortifications and gates.
- **Assyrian period**: a **cuneiform sale deed** records an Israelite, **Netanyahu**, buying
  a field "according to standard **Assyrian** legal practice," his seal bearing Mesopotamian
  lunar symbolism — a vivid sign of **Assyro-Aramean cultural assimilation** in 7th-c. Judah
  (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7; cf. [[arameans]]).

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Gezer is famous for its row of monumental **standing stones (a Middle Bronze "high place")**,
its Bronze Age water system, the disputed **"Solomonic" six-chambered gate**, and the
**Gezer Calendar** — a 10th-century BCE schoolboy's limestone tablet listing the agricultural
months, one of the oldest Hebrew (or Canaanite) inscriptions. **Boundary inscriptions**
("the boundary of Gezer") confirmed the site's identity.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tel Gezer**, on the Shephelah foothills of central Israel near Kibbutz Gezer, guarding the
ascent from the coastal plain toward [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]].

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Gezer epitomizes the **unfinished conquest** (the Canaanites who remained), the **Solomonic
building program** (Hazor–Megiddo–Gezer), and — through the Netanyahu deed — the deep
**cultural assimilation** of Judah under Assyrian dominance. Its Merneptah and Amarna notices
make it a touchstone for dating Israel's emergence.

## Key Events and Controversies

The Amarna Milkilu/Apiru affair; the Merneptah Stela (city vs. people determinatives);
Solomon's Egyptian-dowry rebuilding and the dating of the "Solomonic" gates; the Gezer
Calendar; Assyrian-era assimilation. Related: [[hazor]], [[megiddo]], [[jerusalem]],
[[canaanites]], [[apiru-habiru]], [[sea-peoples]], [[ekron]], [[arameans]],
[[herem-canaanite-conquest]].
