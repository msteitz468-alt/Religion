# Kartarpur

The township [[guru-nanak|Guru Nanak]] founded on the banks of the Ravi — "the abode of the Creator" —
where he spent his last years and established the pattern of settled Sikh community life. This page draws on
**Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1, Ch. 2**.

## In Sikh History (Khushwant Singh)

After his travels, Nanak settled at Kartarpur, "a new township that he had built earlier on the banks of the
Ravi" (Singh, ch. 2), and set the daily routine — rising before daybreak, cold bath, morning prayer and
hymns, worldly work, evening congregation — that became the template of the Sikh **[[langar|langar]]**
community. Here he died on **22 September 1539**, having appointed his disciple Lehna —
[[guru-angad|Angad]] — as successor. Singh notes the *janam-sākhī* legend that at his death the flowers laid
by Hindus and Muslims alike were found still fresh, both communities claiming him. Khushwant Singh
distinguishes this Kartarpur from a **second** Kartarpur in the Jullundur Doab founded later by
[[guru-arjan|Guru Arjan]].
