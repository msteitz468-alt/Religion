# Jerusalem

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Jerusalem sits on the Judean watershed, its oldest core the narrow **Ophel/City of
David** ridge above the **Gihon Spring**. Strategically secondary in the Bronze Age
(off the main coastal route, in sparsely settled hills), it rose to supreme religious
importance as the capital of Judah and the seat of the Temple. Across its history it
was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt — most decisively in **586 BCE** (Babylon) and
**135 CE** (Rome).

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Genesis 14**: "Salem," city of [[melchizedek]], priest of El Elyon (see
  [[divine-name]]).
- **Late Bronze Age (Amarna)**: a [[canaanites|Canaanite/Jebusite]] city-state whose
  king **Abdi-Hepa** writes to the pharaoh begging troops against rivals and the
  [[apiru-habiru|Apiru]] (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1). Sparsely fortified, far from the
  lowland cities.
- **Davidic/Judahite**: captured by **[[david|David]]** from the Jebusites (c. 1000
  BCE), made capital; **[[solomon|Solomon]]** builds the First Temple. Capital of the
  kingdom of Judah after the division.
- **8th–7th c. (Assyrian era)**: the city **tripled or quadrupled** in size with
  refugees fleeing the Assyrian conquest of the north; new quarters (the Mishneh,
  Maktesh). **Hezekiah** fortified the western hill (a 7 m-wide wall excavated) and dug
  the **Siloam Tunnel** (533 m, with its famous inscription) to secure water before
  **Sennacherib's siege of 701** — Jerusalem "shut up like a bird in a cage," but,
  uniquely, *not* taken (Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7).
- **586 BCE**: Nebuchadrezzar's army **burns the Temple, palace, and city** and breaches
  the walls (probably on the north); the elite are deported. See [[babylonians]],
  [[lamentations]].
- **Persian period**: a **tiny** capital of the province of [[samaria|Yehud]] — perhaps
  475–500 people in Persian I, ~1,750 in Persian II — confined to the eastern hill and
  Temple Mount; the **Second Temple** is rebuilt under Zerubbabel/Joshua (c. 516/515)
  and the walls under **Nehemiah** (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8). See [[persians]],
  [[ezra-nehemiah]].

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

The 14th-c. Amarna letters give the city its earliest "voice" (Abdi-Hepa). The
**Siloam Tunnel inscription** documents Hezekiah's waterworks; **Sennacherib's annals**
and the **Lachish reliefs** frame the 701 campaign; the **Babylonian Chronicle** dates
the 597 capture. Babylonian-style arrowheads on the north and burnt destruction levels
attest the 586 fall. The Temple Mount itself remains unexcavated.

## Modern Identification and Geography

The ancient city is firmly identified with modern **Jerusalem**; the biblical core is
the **City of David** archaeological site on the Ophel, with the Temple platform now
the **Haram al-Sharif / Temple Mount**. The city is among the most politically
contested places on earth (see Controversies).

## Theological and Symbolic Significance

- **Judaism**: **Zion** — the chosen dwelling of Yahweh, the Temple, the focus of
  pilgrimage, lament (Ps. 137; Lamentations), and restoration hope.
- **Christianity**: the city of Jesus' passion and resurrection; the "heavenly
  Jerusalem"; holy-site traditions (Golgotha, Holy Sepulchre).
- **Islam**: **al-Quds**, third holiest city, site of the Night Journey and the Dome of
  the Rock on the Haram al-Sharif.

## Key Events, Figures, and Controversies

David's capture; Solomon's Temple; Hezekiah and Sennacherib (701); the Babylonian
destruction (586); the Second Temple restoration; Roman Aelia Capitolina (135). The
**status of the Temple Mount / Haram al-Sharif** and competing modern claims to the
city are among the world's most intractable disputes. Related: [[samaria]],
[[babylon]], [[lachish]], [[shechem]], [[melchizedek]], [[david]], [[solomon]],
[[assyrians]], [[babylonians]], [[persians]].
