# Nayriz

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23604–23646.*

## Bábí Conflict (1850)
Nayriz was the site of armed conflict between Bábís under Sayyid Yaḥyá Dárábí ([[vahid|Vahíd]]) and provincial troops. Vahíd arrived on 27 May 1850, and as a local notable and religious leader in the Chinár Shákhih quarter, soon converted a large number of townspeople, perhaps 1,500 in all. An armed struggle developed in which Vahíd and some of his followers withdrew to a nearby fort, where they successfully resisted attacks by forces of the town governor, Ḥájí Zaynu'l-'Ábidín Khán, and subsequent reinforcements (lines 23604–23625).

Responding to a truce offer, Vahíd later surrendered to the government forces (17 June), and instructed his followers to abandon their positions. Vahíd and many of his companions were then killed, and the 'Bábí quarter' of the town was plundered (lines 23623–23629).

## Second Conflict (1853)
In revenge, some of the Bábís later killed the governor (26 March 1853), his replacement in turn arresting suspected Bábís and seizing their property. This sparked off a second conflict, with a large group of Bábís retiring to a nearby mountain where they were able to resist troops sent against them until November 1853. A massacre of suspected Bábís followed, and several hundred of the women were enslaved (lines 23630–23642).

## Aftermath
An active Bahá'í community subsequently developed, itself subject to persecutions (lines 23640–23643).
