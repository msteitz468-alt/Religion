# Jericho

## Geographical and Historical Overview

Jericho (**Tell es-Sultan**), beside a perennial spring in the **Jordan Rift Valley**
~250 m below sea level, is one of the **oldest towns on earth** — boasting a famous
**Pre-Pottery Neolithic tower and wall** (c. 9000 BCE). It is the iconic first conquest
of the book of Joshua, and a central test case for the historicity of that conquest.

## Primary Textual Appearances and Inhabitants (by period)

- **Joshua 2 and 6**: the spies and **Rahab**; the walls fall at the trumpet blast — the
  paradigmatic conquest miracle.
- **The archaeological problem**: Coogan's volume is blunt — "this miracle has no
  archaeological reflex; in fact, there is little or no occupation at Jericho in the
  thirteenth century" (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). Kathleen Kenyon's excavations found the
  Late Bronze city absent (not merely eroded; tombs and even potsherds are lacking).
  With **Ai** ("the ruin," also unoccupied), Jericho is the showcase of the **failure of
  the literal conquest model**. See [[herem-canaanite-conquest]], [[israelites]],
  [[hazor]].
- **2 Kings 2**: Elijah's ascent and Elisha's healing of the spring at Jericho.
- **NT (Luke 10; 19)**: the Good Samaritan road "down to Jericho"; Zacchaeus; blind
  Bartimaeus — set at the **Herodian** Jericho on a nearby site.

## Archaeological and Extra-Biblical Data

Excavated by **John Garstang** (who thought he had Joshua's walls) and then **Kathleen
Kenyon** (whose stratigraphy showed no Late Bronze city) — a classic episode in the
history of biblical archaeology. The Neolithic tower remains one of the most remarkable
early monuments anywhere.

## Modern Identification and Geography

**Tell es-Sultan**, immediately northwest of modern **Jericho (Ariha)** in the West
Bank, in the Jordan Valley — the lowest town on earth. Herodian/NT Jericho lies a short
distance away at **Tulul Abu el-Alayiq**.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Jericho is the **threshold of the Promised Land** and the emblem of Yahweh's gift of the
land by miracle rather than by Israelite might (the walls fall at a shout). It is
precisely this theological weight that makes the archaeological silence so significant
for the conquest debate.

### Woudstra (NICOT Joshua) — brief

Attributed to [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] ([[woudstra-joshua-nicot]]; digests A, C): ch. 6
celebrates the outstanding instance of God’s “giving” the land; historical narrative **and**
theological presentation, not a mere product of Israel’s faith. March is not magical
circumambulation (command from the Lord; ark present). Most rigorous *ḥerem* “by way of
example”; metals to the Lord’s treasury explain Achan (ch. 7); Rahab’s rescue is singular
goodness under the ban. On archaeology: usually Tell eṣ-Ṣultan; identification questions
remain; miracle does not rule out natural causes, but the story is silent and means to
celebrate God’s act; archaeology has limited role (“does not answer all questions”).
Conquest-date stalemate: Kenyon challenges Garstang; for Woudstra Jericho (and Ai) are
“silent where eloquence wanted.” See [[herem|Ḥerem]], [[herem-canaanite-conquest]].

## Key Events and Controversies

Rahab and the fall of the walls; the **conquest-historicity controversy** (Jericho/Ai
unoccupied vs. the Joshua narrative); Elijah/Elisha; the NT Jericho-road stories.
