# Shaykh Tabarsí

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Overview

Shaykh Tabarsí was a small shrine of Shaykh Ahmad ibn Abí Tálib Tabarsí, about 14 miles southeast of Bárfurúsh (modern Babol) in northern Iran. It became a Babi stronghold and was the site of the most famous of the conflicts between the Babis and their opponents (lines 30109–30114).

## Events

Mullá Husayn Bushrú'í had led a growing band of armed Babis westward from Mashhad carrying the messianic symbol of the Black Standard. On entering Bárfurúsh (10 October 1848) they were attacked; they killed several of their adversaries. Attacked again, they retired to Tabarsí (12 October), which they fortified. Other Babis later joined them, including [[quddus|Quddús]], their total numbers rising to perhaps six hundred (lines 30115–30128).

Fighting in what they came to see as a re-enactment of [[karbala|Karbala]], the Babis held out against large numbers of professional troops until, weakened by attrition and starvation, they responded to a false truce. Most were massacred on 10 May 1849 (lines 30128–30135). Nine of the [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] were killed at Tabarsí or in its immediate aftermath (lines 30136–30139).

## Significance

The siege of Shaykh Tabarsí is the most celebrated of the Babi military conflicts. It became a symbol of Babi martyrdom and heroism and is understood by Bahá'ís as evidence of the power of the Babi revelation — the Babis consciously identified their struggle with the paradigmatic Shi'i martyrdom narrative of Karbala.
