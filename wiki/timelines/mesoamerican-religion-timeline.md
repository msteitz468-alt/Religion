# Timeline of Mesoamerican Religion

A chronological hub for the religions of pre-Columbian Mesoamerica — the shared cosmology of the Olmec, Maya, and Aztec worlds, with its calendrics, cyclical world-ages, and sacrificial ritual. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerican religion]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

## I. The Formative / Olmec Era (c. 1500–400 BCE)

The Olmec "mother culture" establishes the enduring Mesoamerican deity complexes (the feathered serpent, the rain god, the maize god) and the ritual calendar.

| Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|
| the [[mesoamerican-sacrifice|sacrificial]] worldview; calendar and cosmos | the feathered-serpent and rain-deity complexes |

## II. The Classic Period (Maya & Teotihuacan, c. 250–900 CE)

The Maya city-states elaborate a sophisticated theology of time and kingship; the [[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]] preserves the K'iche' Maya creation myth and the [[hero-twins|Hero Twins']] descent into [[xibalba|Xibalba]].

| Deities | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[itzamna]] · [[ix-chel]] · [[chaac]] · [[ah-puch]] · [[maize-god]] · [[hero-twins]] | [[popol-vuh]] | sacred kingship; the calendar round; the underworld [[xibalba|Xibalba]] |

## III. The Postclassic Period (Toltec & Aztec, c. 900–1521 CE)

The Aztec (Mexica) inherit and systematize the pantheon around the doctrine of the [[five-suns|Five Suns]] and the cosmic necessity of [[mesoamerican-sacrifice|human sacrifice]].

| Deities | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[quetzalcoatl]] · [[tezcatlipoca]] · [[huitzilopochtli]] · [[tlaloc]] · [[xipe-totec]] · [[tonatiuh]] · [[mictlantecuhtli]] · [[coatlicue]] | [[five-suns]] · [[mesoamerican-sacrifice]] | feeding the sun; the *tonalpohualli* calendar; flower wars |

## IV. Conquest & Survival (1521 – present)

The Spanish conquest and forced Christianization; the persistence of indigenous belief in syncretic form.

|---|---|
| the Conquest (1519–21); evangelization | religious syncretism; surviving Maya and Nahua practice |
