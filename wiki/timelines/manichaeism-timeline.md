# Timeline of Manichaeism

A chronological hub for Manichaeism — the dualist world religion founded by [[mani|Mani]] in third-century Sasanian Babylonia, which once stretched from the Atlantic to China. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Mani & the Founding (c. 216–277 CE)

[[mani|Mani]], raised in a Jewish-Christian baptist sect in Babylonia, receives revelations from his heavenly "Twin" and proclaims a universal religion synthesizing Christian, [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]], and Buddhist elements; he is martyred under the Sasanian shah.

| Figures  | Concepts            | Themes                                       |                        |                                                           |
| -------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[mani]] | radical [[asha-druj | [[Light–Darkness dualism]]; the [[demiurge]] | evil maker of matter]] | the two principles and three times; the Religion of Light |

## II. Rapid Spread (c. 277–600 CE)

Manichaeism spreads west across the Roman Empire — where [[augustine|Augustine]] was a "hearer" for nine years before converting to [[christianity|Christianity]] — and east into Central Asia, despite fierce persecution by Rome, Sasanian Persia, and the Church.

| Figures | Themes |
|---|---|
| [[augustine]] (former Manichaean) | the *electi* and *auditores*; condemnation as a [[gnosticism|Gnostic]] heresy |

## III. Eastern Flourishing (c. 600–1000 CE)

Suppressed in the West, Manichaeism flourishes along the Silk Road and becomes, briefly, the state religion of the Uyghur Khaganate; it penetrates Tang China.

| Region | Themes |
|---|---|
| Central Asia; the Uyghur Khaganate; Tang China | the eastern Manichaean church; Sogdian and Uyghur texts |

## IV. Decline (c. 1000–1400 CE)

Gradual extinction under Islamic, Confucian, and Buddhist pressure; the last communities survive in south China into the late medieval period.

| Outcome | Legacy |
|---|---|
| extinction as a living religion | influence on later dualisms (Paulicians, Bogomils, Cathars — disputed) |
