# Timeline of Egyptian Religion

A chronological hub for the religion of ancient Egypt — its gods, funerary texts, and the slow democratization of the afterlife across three millennia. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[egyptian-religion|Egyptian religion]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Early Dynastic & Old Kingdom (c. 3100–2181 BCE)

Divine kingship and the solar theology of [[ra|Ra]]; the [[pyramid-texts|Pyramid Texts]] secure the king's afterlife.

| Deities | Texts / Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[ra]] · [[ptah]] · [[horus]] · [[set]] · [[geb]] · [[nut]] | [[pyramid-texts]] · [[maat|Maʻat (cosmic order)]] · [[ka-ba-akh|ka / ba / akh]] | the pharaoh as Horus; solar cult; the Heliopolitan Ennead |

## II. Middle Kingdom (c. 2055–1650 BCE)

The rise of [[osiris|Osiris]] and the "democratization" of the afterlife; the Coffin Texts extend funerary hope beyond the king.

| Deities | Texts / Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[osiris]] · [[isis]] · [[anubis]] · [[thoth]] | [Coffin Texts]; the judgment of the dead | the Osirian myth of death and resurrection |

## III. New Kingdom & the Amarna Interlude (c. 1550–1069 BCE)

The imperial cult of [[amun|Amun-Ra]]; the [[book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]]; [Akhenaten]'s brief revolution exalting the [[aten|Aten]].

| Deities | Texts / Concepts | Events |
|---|---|---|
| [[amun]] · [[aten]] · [[hathor]] · [[sekhmet]] | [[book-of-the-dead]]; the weighing of the heart against [[maat|Maʻat]] | the [Amarna] reform under [Akhenaten] and its reversal |

## IV. Late, Ptolemaic & Roman Period (c. 1069 BCE – 400 CE)

Temple religion continues under foreign rule; the cult of [[isis|Isis]] spreads across the Roman Empire as one of the great [[mystery-cults|mystery religions]]; native worship ends with Christianization.

| Deities | Legacy | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[isis]] · [[sobek]] · [[khnum]] · [[nephthys]] | the [[mystery-cults|Isis mysteries]] in the Greco-Roman world | the last hieroglyphic inscription (394 CE); Christianization |
