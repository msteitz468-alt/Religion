# Timeline of Celtic Religion

A chronological hub for the religion of the Celtic peoples — known partly from classical observers and archaeology, but mostly through the medieval Irish and Welsh literary cycles recorded by Christian scribes. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages. Note: dates are largely *attestation* dates; the living pagan religion long predates its written sources.

---

## I. Pagan Celtic Religion (Iron Age, c. 800 BCE – 400 CE)

The religion of the continental and insular Celts — [Druids], sacred groves, and a pantheon known from Greco-Roman writers and archaeology.

| Concepts | Sources | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| the [[the-otherworld|Otherworld]]; sacrifice and seasonal festival | classical authors; votive archaeology | [Druidic] priesthood; the cult of springs and heads |

## II. The Irish Mythological Tradition (recorded c. 700–1200 CE)

The Irish cycles preserve the gods as the [[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]], a divine race, in narratives written down by Christian monks.

| Deities | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[the-dagda]] · [[lugh]] · [[morrigan|the Mórrígan]] · [[brigid]] · [[nuada]] · [[dian-cecht]] · [[balor]] · [[manannan]] | [[gods-and-fighting-men]] | the Mythological Cycle; the battles of Mag Tuired |

## III. The Welsh Tradition (recorded c. 1100–1400 CE)

The Welsh tales of the [[mabinogion|Mabinogi]] preserve a parallel mythology and the Otherworld realm of [[annwn|Annwn]].

| Figures | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[pwyll]] · [[rhiannon]] · [[branwen]] · [[bran-the-blessed]] · [[arawn]] | [[mabinogion]] | the [[annwn|Annwn]] Otherworld; sovereignty and the cauldron |

## IV. Christianization & Legacy (c. 400 CE onward)

The pagan religion yields to Christianity, surviving as mythological literature and folklore; the gods become the *aos sí* (fairy folk).

| Outcome | Legacy |
|---|---|
| conversion of Ireland and Britain | the Tuatha Dé as the *sídhe*; modern Celtic revival |
