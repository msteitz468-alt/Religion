# Timeline of Daoism

A chronological hub for Daoism (*Dàojiào*) — from the classical philosophy of the [[dao|Dào]] to the organized religion of revelation, ritual, and longevity-cultivation. The wiki tracks both the [[taochia-taochiao-dichotomy|"philosophical vs. religious" framing]] and the two great readings of the [[daodejing|Daodejing]]. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[daoism|Daoism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Classical / "Philosophical" Daoism (4th–3rd c. BCE)

The foundational texts of [[philosophical-daoism|classical Daoism]] — attributed to [[laozi|Laozi]] and [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]] — articulate the [[dao]], [[wu-wei|wú-wéi]], and [[ziran|zìrán]].

| Figures | Texts | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| [[laozi]] · [[zhuangzi]] | [[daodejing]] · [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi (the book)]] · [[nei-yeh|Nèiyè]] | [[dao]] · [[wu-wei]] · [[ziran]] · [[biospiritual-cultivation]] |

## II. Han: Huang-Lao & the First Churches (2nd c. BCE – 220 CE)

Huang-Lao statecraft, then the founding of organized [[religious-daoism|religious Daoism]] with the [[heavenly-masters|Way of the Celestial Masters]].

| Schools | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[heavenly-masters|Celestial Masters]] · [[religious-daoism]] | communal ritual; confession and healing | the deification of Laozi; the first Daoist church |

## III. Six Dynasties & Tang: Revelation & Commentary (220–907 CE)

The great revealed scriptural traditions ([[shang-ching|Shangqing]], [[ling-pao|Lingbao]]); the two classic readings of the *Daodejing* — [[wang-bi|Wang Bi's]] metaphysical (*xuanxue*) vs. [[heshang-gong|Heshang Gong's]] longevity reading (the [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|Wang Bi vs. Heshang Gong]] contrast); [[ko-hung|Ge Hong]] on alchemy; [[lu-hsiu-ching|Lu Xiujing]] codifies the canon.

| Figures | Schools | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| [[wang-bi]] · [[heshang-gong]] · [[ko-hung]] · [[lu-hsiu-ching]] | [[shang-ching]] · [[ling-pao]] | inner/outer alchemy; [[temple-dwelling-monks|monastic Daoism]] |

## IV. Song to Modern (960 – present)

New monastic and liturgical orders — the ascetic [[quanzhen|Quanzhen]] and the ritual [[cheng-i|Zhengyi]] — define later Daoism; Tang–Song masters systematize practice.

| Figures | Schools | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[ssu-ma-cheng-chen|Sima Chengzhen]] · [[tu-kuang-ting|Du Guangting]] | [[quanzhen]] · [[cheng-i]] | inner alchemy (*nèidān*); the modern Daoist priesthood |
