# Timeline of Shinto

A chronological hub for Shinto (*kami no michi*, the [[path-of-the-gods|"way of the kami"]]) — the indigenous religion of Japan, from ancient [[kami]] worship through its long entanglement with Buddhism to State Shinto and the present. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[shinto|Shinto]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Ancient Kami Worship (Yayoi–Kofun, c. 300 BCE – 538 CE)

Animistic veneration of the [[kami]] — the spirits of nature, place, and ancestry — through local cult, purification, and festival, long before the religion is named.

| Concepts | Practices | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[kami]] · [[musubi|musubi (generative force)]] | [[harae|harae (purification)]] · [[matsuri|matsuri (festival)]] | clan tutelary deities (*ujigami*); sacred space |

## II. Classical Codification (Nara–Heian, 710–1185)

The court compiles the [[kojiki|Kojiki]] (712) and [[nihongi|Nihon Shoki]] (720), fixing the myth-cycle that legitimates the imperial line; encounter and fusion with [[buddhism|Buddhism]] (*shinbutsu-shūgō*).

| Deities | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[amaterasu]] · [[susanoo]] · [[tsukuyomi]] · [[izanagi-and-izanami]] · [[ninigi]] · [[jimmu|Jimmu]] · [[ame-no-uzume]] · [[okuninushi]] | [[kojiki]] · [[nihongi]] | the descent of the imperial line from [[amaterasu]]; *kami*–Buddha syncretism |

## III. Medieval Syncretism (Kamakura–Edo, 1185–1868)

Shinto is largely assimilated to Buddhism (*honji suijaku*); later, the *kokugaku* ("national learning") movement seeks to recover a "pure" Shinto.

| Deities | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[inari]] · [[hachiman]] | *honji suijaku*; kokugaku revival | Buddhist–Shinto combinatory cults; nativist reaction |

## IV. State Shinto & Modern Shinto (Meiji 1868 – present)

The Meiji state separates Shinto from Buddhism and fashions State Shinto around the emperor; after 1945 it is disestablished and reorganized as Shrine Shinto.

| Themes | Outcome |
|---|---|
| State Shinto and emperor-veneration | postwar disestablishment; Shrine Shinto today |
