# Timeline of Jainism

A chronological hub for Jainism — the Indian *śramaṇa* tradition of radical [[ahimsa|non-violence]] and the liberation of the [[jiva|soul]] from [[karma|karmic]] matter. Jains regard their teaching as eternal, revealed afresh by each [[tirthankara|Tīrthaṅkara]]. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[jainism|Jainism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

## I. The Tīrthaṅkaras & Pārśva (legendary – c. 8th c. BCE)

The tradition counts twenty-four [[tirthankara|Tīrthaṅkaras]] ("ford-makers"); the first is [[rshabha|Ṛṣabha]] and the twenty-third, [[parshvanatha|Pārśvanātha]], is generally treated by historians as a genuine pre-Mahāvīra teacher.

| Figures | Concepts |
|---|---|
| [[rshabha]] · [[parshvanatha]] | [[tirthankara]] · [[ahimsa]] · [[jiva]]/[[ajiva]] |

## II. Mahāvīra & the Fourfold Order (c. 6th–5th c. BCE)

[[mahavira|Mahāvīra]], the twenty-fourth Tīrthaṅkara and a contemporary of the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]], establishes the mendicant and lay community and articulates the core doctrines.

| Figures | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[mahavira]] | [[anekantavada]] · [[syadvada]] · [[mahavrata]]/[[anuvrata]] · [[kevala-jnana]] | the five great vows; the fourfold *saṅgha* |

## III. Schism & the Canon (c. 300 BCE – 500 CE)

The community divides into the [[digambara|Digambara]] and [[svetambara|Śvetāmbara]] traditions; the [[jain-agamas|Āgamas]] are redacted; Umāsvāti and [[kundakunda|Kundakunda]] systematize doctrine.

| Figures | Texts | Schools |
|---|---|---|
| [[kundakunda]] · [Umāsvāti] | [[jain-agamas]] · [[acaranga-sutra]] · [[tattvartha-sutra]] · [[samayasara]] · [[kalpasutra]] | [[digambara]] · [[svetambara]] |

## IV. The Medieval Period (c. 500–1500 CE)

Royal patronage, temple-building, and great scholar-monks; the rise of the [[bhattaraka|bhaṭṭāraka]] clerical office.
