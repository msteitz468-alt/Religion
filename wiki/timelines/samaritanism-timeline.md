# Timeline of Samaritanism

A chronological hub for the Samaritans, the small Israelite community that shares the Torah and the patriarchal–Mosaic heritage with [[judaism|Judaism]] but locates the chosen sanctuary at Mount Gerizim rather than Jerusalem. Coverage in the wiki is modest; this hub gathers what exists. For placement among the other traditions see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[samaritanism|Samaritanism]].

## I. Israelite Roots & the Schism (pre-Hellenistic)

The [[samaritans|Samaritans]] claim descent from the northern tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh and continuity with pre-exilic Israelite religion; Jewish tradition (2 Kings 17) instead traces them to foreign settlers. The decisive break with the Judean community concerns the legitimate place of worship — the dispute over [[gerizim-vs-jerusalem|Gerizim vs. Jerusalem]].

## II. The Second Temple & Hellenistic Period (5th–1st c. BCE)

A temple is built on Mount Gerizim; relations with the Judeans (Ezra–Nehemiah onward) are hostile. The Hasmonean [John Hyrcanus] destroys the Gerizim temple c. 110 BCE.

| Figures / Groups | Events |
|---|---|
| [[samaritans]] · [[ezra]] · [[nehemiah]] | the Gerizim temple; its destruction (c. 110 BCE); intermarriage disputes ([[intermarriage-ezra-nehemiah|Ezra–Nehemiah]]) |

## III. The Roman & Late Antique Period (1st–6th c. CE)

The Samaritans feature in the New Testament (the parable of the Good Samaritan; the woman at the well, John 4). A 4th-century revival under [Bābā Rabbā] is followed by harsh suppression after revolts against Byzantium.

| Texts / Concepts | Events |
|---|---|
| NT Samaritan episodes; Samaritan liturgy | [Bābā Rabbā] revival (4th c.); Byzantine-era revolts and decline |

## IV. Medieval to Modern (7th c. – present)

Under Islamic and Ottoman rule the community contracts to a remnant; today a few hundred Samaritans survive at Mount Gerizim (Kiryat Luza, near Nablus) and Holon.

|---|---|
| a surviving remnant community | Mount Gerizim / Nablus; Holon (Israel) |
