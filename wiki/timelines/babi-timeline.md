# Timeline of the Bábí Faith

A chronological hub for the short-lived but pivotal Bábí movement — the messianic religion founded by the [[the-bab|Báb]] in 1844 out of [[shaykhism|Shaykhī]] Shīʻism, which prepared the way for the [[bahai|Baháʼí Faith]]. For the continuation see the [[bahai-timeline|Baháʼí timeline]]; for its Islamic matrix see the [[islam-timeline|Islam timeline]]. Parent page: [[babi|Bábí Faith]].

## I. Shaykhí Background & the Declaration (1843–1844)

[[shaykhism|Shaykhism]] cultivates expectation of the imminent return of the Hidden Imām (the [[qaim|Qāʼim]]). On the eve of 23 May 1844 the [[the-bab|Báb]] declares his mission to [[mulla-husayn|Mullá Ḥusayn]], the first to believe.

## II. The Community Forms (1844–1848)

The first eighteen disciples — the [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] — spread the cause across Iran; at the conference of Badasht the Bábís proclaim a break with Islamic law.

## III. Persecution & the Upheavals (1848–1850)

State and clerical opposition erupts into armed clashes; the Báb is executed at Tabríz on 9 July 1850.

| Figures | Events |
|---|---|
| [[mulla-husayn]] · [[quddus]] · [[seven-martyrs-of-tehran]] | the [Shaykh Ṭabarsí], [Nayríz], and [Zanján] upheavals; the **execution of the Báb (1850)** |

## IV. Aftermath & Division (1850–1863)

After a Bábí attempt on the Shah (1852), the community is shattered and exiled to Baghdad. Leadership is contested between [[subh-i-azal|Ṣubḥ-i-Azal]] and his half-brother [[bahaullah|Baháʼuʼlláh]], who in 1863 declares himself the promised one — beginning the [[bahai-timeline|Baháʼí dispensation]]. The minority following Ṣubḥ-i-Azal becomes [[azali-babism|Azalí Bábism]].
