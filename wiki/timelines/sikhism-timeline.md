# Timeline of Sikhism

A chronological hub for the Sikh tradition — from [[guru-nanak|Gurū Nānak]] and the line of ten Gurus to the [[guru-granth-sahib|Gurū Granth Sāhib]] as eternal Guru, the [[khalsa|Khālsā]], and the modern community. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[sikhism|Sikhism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Gurū Nānak & the Founding (1469–1539)

[[guru-nanak|Gurū Nānak]] preaches devotion to the one formless God ([[akal-purakh|Akāl Purakh]]) through [[naam|nām]], rejecting ritualism and caste; he founds the community at Kartarpur.

| Figures | Texts / Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[guru-nanak]] | [[japji|Japjī]] · [[naam]] · [[akal-purakh]] · [[gurmat]] | the [[langar|langar]] and *saṅgat*; devotion over ritual |

## II. The Ten Gurus (1539–1708)

The line of Gurus builds the community, compiles scripture, and — under persecution — takes up arms; it culminates in the [[khalsa|Khālsā]] and the vesting of authority in the eternal scripture.

| Figures | Texts / Concepts | Events |
|---|---|---|
| [[guru-angad]] · [[guru-amar-das]] · [[guru-ram-das]] · [[guru-arjan]] · [[guru-hargobind]] · [[guru-har-rai]] · [[guru-har-krishan]] · [[guru-tegh-bahadur]] · [[guru-gobind-singh]] | [[guru-granth-sahib|Ādi Granth]] (1604) · [[dasam-granth]] · [[sukhmani-sahib]] · [[jap-sahib]] · [[miri-piri]] · [[five-ks]] · [[amrit-sanskar]] | martyrdoms of [[guru-arjan|Arjan]] (1606) and [[guru-tegh-bahadur|Tegh Bahādur]] (1675); founding of the [[khalsa|Khālsā]] (1699) |

## III. Post-Guru Period: Misls & Empire (1708–1849)

After [[guru-gobind-singh|Gobind Singh]] vests Guruship in the [[guru-granth-sahib|Granth]] (and Panth), Sikh political power rises — [[banda-bahadur|Banda Singh Bahādur]], the misls, and the empire of [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]] — until British annexation.

|---|---|---|
| [[banda-bahadur]] · [[ranjit-singh]] | the [Sikh misls]; the Sikh Empire (1799–1849); [[annexation-of-the-punjab|annexation of the Punjab]] | sovereignty; the [[ghallughara|Ghallūghārās]] (massacres) |

## IV. Colonial Era & Reform (1849–1947)

Under British rule, reform movements purify practice and define Sikh identity; the [[rahit-maryada|Rahit Maryādā]] codifies it.
