# Timeline of Confucianism

A chronological hub for the Confucian tradition (*Rújiā*) — the ethical and ritual teaching of [[confucius|Confucius]] that became the framework of Chinese statecraft and self-cultivation. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[confucianism|Confucianism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Confucius & the Classics (6th–5th c. BCE)

[[confucius|Confucius]] (Kǒngzǐ) teaches a renewal of ritual and virtue; his sayings are gathered in the [[analects|Analects]].

| Figures | Texts | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| [[confucius]] | [[analects]] · [[yijing|the Yijing]] (among the Classics) | [[ren|rén (humaneness)]] · [[li-confucian|lǐ (ritual propriety)]] |

## II. Classical Development (5th–3rd c. BCE)

[[mencius|Mencius]] argues for innate human goodness (Xunzi takes the opposite view); the Confucian school becomes one of the "hundred schools."

| Figures | Texts | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| [[mencius]] · [Xunzi] | [[mencius|Mencius (the book)]] | the goodness (or otherwise) of human nature; the Mandate of Heaven |

## III. Han Orthodoxy (206 BCE – 220 CE)

Confucianism becomes state ideology; the Five Classics anchor the imperial examination tradition and cosmological correlative thought.

| Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|
| the Five Classics; correlative cosmology | Confucianism as [[classical-confucianism|state orthodoxy]] |

## IV. The Neo-Confucian Revival (Song–Ming, 960–1644)

A metaphysically ambitious revival ([[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]]) reformulates Confucian ethics on a cosmological basis; [[zhu-xi|Zhu Xi]] systematizes the [[daotong|orthodox transmission of the Way]].

| Figures | Texts | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| [[zhou-dunyi|Zhou Dunyi]] · [[zhu-xi|Zhu Xi]] · [Wang Yangming] | [[taijitu-shuo]] · [[tongshu]] | [[taiji|the Supreme Polarity]] · [[daotong]] · principle (*lǐ*) and material force (*qì*) |

## V. Modern Confucianism (1600 – present)

Late-imperial evidential scholarship, the twentieth-century crisis, and the New Confucian revival.

| Movements | Themes |
|---|---|
| New Confucianism; East Asian modernity | reinvention after the end of the examination system |
