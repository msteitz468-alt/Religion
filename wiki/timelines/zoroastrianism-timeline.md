# Timeline of Zoroastrianism

A chronological hub for the religion of [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]] — one of the oldest revealed faiths, whose dualist cosmology and eschatology left deep marks on the Abrahamic traditions. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. Zarathustra & the Gāthās (c. 1200–1000 BCE, disputed)

The prophet [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]] proclaims [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] as the one wise Lord, opposed by the hostile spirit [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] — the root of Zoroastrian [[asha-druj|dualism]]. His own words survive in the [[gathas|Gāthās]].

| Figures / Concepts | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[zarathustra]] · [[ahura-mazda]] · [[angra-mainyu]] · [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]] | [[gathas]] | the cosmic struggle of [[asha-druj|aša vs. druj]] (truth vs. the lie); moral choice |

## II. The Achaemenid Period (c. 550–330 BCE)

Zoroastrian (or Mazdean) religion becomes the faith of the Persian Empire; the [[magi|Magi]] as priestly caste; encounter with the [[judaism|Jewish]] exiles.

| Figures | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[magi]] · [Darius] · [Cyrus] | [[fravashi|fravashis]] · [[xwarenah|khvarenah (divine glory)]] · [[anahita]] · [[mitra]] | royal patronage; possible influence on Jewish [[angels|angelology]] and eschatology |

## III. The Sasanian Codification (224–651 CE)

Zoroastrianism becomes the established state church; the [[avesta|Avesta]] is compiled and the cosmology systematized in works like the [[bundahishn|Bundahišn]].

| Texts | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[avesta]] · [[bundahishn]] | [[chinvat-bridge|the Chinvat Bridge]] · [[frashokereti|frašō.kərəti (final renovation)]] · [[saoshyant|the Saošyant]] | judgment, resurrection, and the final defeat of evil |

## IV. After Islam: Iran & the Parsis (651 CE – present)

The Arab conquest reduces Zoroastrianism to a minority; many migrate to India, becoming the [[parsis|Parsis]]; small communities survive in Iran and the diaspora.

| Communities | Themes |
|---|---|
| the [[parsis|Parsis]] of India; Iranian Zoroastrians | survival as a minority; preservation of fire-temple worship |
