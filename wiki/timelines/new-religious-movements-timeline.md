# Timeline of New Religious Movements (1800–present)

A synchronic hub for the wave of **new religious movements (NRMs)** that emerged from the early nineteenth century onward. These are not structural outliers — they receive the same page types (tradition / sect / figure / text / concept / controversy) and the same chronological treatment as ancient traditions; they are simply *recent*. What unites them is a shared historical moment (post-Enlightenment religious entrepreneurship, the American "burned-over district," global modernity) rather than a single doctrine.

Each movement is typed by its **lineage** exactly as older sects are: some are *Restorationist offshoots* of [[christianity|Christianity]], some grow out of [[islam|Shīʻa Islam]], and some are *new-revelation* traditions in their own right. For placement among the older traditions, see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]] (Modern era band).

## I. The Restorationist Surge (c. 1800–1850)

The Second Great Awakening and the New York "burned-over district" produce a cluster of movements claiming to *restore* primitive Christianity; in Iran, a structurally parallel messianic movement arises out of [[shaykhism|Shaykhī]] Shīʻism.

## Studying New Religious Movements

Because these movements are recent, two features are sharper than for ancient traditions, and the wiki handles both with its standard machinery:

- **Sociological analysis.** [[rodney-stark|Rodney Stark]]'s work on conversion and growth ([[network-conversion]], [[religious-economy]]) applies the same models to NRMs as to early Christianity — recruitment through social networks, the dynamics of high-tension "sects."
- **Contested facts (Contradiction Protocol).** Living movements generate litigated, disputed-fact controversies (Scientology's [[scientology-religion-or-commercial-enterprise|religion-or-commercial-enterprise]] question, the [[hubbard-military-records-forgery|Hubbard biography disputes]]). The wiki records the competing positions without adjudicating, exactly as it does for ancient disputes.
