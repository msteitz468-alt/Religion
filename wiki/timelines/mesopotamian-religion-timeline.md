# Timeline of Mesopotamian Religion

A chronological hub for the religions of ancient Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, and Assyria — the oldest literate religious tradition in the wiki. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamian religion]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages.

---

## I. The Sumerian Period (c. 3500–2300 BCE)

City-state religion centred on temple and ziggurat; each city the household of its patron deity. The high gods of the pantheon take shape.

| Deities | Concepts / Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[anu]] · [[enlil]] · [[enki]] · [[ishtar|Inanna]] · [[nanna-sin]] · [[utu-shamash]] · [[ninhursag]] · [[dumuzi]] | the temple economy; [[sacred-kingship|divine kingship]] | the gods as city patrons; the *me* (divine ordinances) |

## II. The Akkadian & Old Babylonian Period (c. 2300–1600 BCE)

Political unification under Akkad and Babylon; [[marduk|Marduk]] rises to head the pantheon; the classic literary myths are composed.

| Figures / Deities | Texts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [[marduk]] · [[tiamat]] · [[apsu]] · [[gilgamesh]] · [[hammurapi]] | [[enuma-elish|Enūma Eliš]] · [[epic-of-gilgamesh|Epic of Gilgamesh]] | creation by combat; the flood; the quest for immortality |

## III. The Assyrian & Neo-Babylonian Period (c. 1600–539 BCE)

The Assyrian state god [Aššur] ascends; astral religion, divination, and omen-reading dominate; the underworld theology matures.

| Deities | Concepts | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| [Aššur] · [[nergal]] · [[ereshkigal]] | the [[mesopotamian-underworld|underworld (Kur/Irkalla)]]; astrology and omens | imperial cult; celestial divination |

## IV. Persian, Hellenistic & Decline (539 BCE – c. 100 CE)

Under Persian and Greek rule the temple cults persist but wane; the last datable cuneiform texts are astronomical. The tradition's myths leave deep traces on the [[judaism|biblical]] world ([[genesis-creation-accounts|creation]], [[flood-universality|flood]]).

| Legacy | Influence |
|---|---|
| astronomical/astrological cuneiform to the 1st c. CE | parallels in [[enuma-elish|Genesis cosmogony]] and the [[epic-of-gilgamesh|flood narrative]] |
