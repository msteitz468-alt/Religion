# Timeline of Cārvāka (Lokāyata)

A chronological hub for Cārvāka (also *Lokāyata*) — the materialist and skeptical school of Indian philosophy that denied the soul, rebirth, and liberation, accepting only perception as a means of knowledge. It survives almost entirely through the refutations of its opponents. For the cross-tradition view see the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]. Parent page: [[carvaka|Cārvāka]].

> Bracketed plain names (no link) are not yet developed as wiki pages. Cārvāka is a *school of thought*, not a cult; "eras" track its intellectual career.

---

## I. Origins in the Śramaṇa Milieu (c. 6th c. BCE)

Materialist and skeptical currents appear among the heterodox (*nāstika*) movements of the [[axial-age|Axial Age]] — e.g. the proto-materialist [Ajita Kesakambalī] — denying [[atman|ātman]], [[samsara|rebirth]], and [[moksha|liberation]].

| Figures | Doctrine |
|---|---|
| [Ajita Kesakambalī]; (legendary) [Bṛhaspati] | only the four elements are real; consciousness arises from matter |

## II. Classical Formulation (c. 400 BCE – 600 CE)

The school is systematized (the lost *Bṛhaspati Sūtra*); it accepts only **perception** (*pratyakṣa*) as valid knowledge, rejecting inference and scripture, and counts as one of the [[saddarshana|six-darśana]] surveys' standard opponents.

| Concepts | Position |
|---|---|
| perception-only epistemology; hedonist ethics | no afterlife, no karmic recompense, no liberation |

## III. Medieval Survival Through Refutation (c. 600–1600 CE)

Cārvāka is known mainly as the [[purva-paksha|*pūrvapakṣa*]] (opponent's view) refuted at the opening of rival systems' treatises, and through doxographies (e.g. the *Sarva-darśana-saṅgraha*).

| Mode of survival | Themes |
|---|---|
| polemical citation by [[advaita-vedanta|Vedānta]], [[buddhism|Buddhist]], and [[jainism|Jain]] authors | the standard foil for debates on the soul and the means of knowledge |
