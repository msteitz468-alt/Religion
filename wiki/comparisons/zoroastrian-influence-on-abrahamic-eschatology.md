# Zoroastrian Influence on Abrahamic Eschatology

The wiki's first **influence-tracing** page (the *Needed Sources.md* schema flag for Zoroastrianism). It
asks a disciplined historical question: how far did [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] ideas shape the
eschatology that the Abrahamic traditions came to share? The honest answer is **contested** — the
parallels are striking, the transmission plausible, and the proof elusive (recorded, per the Contradiction
Protocol, without overclaiming).

## The Parallels

Ideas prominent in Zoroastrianism (the [[gathas|Gathas]] and later Avestan/Pahlavi tradition) that are
faint or absent in the **earliest** Hebrew Bible but pervasive by the **Second Temple** period:

| Theme | Zoroastrian form | Abrahamic development |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmic good–evil dualism | Asha vs. Druj; Spenta vs. Angra Mainyu | Satan as adversary; [[gnosticism|Gnostic]] / [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]; [[antichrist|the antichrist]] |
| Ranked angels & demons | Amesha Spentas; daevas | the angelology/demonology of [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]] and later Judaism |
| Resurrection of the dead | bodily resurrection at the renovation | Dan 12; [[resurrection-body|NT resurrection]] |
| Final judgment & afterlife | Chinvat Bridge; heaven/hell | judgment, heaven, and hell across the Abrahamic traditions |
| A coming savior | the **Saoshyant** | messianic/eschatological savior expectation |
| Renewal of the world | **Frashokereti** | "new heavens and new earth" ([[new-creation|new creation]]) |

## The Historical Hinge

The plausible point of contact is the **Persian period**: the [[persians|Achaemenid Empire]] under
[[persians|Cyrus]] ended the [[babylon|Babylonian Exile]] and ruled Yehud for two centuries — exactly when
much Second Temple literature took shape and these themes surface (Daniel, [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]],
[[apocalyptic-literature|apocalyptic]] generally). The Bible itself uniquely calls Cyrus YHWH's "messiah"
(Isa 45:1) — see [[persians|Persians]].

## The Caveats (why it stays a controversy)

- **Dating**: the surviving Zoroastrian sources that state the eschatology most fully (the Pahlavi
  Bundahishn) are *late* (post-Islamic), so what Persians believed in the 5th c. BCE must be inferred —
  risking circularity.
- **Independent development**: resurrection and judgment can arise from inner-biblical logic (theodicy
  after the Exile) without borrowing.
- **Direction**: a minority argue for influence the other way, or a shared Near Eastern matrix.

The result is a genuine, unresolved cross-tradition influence question — held open here, with the
parallels and the caveats both on the record.
