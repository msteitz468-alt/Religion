# Scripture, Canon & Interpretation Across Traditions

A cross-tradition hub for three linked questions: **what becomes scripture** (canon), **how its authority is grounded** (inspiration/revelation), and **how it is read** (the literal–figurative–esoteric spectrum). This page gathers and links the wiki's material without adjudicating between the traditions' competing claims.

> Thematic navigation hub. Bracketed plain names lack a page yet.

## Recurring Structures

- **Canon as a *relation*, not a property** — the same text is scripture for one community, rejected by another (see any text page's `canon_scope`); canon-formation is a historical process, not a single event ([[nt-canon-formation]]).
- **Theories of inspiration/revelation** — verbal vs. ecstatic, dictated vs. authored ([[inspiration-of-scripture]], [[revelation]], [[word-of-god]]).
- **The literal–figurative–esoteric spectrum** — every tradition develops rules for when the surface sense yields to a deeper one ([[allegorical-reading]] ↔ [[theoria]]; [[zahir-batin]]; [[neyartha-nitartha]]).
- **Written + oral/normative supplement** — scripture paired with an authoritative interpretive tradition ([[oral-torah]], [[rule-of-faith]], [[sunna]]).

## Tradition by Tradition

### Judaism
A written Torah paired with the [[oral-torah|Oral Torah]]; the multilayered [[pardes|PaRDeS]] (peshat/derash/remez/sod) and the homiletical [[midrash|midrash]]; the formal rules of [[talmudic-hermeneutics|Talmudic hermeneutics]]. The [[septuagint|Septuagint]] marks an early canon divergence.

### Christianity
The closing of the [[nt-canon-formation|New Testament canon]] and the [[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]] as interpretive key; the [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]] vs. [[theoria|Antiochene theoria]] split, systematized in the [[fourfold-sense|fourfold sense (Quadriga)]] and [[typology|typology]]; patristic [[inspiration-of-scripture|theories of inspiration]]. (Modern: [[threefold-word-of-god|Barth's threefold Word]].)

### Islam
The [[quran|Qurʼān]] as uncreated, dictated revelation; the sciences of [[tafsir|tafsīr]], [[naskh|abrogation]], and the [[qiraat-variant-readings|variant readings (qirāʼāt)]]; the [[isnad|isnād]]-validated [[sunna|Sunna]]; and the [[zahir-batin|ẓāhir/bāṭin]] (exoteric/esoteric) layering favored by Sufism and Shīʻism.

### Buddhism
No single closed canon; the hermeneutical key is [[neyartha-nitartha|interpretable (neyārtha) vs. definitive (nītārtha)]] teaching, correlated with the [[two-truths|two truths]] — a device for ranking the Buddha's statements by level.

### Baháʼí (and the Abrahamic horizon)
[[progressive-revelation|Progressive revelation]] reframes canon across traditions: scripture is the renewed word of successive [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestations of God]], relativizing any single closure.
