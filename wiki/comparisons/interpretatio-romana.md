# Interpretatio Romana — Greek and Roman God Equivalences

**Interpretatio romana** is the ancient Roman practice of **identifying foreign gods with Roman ones** —
above all, the wholesale equation of the **Greek** pantheon with the **Roman**. By the late Republic the
Romans had so thoroughly mapped their gods onto the Greek Olympians (and adopted Greek myth via
[[ovid-metamorphoses|Ovid]] and [[virgil-aeneid|Virgil]]) that the two pantheons function as **one
mythology under two sets of names** — which is why the wiki pairs them on single figure pages.

## Caveats

The equivalence is **not exact**. [[ares|Mars]] was a dignified founder-god of Rome, not the despised
Greek Ares; [[hermes|Mercury]] is narrower than Hermes; **Saturn** and **[[janus|Janus]]** carry Italic
meanings with no Greek twin. And several great Roman gods — **[[janus|Janus]]**, **Quirinus**, the
**[[lares-and-penates|Lares and Penates]]**, **Bellona**, **Terminus** — are **genuinely Roman**, with no
Greek equivalent at all. The mapping is a real historical syncretism, not a perfect translation.
