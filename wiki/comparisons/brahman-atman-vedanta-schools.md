# One Canon, Rival Readings: Brahman and Ātman Across the Schools

This is the wiki's prime exhibit of its central Hindu phenomenon: **a single shared canon — the
prasthāna-trayī ([[upanishads|Upanishads]], [[bhagavad-gita|Gita]], [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]]) —
read into mutually irreconcilable metaphysics.** The division between the three classical Vedanta schools
is **hermeneutical, not canonical**: all accept the same texts as authoritative; they disagree on what
those texts *mean*, above all on the relation of the self ([[atman|ātman]]) to ultimate reality
([[brahman|Brahman]]). A fourth voice — [[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta's]] [[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir
Śaiva]] reading — stands *outside* Vedanta but comments on the same Gita, and so belongs in the contrast.

## The Test Sentence: *tat tvam asi* ("That thou art")

The Chāndogya Upaniṣad's *mahāvākya* "**that thou art**" is the single phrase each school must own:

| School | Reading of *tat tvam asi* | Ātman–Brahman relation |
|---|---|---|
| [[advaita-vedanta\|Advaita]] (Śaṅkara) | **literal identity** — "thou *art* That" | ātman **is** Brahman; difference is [[maya\|māyā]] |
| [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta\|Viśiṣṭādvaita]] (Rāmānuja) | inseparable **dependence** — thou art the *body* of That | distinct but inseparable; soul is Brahman's mode |
| [[dvaita-vedanta\|Dvaita]] (Madhva) | **direct reading on the basis of difference** (*mukhyārtha*), so the Advaitin's non-difference meaning is an illegitimate figurative *lakṣaṇā*; popularly also re-parsed *atat tvam asi*, "thou art *not* That" | eternally, really **distinct**; soul depends on God |
| [[kashmir-shaivism\|Kashmir Śaivism]] (Abhinavagupta) | **recognition** — thou art already Śiva, only forgotten | identity, but as dynamic self-recognition ([[pratyabhijna\|pratyabhijñā]]) |

## The Three Vedanta Poles

**[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] — non-dualism ([[shankara|Śaṅkara]], 8th c.).** Brahman is **nirguṇa**
(without attributes), the sole reality; the plural world and the personal God belong to
**[[maya|māyā]]**, superimposed on Brahman by ignorance ([[adhyasa|adhyāsa]]). The individual self, once
the not-Self is withdrawn, **is** Brahman. [[moksha|Liberation]] is **knowledge** (*jñāna*) that dispels
ignorance, possible while living (*jīvanmukti*). Method: identity-texts literal, difference-texts
provisional, via the [[two-truths|two levels of truth]].

**[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Viśiṣṭādvaita]] — qualified non-dualism ([[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]], 11th–12th
c.).** Reality is **one organic whole** in which souls (*cit*) and matter (*acit*) are the real **body**
of Brahman = the personal God **Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa**. Unity *and* real distinction: the self is inseparable
from God but never identical. [[maya|Māyā]] is God's **real creative power**, not illusion. Liberation is
**eternal loving communion** (*bhakti* + *prapatti*), never absorption.

**[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] — dualism ([[madhva|Madhva]], c. 1197–1276).** **[[panchabheda|Fivefold
eternal difference]]** (*pañca-bheda*): God, souls, and matter are all really and beginninglessly distinct.
Reality divides into the **independent** (*svatantra* = **Brahman = Viṣṇu**, the sole self-standing
reality) and the **dependent** (*asvatantra* = souls and matter). Souls are hierarchically ranked, and the
lowest are **eternally damned** (a doctrine near-unique in Indian thought). Liberation is **graded blissful
service**, never identity. Method: difference-texts literal, identity-texts figurative — the exact inverse
of Advaita.

## The Śaiva Fourth Voice

**[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaivism]] — supreme non-duality ([[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta]], 10th–11th
c.).** Not a Vedanta school: its scripture is the **Śaiva Āgamas/Tantras**, not the Upaniṣads, and it
holds reality to be the dynamic self-**recognition** ([[pratyabhijna|pratyabhijñā]]) of the one
consciousness, **Śiva**, who freely manifests as all things (*svātantrya*). Where Advaita's non-duality
is *static* (a passive witness behind an illusory world), the Śaiva non-duality is *dynamic* — the world
is Śiva's **real** self-expression, not māyā-as-error. Abhinavagupta's Gita is thus "more esoteric than
Vedanta" (*guhyāt vedāntād api guhyam*, Gītārtha-saṃgraha 18.63).

## The Knowledge/Action Crux

The same fault line runs through the **Gita**: does **knowledge alone** liberate (Śaṅkara), or knowledge
**with** devoted action ([[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]]'s ongoing bhakti; the Śaiva *dṛk*–*kriyā* unity)? That
debate has its own page: [[jnana-karma-samuccaya|jñāna-karma-samuccaya]].

## Cross-Tradition Resonances

The three Vedanta poles also orient Hinduism toward the other traditions: the **Advaita** pole approaches
monism (cf. [[kabbalah|Kabbalah's]] [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]]); the **Dvaita** pole approaches Abrahamic theism
(cf. [[tawhid|tawhid]], a personal creator eternally distinct from creatures); and the **[[maya|māyā]]
debate** — is the world illusion or real? — touches the Hindu–Buddhist quarrel over
[[sunyata|emptiness]]. The same schema point recurs throughout the wiki: **`canon_scope` can be identical
across sects while interpretation is opposite.**
