The **Heart Sutra** is the most concise and most recited text of the **Prajnaparamita** ("Perfection
of Wisdom") literature — the doctrinal heart of [[mahayana|Mahayana]] Buddhism. In a few hundred words
it distills the teaching of **[[sunyata|emptiness]]** (*sunyata*).

## The Core Statement

The bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara, contemplating deeply, sees that the five *skandhas* (the constituents
of the person) are empty, and addresses the arhat Shariputra with the sutra's famous formula: **"Form
is emptiness, emptiness is form"** — and likewise for sensation, perception, formation, and
consciousness. It then negates the standard categories of early Buddhist analysis (no eye, ear,
nose…; no [[four-noble-truths|suffering, origin, cessation, path]]; no attainment), closing with the
mantra *gate gate paragate parasamgate bodhi svaha*.

## Why it is Canon-Divergent

The Heart Sutra is `canonical_for: [mahayana, vajrayana]` but `rejected_by: [theravada]`, which does
not accept the Prajnaparamita sutras as the Buddha's word. Doctrinally it presses
[[anatta|non-self]] further than the early canon — emptiness applies not only to the self but to *all
phenomena (dharmas)*, including the Buddhist categories themselves. This is the conceptual frontier
between the two great Buddhist canons.

## Hermeneutics

Read as **[[neyartha-nitartha|nitartha]]** (definitive) by Mahayana; its negations are understood via
the **two truths** (conventional vs. ultimate), not as nihilism. Its esoteric, paradoxical register is
central to Zen and to [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] practice.
