# The Bundahishn ("Primal Creation")

The **Bundahishn** ("Creation" / "Primal Creation") is the great **Pahlavi (Middle Persian)
compendium of Zoroastrian cosmology, cosmogony, and eschatology** — the fullest surviving account
of how [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] made the world, how [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] assailed it,
and how the [[the-three-times|drama of the Three Times]] ends in [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]]. The
word *bundahishn* is itself the technical term for the **act of creation**.

## Nature and Date

The Bundahishn is **not Avestan scripture** but a **post-canonical priestly synthesis**, compiled
in roughly the **9th century CE** (after the Islamic conquest) from the **Zand** — the Pahlavi
translation-and-commentary on the now largely lost [[avesta|Avesta]]. It therefore **preserves
much older material** (Avestan cosmogony, otherwise lost) within a late redactional frame. Two
recensions survive: the longer **Greater (Iranian) Bundahishn** and the shorter **Indian
Bundahishn**. For this wiki it carries doctrine the fragmentary Avesta only implies, which is why
[[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]] draws the creation-and-renovation myth largely
from it.

## Contents

- **Cosmogony** — creation in two stages, *menog* (immaterial) then *getig* (material); the **seven
  creations** (sky, water, earth, plant, the Uniquely-created Bull, the First Man Gayomard, fire),
  each guarded by an [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spenta]].
- **The Assault** — Angra Mainyu breaks through the stone sky, salts the waters, makes deserts,
  withers the primal plant, and slays the Bull and the First Man, inaugurating the age of
  **Mixture**.
- **The 3,000-year schema** — the systematized scheme of **limited time** (millennial blocks) that
  postpones the consummation Zoroaster expected soon into a remote, calculable future (see
  [[the-three-times|the Three Times]]).
- **Eschatology** — the resurrection of the dead, the coming of the [[saoshyant|Saoshyant]], the
  **river of molten metal** through which all must pass, and the final renovation — the locus
  classicus (GBd XXXIV) quoted for "for the righteous it will seem like warm milk, and for the
  wicked, as if walking through molten metal."
- Geography, calendar, genealogies of the Kayanian kings, and the nature of the
  [[chinvat-bridge|soul's fate after death]].

## Canon and Reception

The Bundahishn is **authoritative and deeply esteemed** within Zoroastrianism as the systematic
exposition of the faith's cosmology, but it is **not part of the Avestan canon** — hence
`authoritative_for: [zoroastrianism]` rather than `canonical_for`. It is the principal text behind
the developed Zoroastrian eschatology that the
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|influence page]] compares with
[[apocalyptic-literature|apocalyptic]] periodization of history.
