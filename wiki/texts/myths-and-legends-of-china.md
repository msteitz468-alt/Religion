# Myths and Legends of China

**Myths and Legends of China** (1922) by **E. T. C. Werner** — British consular official and sinologist — is
the wiki's gateway compilation of **[[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk]] mythology**. It is **not scripture**
but a systematic Western retelling, organized largely by **division of the [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial
bureaucracy]]**: after chapters on Chinese sociology and the nature of Chinese mythology, it proceeds through
the gods ministry by ministry.

## Contents and structure

- **Cosmogony — [[pangu|P'an Ku]] and the creation myth**: the cosmic giant who separates Heaven and Earth and
  whose body becomes the world; the dualism of **yin and yang**.
- **The Gods of China**: the **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]** under the
  **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]**; the God of Literature (Wenchang); the structure of apotheosis.
- **Myths of the Stars; of Thunder, Lightning, Wind, and Rain; of the Waters; of Fire; of Epidemics, Medicine,
  and Exorcism** — the ministry-gods (Leigong the thunder-god, the Dragon-Kings of the waters, the plague and
  medicine gods).
- **The Goddess of Mercy** — the legend of **[[guanyin|Guanyin]]** (Miao Shan).
- **The Eight Immortals** — the [[the-eight-immortals|Daoist immortals]] and their attributes.
- **The Guardian of the Gate of Heaven; A Battle of the Gods** — material from the **[[investiture-of-the-gods|Fengshen
  Yanyi]]** (Li Jing, Nezha, the Shang–Zhou war that enrolls the gods).
- **How the Monkey Became a God** — the legend of **[[sun-wukong|Sun Wukong]]** from *Journey to the West*.
- **Fox Legends; Miscellaneous Legends** — the fox-spirit (húli jīng) lore and popular tales.

## Value and limits

Werner's compilation is **invaluable for its systematic map** of the pantheon as a bureaucracy, but reflects
its 1922 moment: dated transliteration (Wade-Giles, e.g. "P'an Ku," "Kuan Yin"), occasional colonial framing,
and a tendency to harmonize diverse local traditions. The deeper mythological **sources behind it** are the
Ming novels **[[investiture-of-the-gods|Investiture of the Gods]]** and **Journey to the West**, plus countless
local temple legends.
