# The Tibetan Book of the Dead (Bardo Thödol)

The **"Tibetan Book of the Dead"** is the Western name — coined by [[w-y-evans-wentz|W. Y.
Evans-Wentz]], editor of the first (1927) translation — for a cycle of [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]]
Buddhist texts of the [[nyingma|Nyingma]] school. Its own titles are ***Bar-do thos-grol
chen-mo*** ("The Great Liberation by Hearing in the Intermediate States") for the famous
funerary core, and ***Zab-chos zhi-khro dgongs-pa rang-grol*** ("Peaceful and Wrathful
Deities: Natural Liberation through Recognition of Enlightened Intention") for the whole cycle,
also known by its colophons as the **kar-gling zhi-khro** (the *zhi-khro* — "peaceful and
wrathful" — cycle of Karma Lingpa). It is a [[terma|treasure-text]] (*gter-ma*) attributed to
[[padmasambhava|Padmasambhava]] (8th c.) and revealed by the treasure-finder
[[karma-lingpa|Karma Lingpa]] in the 14th century.

> This page reflects the Penguin Classics *First Complete Translation* (Coleman, Jinpa, Dorje;
> see [[coleman-dorje-tibetan-book-of-the-dead|source page]]), the first English version of the
> entire cycle — fourteen chapters, of which Evans-Wentz translated only three. (The general
> Introduction speaks of the compilation as "twelve chapters," reflecting a different
> enumeration of the same material; the volume itself presents fourteen.) Not to be confused
> with the [[book-of-the-dead|Egyptian Book of the Dead]] — the shared English title is
> Evans-Wentz's coinage, not a textual relationship.

## Textual Overview and Structure

The cycle is a complete ritual-meditative system for **living and dying**, not merely a funerary
manual. The fourteen chapters move from lifetime practice to the moment of death to the
after-death journey:

1. **Natural Liberation of the Nature of Mind: The Four-session Yoga** — the preliminary
   practice; sung melodically in the early morning. (Attributed to Nyinda Choje Lingpa, Karma
   Lingpa's son; written down by Nyinda Özer.)
2. **A Prayer for Union with the Spiritual Teacher** — guru-yoga; the teacher as the three
   [[trikaya|buddha-bodies]]; "natural liberation **without renunciation** of the three poisons."
3. **Root Verses of the Six Intermediate States** — the verse summary of the six
   [[bardo|bardos]], for daily memorization.
4. **The Introduction to Awareness: Natural Liberation through Naked Perception** — the
   [[dzogchen|Dzogchen]] *pointing-out* text, direct introduction to [[rigpa|intrinsic awareness]].
5. **Natural Liberation of Habitual Tendencies** — the concise *generation-stage* sādhana that
   installs the [[peaceful-and-wrathful-deities|hundred peaceful and wrathful deities]] in one's
   own body.
6. **Natural Liberation of Negativity and Obscuration** — the Hundredfold Homage (110 prostration
   verses, one per deity).
7. **Natural Liberation through Acts of Confession** — tantric confession by the four antidotal
   powers, in the presence of the deities.
8. **Recognition of the Visual Indications and Signs of Death** — diagnostics of dying (external,
   internal, secret signs; the dissolution of the elements).
9. **Natural Liberation of Fear through the Ritual Deception of Death** — rites to avert untimely
   death.
10. **Consciousness Transference** — [[phowa|phowa]], ejecting consciousness at death.
11. **The Great Liberation by Hearing** — the core *Bardo Thödol* proper: read aloud to the dying
    and dead through the bardos of dying, reality (the deities), and rebirth.
12. **Aspirational Prayers** — recited at the time of death.
13. **A Masked Drama of Rebirth** — an allegorical morality play (Yama's judgment of the sinner
    Lakṣanāraka and the virtuous Śrījāta), still performed at Tibetan festivals.
14. **Liberation by Wearing** — *btags-grol*; mantra-diagrams worn on the body that liberate
    by being worn, seen, touched, heard, or felt.

## The Six Intermediate States (Bardos)

The organizing scheme (Chapter 3) extends the early-Buddhist *antarābhava* (the gap between
death and rebirth) into **six [[bardo|intermediate states]]** spanning life and death: (1)
**living** (birth to death), (2) **dreams**, (3) **meditative concentration**, (4) **the time
of death** (dying, culminating in the [[inner-radiance|inner radiance of the ground]]), (5)
**reality** (*chönyi* — the arising of the [[peaceful-and-wrathful-deities|peaceful and wrathful
deities]]), and (6) **rebirth** (*sipa* — the mental body seeking a womb among the
[[six-realms|six classes of beings]]). Each verse opens "Alas, now as the intermediate state of
X arises before me…" and prescribes the recognition appropriate to it.

## The After-Death Journey (Chapter 11)

- **Bardo of the time of death**: when respiration ceases, the consciousness arises as the
  [[inner-radiance|inner radiance of the ground]] (the "mother" radiance). Recognised, it *is*
  the [[trikaya|dharmakāya]] — "Just to recognise this is enough!" The officiant reads the
  pointing-out aloud into the corpse's ear, naming Samantabhadra/Samantabhadrī (awareness and
  emptiness in union).
- **Bardo of reality (*chönyi*)**: over "days," the [[peaceful-and-wrathful-deities|forty-two
  peaceful deities]] arise (the five buddha families, their consorts, bodhisattvas, gatekeepers,
  awareness-holders), each paired with a brilliant [[five-pristine-cognitions|pristine
  cognition]] and a dull competing light of a rebirth realm; then the **fifty-eight wrathful
  blood-drinking herukas**. The constant instruction: these "have spontaneously arisen… from the
  natural expressive power of your own awareness" — recognise them and be liberated.
- **Bardo of rebirth (*sipa*)**: failing recognition, the consciousness takes a **mental body**,
  is judged by [[yama|Yama]] in the mirror of past actions (a projection), and is driven toward
  rebirth. The text gives **five methods for obstructing the womb-entrances** and instructions for
  choosing a favourable birth or transferring to a pure buddha-field (Sukhāvatī, the western paradise of Amitābha).

Three graded outcomes structure the whole: recognition at death → dharmakāya; in the bardo of
reality → sambhogakāya; in the bardo of rebirth → nirmāṇakāya.

## Canon and Reception

The cycle is **canonical scripture for the [[nyingma|Nyingma]]** as a revealed [[terma|treasure]]
and broadly **authoritative within [[vajrayana|Tibetan Buddhism]]** as a funerary and contemplative
text; it is **not** part of any shared Buddhist canon and has no standing in
[[theravada|Theravada]] or East-Asian [[mahayana|Mahayana]]. Its transmission was esoteric and
restricted until the mid-15th century (Padmasambhava reportedly forbade even whispering its name to
the wind). The shorter funerary anthology was standardised by Rigdzin Nyima Drakpa (1647–1710);
the first xylograph was cut at Dzogchen Monastery in the mid-18th century. In the West, the title
and fame derive from Evans-Wentz and Kazi Dawa Samdup (1927), to which **Carl Jung** appended a
psychological commentary, reading the text "backwards" as a map of the psyche.

## Key Interpretive Cruxes

- **What the deities are.** The text insists the visions are projections of one's own awareness,
  not external beings — "Emptiness cannot be harmed by emptiness." This is the hinge between a
  literal-visionary and a psychological/[[dzogchen|Dzogchen]] reading (see Jung).
- **Liberation by hearing/wearing.** That recitation to a corpse, or a worn mantra-diagram, can
  liberate "without meditation" — even those guilty of the five inexpiable crimes — is the
  cycle's most distinctive and contested claim (see Hermeneutics).
- **The "twelve vs. fourteen chapters"** enumeration, and the relation of the long and short
  recensions.

## Hermeneutics

The cycle reads as [[dzogchen|Dzogchen/Atiyoga]]: Chapter 4 is the *khregs-chod* (Cutting through
Resistance) pointing-out; Chapter 11's bardo-of-reality visions are framed as an expression of
*thod-rgal* (All-surpassing Realisation). Its signature soteriology is **[[natural-liberation|natural
liberation]]** (*rang-grol*) — dissonant states are recognised as primordially pure, not
renounced. The "liberation by hearing" mechanism rests on: the heightened lucidity of awareness
after death; the persistence of hearing in the mental body; the meeting of the "mother" (ground)
and "child" (path) [[inner-radiance|inner radiances]] cultivated in life; and recognition itself
as the act that liberates. It is, throughout, a [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] system of generation and
perfection stages, deity yoga, and guru transmission.
