The **Yoga Sutras of Patanjali** are the foundational text of the **Yoga** *darshana* — one of the six
orthodox schools (*darshanas*) of [[hinduism|Hindu]] philosophy — distinct from the Vedanta schools
this tier centers on. In 196 aphorisms it lays out a practical psychology of liberation.

## Teaching

The opening defines the goal: *yogas chitta vritti nirodhah* — "yoga is the stilling of the
fluctuations of the mind." Patanjali sets out the **eight limbs** (*ashtanga*): *yama* and *niyama*
(ethical restraints and observances), *asana* (posture), *pranayama* (breath control), *pratyahara*
(withdrawal of the senses), *dharana* (concentration), *dhyana* (meditation), and *samadhi*
(absorption). The metaphysics is broadly **Samkhya** (a dualism of *purusha*, pure consciousness, and
*prakriti*, nature), with a distinctive role for **Ishvara** (a special purusha / God) as an object of
devotion and aid.

## Relation to the Vedanta Tier

The Yoga Sutras supply the **practical-meditative** dimension that the Vedanta schools presuppose but do
not systematize; the [[moksha|liberation]] (here *kaivalya*, "isolation" of consciousness) is conceived
differently from the Vedanta *moksha*-as-union/identity. A cross-school comparison worth a future page.
