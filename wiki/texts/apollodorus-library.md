**The Library** (*Bibliotheca*) attributed to **Apollodorus** is the most important surviving **handbook of
Greek mythology** — a systematic prose summary of the myths from the origin of the gods through the heroic
genealogies to the Trojan War and its aftermath.

## Value

Where [[hesiod-theogony|Hesiod]] is poetic and [[homer|Homer]] selective, the *Library* is a **comprehensive
reference** that organizes the whole mythological tradition into a connected genealogical narrative — the
go-to source for "who was the child of whom" and "what is the myth of X." It is invaluable precisely because
it is plain, complete, and orderly.
