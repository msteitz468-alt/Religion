**Scientology: The Fundamentals of Thought** (1956), by **[[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]**, is the
standard **introductory book of Scientology** — subtitled "The Basic Book of the Theory and Practice of
Scientology for Beginners." Where [[dianetics|Dianetics]] (1950) and [[science-of-survival|Science of
Survival]] (1951) belong to the early, ostensibly secular phase, *Fundamentals of Thought* presents the
**religion's** core framework in compact form.

## Core Teachings Introduced

This is the book that lays out, for beginners, the doctrines that organize [[scientology|Scientology]]:

- **The parts of man** — the [[thetan|thetan]] (the spiritual being), the mind, and the body.
- **The [[eight-dynamics|Eight Dynamics]]** — the urge to survive expressed across eight expanding spheres
  (self, sex/family, group, mankind, all life, the physical universe, spirit, and infinity/the Supreme
  Being).
- **The [[arc-triangle|ARC triangle]]** — Affinity, Reality, and Communication as the interrelated
  components of understanding (and of [[auditing|auditing]]).
- **The conditions of existence** — Be, Do, Have; the **cycle of action** (create–survive–destroy); and
  the goal of increasing survival along the dynamics.

## Place in the Corpus

As the **basic book**, *Fundamentals of Thought* is typically the entry point to Scientology's theory; it
is "Source," applied as **standard tech**. It distills the conceptual map that the graded [[auditing|
Bridge]] then works through in practice.
