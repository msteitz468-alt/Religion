The **Popol Vuh** ("Book of the Community") is the great sacred book of the **K'iche' Maya** — the single most
important surviving text of [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerican religion]] — preserving the **creation of the
world**, the epic of the **[[hero-twins|Hero Twins]]**, and the origin of the K'iche' people. Most Maya books
were burned by the Spanish; the Popol Vuh survived because it was written down (in K'iche' using Latin
letters) c. 1550.

## The Story (in detail)

**Creation.** In the beginning there was only the still sea and the empty sky. The creator gods —
**[[quetzalcoatl|Gucumatz (the Feathered Serpent)]]**, Tepeu, and the **Heart of Sky (Huracan)** — **spoke the
world into being**: they raised the earth from the water, the mountains, and the trees, and made the animals.
But the animals **could not speak or worship** their makers, so the gods set them to be eaten and resolved to
make a better creature who would **honor and feed them**.

**The failed humanities.** The gods first made people of **mud**, but they were formless and **dissolved**.
They next carved people of **wood**: these walked and multiplied but had **no hearts, no minds, and did not
remember or worship their makers** — so the gods destroyed them in a **great flood and rain of resin**, and
even their own dogs, grinding-stones, and cooking-pots **rose up against them** in revenge; the survivors
became the **monkeys** of the forest.

**The Hero Twins and Xibalba.** The longest section tells of the **[[hero-twins|Hero Twins, Hunahpu and
Xbalanque]]**. Their father and uncle, great ballplayers, had been summoned to **[[xibalba|Xibalba]]** (the
underworld) by its death-lords and **murdered** — the father's severed head hung in a calabash tree. A maiden
of Xibalba, **Xquic**, conceived the Twins from the skull's spittle and fled to the upper world to bear them.
Grown into clever tricksters and ballplayers, the Twins were themselves summoned to Xibalba and made to endure
its deadly trials — the Houses of **Gloom, Cold, Jaguars, Fire, and Bats** (a bat decapitated Hunahpu, but his
brother restored his head). By cunning they **defeated the Lords of Death**: they performed the "resurrection
trick" — sacrificing and reviving each other — and lured the chief lords into demanding the same, **sacrificing
them and refusing to bring them back**. Victorious over death, the Twins **rose into the sky as the Sun and the
Moon**.

**The people of maize.** At last the gods succeeded in making true humanity: they ground **white and yellow
maize** into dough and shaped the **first four men**, who could speak, think, and **worship**. But these first
people **saw and knew everything**, like gods — so the makers **clouded their vision**, leaving them wise but
limited. From them descend the K'iche', who **honor the gods**, fulfilling creation's purpose.

## Significance

The Popol Vuh enshrines the central Mesoamerican ideas — humanity **created of maize to nourish and worship
the gods**, the **conquest of death** by the Hero Twins (who become sun and moon), and the **cyclical
creations** — and is the wiki's window onto the [[maya-religion|Maya]] sacred world.
