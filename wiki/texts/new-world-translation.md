# New World Translation of the Holy Scriptures

The **New World Translation of the Holy Scriptures** (NWT) is the Bible translation produced by the Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society for use by [[jehovahs-witnesses|Jehovah's Witnesses]]. First released in stages beginning in 1950 (New Testament) and completed in 1961, with major revisions in 1984 and 2013, it is the exclusively authorized scripture for JW worship and study. The version in `raw/` is the **2013 revised edition** (2015 printing).

The NWT is one of the few Bible translations produced by a religious organization specifically to reflect that organization's distinctive theology. It is widely regarded by mainstream scholars as tendentious in its rendering of key theological passages — particularly on the Trinity, the divine name, and the nature of the soul — though its readability and idiomatic quality are generally acknowledged.

## Canon and Reception

**Canonical for**: [[jehovahs-witnesses|Jehovah's Witnesses]] — the NWT is the sole authorized Bible text; all other translations are treated as secondary references at most.

**Rejected by**: Mainstream Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Christianity — not on the basis of the canon's *contents* (the NWT uses the standard 66-book Protestant canon, rejecting the deuterocanon) but because of its **translation choices** on theologically critical passages. The NWT is regarded as tendentious rather than as a translation error by most mainstream scholarship.

## Translation Philosophy and the Committee

The title page states: "Rendered From the Original Languages by the NEW WORLD BIBLE TRANSLATION COMMITTEE." The identities of the committee members were never publicly disclosed by the Watch Tower Society (attributed to humility). Critics have noted that among the known members, few had formal training in biblical languages. Mainstream scholarship regards this anonymity as problematic for evaluating the translation's textual decisions.

The translation philosophy, per the NWT and Watch Tower literature:
- Restoring the divine name **Jehovah** (יהוה) wherever it appears in the Hebrew text, and also in those NT passages where the Governing Body judges the Greek *kyrios* (Lord) or *theos* (God) to stand in place of an underlying Tetragrammaton
- Translating each Hebrew and Greek word as consistently as possible (formal equivalence)
- Producing a readable modern English text

## Structure: Hebrew Scriptures / Greek Scriptures

The NWT uses the terms **"Hebrew Scriptures"** (for the Old Testament) and **"Greek Scriptures"** (for the New Testament) — deliberately rejecting "Old Testament" and "New Testament" as inaccurate or implying the Hebrew Bible is "old" or superseded. This naming is itself a theological stance: JWs do not use "Old Testament" in official literature.

The front matter also includes a 20-question introductory section ("An Introduction to God's Word") with thematic verse selections — including both OT and NT verses — preceding the Genesis text. This thematic section covers: who is God; what happens at death (annihilationist selection); what hope for the dead; the future paradise earth; and more.

## Distinctive Translation Features

### 1. The Divine Name: "Jehovah"

The NWT restores "Jehovah" for the Tetragrammaton (יהוה, YHWH) throughout the Hebrew Scriptures. The first occurrence in the text body is **Genesis 2:4**, where a footnote reads: *"The first occurrence of God's distinctive personal name, יהוה (YHWH). See App. A4."*

"Jehovah" is then used throughout — "Jehovah God" is the standard compound from Genesis 2 onward. "Sovereign Lord Jehovah" renders *adonai YHWH* (Gen 15:2). The NT thematic selections (in the introductory "20 Questions" section) confirm the NT also uses Jehovah: **Romans 10:13** — "Everyone who calls on the name of **Jehovah** will be saved" (a verse where mainstream translations render *kyrios* as "Lord").

### 2. Exodus 3:14 — "I Will Become What I Choose to Become"

The NWT renders the divine self-disclosure at the burning bush (*Ehyeh asher Ehyeh*, Exodus 3:14) as:

> **"I Will Become What I Choose to Become."**

The footnote offers the alternate: "Or 'I Will Prove to Be What I Will Prove to Be.' See App. A4."

Standard translations render this "I AM WHO I AM" (ESV, NRSV) or "I am that I am" (KJV), emphasizing timeless, absolute Being. The NWT's rendering emphasizes **dynamic purposive action** rather than timeless ontology — a translation choice that supports JW theology (God is actively choosing to fulfill his purposes; he is not a philosophical Absolute) and avoids the static-Being language that historically undergirded Trinitarian theology.

### 3. Genesis 1:2 — "God's Active Force"

The NWT renders *ruach Elohim* (Genesis 1:2):

> "God's **active force** was moving about over the surface of the waters."

The footnote offers "Or 'God's spirit.'" This rendering is one of the NWT's most theologically significant choices: it denies **personhood** to the Holy Spirit. In JW theology, "holy spirit" (*pneuma*) is not the third person of the Trinity but God's impersonal power or energy — his "active force" in the world. Mainstream translations render this "the Spirit of God" (ESV), "God's Spirit" (NIV), or "God's breath/wind" (various), with the personhood question debated but the rendering at least permitting personal interpretation.

### 4. Genesis 2:7 — "Living Person" (Soul)

The NWT renders *nefesh chayyah* at Genesis 2:7:

> "Jehovah God went on to form the man out of dust from the ground and to blow into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a **living person**."

The footnote reads: "Or '**soul**.' Hebrew, *neʹphesh*, which literally means 'a breathing creature.' See Glossary."

Standard translations "living soul" (KJV) or "living being" (ESV/NRSV). The NWT's "living person" — with the footnote explaining *nefesh* as "a breathing creature" — reflects **JW annihilationism**: the soul is not an immortal spiritual substance distinct from the body. A human *is* a soul (a living body); humans do not *have* immortal souls. This is the anthropological foundation for JW rejection of hell-fire and for the doctrine of death as a sleep state (*nefesh* = the whole living creature, which dies completely at death and is resurrected as a whole person).

The "soul" footnotes recur consistently throughout the narrative: Gen 1:20 ("living creatures / Or 'souls'"), Gen 2:19 ("living creature / Or 'soul'"), Gen 9:4 ("its life / Or 'soul'"), etc. The translation deliberately avoids "soul" in the body text to prevent immortal-soul inference.

### 5. "The True God" vs. "God"

The NWT sometimes uses the phrase "the true God" where the Hebrew simply reads *haElohim* (with the definite article). For example:
- "the mountain of the **true God**" (Exodus 3:1)
- Moses addressing "the **true God**" (Exodus 3:11)
- "serve the **true God**" (Exodus 3:12)

This rendering emphasizes the uniqueness of Jehovah against competing gods — consistent with JW strict monotheism (Jehovah alone is "the true God"; Jesus and spirit creatures are real but secondary).

### 6. "Stake" for Impalement Incidents

The NWT uses "stake" in OT contexts of execution by impalement or hanging: Joshua 10:26, "he struck them and put them to death and hung them on five **stakes**." This is the OT background for the NT term "**torture stake**" in the passion narratives (confirmed in the "20 Questions" front matter: John 19:23 — "when the soldiers had **nailed Jesus to the stake**"). The NWT rejects the traditional "cross" in favor of "torture stake" (Greek *stauros*), holding that the instrument of Jesus' death was an upright stake without a crossbeam.

### 7. Death as Sleep; "the Grave" not Hell

The "20 Questions" section explicitly uses the NWT's annihilationist anthropology:
- Question 11 ("What happens when someone dies?"): **Ecclesiastes 9:5, 10** — "the dead know nothing at all"; "no work nor planning nor knowledge nor wisdom in the **Grave**"
- **Psalm 146:4** — "His spirit goes out, he returns to the ground; on that very day his **thoughts perish**."
- **John 11:11-14** — Lazarus has "fallen asleep"; death = sleep
- **Revelation 20:12-13** — "death and **the Grave** gave up the dead" — "Grave" (*Hades* in Greek) is used where other translations use "Hell."

This confirms the NWT's systematic replacement of "Hell/Hades" with "the Grave" — reflecting JW rejection of conscious post-mortem punishment.

## Canon Scope Note

The NWT accepts the **66-book Protestant canon** and explicitly omits the deuterocanonical books. This is notable given JW origins in 19th-century American Restorationism, which inherited Protestant canon norms.

## Critical Assessment (Recorded without Adjudication)

**Mainstream scholarship**: The translation of key Christological passages (particularly John 1:1 in the Greek Scriptures, *not present in the ingested file*) has been the most contested ground. Scholars such as Bruce Metzger and others have argued that the NWT's Christological renderings are tendentious and grammatically unjustified. The Watch Tower Society and JW apologists dispute these assessments.

**The anonymous committee**: The refusal to name the translation committee has been criticized as preventing scholarly accountability. Where individual identities have been established (e.g., via court testimony), critics have argued the committee lacked the linguistic credentials for the translation decisions made.

**The Tetragrammaton in the NT**: The NWT's insertion of "Jehovah" in the NT is not supported by any known NT manuscript. The Watch Tower argues this represents a *restoration* of the divine name from underlying documents no longer extant; mainstream scholarship regards this as a theological interpolation rather than a textual recovery.

These positions are recorded without adjudication per the Contradiction Protocol. See [[word-was-god-1-1|John 1:1 controversy]].
