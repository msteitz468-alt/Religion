# The First Letter to the Corinthians

One of Paul's longest and most wide-ranging letters, 1 Corinthians addresses a largely Gentile urban congregation in Roman Corinth. [[roy-ciampa-brian-rosner|Ciampa and Rosner]] (PNTC, 2010) read it as a coherent pastoral response to the infiltration of Corinthian social values into the church — not as an ad hoc list of unrelated problems, nor primarily as deliberative rhetoric aimed at unity alone.

## Textual Overview

**Genre and form.** A real occasional letter from Paul (and Sosthenes, 1:1) to "the church of God in Corinth" (1:2), with a formal salutation (1:1–3), thanksgiving (1:4–9), body structured in major thematic panels, and a personal closing (16:1–24). Pauline authorship is not disputed in the critical tradition represented by this source.

**Macro-structure (Ciampa & Rosner).** Drawing on their 2006 *NTS* article and the full commentary, C&R argue that the letter follows a biblical/Jewish moral template for Gentile converts:

| Unit | Passage | Panel pattern |
|---|---|---|
| I. Letter opening | 1:1–9 | Salutation + thanksgiving |
| II. True and false wisdom / factionalism | 1:10–4:17 | Negative: no boasting in human leaders (1:18–2:5) → Positive: boast in the Lord (2:6–3:4) → application to ministers (3:5–4:17) |
| III. Sexual immorality | 4:18–7:40 | Negative: flee *porneia* (and greed) (4:18–6:20) → Positive: glorify God with your bodies (7:1–40) |
| IV. Idolatry and worship | 8:1–14:40 | Negative: flee idolatry (8:1–11:1) → Positive: glorify God in worship (11:2–14:40) |
| V. Resurrection and consummation | 15:1–58 | Theological climax |
| VI. Letter closing | 16:1–24 | Collection, travel, greetings |

The same four-element shape (cross/wisdom → sexual purity → anti-idolatry/proper worship → resurrection hope) appears, in C&R's reading, across the Pauline corpus (e.g. Rom 1:21–28; 1 Thess 1:9–10; Rom 15:5–16).

## Major Themes / Narrative Summary

**1. The cross as wisdom and power (1:18–2:5).** The "word of the cross" ends human pretension. C&R stress Isaiah as the scriptural backbone of this unit (Isa 29:14 and related judgment texts): God nullifies human wisdom so that faith rests on God's power, not rhetorical display.

**2. Social values, not a single heresy.** Against overrealized-eschatology or proto-Gnostic reconstructions as *the* key, C&R align with a rough scholarly consensus that the problems reflect uncritical acceptance of Roman Corinthian status-consciousness, litigation culture, sexual license, idol-table sociality, and disdain for bodily resurrection. Paul diagnoses the Corinthians as *sarkikoi* — "worldly, acting like mere human beings" (3:3).

**3. Church as eschatological temple.** Collective temple (3:16–17) and individual body-temple (6:19) ground ethics: sexual purity, idol-table abstinence, and ordered worship protect God's dwelling. See [[eschatological-temple|Eschatological Temple]].

**4. Flee *porneia* / flee idolatry.** The two defining Gentile vices in Jewish moral tradition structure the letter's ethical core. Greed appears as a tertiary member of the triad (5:10–11; 6:10; lawsuits in 6:1–11).

**5. Glory of God as ultimate goal.** "Do everything for the glory of God" (10:31); the end is God "all in all" (15:28). C&R treat glory as more fundamental than unity as the letter's telos, though unity remains real.

**6. Lordship of Christ and modified monotheism.** Christ titles pervade the letter. At 8:4–6 Paul reformulates the [[shema|Shema]] (Deut 6:4) with "one God, the Father" and "one Lord, Jesus Christ" — a primary locus for [[dyadic-devotion|dyadic devotion]] (Hurtado) that C&R also treat as creational monotheism + highest Christology underwriting anti-idolatry ethics.

**7. Love as criterion of gifts (ch. 13).** Love (*agapē*) is the "more excellent way" that reorders Corinthian pursuit of spectacular *charismata*; gifts are temporary, love endures.

**8. Bodily resurrection (ch. 15).** Climax of the letter: historical kerygma, Adam/Christ typology, Spirit-animated resurrection body (*sōma pneumatikon*), transformation at the last trumpet. See [[resurrection-body|The Resurrection Body]].

## Textual and Manuscript Tradition

C&R work from the Greek text with TNIV as base English translation; they discuss text-critical issues when interpretation turns on them (e.g. 13:3 "boast" vs "be burned"; placement of 14:34–35 in Western witnesses; shorter grace formula at 16:23). Full critical apparatus is not the commentary's focus. Early reception includes extensive patristic engagement (Clement of Rome writes to Corinth; later Fathers cite the letter heavily). Placement among the Paulines is stable in the major codices.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

| Framework | How it operates on this letter (per sources ingested) |
|---|---|
| **Biblical-theological / OT-first (Ciampa–Rosner)** | Structure, ethics, and self-understanding of Paul drawn primarily from Deuteronomy, Isaiah, Malachi, and Jewish moral tradition for Gentiles — not from Greco-Roman deliberative rhetoric as primary frame |
| **Grammatico-historical** | Attention to Greek verbal aspect without naïve tense theology; social-historical Corinth (status, *macellum*, triclinium/atrium) |
| **Salvation-historical / apocalyptic** | Cross as end of the age; inaugurated kingdom; God all in all |
| **Anti-rhetoric-as-primary-framework** | C&R resist Mitchell/Witherington deliberative-rhetoric thesis: handbooks concern oral speech; Paul's Greek shows Semitic/LXX influence; ancient interpreters did not find formal rhetoric as the key |
| **Evangelical-critical appropriation** | Scripture for the church; "penultimate" OT before "ultimate" gospel word (Bonhoeffer cited in method) |

## Canon and Reception

Canonical for catholicism, eastern-orthodoxy, and protestantism (`canon_scope` above). Universally received as Pauline in the ancient church; modern critical scholarship also affirms authenticity. In Protestant evangelical reception (PNTC series), the letter is primary for ethics, charismata, and resurrection doctrine. Patristic and medieval reception treat it as apostolic Scripture; Reformation ethics and modern denominational debates (women in worship, spiritual gifts, Lord's Supper discipline) repeatedly return to chs. 11–14 and 15.

## Scholarship and Historiography

**Dating and setting (Ciampa & Rosner).** Written from Ephesus (16:8) in the spring of AD 54 or 55, on the third missionary journey, a few years after Paul founded the church during an eighteen-month stay (Acts 18:1–18). Occasion: oral reports from "Chloe's people" (1:11) plus a Corinthian letter ("now concerning," 7:1 etc.). A previous Pauline letter is lost (5:9–11).

**Authorship.** Undisputed Pauline.

**Partition theories.** Some scholars divide 1 Corinthians into multiple letters (especially chs. 8–10). C&R defend literary unity: the panels cohere under the social-values diagnosis and the flee-vice / glorify-God structure; ch. 9 is example, not intrusion.

**History of interpretation of the problem.** Half a century of reconstructions (Gnosticism, Hellenistic Judaism, Stoicism/Cynicism/Epicureanism, overrealized eschatology) has, in C&R's account, yielded a rough consensus on cultural infiltration. C&R claim to apply the social-values thesis *more consistently* than peers (especially through ch. 7).

## Key Interpretive Cruxes

**1. Strong and weak / food offered to idols (chs. 8–10).** See [[food-offered-to-idols|Food Offered to Idols]]. C&R: unified argument; market meat free; cultic idol-table forbidden; love limits rights.

**2. Baptism for the dead (15:29).** Over forty interpretations exist. C&R reject vicarious proxy baptism: baptized *on account of* the righteous dead — motivated by hope of joining their resurrection; ordinary conversion baptism as an act of faith. Argumentative force does not require reconstructing every detail of the practice.

**3. Women's silence (14:34–35).** See [[women-and-worship|Women and Worship]]. C&R: authentic text (no MS omits it; Western transposition secondary); does *not* cancel 11:2–16 permission for women to pray/prophesy; *sigaō* is situational silence (// uninterpreted tongues, yielding the floor) — preferred sense: disruptive/shameful public questioning (often wives of others' husbands or of prophets) → ask own husbands at home. Application: principles of decorum travel; first-century form does not automatically transfer where mixed public speech is not shameful.

**4. Head coverings (11:2–16).** *Kephalē* as authority (not pure "source"); glory/shame chain to God; creation arguments; possible "new Roman woman" background. Women praying/prophesying assumed.

**5. *Sōma pneumatikon* (15:44).** Not immaterial spirit-stuff. Adjectives describe what *animates* the body (soul-life vs Spirit), not composition (Wright's ship analogy as C&R deploy it). Continuity with difference; model is Christ's risen body.

**6. "All things are lawful" (6:12; 10:23).** Corinthian slogans Paul cites and qualifies (beneficial; not mastered; build up).

**7. Lord's Supper abuses (11:17–34).** Class stratification (triclinium vs atrium reconstructions reported carefully); "discern the body" = the community as Christ's body; judgment already falling; wait for one another.

**8. Present crisis in ch. 7.** C&R favor real circumstances (including famine pressures) alongside eschatological urgency — not pure ascetic slogan-mirroring as the only frame.

## Authorship, Date, and Occasion (summary)

Pauline; Ephesus; spring 54 or 55; Chloe's report + Corinthian letter; previous letter lost. Congregation predominantly former Gentiles (12:2) in a status-conscious Roman colony.
