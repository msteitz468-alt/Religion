# Summa Theologica (Summa Theologiae)

The *Summa Theologiae* ("Summary of Theology") of [[thomas-aquinas|Thomas Aquinas]]
is the central work of medieval scholastic theology and the most influential
systematic theology in the Western Christian tradition. Composed c. 1265–1274 as a
teaching text for beginners in sacred doctrine, it was left unfinished at Aquinas's
death in 1274. It is **authoritative**, not scriptural: it is a work of theology *about*
the canon, not part of it. Within [[catholicism|Catholicism]] it acquired a quasi-official
status — Leo XIII's *Aeterni Patris* (1879) made Thomism the recommended framework for
Catholic philosophy and theology, and the Council of Trent reportedly placed the *Summa*
on the altar beside Scripture.

## Structure: the question–article method

The *Summa* is organized into three Parts (the Second Part itself split in two), each a
sequence of **Questions** (*quaestiones*), each Question divided into **Articles**
(*articuli*). Every Article follows the scholastic *disputatio* form:

1. The question posed as *Whether…?* (*Utrum…*)
2. **Objections** (*videtur quod*) — arguments for the position Aquinas will reject
3. **On the contrary** (*sed contra*) — an authority or argument for the true position
4. **I answer that** (*respondeo dicendum*) — Aquinas's own determination (the *corpus*)
5. **Replies to the Objections** — answering each objection in turn

Citations in this wiki follow the standard form: Part, Question, Article — e.g. *ST* I,
Q2, A3 for the Five Ways.

### The three Parts

- **Prima Pars (Part I)** — God, Trinity, creation, the angels, man, divine government.
  *(The scope of this ingest.)*
- **Prima Secundae (Part I-II)** — the end of man (beatitude), human acts, passions,
  habits, virtues and vices, law, grace.
- **Secunda Secundae (Part II-II)** — the theological and cardinal virtues treated in
  particular; the states of life.
- **Tertia Pars (Part III)** and the **Supplement** — Christ, the sacraments, the last
  things.

These correspond to the four raw files (`Summa Theologica - Part 1…4`); each is a
separately-scoped ingest.

## Major themes

The Prima Pars exhibits the *exitus–reditus* ("going out and return") architecture
Aquinas inherits and transforms: God in himself (Q1–43), the procession of creatures
*from* God (Q44–119), preparing for the return of the rational creature *to* God treated
in Parts II–III. Aquinas's method synthesizes Aristotelian philosophy ("the Philosopher")
with Christian revelation, alongside heavy use of [[augustine|Augustine]], Pseudo-Dionysius,
[[boethius|Boethius]], John Damascene, and Islamic and Jewish philosophers
([[avicenna|Avicenna]], [[averroes|Averroes]], [[maimonides|Maimonides]] — "Rabbi Moses").

## Key interpretive cruxes

- **The Five Ways** (*ST* I, Q2, A3) — five *a posteriori* demonstrations of God's
  existence from motion, efficient causation, contingency, gradation, and final cause.
- **Divine simplicity and the divine names** (Q3, Q13) — whether and how human language
  can signify God; the doctrine of [[analogy|analogical predication]].
- **God's knowledge of future contingents and predestination** (Q14, Q22–23).
- **Whether the world is eternal** (Q46) — Aquinas's distinction between what reason can
  demonstrate and what is held by faith.
- **Evil as privation** (Q48–49) and the problem of its cause.
- **The soul as the form of the body** (Q75–76) — the unicity of substantial form.

## Hermeneutical framework

Aquinas reads Scripture according to the medieval **fourfold sense** (the Quadriga:
literal, allegorical, tropological, anagogical), but with a decisive insistence —
articulated already at *ST* I, Q1, A10 — that *all* the spiritual senses are founded on
the **literal sense**, and that no argument can be drawn from the spiritual senses alone.
This "primacy of the literal" is a hallmark of his exegesis. See [[fourfold-sense]].

## Canon and Reception

The *Summa* is not part of any biblical canon; it is the pre-eminent work of
**scholastic** systematic theology. Its reception is itself a marker of tradition
identity: embraced and made normative within [[catholicism|Catholicism]] (Dominican
Thomism, the Neo-Thomist revival), engaged critically by the Protestant Reformers (who
prized Aquinas's doctrine of God while rejecting elements of his sacramental theology and
his synthesis with Aristotle), and read with interest but without the same authority in
[[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]]. Its standing is recorded in `canon_scope` as
*authoritative_for* Catholicism rather than canonical.

### Tertia Pars (Part III) + Supplement — Part 4 file

The Tertia Pars treats **Christ** (the Incarnation, QQ1–59) and the **sacraments** (QQ60–90),
breaking off mid-treatise on Penance at Aquinas's death (December 1273). The **Supplement**
(*Supplementum*, "XP"), compiled posthumously by Fra Rainaldo da Piperno largely from Aquinas's
earlier *Scriptum* on the *Sentences*, completes the unfinished matter: the rest of Penance,
Extreme Unction, Holy Orders, Matrimony, and the **Last Things** (resurrection, judgment, the
glorified body, hell), closing with two Appendices (on purgatory and the like). Question numbering:
the Tertia Pars runs Q1–90; the Supplement restarts at Q1. The body begins at line 1 (no table of
