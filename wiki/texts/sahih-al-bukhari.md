The **Sahih al-Bukhari** is the most authoritative **hadith** collection in [[sunni-islam|Sunni
Islam]] — for Sunnis, the most reliable book after the [[quran|Qur'an]] itself. Compiled by **Muhammad
ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari** (810–870) from a reported 600,000 traditions, of which he judged ~7,275
(with repetition) authentic (*sahih*), it is the first and foremost of the Sunni *Kutub al-Sittah*
(Six Books).

## Why this page is a canon-split exemplar

The Qur'an's [[quran|`canon_scope`]] is identical for every Muslim. The **hadith canon is where the
sects divide**, and this page makes that structural: Sahih al-Bukhari is `canonical_for:
[sunni-islam]` but `rejected_by: [shia-islam]`. Twelver [[shia-islam|Shia]] reject the Sunni
collections because their chains of transmission (*isnad*) run through Companions the Shia hold to
have wronged [[islam|Ali]]; the Shia receive instead their own **Four Books** (al-Kafi, etc.). One
text, opposite canonical standing in two sects of one tradition — exactly what the `canon_scope`
field exists to record.

## Content and Method

The collection is organized topically into *kitabs* (books) — faith, knowledge, prayer, alms,
pilgrimage, jihad, marriage, transactions, and the famous opening on the *beginning of revelation*.
Each report pairs an *isnad* (chain of transmitters back to the Prophet) with a *matn* (the text of
the saying or deed). Al-Bukhari's reputation rests on the **rigor of his isnad criticism** — his
demand that every link in the chain be a known, reliable transmitter with attested contact with the
next — which became a model for the science of hadith (*ilm al-rijal*).

## Significance

As the principal record of the *sunna* (the Prophet's normative practice), Sahih al-Bukhari is a
primary source of Sunni law (*fiqh*) and theology, second only to the Qur'an. Its hermeneutics are
those of *[[tafsir|tafsir bi'l-ma'thur]]* — the Qur'an interpreted through the Prophet's transmitted
words and deeds.
