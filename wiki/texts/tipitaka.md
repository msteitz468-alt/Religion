The **Tipitaka** ("Three Baskets") is the scripture of [[theravada|Theravada]] Buddhism and the oldest
complete Buddhist canon — the closest surviving witness to the teaching of the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]].

## The Three Baskets

- **Vinaya Pitaka** — monastic discipline (the rules of the *sangha*).
- **Sutta Pitaka** — the discourses of the Buddha, in five *Nikayas* (Digha, Majjhima, Samyutta,
  Anguttara, and the Khuddaka, which contains the [[dhammapada|Dhammapada]]).
- **Abhidhamma Pitaka** — systematic doctrinal analysis (the "higher teaching").

## Canon Scope

The Tipitaka is `canonical_for: [theravada]` and treated as `authoritative_for` the
[[mahayana|Mahayana]] and [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]], which preserve parallel early discourses (the
*agamas*) but **subordinate** them to the later Mahayana sutras. Conversely, Theravada
[[heart-sutra|rejects]] the Mahayana sutras — the defining Buddhist canon divergence.

## Significance and Hermeneutics

Transmitted orally for some four centuries and written down in Sri Lanka (1st c. BCE), the canon is
read in Theravada through the classical **[[buddhaghosa|commentaries of Buddhaghosa]]**. It is the
documentary basis for the [[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]], [[anatta|non-self]], and the path.
