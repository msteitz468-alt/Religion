# The Ifa Corpus (Odu Ifa)

The **Ifa corpus** is the great body of **divinatory verse** that functions as the
effective scripture of **[[yoruba-religion|Yoruba religion]]** and its diaspora forms
(**[[santeria|Santería]]**, **[[candomble|Candomblé]]**). It is **oral, not written**:
"the spoken word is his métier," said of its owner-orisha **Orunmila/Ifa**, applies to the
corpus as a whole. UNESCO has recognized the **Ifá divination system** as a Masterpiece of
the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity (2005/2008).

## Structure: 256 Odu

The corpus is organized by the **256 signatures (*odu*)** produced by Ifa divination —
**16 × 16** combinations cast with sixteen sacred palm nuts (*ikin*) or an eight-half-shell
divining chain (*opele*). Each *odu* carries a set of memorized **verse-stories** (*ese*) —
"typically at least four per signature," and a fully trained **babalawo** (diviner) holds
**at least a thousand** such verses. The verses narrate myths of the orishas, precedents,
proverbs, ethical lessons, and — crucially — the **sacrifices (*ebo*) prescribed** for the
client's situation.

## Use in Divination

The corpus is activated in a **client-centred** rite (see
[[yoruba-religion#Ifa Divination and the Babalawo|Ifa divination]]): the client does not
disclose her problem; the babalawo casts the *odu* and chants its verses; and **the client
chooses** which story fits her circumstance. The diviner is a **mediator**, not an oracle —
"You know the mystery … I know nothing." The corpus thus works less by delivering external
information than by helping the client **recover her own forgotten destiny** (the *ori*),
an operation [[stephen-prothero|Prothero]] compares to **Socratic recollection**.

## Canon and Reception

As an **oral, performance-bound canon**, the Ifa corpus differs structurally from the
written scriptures elsewhere in the wiki: there is **no fixed, closed text**, authority lies
in **trained memory and lineage** rather than in a book, and its power is held to reside in
**sound and correct recitation** as much as in semantic content. It is **canonical across
both the African and the New-World forms** of the tradition, transmitted in Yoruba in West
Africa and in creolized **Lucumí** (Cuba) and Portuguese-inflected (Brazil) registers in the
diaspora; the diaspora forms preserve the same *odu* structure while losing or renaming
parts of the African verse-stock.
