**1 Enoch** is the most important of the Jewish **pseudepigrapha** — a composite apocalyptic work
attributed to the antediluvian patriarch [[enoch|Enoch]] ("the seventh from Adam"). It is a cornerstone
of [[apocalyptic-literature|apocalyptic literature]] and a key witness to Second Temple Judaism, and its
canon history is among the most instructive in the wiki.

## The Richest Canon-Scope in the Wiki

One text, **five different standings** across communities — exactly what `canon_scope` exists to capture:

- **`canonical_for: [ethiopian-orthodoxy]`** — fully canonical scripture in the Ethiopian Orthodox
  Tewahedo Church (and historically among the Beta Israel, Ethiopian Jews) — the only living tradition to
  receive it.
- **`authoritative_for: [essenes]`** — the [[essenes|Qumran]] community treated it as authoritative
  (multiple Aramaic copies among the Dead Sea Scrolls), part of their expansive canon.
- **`disputed_by: [proto-orthodox-christianity]`** — some Fathers (notably Tertullian) esteemed it;
  others rejected it; [[jude|Jude]] 14–15 **quotes it as prophecy** (the basis of the
  [[jude-1-enoch-canon|Jude and 1 Enoch controversy]]).
- **`rejected_by: [rabbinic-judaism, catholicism, eastern-orthodoxy, protestantism]`** — excluded from
  the rabbinic, Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant canons alike.

## Content (five booklets)

- **The Book of the Watchers** (1–36) — the fall of the angelic "Watchers" who take human wives
  (expanding Gen 6:1–4), their giant offspring, and the origin of evil; Enoch intercedes. The source of
  the [[sons-of-god|sons of God]] tradition and of much later angelology and demonology.
- **The Book of Parables / Similitudes** (37–71) — the heavenly **Son of Man**, a pre-existent
  messianic judge (important for NT Christology).
- **The Astronomical Book** (72–82) — the solar calendar (cf. the [[essenes|Essene]] calendar dispute).
- **The Book of Dream Visions** (83–90) — the "Animal Apocalypse" of Israel's history.
- **The Epistle of Enoch** (91–108) — woes and exhortations; the Apocalypse of Weeks.

## Significance

1 Enoch shaped Second Temple and early Christian ideas of fallen angels, final judgment, resurrection,
and the Son of Man, and back-fills several existing wiki nodes: the [[enoch|Enoch]] figure, the
[[essenes|Essene]] expansive canon, the [[sons-of-god|sons of God / Watchers]] reading, and the
[[jude-1-enoch-canon|Jude controversy]].

## Dead Sea Scrolls Attestation

Aramaic fragments of 1 Enoch were found in multiple Qumran caves (4Q201–212: the Book of the Watchers, the Epistle, and the Astronomical Book; 4Q530-3: the related **Book of Giants**). The Qumran fragments confirm the Aramaic original of the Enochic texts and establish that the **Book of Parables (1 Enoch 37–71) is absent from Qumran** — the section containing the most developed "Son of Man" speculation. The community treated the Enochic literature as authoritative, not merely read. See [[book-of-enoch-dss]] for the full analysis; source: [[dead-sea-scrolls-vermes]].

VanderKam (2010) raises an important hypothesis about the structural gap: **the Book of Giants** (found in 9-10 Qumran copies; 4Q530-3) may have **occupied the slot of the missing Similitudes** in the Qumran version of 1 Enoch. If so, the Qumran "Enoch collection" replaced the Similitudes (with their developed Son of Man Christology) with the Book of Giants (expanding on the Watchers and their offspring). The Similitudes may be a later composition that entered the Ethiopic Enoch tradition after the Qumran period. See [[dead-sea-scrolls-vanderkam]].
