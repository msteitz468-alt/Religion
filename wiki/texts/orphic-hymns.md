The **Orphic Hymns** are a collection of 87 short ritual hymns invoking the gods, transmitted under the name
of the mythic poet **Orpheus** — part of the body of sacred Orphic literature that made [[orphism|Orphism]]
the rare Greek cult **with scriptures**.

## Character

Unlike the narrative [[homeric-hymns|Homeric Hymns]], the Orphic Hymns are **invocatory and theological** —
strings of cult-titles and epithets addressed to each deity, evidently for ritual use (with offerings of
incense). They present a distinctive, often **pantheistic and syncretic** vision in which the gods are
aspects of a single divine order, reflecting Orphism's [[orphism|theology of the soul]] and the cosmos.

## Significance

The hymns (with the Orphic theogonies and the gold-leaf afterlife tablets) document the Greek tradition that
came closest to a **doctrinal salvation religion** — immortal soul, reincarnation, purity, and release —
and that shaped Pythagoras and Plato.
