# The Kojiki

The **Kojiki** (古事記, "Record of Ancient Matters," **712 CE**) is the **oldest extant Japanese book** and the
primary scripture-like text of **[[shinto|Shinto]]**. Compiled by **Ō no Yasumaro** from the memorized
recitations of **Hieda no Are** at the command of Empress Genmei, it preserves the **age of the gods** (*kamiyo*)
and the legendary early imperial line, and grounds the **imperial house's descent from the sun-goddess**. The
wiki's source is **Basil Hall Chamberlain's** translation (1882).

## Structure and contents

Three books:

- **Book I — the Age of the Gods**: the spontaneous emergence of the first kami; the creation of the islands and
  gods by **[[izanagi-and-izanami|Izanagi and Izanami]]**; Izanami's death bearing the fire-kami
  **[[kagutsuchi|Kagutsuchi]]** and Izanagi's failed descent to **Yomi** (the underworld); the **purification**
  from which **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** (sun), **[[tsukuyomi|Tsukuyomi]]** (moon), and **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]**
  (storm) are born; the **heavenly rock-cave**; Susanoo's slaying of the **[[yamata-no-orochi|eight-forked
  serpent]]**; the cycle of **[[okuninushi|Ōkuninushi]]** and the ceding of the land; and the **descent of
  [[ninigi|Ninigi]]**, Amaterasu's grandson.
- **Book II** — from the first emperor **[[jimmu|Jimmu]]** through Emperor Ōjin: the foundation of the imperial
  line, half-legendary.
- **Book III** — emperors to the early 7th century: increasingly historical, with songs and genealogies.

## Significance and cruxes

- the **imperial-descent myth** — Amaterasu → Ninigi → Jimmu — the charter of the throne, made into ideology by
  **[[state-shinto|State Shinto]]**;
- the **Three Sacred Treasures** (the mirror *Yata-no-Kagami*, the sword *Kusanagi*, the jewel *Yasakani-no-Magatama*)
  given by Amaterasu to Ninigi — still the imperial regalia;
- the centrality of **purity and pollution** (Izanagi's purification, *[[harae|harae]]*) over moral law;
- **Kokugaku**: **Motoori Norinaga**'s 35-year *Kojiki-den* commentary (completed 1798) made the Kojiki the
  cornerstone of a "pure Japanese way" recovered beneath Chinese/Buddhist influence.
