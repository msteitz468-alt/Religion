# On the Celestial Hierarchy (*Peri tes Ouranias Hierarchias*)

**On the Celestial Hierarchy** is one of the two "hierarchical" treatises of [[pseudo-dionysius|
Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite]], addressed to his "fellow presbyter Timothy." It establishes
the theological framework of **hierarchy** (*hierarchia* — a Dionysian coinage) as ordered,
graded mediation of divine illumination, and applies it to the nine orders of angels arranged
in three triads. The work provides the most influential systematic treatment of angelology in
Christian tradition.

## The Dionysian Definition of Hierarchy

Dionysius coins the term *hierarchia* to mean: a sacred ordering of intelligences, each of
which is purified, illuminated, and perfected, and in turn purifies, illuminates, and perfects
those below it, in proportion to their capacity to receive divine light. The purpose of
hierarchy is [[theosis-divine-nature|deification]] (*theosis*) — the elevation of each
being toward likeness with God.

## The Nine Orders of Angels (Three Triads)

### First Triad (closest to God; direct participation in divine light)

- **Seraphim** (*"Burning"* / *"Kindling"* in Hebrew): "ever moving around things Divine, and
  constancy, and warmth, and keenness, and the seething of that persistent, indomitable, and
  inflexible perpetual motion" (Caput VII §I). They burn with fire-like ardor toward God and
  "purify through fire and burnt-offering." Their nearness to God is unmediated; they receive
  the divine light at "first hand."
- **Cherubim** (*"fulness of knowledge"* / *"stream of wisdom"* in Hebrew): their name "denotes
  their knowledge and their vision of God, and their readiness to receive the highest gift of
  light, and their power of contemplating the super-Divine comeliness in its first revealed
  power" and "ungrudging communication to those next to them, by the stream of the given
  wisdom" (Caput VII §I).
- **Thrones**: "manifest exaltation above every grovelling inferiority, and their supermundane
  tendency towards higher things… and their bearing God; and the ardent expansion of themselves
  for the Divine receptions" (Caput VII §I). They are the receptacle of God's immediate
  presence.

These three are "established immediately around God" and constitute "one… first Hierarchy, than
which there is not another more Godlike or immediately nearer to the earliest illuminations of
the Godhead" (Caput VI). They are purified, illuminated, and perfected *by the Godhead itself*,
without intermediary.

### Second Triad
- **Lordships** (*Dominations*): "unslavish elevation, free from all grovelling subserviency…
  superior to every kind of cringing slavery… ever aspiring to the true Lordship, and source of
  Lordship" (Caput VIII §I). They model free, Godlike authority, untouched by any servile
  passion.
- **Powers** (*Virtues*): "courageous and unflinching virility, for all those Godlike energies
  within them — not feebly weak for the reception of any of the Divine illuminations…
  vigorously conducted to the Divine imitation" (Caput VIII §I).
- **Authorities** (*Principalities*): "the beautiful and unconfused good order, with regard to
  the Divine receptions, and the discipline of the supermundane and intellectual authority, not
  using the authoritative powers imperiously for base purposes" (Caput VIII §I).

This middle triad receives divine illumination "at second hand, through the first Hierarchical
Order, and passing through this middle as a secondary manifestation" (Caput VIII §I).

### Third Triad (closest to the human world; mediating divine illumination downward)
- **Principalities**: their "princely and leading function, after the Divine example… being
  wholly turned to the super-princely Prince, and leading others in princely fashion" (Caput
  IX §I). They govern earthly nations through angelic superintendence (Michael assigned to
  Israel; other angels to other nations, per Caput IX §II).
- **Archangels**: middle position within the third triad, "belongs alike to the most holy
  Principalities and to the holy Angels." They "receive hierarchically the Divine illuminations
  from the first powers, and announce the same to the Angels in a godly manner" (Caput IX
  §II). Gabriel and Michael are cited by name.
- **Angels**: "they are more properly named Angels by us than those of higher degree, because
  their Hierarchy is occupied with the more manifest, and is more particularly concerned with
  the things of the world" (Caput IX §II). They are humanity's immediate intermediaries,
  communicating divine law and prophetic revelation.

## Structure and Contents

### Caput I — Every Divine Illumination Goes Forth While Remaining Simple

Addressed to Timothy. Dionysius invokes "Jesus, the Paternal Light, the Real, the True, 'which
lighteth every man coming into the world'" as the source of all hierarchical illumination
(Caput I §II). The treatise's fundamental premise: every divine illumination remains *simplex*
even as it goes forth through many levels; its procession does not compromise its unity. The
sensible, symbolic forms used by Scripture (wings, fire, animal heads) are pedagogical veils
for minds unable to ascend directly to intelligible contemplation — "the material guidance
suitable to itself" (Caput I §III). Parker's translator frame presents this as authentic
apostolic teaching to Paul's disciple Timothy.

### Caput II — Fitness of Dissimilar Symbol for Celestial Beings

Dionysius argues for the *superiority* of *dissimilar* symbols (fire, beasts, strange forms)
over apparently dignified ones (gold, light, human form). The risk of similar symbols is
literalism — readers might "rest in the types as though they were true" (Caput II §II).
Dissimilar figures shock the mind upward, preventing it from confusing the image with the
reality. The same principle governs both cataphatic praise and apophatic negation: "the
negations respecting things Divine are true, but the affirmations are inharmonious" (Caput II
§III). The sacred hiddenness keeps "the holy and secret truth respecting the supermundane
minds inaccessible to the multitude" (Caput II §II).

### Caput III — What is Hierarchy; What Its Use

The foundational definition: "Hierarchy is, in my judgment, a sacred order and science and
operation, assimilated, as far as attainable, to the likeness of God, and conducted to the
illuminations granted to it from God, according to capacity, with a view to the Divine
imitation" (Caput III §I). Its purpose: "the assimilation and union, as far as attainable,
with God, having Him Leader of all religious science and operation" (Caput III §II). Each
rank performs three functions — purification, illumination, perfection — toward those below
it, and receives the same from those above it (Caput III §§II–III). "For it is not lawful for
the Mystic Rites of sacred things… to practise anything whatever beyond the sacred regulations
of their own proper function" (Caput III §II). Hierarchy is the structure of deifying grace.

### Caput IV — What the Name "Angel" Means

All created rational and intellectual beings share in God's goodness in graduated degrees.
The angelic beings participate "in a higher degree than things which merely exist, or which
lead an irrational life" (Caput IV §II). They are called *Angels* ("messengers") because "the
supremely Divine illumination comes to them at first hand, and, through them, there pass to us
manifestations above us" (Caput IV §II). Dionysius cites angelic mediation of the Mosaic Law,
prophetic visions, and even the Annunciation as instances of this descending illumination.
"Angels first were initiated in the Divine mystery of the love of Jesus towards man, then,
through them, the gift of its knowledge passed to us" (Caput IV §IV).

### Caput V — Why All Heavenly Beings Are Called "Angels" Generally

The higher orders (Seraphim, Thrones, Principalities) are not *properly* named "angels,"
since "it does not possess the highest powers, but, as it conducts our inspired Hierarchs to
the splendours of the Godhead known to it" (Caput V). The name "angel" is applied to all in
common because *every* celestial order shares the characteristic of communicating divine
illumination downward; yet the lowest order bears it most properly because they deal most
directly with human affairs. "Throughout every sacred ordinance the superior ranks possess the
illuminations and powers of their subordinates, but the lowest have not the same powers as
those who are above them" (Caput V).

### Caput VI — What Is the First Order; Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones

The "Word of God has designated the whole Heavenly Beings as nine, by appellations, which
shew their functions," divided into three threefold orders. The first triad is "established
immediately around God, with a nearness superior to all" (Caput VI). The specific names and
properties are treated in Caput VII. The ordering was transmitted to Dionysius by his "Divine
Initiator" (Hierotheus, his teacher).

### Caput VII — The First Hierarchy: Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones

Full etymological and theological treatment of the first triad (see "Nine Orders" above). The
key claim about their Hierarchical function: they are "purified, and enlightened, and perfected,
by being ministered from the very Author of initiation, through its elevation to It
immediately" (Caput VII §III). They do not receive illumination through intermediaries: "not
through other holy Beings, but being ministered from the very Godhead" (Caput VII §II). The
Seraphim's cry "Holy, Holy, Holy" (Isaiah 6) is interpreted as transmission of divine theology
downward — "the first impart their knowledge of divine things to the second" (Caput X §II).
Section III discusses the Isaiah 6 theophany: the Seraphim's six wings (covering faces and
feet, flying with middle wings) symbolize "complete covering with wings, and their manifold
faculty of leading to the Really Being" (Caput XIII §IV / Caput XV treatment).

### Caput VIII — The Middle Hierarchy: Lordships, Powers, Authorities

The second triad (titled "Dominations" / *Lordships* in Caput VIII) receives illumination
"at second hand, through the first Hierarchical Order." Dionysius provides two Old Testament
illustrations: Zechariah's vision, in which a higher angel learns from God the comforting words
for Israel, then instructs a lower angel to reveal them to the prophet (Caput VIII §II); and
Ezekiel's vision, in which a Cherub-adjacent figure (the man in linen) receives God's judgment
before the battle-axe angels (Caput VIII §II). "The fulness of Divine things manifested
directly to ourselves is more perfecting than the Divine contemplations imparted through
others" (Caput VIII §II) — mediation is real but attenuates light.

### Caput IX — The Third Hierarchy: Principalities, Archangels, Angels

The Principalities model divine *principality* — leadership ordered toward God. Archangels
occupy the "middle position in the Hierarchy between the extremes" of this third triad,
mediating between Principalities and Angels. The Angels are "more properly named Angels by us
than those of higher degree, because their Hierarchy is occupied with the more manifest, and
is more particularly concerned with the things of the world" (Caput IX §II). Angelic
superintendence of nations: "the Word of God has assigned our Hierarchy to Angels, by naming
Michael as Ruler of the Jewish people, and others over other nations" (Caput IX §II). Israel's
privilege was self-elected faithfulness, not arbitrary divine favoritism (Caput IX §III).
Melchizedek cited as a non-Jewish hierarch "conducted to the true and only Godhead" through his
own angel (Caput IX §III).

### Caput X–XI — Summary; Why All Angels Are Called "Heavenly Powers"

Caput X restates the principle of graduated mediation: "from the most reverend Order… is
purified, and illuminated, and perfected by a gift of light from the Godhead… and from this
(Order) again, in due degree, the second, and from the second, the third, and from the third,
our Hierarchy" (Caput X §I). All divine minds are "distributed into three — into essence, and
power, and energy" (Caput XI §II). The name "Heavenly Powers" is applied to all in common
because all share in supermundane being, but it does not dissolve the distinctions between
orders (Caput XI §II).

### Caput XII — Why Human Hierarchs Are Called "Angels"

The human bishop (*hierarch*) is called "Angel of the Sovereign Lord" in Scripture (Malachi
2:7) not because he possesses the full angelic power but because he "participates, according
to his own capacity, in the messenger characteristic of the Angels, and elevates himself, as
far as attainable to men, to the likeness of their revealing office" (Caput XII §II). The
inferior shares proportionally in the characteristics of the superior; it does not share them
identically. Dionysius also uses "gods" (*theoi*) for both heavenly minds and holy men who are
"entirely and wholly turned to Its Oneness as far as possible" (Caput XII §III).

### Caput XIII — Why Isaiah Was Purified by a Seraphim

An extended discussion of the Isaiah 6 commissioning. Two interpretations offered: (1) the
being who purifies Isaiah was a *lower* angel given the *name* Seraphim because he acted
through fire; (2) the angel who appeared referred his own purifying action upward to God and
to the first hierarchy as the true source, acting as their instrument (Caput XIII §§II–III).
Dionysius prefers the second: "the Angel who effected the purification of the Theologian
attributes his own purifying science and power to God, indeed, as Cause, but to the Seraphim
as first-operating Hierarch" (Caput XIII §III). The analogy: just as our bishop (*hierarch*)
is said to purify through his priests, the Seraphim purifies through lower angels. The divine
light passes through the most transparent media: "the distribution of the sun's ray passes
with easy distribution to first matter, as being more transparent than all" (Caput XIII §III).

### Caput XIV — The Number of Angels

The scriptural "thousand thousands and myriad myriads" (Dan 7:10) does not set an exact
number but indicates that "the ranks of the Heavenly Beings cannot be numbered by us" (Caput
XIV). The angelic hosts "surpassing the weak and contracted measurement of our material number"
are known only by their own supermundane intelligence and by God.

### Caput XV — Symbolic Forms of Angels in Scripture

An extensive enumeration and theological explanation of every material symbol applied to
angels in Scripture: **fire** (most prominent) — Dionysius gives a lengthy phenomenology of
sensible fire as analogy for the divine: "the sensible fire is, so to speak, in everything,
and passes through everything unmingled… It is both uncontrollable and invisible, self-subduing
all things" (Caput XV §II). **Human form**: signifies intellectual faculty, upright posture,
governing power; individual body parts allegorized (eyes = transparency for divine light;
nostrils = discrimination; ears = reception of divine inspiration; hands = power to act;
feet = swift movement; wings = elevation; nakedness = freedom from earthly attachment)
(Caput XV §III). **Robes and implements**: shining garments = likeness to fire and divine
enlightenment; girdles = gathered uniformity of orientation; rods = kingly direction; spears
and battle-axes = discriminating separation; geometric tools = building up the subordinate
orders (Caput XV §§IV–V). **Animal forms**: Lion = leading, robust, concealment of footprints
(divine hiddenness); Ox = mature strength, receptivity; Eagle = kingly vision, unflinching
gaze at divine light; Horses = obedience and docility, with colors signifying different divine
qualities (white = divine light; dark blue = hiddenness; red = fiery vigor; piebald = union
of extremes) (Caput XV §§VII–VIII). **Rivers, wheels, chariots**: rivers of fire = ungrudging
divine streams; chariots = conjoined fellowship; wheels (*Gel, Gel* in Hebrew = "revolutions
and revelations") = perpetual movement around the Good and manifestation of hidden things
(Caput XV §IX). **The joy of angels** = "Godlike and ungrudging rejoicing over the care and
salvation of those who are turned to God" (Caput XV §IX).

## Principle of Hierarchical Mediation

A central Dionysian claim: lower beings cannot receive divine illumination directly from the
highest source; it must be transmitted through intermediate orders in proportion to each being's
capacity. This applies both to the celestial hierarchies and (in the *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*)
to the human church. Direct contact with the divine light would overwhelm rather than elevate
lesser natures. The principle generates the nine-fold structure and justifies the use of
symbolic, material forms in scripture as pedagogical accommodations for lower (human) capacity.

## Influence on Angelology

The Dionysian nine-fold scheme became *the* standard Christian angelology in both East and
West: adopted by [[thomas-aquinas|Thomas Aquinas]] (Summa Theologica I, qq. 106–114),
influencing Dante's *Paradiso*, and shaping Eastern Orthodox liturgical theology.
