# The Avesta

The **Avesta** (a title that probably means something like "Authoritative Utterance") is the
collected scripture of [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], composed in the otherwise-unknown
**Avestan** language and transmitted for many centuries **orally** by the priesthood before being
committed to writing. Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], the **surviving
corpus is only a fraction** of the great collection assembled under the Sasanians; much was lost
after the Islamic conquest.

## Language Strata

- **Old (Gathic) Avestan** — the archaic dialect of [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]] himself, close to
  Vedic Sanskrit and linguistically very difficult (full of *hapax legomena*). Carries the
  **[[gathas|Gathas]]** and the **Yasna Haptanhaiti** ("Worship of the Seven Chapters").
- **Younger Avestan** — the later liturgical language of the rest of the corpus; some of its
  material is nonetheless **very old** (notably certain *yashts*).

Western scholars distinguish the post-Gathic texts as the "Younger Avesta," but to the tradition
**all** of it embodies aspects of Zoroaster's revelation and is reverenced accordingly.

## Major Divisions

- **Yasna** — the core liturgy recited at the daily act of worship (the *yasna* ceremony); its
  heart is the **[[gathas|Gathas]]** (Y 28–34, 43–51, 53) embedded within it, framed by the great
  manthras (Ahuna Vairya, Ashem Vohu, Yenhe hatam, Airyema ishyo).
- **Visperad** — supplementary invocations extending the Yasna for festivals.
- **Yashts** — 21 hymns to individual divinities (the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]] and
  Yazatas — Mithra in Yt 10, the [[fravashi|fravashis]] in Yt 13, etc.); a rich repository of old
  Iranian myth.
- **Vendidad (Videvdad)** — "the law against the demons (Daevas)": purity law, the myth of Yima,
  and prescriptions against pollution.
- **Khordeh Avesta** — the "Little Avesta," a lay prayer-book of selected daily texts.

## The Zand

The Avesta is transmitted alongside its **Zand** — the Middle Persian (Pahlavi)
**translation-and-commentary** that made the archaic scripture intelligible. (The old Western label
"Zend-Avesta" conflated the two.) The Zand is the bridge to the later **Pahlavi books** — notably
[[bundahishn|the Bundahishn]] and the Denkard — which preserve the developed cosmology and
eschatology that the fragmentary Avesta only implies. Reciting and expounding the Avesta through
the Zand is the tradition's native **hermeneutical framework**.

## Canon and Reception

The Avesta is **scripture for Zoroastrianism** and for it alone; the Gathas form its most sacred and
authoritative core (see the **[[gathas|Gathas page]]** for the canon detail). Its theological
vocabulary — [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]], [[asha-druj|asha]], the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]],
[[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]], [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]] — is the source material for the
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|influence question]].
