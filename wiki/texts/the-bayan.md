# The Bayán

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from lines 7792–7829 unless noted.*

## Overview

The Bayán (Arabic: "Exposition," "Explanation") is the title of two works by [[the-bab|the Báb]]: a longer one in Persian written whilst he was a prisoner at Maku (1847–8), and a shorter one in Arabic from the same period. Both books were left incomplete (lines 7792–7799).

## Structure and Content

Both works were envisaged as consisting of nineteen "unities" (*váhids*), each of nineteen chapters (i.e., 19 × 19 = 361), this number having numerological significance. The Persian Bayán stops in its ninth *váhid* and the Arabic in its eleventh, the Báb leaving their completion to the prophetic figure "He Whom God Shall Make Manifest" (*Man-yuzhiruhu'lláh*). Bahá'ís regard [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]]'s *Kitáb-i-Íqán* as the completion (lines 7799–7812).

The major contents of the book are: (1) the elements of Babi religious law; (2) discussion of various religious concepts; and (3) glorification of the expected "He Whom God Shall Make Manifest" (lines 7812–7815).

## Theological Significance

Shoghi Effendi regarded the Bayán primarily as "a eulogy of the Promised One," which had at the same time: (a) abrogated the laws of Islam; (b) proclaimed the advent of the Day of Resurrection; and (c) prophesied the future rise of the Bahá'í World Order (*GPB* 24–6) (lines 7815–7821).

## Canon and Reception

Within the Bahá'í tradition the Bayán has an ambiguous canonical status: it is theologically important and doctrinally formative, but its laws have been superseded by the *Kitáb-i-Aqdas*. It is not binding for Bahá'ís in terms of practice (see [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia]], lines 8750–8753).

## Translations and Scholarship

There are French translations of both works (by A.L.M. Nicolas). Browne produced an abstract and index of the Persian Bayán (*Selections* 316–406; *Kitáb-i-Nuqtatu'l-Káf* liv–xcv). English translations of selected extracts appear in *Báb, Selections* 77–113 (lines 7822–7829).
