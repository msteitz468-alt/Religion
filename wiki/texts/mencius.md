The **Mencius** (*Mengzi*) records the teachings and debates of **Mencius** (Mengzi, c. 372–289 BCE),
the "Second Sage" of [[confucianism|Confucianism]] and its most important classical thinker after
[[confucius|Confucius]]. One of the **Four Books**, it is more developed and argumentative than the
[[analects|Analects]].

## Central Teachings

- **The innate goodness of human nature** — Mencius's signature doctrine: all people are born with the
  **"four sprouts"** (incipient compassion, shame, courtesy, and a sense of right/wrong) that grow into
  the cardinal virtues if cultivated. This sets the classical **human-nature debate** against **Xunzi**
  (who held nature bad) — a defining Confucian controversy.
- **Benevolent government (*ren zheng*)** — a ruler who cares for the people's welfare gains the
  **Mandate of Heaven** (*Tianming*); a tyrant forfeits it, and Mencius boldly affirms a **right of the
  people** against a ruler who has become a mere "fellow" (a near-justification of just rebellion).
- The nurturing of the **"flood-like *qi*"** (vast, moral energy) through righteousness.

## Significance

Zhu Xi's elevation of the Mencius into the Four Books made Mencius's "innate goodness" the orthodox
Confucian view of human nature for late imperial East Asia, shaping ethics, education, and political
thought. Its theory of legitimate rule by the people's welfare is among the most consequential political
ideas in the Chinese tradition.
