# Gods and Fighting Men

**Gods and Fighting Men** (1904) is **Lady Augusta Gregory's** continuous retelling of the two great
divisions of **Irish [[celtic-religion|Celtic]] mythology** — the **Mythological Cycle** ("the gods") and the
**Fenian / Ossianic Cycle** ("the fighting men"). It is the wiki's accessible gateway to the Irish material
that survives scattered across Middle Irish manuscripts (the *Lebor Gabála Érenn* / "Book of Invasions,"
*Cath Maige Tuired* / "The Battle of Mag Tuired," the *Acallam na Senórach*, and the Fenian lays).

## Part I — The Gods (Mythological Cycle)

The coming and reign of the **[[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]]**, "the people of the goddess Danu":

- their arrival, their four talismans (the **Lia Fáil** / Stone of Destiny, the **Spear of [[lugh|Lugh]]**,
  the **Sword of [[nuada|Nuada]]**, and the **Cauldron of the [[the-dagda|Dagda]]**);
- the **First Battle of Mag Tuired** against the **Fir Bolg**, in which Nuada loses his hand and so his
  kingship (a blemished king cannot rule), until **[[dian-cecht|Dian Cécht]]** makes him a silver one;
- the coming of **[[lugh|Lugh]]** "of the Long Arm," master of every art;
- the climactic **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**, where the Tuatha Dé throw off the **Fomorian** tyranny and
  **Lugh slays his grandfather, the one-eyed [[balor|Balor]]**, by putting out his death-dealing eye with a
  sling-stone;
- the **[[morrigan|Morrígan]]** over the battlefield; the **[[the-dagda|Dagda]]** and his cauldron;
  **[[manannan|Manannán mac Lir]]** of the sea; and the eventual **defeat by the Milesians** and the Tuatha
  Dé's withdrawal into the **[[the-otherworld|síde]]**.

## Part II — The Fighting Men (Fenian Cycle)

The heroic saga of **Fionn mac Cumhaill (Finn MacCool)** and the **Fianna** war-band — Fionn's gaining of
wisdom from the **Salmon of Knowledge**, his son the poet **Oisín**, the grandson **Oscar**, the tragic
elopement of **Diarmuid and Gráinne**, and **Oisín's journey to [[the-otherworld|Tír na nÓg]]** ("the Land of
the Young") with Niamh and his fatal return to a Christianized Ireland three hundred years later.

## Mythological cruxes

- the **euhemerization** of the Tuatha Dé Danann from gods into an "invading race" in the *Lebor Gabála*;
- **sovereignty**: the rule that a **blemished king (Nuada) cannot reign** — kingship bound to bodily
  wholeness and to the land-goddess;
- **[[lugh|Lugh]]** as the pan-Celtic light/skill god (Gaulish **Lugus**; Welsh **Lleu**);
- the **Fomorians** as chaos-powers (cf. the [[the-combat-myth|combat myth]]; Balor's blighting eye).
