# Sukhmani Sahib

**Sukhmani Sahib** ("the Jewel of Peace" / "Bead of Bliss" — *sukh* = peace/comfort, *mani* = gem/jewel) is a long hymn by [[guru-arjan|Guru Arjan]] (Mehl 5), Angs 262–296 of the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]. Composed in Raag Gauree, it is one of the most beloved and recited compositions in the Sikh tradition, frequently read in full as a daily or weekly devotional practice. The composition names itself in its final Ashtapadī: "O Nanak, by these Glorious Virtues, this is named Sukhmani, Peace of Mind." (L66597–66600, Ang 296)

## Structure

24 **Ashtapadīs** (8-stanza hymns), each preceded by a two-line **Shalok** (couplet) that announces the Ashtapadī's central theme. Closed with the marker "||8||N||" for each Ashtapadī. The entire composition is in Raag Gauree, Mehl 5.

**Opening Rahao (refrain)**:
> "Sukhmani: Peace of Mind, the Nectar of the Name of God. The minds of the devotees abide in a joyful peace." (L58589–58594, Ang 262)

**Simaran formula** (Ang 262):
> "Remembering God, one does not have to enter into the womb again. Remembering God, the pain of death is dispelled." (L58595–58600)

## The Twenty-Four Ashtapadīs

**Ashtapadī 1** — *Simaran: Remembrance as liberation.* Remembering God removes all suffering, enemies, pain, the cycle of birth and death, and fear. All treasures lie in God's love. Only those God Himself inspires can truly remember Him.

**Ashtapadī 2** — *Naam surpasses all religious practices.* Nam-simaran surpasses yoga, asceticism, Jain disciplines, pilgrimage, charity, and physical austerities. "None equal to Naam even once as Gurmukh." The Name is available to all but rare to find.

**Ashtapadī 3** — *Naam as supreme religion.* "Of all religions, the best religion is to chant the Name of the Lord and maintain pure conduct." (L59482–59487, Ang 265) Naam is the best effort, best speech, best location.

**Ashtapadī 4** — *God's grace and human ingratitude.* God gave body, food, family, senses — yet the mortal forgets Him. "You are our Mother and Father; we are Your children." Prayer for divine remembrance is itself a divine gift.

**Ashtapadī 5** — *Mithya: Falseness of worldly things.* Everything worldly is *mithya* (false): body, wealth, family, ego, possessiveness, power, youth, desire, anger — all false. Only devotion and Saadh Sangat are permanent.
> "False are body, wealth, and all relations. False are ego, possessiveness and Maya." (L60030–60036, Ang 269)

**Ashtapadī 6** — *"Jih parsaad" (By His Grace).* Structured around the anaphora "by His Grace" — by His Grace you have food, scent, silk, sleep, body, honor, wealth, pleasures. "Nothing is in our hands."

**Ashtapadī 7** — *Saadh Sangat: Power of Holy Company.* Face becomes radiant, ego removed, wisdom revealed, God comes near, conflicts settled — all through Saadh Sangat. "There is no difference between the Holy people and God."

**Ashtapadī 8** — *Brahm Gyani: The God-conscious being.* The Brahm Gyani is unattached like the lotus in water, unstained like the sun, impartial like the wind, patient like the earth, humble despite being highest of all, free of sorrow, not bound by karma. The culminating identification:
> "The God-conscious being is always unattached, as the lotus in the water remains detached." (L60942–60948, Ang 273)
> "The God-conscious being is the highest of the high. Within his own mind, he is the most humble of all." (L61001–61006, Ang 273)
> "O Nanak, the God-conscious being is Himself the Supreme Lord God." (L61145–61148, Ang 274)

**Ashtapadī 9** — *Apras: The "touch-nothing" Saint; functional virtue.* Redefines Hindu religious roles in devotional terms. The true Vaishnaav lives apart from Maya, serves without seeking reward; the true Pandit instructs his own mind. Universal access: "The Beej Mantra, the Seed Mantra, is spiritual wisdom for everyone. Anyone, from any class, may chant the Naam." (L61404–61409, Ang 275)

**Ashtapadī 10** — *Vastness of creation: "Ka-ee kot" anaphora.* Extended catalogue of God's creation — millions of worshippers, siddhas, kings; millions of birds, snakes, stones, trees; millions of moons, suns, stars; millions of demons, heavens, hells. All strung on His thread. "He is near to all, and yet far from all."

**Ashtapadī 11** — *Hukam: God as sole Doer; merger.* All governed by Hukam — earth, creation, occupation, colors, forms. The soul wandering through incarnations finds Saadh Sangat and does not return.
> "As water comes to blend with water, his light blends into the Light. Reincarnation is ended, and eternal peace is found." (L62242–62253, Ang 278)

**Ashtapadī 12** — *Pride vs. Humility.* Pride of power, youth, good deeds, and wealth each leads to further bondage. True humility (*gareebee*) brings liberation. "Without contentment, no one is satisfied." God alone is the Doer.

**Ashtapadī 13** — *Slanderer of Saints.* Extended anaphora on "sant kaa dokhee" — the slanderer of Saints: shortened life, fallen into hell, polluted intellect, like a fish out of water. Yet repeatedly: "if the Compassionate Saint shows His Kindness, O Nanak, in the Company of the Saints, the slanderer may still be saved."

**Ashtapadī 14** — *God in all things; Jivan Mukta.* Defines liberation while alive: as is joy, so is sorrow; as is gold, so is dust; as is nectar, so is poison; as is honor, so is dishonor — the Jivan Mukta sees all equally, abiding in God's Will. "Know that God and His humble servant are one and the same." (L63225, Ang 283)

**Ashtapadī 15** — *Guru's Word as support; "Sat" as ultimate descriptor.* The Guru's Word supports the mind as pillars a palace. A stone in a boat crosses the river. Extended "sat" (true) refrain: True is His form, True is His place, True is His Word, True is His creation.

**Ashtapadī 16** — *Naam as cosmic support ("Naam kay dhaaray").* Extended anaphora: Naam supports all creatures, all earthly and solar systems, the Vedas and Puraanas, spiritual wisdom and meditation, the Akaashic ethers and nether regions, all bodies and worlds. "Those whom the Lord mercifully attaches to His Naam — in the fourth state, those humble servants attain salvation."

**Ashtapadīs 17–21** — Continuation of thematic development: God's truth in all dimensions; the soul's wandering and return; God's pervading presence; soul in God's hands; God as sole Actor.

**Ashtapadī 22** — *God's presence; mystical body.* "All the creation is His Body." "Through all eyes, He Himself is watching." "The nine treasures are in the Ambrosial Name of God. Within the human body is its place of rest. The Deepest Samaadhi, and the unstruck sound current of the Naad are there."

**Ashtapadī 23** — *The blessed servant; Jivan Mukta who liberates others.* "Jivan Mukta — liberated while alive; the Lord God abides in his heart." "Blessed, blessed, blessed is the coming of that humble being; by his grace, the whole world is saved."

**Ashtapadī 24** — *Final recapitulation; self-naming.* Enumerates all fruits of the Naam — comfort, peace, wisdom, learning, spiritual powers, opening of heart-lotus, beauty, intelligence, realization of reality, impartial vision. Names itself:
> "Sukhmani is the peaceful ease, the Glory of God, the Naam. When it abides in the mind, one becomes wealthy." (L66449–66456, Ang 296)
> "O Nanak, by these Glorious Virtues, this is named Sukhmani, Peace of Mind." (L66597–66600, Ang 296)

## Theological Distinctives

The Sukhmani Sahib develops several concepts from the Granth to their fullest expression:

**[[naam|Naam]] as supreme**: Surpasses yoga, asceticism, pilgrimage, charity — available to all castes, the seed mantra for everyone.

**[[brahm-gyani|Brahm Gyani]]**: Ashtapadī 8 provides the Granth's most sustained portrait of the spiritually perfected person — culminating in identity with God itself.

**Jivan Mukta (liberation while living)**: Ashtapadī 14 defines the fully liberated state as equanimity in the present — not deferred to death or afterlife.

**Mithya**: Ashtapadī 5 is the most comprehensive statement of worldly falseness in the Granth; all social and material attachments are equally "false."

**[[saadh-sangat|Saadh Sangat]]**: The company of the Saints is the primary institutional and social vehicle for liberation — appearing in virtually every Ashtapadī.

**[[hukam|Hukam]]**: God alone is the Doer; human agency is illusory. Yet contentment with Hukam is liberation.

**Hermeneutical approach**: Non-allegorical, direct devotional (bhakti) mode. The text does not employ layers of peshat/derash or Zahir/Batin; its interpretive principle is experiential internalization of Naam through Simran.
