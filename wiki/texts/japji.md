Japji is the opening composition of the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] (Adi Granth) and the foundational daily prayer of the [[sikhism|Sikh]] tradition. It is the sole work in the Adi Granth not intended to be sung — "Japji is the only work recorded in the Adi Granth which is not intended to be sung. Instead it is chanted." (McLeod ~L1387–1388). It is composed by [[guru-nanak|Guru Nanak]] and constitutes the first element of the early-morning order of daily worship.

## Canon and Reception

Japji holds uncontested canonical status within all streams of [[sikhism|Sikhism]] including the [[khalsa|Khalsa]]. It opens the Adi Granth and frames the entire scripture's theological vision. No tradition within Sikhism disputes its centrality.

## Structure and Content

### The Mool Mantar (Basic Credal Statement)

Japji opens with the **Mool Mantar** — the foundational theological axiom prefixing the entire Granth and each new rāg section (Thind/Khalsa translation, L31–34, Ang 1):

> One Universal Creator God. The Name Is Truth. Creative Being Personified. No Fear. No Hatred. Image Of The Undying, Beyond Birth, Self-Existent. By Guru's Grace ~

Transliteration: *ik-oNkaar sat naam kartaa purakh nirbha-o nirvair akaal moorat ajoonee saibhaN gurparsaad*

Each phrase is a distinct theological assertion:
- **Ik Oankaar** ("One Universal Creator God"): radical monotheism; the numeral 1 + the primal sound Om/Aum, asserting the oneness and creativity of the divine
- **Sat Naam** ("The Name Is Truth"): God's nature is identical with Truth; the Name carries divine reality, not merely a label
- **Kartaa Purakh** ("Creative Being Personified"): God is an active personal Creator, not an abstract principle
- **Nirbhau / Nirvair** ("No Fear / No Hatred"): God's fearlessness and freedom from enmity become ethical standards
- **Akaal Moorat** ("Image of the Undying"): God is timeless (akaal = beyond death/time)
- **Ajoonee** ("Beyond Birth"): God does not take birth — an implicit contrast with Hindu avatar doctrine
- **Saibhang** ("Self-Existent"): God is self-caused, not created
- **Gur Prasaad** ("By Guru's Grace"): access to God comes through the Guru's grace, not individual striving alone

McLeod summarizes: "There is one Supreme Being, the Eternal Reality, the Creator, without fear and devoid of enmity, immortal, never incarnated, self-existent, known by grace through the Guru" (~L1390–1394).

### The Thirty-Eight Pauris (Stanzas)

The Granth's Thind/Khalsa translation renders the 38 stanzas as follows. Key thematic clusters:

**Pauri 1 — The Problem** (Ang 1): Thinking, silence, fasting, and worldly accumulation all fail to reach God. The solution: "O Nanak, it is written that you shall obey the Hukam of His Command, and walk in the Way of His Will." (L72–84)

**Pauri 2 — Hukam** (Ang 1): The fullest treatment of [[hukam|Hukam]] in the text. All bodies, souls, high/low stations, blessings, and wanderings are within Hukam. The critical move: "Everyone is subject to His Command; no one is beyond His Command. O Nanak, one who understands His Command, does not speak in ego." (L116–122) — understanding Hukam dissolves [[haumai|haumai]] (ego).

**Pauri 3** (Angs 1–2): God as the inexhaustible Great Giver; the Giver never exhausts His gifts across millions receiving across all ages.

**Pauri 4 — Amrit Vela** (Ang 2): "In the Amrit Vaylaa, the ambrosial hours before dawn, chant the True Name, and contemplate His Glorious Greatness. By the karma of past actions, the robe of this physical body is obtained. By His Grace, the Gate of Liberation is found." (L214–222) — establishes the Sikh pre-dawn devotional discipline.

**Pauri 5 — The Guru's Word** (Ang 2): "The Guru's Word is the Sound-current of the Naad; the Guru's Word is the Wisdom of the Vedas; the Guru's Word is all-pervading. The Guru is Shiva, the Guru is Vishnu and Brahma" — a rhetorical subsumption of Hindu deities under the Guru principle, not independent divine affirmation.

**Pauri 6 — Grace over ritual** (Ang 2): "If I am pleasing to Him, then that is my pilgrimage and cleansing bath. Without pleasing Him, what good are ritual cleansings?" (L281–283)

**Pauri 7 — Nadar** (Ang 2): Even perfect worldly reputation across four ages is worthless without God's [[nadar|Nadar]] (Glance of Grace). "God blesses the unworthy with virtue, and bestows virtue on the virtuous. No one can even imagine anyone who can bestow virtue upon Him." (L337–344)

**Pauris 8–11 — Suniai (Listening)** (Angs 2–3): Four parallel stanzas each opening "Suniai" (Listening). Through listening to the Naam/Shabad: Death cannot touch the listener; spiritual wisdom, yoga's secrets, and the Shaastras all become known; the blind find the Path; the Unreachable comes within grasp. Refrain: "O Nanak, the devotees are forever in bliss. Listening — pain and sin are erased."

**Pauris 12–15 — Mannai (Faith)** (Ang 3): Four parallel stanzas on the "state of the faithful." "The state of the faithful cannot be described. One who tries to describe this shall regret the attempt." (L454–474) Consequences of faith: intuitive awareness, freedom from Death's messenger, the Door of Liberation, family redemption.

**Pauri 16 — Dharma as the cosmic Bull** (Ang 3): "The mythical bull is Dharma, the son of compassion; this is what patiently holds the earth in its place." (L561–569); then the universe created from a single Word: "You created the vast expanse of the Universe with One Word! Hundreds of thousands of rivers began to flow." (L612–617)

**Pauris 17–18 — Asankh (The Countless)** (Angs 3–4): Paired catalogues — countless meditators, lovers, scholars, warriors, givers on one side; countless fools, thieves, oppressors, sinners on the other. Both end: "Whatever pleases You is the only good done, You, Eternal and Formless One."

**Pauri 19 — The Word and Destiny** (Ang 4): "From the Word, comes the Naam; from the Word, comes Your Praise. From the Word, comes spiritual wisdom... From the Word, comes destiny, written on one's forehead." (L740–757). "The created universe is the manifestation of Your Name." (L774–779)

**Pauri 20 — Karma** (Ang 4): The mind stained by sin can only be cleansed by love of the Name. "actions repeated, over and over again, are engraved on the soul. You shall harvest what you plant. O Nanak, by the Hukam of God's Command, we come and go in reincarnation." (L822–832)

**Pauri 21 — The Unknown Moment of Creation** (Angs 4–5): The question of when creation began — Pandits cannot find it in the Puranas, Qazis cannot find it in the Quran, Yogis do not know. "The Creator who created this creation — only He Himself knows." (L884–894)

**Pauris 22–26 — God's Infinity** (Angs 5–6): Countless worlds, no written account could capture creation. One not equal to an ant that does not forget God — all hierarchy overturned. God alone knows His own extent. "If anyone presumes to describe God, he shall be known as the greatest fool of fools!" (L1211–1216)

**Pauri 27 — The Cosmic Concert** (Angs 6–7): Reprised in the Sodar evening prayer. "Where is that Gate, and where is that Dwelling, in which You sit and take care of all? The Sound-current of the Naad vibrates there, and countless musicians play on all sorts of instruments there." (L1217–1225). All creation praises God; only those pleasing to His Will truly sing.

**Pauris 28–31 — The Yogi Stanzas** (Angs 6–7): Yogic disciplines radically reinterpreted as inner virtues. "Make contentment your ear-rings, humility your begging bowl, and meditation the ashes you apply to your body... See the brotherhood of all mankind as the highest order of Yogis; conquer your own mind, and conquer the world." (L1358–1376)

**Pauri 33 — No Power** (Ang 7): The most systematic statement of divine sovereignty: "No power to speak, no power to keep silent. No power to beg, no power to give. No power to live, no power to die... O Nanak, no one is high or low." (L1511–1545)

**Pauris 34–37 — The Five Khands (Realms)** (Angs 7–8):
- **Dharam Khand** (Realm of Dharma, Pauri 34): The earth as "a home for Dharma"; judgment by deeds; the Mark of Grace distributed
- **Gian Khand** (Realm of Wisdom, Pauri 35): Countless manifestations; Naad vibrates; "His limit has no limit!"
- **Saram Khand** (Realm of Humility, Pauri 36): "Forms of incomparable beauty are fashioned there"; consciousness and mind are shaped
- **Karam Khand** (Realm of Grace, Pauri 37a): Only mighty spiritual warriors dwell there; "totally fulfilled, imbued with the Lord's Essence"
- **Sach Khand** (Realm of Truth, Pauri 37b): "The Formless Lord (Nirankaar) abides there. Having created the creation, He watches over it. By His Glance of Grace, He bestows happiness." (L1706–1716). "O Nanak, to describe this is as hard as steel!" (L1726–1734)

**Pauri 38 — The Smith's Metaphor** (Ang 8): The culminating synthesis. "Let self-control be the furnace, and patience the goldsmith. Let understanding be the anvil, and spiritual wisdom the tools. With the Fear of God as the bellows, fan the flames of tapa, the body's inner heat. In the crucible of love, melt the Nectar of the Name, and mint the True Coin of the Shabad, the Word of God." (L1735–1755)

### Closing Slok

> Air is the Guru, Water is the Father, and Earth is the Great Mother of all. Day and night are the two nurses, in whose lap all the world is at play. Good deeds and bad deeds — the record is read out in the Presence of the Lord of Dharma. (L1768–1783, Ang 8)

> Those who have meditated on the Naam, the Name of the Lord, and departed after having worked by the sweat of their brows — O Nanak, their faces are radiant in the Court of the Lord, and many are saved along with them! (L1789–1803, Ang 8)

The closing Slok establishes **kirat karo** (honest labor) alongside Naam meditation as the dual Sikh ethical foundation.

## Major Themes

- **Hukam (Divine Order)**: All of creation operates within the divine Order, and submission to it is the path to Truth. No human effort outside of this alignment suffices.
- **Nam Simran (Remembrance of the Name)**: The Name (*Nam*) — the reality of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] — must be heard, believed, and held within the heart. External religion (pilgrimage, ritual) is valueless without interior love.
- **The Guru's Word (Shabad)**: Listening to the divine Word transmitted through the Guru is the transformative act; it overcomes Death, bestows wisdom, and brings the devout to bliss.
- **Karma and Grace**: Human destiny is shaped by past deeds, but grace alone reveals the door to liberation. Grace and karma are held in tension throughout.
- **Non-gendered Divinity**: McLeod notes that the Sikh concept of Akal Purakh is explicitly non-gendered and that Sant Bhasha uses the same word for he/she. His translation deliberately renders divine epithets in gender-neutral English to reflect this.
- **Five Realms**: The spiritual journey passes through successive realms — Duty, Knowledge, Endeavour, Fulfilment, and Truth — each characterized by intensifying spiritual quality.

## Liturgical Use

Japji is the first element of the early-morning order of Sikh worship. "Having risen early a Sikh should bathe and repeat these works" — Japji, followed by Guru Gobind Singh's Jap Sahib, and then the Ten Savayyas from the Dasam Granth (~L1382–1386). Japji is chanted, not sung, distinguishing it from virtually all other compositions in the Adi Granth.

## Manuscript Tradition and Text Format

McLeod notes that the text of the Adi Granth and Dasam Granth runs continuously to the right-hand margin without line breaks; the end of each hymn-line is marked by "do dande" — two vertical strokes (||) (~L1374–1381).

## Hermeneutical Notes

McLeod's translation adopts a gender-neutral rendering of all divine epithets, departing from earlier translations that used masculine forms. He acknowledges this is "at best, only a partial solution" and that terms like "Creator" or "Maker" may still carry male connotations for readers from Christian or post-Christian backgrounds (~L1325–1338). The terms "Akal Purakh" and "Vahiguru" are omitted from the translations except in Ardas, partly to preserve metrical flow (~L1339–1347).
