# The Incoherence of the Incoherence (Tahafut al-Tahafut)

**[[averroes|Averroes]]**'s (Ibn Rushd's) point-by-point rebuttal of **[[al-ghazali|al-Ghazālī]]**'s
*[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|Tahāfut al-Falāsifa]]*, written c. 1180. Averroes quotes
Ghazālī's text in long extracts and answers each argument in turn, defending the
*[[falsafa|falāsifa]]* — though he also turns on Ghazālī *and* on the philosophers he is defending
(above all [[avicenna|Avicenna]]), insisting that neither party's arguments reach the standard of
strict demonstration. It is the defining reply in the medieval Islamic reason-versus-revelation
debate, and through Calo Calonymus's Latin translation (*Destructio Destructionis*, Arles, 1328)
it shaped Latin scholasticism far more than it shaped the later Islamic world, where Averroes "had
no influence."

## Textual Overview and Method

The work is a **running rebuttal**: Averroes reproduces Ghazālī's argument (often verbatim, the
"G says" passages), then replies ("I say…"). It follows Ghazālī's structure exactly — twenty
"discussions" (*masāʾil*), grouped as **sixteen on the divine sciences (metaphysics)** and **four
"About the Natural Sciences."** Ghazālī's twenty correspond one-to-one; the natural-science First
Discussion is Ghazālī's famous Seventeenth (causality), and the natural-science Fourth is his
Twentieth (bodily resurrection).

Averroes' distinctive posture is **triangular, not partisan**. He does not simply vindicate the
philosophers: he repeatedly judges that the philosophers' proofs are merely **dialectical**, not
demonstrative, and that Ghazālī's objections are often **sophistical** — "opposing difficulty with
difficulty" to produce perplexity rather than refutation. His verdict on the whole controversy:

> "neither the arguments of the theologians for the temporal creation of the world… nor the
> arguments of the philosophers… suffice to reach absolute evidence or afford stringent proof. And
> this is what we have tried to show in this book." (First Disc., src ~1553)

Hence his proposed retitling of Ghazālī's book — "it would be more suitable to call this book
'Incoherence' without qualification… for the only profit it gives the reader is to make him
incoherent" (src ~2367) — and his self-description, late in the work, of the result as "the
Incoherence of both parties together" (src ~11522).

A pervasive **hermeneutical principle** governs the book: divine predicates ("will," "knowledge,"
"life," "agent") are predicated of God and of creatures **equivocally** (more precisely,
analogically, *per prius et posterius*); the whole theological dispute, Averroes holds, partly
dissolves once this is seen — to deny that God "wills" because He does not will as humans do is the
same error as denying that He "knows" (src ~4781). On the most dangerous questions (God's
self-knowledge, God as "the totality of existents") he invokes the
[[harmony-of-religion-and-philosophy|masses-versus-elect]] rule: such truths "must not be written
down… nor made an obligation of faith" (src ~12518).

## Structure: The Twenty Discussions

### The Divine Sciences (Metaphysics) — Discussions 1–16

1. **The Eternity of the World** (the longest discussion; four philosophers' proofs). The pivot of
   the whole book. See [[eternity-of-the-world]] and the controversy
   [[eternity-of-the-world|Eternity of the World vs. Temporal Creation]].
2. **Incorruptibility of the world, time, and motion** — refuting the world's future eternity;
   Galen's empirical argument; four kalām sects on *annihilation*.
3. **God as agent and the world as His act** (long) — divine agency, [[emanation]], and the charge
   that "maker"/"product" are mere metaphor in the philosophers' system. See [[creation-ex-nihilo]].
4. **Whether the philosophers can prove a creator of the world** — the essential/accidental
   infinite distinction. See [[necessary-existent]].
5. **God's unity / impossibility of two necessary existents.** See [[necessary-existent]].
6. **The denial of divine attributes** (the richest of the metaphysical discussions). See
   [[divine-attributes-in-islam]].
7. **Genus and specific difference inapplicable to the First** — Averroes' tool of **analogical
   predication**. See [[divine-attributes-in-islam]].
8. **The First as simple / pure existence** (Ghazālī vs. Avicenna on essence and existence).
9. **The incorporeality of the First.**
10. **Whether the philosophers are forced into materialism/atheism.** See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
11. **Whether the First can be proved to know things besides itself.** See
    [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]].
12. **Whether the First can be proved to know itself.**
13. **God's knowledge of particulars** (the most developed of these). See
    [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]].
14. **Heaven as an animal moving in obedience to God.** See [[separate-intellects]].
15. **The aim that moves heaven.**
16. **Whether the souls of the heavens know all terrestrial particulars** (the "Preserved Tablet").

### About the Natural Sciences — Discussions 1–4

1. **The denial of necessary connection between cause and effect** — the celebrated causality
   debate; Ghazālī's burning-cotton occasionalism vs. Averroes' defense of natural causation. See
   [[occasionalism]].
2. **Whether the soul can be demonstrated to be a spiritual substance.** See
   [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy]].
3. **The philosophers' proofs of the soul's immortality.** See [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy]].
4. **The philosophers' denial of bodily resurrection** — the climactic theological charge. See
   [[bodily-resurrection]] and [[resurrection-body|the controversy]].

## Major Themes

- **The limits of demonstration in theology.** Averroes' recurring claim is epistemic: the
  questions disputed (eternity, divine knowledge, attributes) are too high for the dialectical
  weapons both sides deploy. Genuine *burhān* (demonstration) is the property of metaphysics done
  in Aristotle's own texts, "not in the statements of Avicenna" (src ~9330).
- **Averroes against Avicenna.** Though defending "the philosophers," Averroes systematically
  *detaches* the authentic Aristotelian tradition from Avicenna (and often al-Fārābī), whom he
  blames for non-Aristotelian innovations: the ten-intellect [[emanation]] cascade, the
  necessary/possible distinction, existence as an accident "additional to the quiddity," and the
  failure to grasp analogical predication. Many of Ghazālī's objections, he concedes, were valid
  **against Avicenna** but not against Aristotle or al-Fārābī.
- **The double register of religion and philosophy.** Religious doctrines are true and obligatory
  for the masses and are "necessary political arts"; the demonstrative class grasps their inner
  sense but must uphold them publicly, and to dispute the principles of religion is heresy. See
  [[harmony-of-religion-and-philosophy]].
- **Natural causation and the order of the world.** Averroes makes the denial of efficient causes
  self-refuting: "intelligence is nothing but the perception of things with their causes," so to
  deny causes is to deny knowledge itself (src ~14154). See [[occasionalism]].

## Key Interpretive Cruxes

- **Averroes' suspicion of Ghazālī's sincerity.** He repeatedly suggests Ghazālī did not himself
  hold the Ashʿarite positions he defends, writing only "to flatter the masses of his times" (src
  ~1718), and that "in metaphysics he recurs to the philosophers… most clearly shown… in his book
  called *The Niche for Lights*" (src ~3894). On Ghazālī's promised positive sequel (*The Golden
  Mean in Dogmatic Beliefs*), "perhaps he never composed it." This frames the *Tahāfut
  al-Falāsifa* as a possibly insincere exercise in dialectical perplexity.
- **The eternal agent (Disc. 1).** "The starting-point of His acts is at the starting-point of His
  existence; for neither of them has a beginning" (src ~1557); "a limited act can only be imagined
  of a limited agent, not of the eternal agent whose existence and action are unlimited" (src
  ~3361). The Creator's priority is "the priority of the unchanging timeless existence to the
  changing existence which is in time" — a third kind of priority, neither temporal nor causal.
- **The accidental infinite** (Disc. 1, 4): "An accidental infinite, not an essential infinite, is
  admitted by the philosophers; nay, this type of infinite is in fact a necessary consequence of
  the existence of an eternal first principle" (src ~1517).
- **The burning of cotton** (Natural Sciences Disc. 1): Ghazālī's "The agent of the burning is
  God… fire is a dead body which has no action… observation proves only a simultaneity, not a
  causation" (src ~14034), against Averroes' "To deny the existence of efficient causes which are
  observed in sensible things is sophistry" (src ~14100).
- **Resurrection as simulacra** (Natural Sciences Disc. 4): "what arises from the dead is
  *simulacra* of these earthly bodies, not these bodies themselves, for that which has perished
  does not return individually" (src ~15994) — bodily resurrection affirmed *specifically*, not
  *numerically*.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

The work is a sustained contest between *burhān* (demonstration) as the philosophers' standard and
the **dialectical *[[kalam]]*** of [[asharism|Ashʿarism]], with the
[[zahir-batin|Zāhir/Bāṭin]] exoteric/esoteric layering surfacing in Averroes' masses-versus-elect
rule. Averroes' signal contribution to the technical apparatus is **analogical predication
(*per prius et posterius*)** as a middle term between univocal and equivocal naming of God (see
[[divine-attributes-in-islam]], [[necessary-existent]]).

## Reception

- **Latin West**: translated by Calo Calonymus (Arles, 1328) as *Destructio Destructionis*;
  Averroes became "the Commentator" and a major (and controversial) authority. His influence on
  European medieval and Renaissance thought "was immense."
- **Islamic world**: comparatively little direct influence; Averroes was "the last great
  philosopher in Islam," and *falsafa* in the Sunni mainstream was largely eclipsed by Ghazālī's
  critique.
