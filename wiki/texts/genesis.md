Genesis is the first book of the Hebrew Bible (Torah/Old Testament), covering the creation of the world, the primeval history of humanity, and the stories of the patriarchs Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph. Its name derives from the Greek *génesis* (origin), translating the Hebrew *berēʾšît* (in the beginning), the book's first word.

## Structure

Genesis is organized by the **[[toledot|*toledot* formula]]** ("these are the generations/history of X"), which appears ten times and divides the book into narrative and genealogical units. The broad structure:

- **Primeval History** (1:1–11:32): Creation, Eden, Cain and Abel, the Flood, Table of Nations, Babel
- **Abraham Cycle, Part I** (12:1–17:27): Call of Abram, Lot, covenant (Gen 15 and 17), Hagar and Ishmael
- **Abraham Cycle, Part II / Isaac** (18:1–26:35): Sodom, Akedah, Isaac narratives
- **Jacob Cycle** (25:19–36:43): Jacob's birth, Laban, Peniel, Dinah
- **Joseph Narrative** (37:1–50:26): Joseph in Egypt, famine, reconciliation

Geographically: chs. 1–11 are set in Babylonia; chs. 12–36 in Canaan; chs. 37–50 in Egypt — representing the three poles of Israel's ancient world.

[[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]] (NICOT; V1 Intro §II Structure) provides the wiki’s most detailed analysis of the *tôlĕdôt* formula. He lists the ten occurrences: **2:4a** (heavens and earth); **5:1a** (Adam); **6:9a** (Noah); **10:1a** (sons of Noah); **11:10a** (Shem); **11:27a** (Terah); **25:12a** (Ishmael); **25:19a** (Isaac); **36:1a, 9a** (Esau); **37:2a** (Jacob). Five formulae are followed by narrative (#1 creation, #3 flood, #6 Abraham story via Terah, #8 Jacob story, #10 Joseph story) and should read “this is the story of X”; five head genealogies (Adam, sons of Noah, Shem, Ishmael, Esau). Vertical/linear genealogies (5; 11:10ff.) carry more narrative weight than horizontal/segmented ones (10; 25:12ff.; 36).

Hamilton defends **2:4a as a superscript** introducing 2:4bff., not a subscript to 1:1–2:3: elsewhere the formula is almost universally a superscript; the genitive is always “of the progenitor, never of the progeny,” so 2:4a names what the heavens and earth generate (humanity in 2:5ff.), not the generation of the cosmos itself; tacking 2:4a onto P’s 1:1–2:3 while holding J for 2:4bff. creates “an almost insurmountable problem.” He engages but rejects the Wiseman–Harrison eleven-tablet colophon theory (formulae as end-of-tablet summaries): where the formula precedes genealogy it is hard not to include it with what follows; the model oddly makes Ishmael preserve Abraham’s history, etc.; *tôlĕdôt* from *yālaḏ* must refer to that which is born/produced. Preferred reading: 1:1–2:3 is prologue before ten *tôlĕdôt* units; each unit focuses on key progeny implementing God’s plan (literary-theological unity).

Geographically, Hamilton notes chs. 1–11 Babylonia, 12–36 Palestine, 37–50 Egypt — center bracketed by regions with whose history Israel would be interlocked; impact: Genesis is about world history, Abraham elected so nations might find knowledge of God and blessing. Land arc: 1–11 individuals have land and lose it; 12–50 lack land and move toward it. Four-fifths of the book on four generations (Abraham–Joseph) vs one-fifth on twenty (Adam–Abraham). See [[toledot|Tôlĕdôt]], [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Hamilton Genesis 1–17]].

## Major Themes

**From generation → degeneration → regeneration**: Hamilton’s controlling arc (V1 Intro): Genesis moves “progressively from generation (chs. 1–2), to degeneration (chs. 3–11), to regeneration (chs. 12–50).” Primeval history concentrates on *ʼāḏām* (nearly all of the word’s Genesis occurrences in 1–11); paradise of blessing then judgment/power-grab alienation with grace notes (clothing; seed striking serpent; Cain’s protective mark; no-flood covenant). Election of Abraham is not isolation but vehicle for all families of the earth — “Reconciliation Regained” / “Hope Regained,” resolving 3–11 without recapturing 1–2. The *tôlĕdôt* structure carries this arc from universal failure to the particular family through whom blessing will come to the nations.

**Covenant and promise as connecting bridge**: Concentrating on patriarchal history (~4/5 of the book), Hamilton holds that “the theme of divine promise unites the patriarchal cycles” — the connecting bridge most frequently crossed from narrative to narrative in Gen 12–50 (Clines tallies: descendants 19×; relationship 10× Gen; land 13× Gen). Command and promises (12:1–3) **antedate** formal *berîṯ* (ch. 15). Hamilton reads Gen 15 (unilateral vision-ritual: only the deity-symbol passes between pieces) and Gen 17 (Hiphil *qûm* maintenance; thrice *ʿôlām*; circumcision as *ʾôṯ*) as **one ongoing covenant**, not J vs P two covenants; behavioral expectations do not convert it into a bilateral pact grounded in Abraham’s compliance. Rainbow (9), circumcision (17), and Sabbath (Exod 31) form three “sign of the covenant” stages. See [[covenant|Covenant]], [[circumcision|Circumcision]].

**Divine sovereignty through younger-son (and unloved) reversal**: Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Ephraim over Manasseh, Perez over Zerah; unloved Leah over loved Rachel — “All of these bear witness to God’s gratuitous choice in which the factor in the choice is not age but God’s sovereign will” (Hamilton on 25:23). Ishmael receives blessing and nationhood **without** covenant: “Divine sovereignty dictates the selection of one son over the other, quite apart from personal life-styles.” Paul (Rom 9:10–13) is read as election before works. Moral consequences still operate (nemesis patterns), but election is not reward for virtue.

**God’s faithfulness, not patriarchal morality models**: Despite wife-sister lies, Hagar initiative, Jacob’s deceit, Judah–Tamar, Joseph’s framing, Hamilton observes that “God never clearly rebukes any of the patriarchs” (closest: “Why did Sarah laugh?”). Gen 12–50 joins promises to God’s faithfulness: “Even if the bearers of those promises represent the greatest threat to the promises, the individual lives of the promise bearers cannot abort those promises.” Childs’s “theocentric interpretation” of Gen 12–36 by the rest of the OT canon.

**Providence through human agency (Gen 50:20)**: The Joseph narrative culminates in double intention with the same verb: brothers *ḥăšaḇtem ʿālay rāʿâ*; God *ḥăšābāh leṭôbāh* — “You intended evil… God intended it for good” (50:20). Joseph refuses to play God (*hataḥat ʾĕlōhîm ʾānî*); instrument never substitute. Earlier 45:5–8: God sent me. Neither human malice nor divine purpose cancels the other. Joseph preserves the descendants promise under famine; Judah endangers yet the line continues via Perez (Tamar). See [[divine-providence|Divine Providence]].

**Origins without theogony**: Hamilton stresses the English title “Genesis” = “origin” (via LXX *Génesis*); postbiblical Hebrew *berēʾšîṯ*. The book concerns origins of earth, humankind, institutions, and one chosen family as medium of world blessing — but “There is no *génesis theoú* (theogony)… He is one without *rē’šîṯ* (beginning) and *’aharîṯ* (end).” Vertical vs horizontal genealogies within the *tôlĕdôt* framework; image transmitted generation to generation in Gen 5 (grace emphasis; no scar of ch. 3 on transmission in that genealogy’s presentation).

## Interpretive Cruxes

**Genesis 1:1 — absolute beginning or temporal clause?**: The Hebrew *berēʾšîṯ* can be read as an absolute independent clause (“In the beginning God created…”) or as a temporal/construct clause (“When God began to create…”). Four options circulate; absolute independent clause and construct subordinated to v. 3 (v. 2 parenthetical) have widest support. Stakes: absolute beginning as direct act of God vs matter/chaos prior to creation — “in the beginning there was one—God; or … two—God and preexistent chaos?” [[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]] defends the absolute reading on lexical (Isa 46:9–10 *rēʾšîṯ* absolute for historical time as such; only Gen 1:1 pairs the noun with a finite perfect verb, unlike 2:4b’s infinitive construct), versional (all ancient versions absolute), Masoretic (*ṭiphâ* disjunctive), comparative (Enuma Elish 1–9 closer to Gen 2:4bff. than 1:1–3; Gunkel: no parallel to the Bible’s first word), and stylistic (parataxis) grounds — not theology alone, though Westermann/Childs also exclude preexistent chaos on P’s theology. Gap theory rejected (*hāyâ* circumstantial, not sequential “became”). See [[creation-ex-nihilo|Creation ex Nihilo]].

**The two creation accounts (Gen 1:1–2:3 and Gen 2:4–3:24)**: Source criticism assigns Gen 1 to P and Gen 2–3 to J (divine names, style, order). Hamilton reads them as complementary poles: 1:1–2:3 focuses on humanity’s divine origins/endowments/authority (“you shall,” 1:29); 2:4ff. on mundane origins and creation under authority (“you shall not,” 2:17). *Yahweh ʾĕlōhîm* compound joins sovereign material and moral control; serpent (3:1b–5) alone uses only *ʾĕlōhîm*. Alleged word-vs-action contrast is overstated (only light is strictly fiat). Knowledge of good and evil preferred as **moral autonomy** (legal idiom of deciding good/evil), not sexuality or omniscience. See [[genesis-creation-accounts|Genesis: Two Creation Accounts]].

**The *imago dei* (Gen 1:26–27)**: Options: specific faculty (reason, conscience), functional dominion, or holistic personhood. Hamilton: v. 26 “is not interested in defining what is the image of God in man… to be human is to bear the image of God” — unity, no part subordinated. Democratizes ANE royal image language to all humanity; dominion = responsible care. Male and female both image-bearers; sexuality gift. Image transmitted in Gen 5 (grace emphasis). NT: Christ as image (substantial identity); believers conformed to Christ’s image. See [[imago-dei|Imago Dei]].

**"Let us make" (Gen 1:26)**: Options: mythological other gods; heavenly court/angels; earth as co-addressee; plural of majesty; plural of deliberation; Trinitarian foreshadowing. Hamilton’s preferred: Hasel’s “plural of fullness” / Clines’s “duality within the Godhead” (God speaks to the Spirit of 1:2) — approaches trinitarian understanding without claiming Moses as trinitarian monotheist. Narrator’s singular “his image” (v. 27) undercuts pure divine-council readings.

**The "sons of God" (Gen 6:1–4)**: Three main views: (a) angels (LXX; Job; 1 Enoch; Jude 6 / 2 Pet 2:4 reception); (b) dynastic rulers / early aristocracy; (c) godly Sethites + ungodly Cainite daughters. Hamilton: “it is impossible to be dogmatic… the evidence is ambiguous.” Marriage language (*lāqaḥ* wives), not rape; stimulus *kî ṭōḇōt* // Eve and the tree; 120 years preferred as Flood grace period, not lifespan cap; Nephilim parenthetical contemporaries, distinct from *gibbôrîm* offspring. See [[sons-of-god|Sons of God (Gen 6:1-4)]].

**Flood: universal or local?**: In 6:5–13 *hāʾāreṣ* eight times looks universal; *kol-hāʾāreṣ* usually “earth” though Gen 13:9, 15 show “whole land” is possible even in Genesis. Hamilton treats the geographic claim as universal-leaning while acknowledging local-land possibilities. Theologically load-bearing for him: clear moral motivation (vs Gilgamesh vagueness / Atrahasis noise-overpopulation); Flood un-creates (waters above/below // 1:6–7); all flesh dies as divine penalty (*mētû* not “drowned”); Noachian covenant never again all flesh cut off by floodwaters; unilateral (animals included). See [[flood-universality|The Flood's Scope]].

**Genesis 15 vs. Genesis 17 — one covenant or two?**: Gen 15: vision, animal halves, smoking pot/flaming torch; only deity-symbol passes between — Hamilton: unilateral; “Nothing… in this chapter is imposed on Abram”; *berîṯ* as obligation Yahweh lays on himself. Gen 17: El Shaddai; name changes; circumcision; thrice *ʿôlām*; Hiphil *qûm* (maintain/establish), not *kāraṯ*. Critical consensus: J (15) vs P (17) two accounts. Hamilton’s core position: **one ongoing covenant** — ch. 17 reconfirms after ≥11 years, sterile Sarah, household strife; thrice *eternal* stresses that expectations (walk before me, blameless, keep covenant, circumcise) do **not** shift ch. 15’s unilateral emphasis into a bilateral pact. Circumcision is identity *ʾôṯ* / amen parallel to 15:6 faith (Rom 4: seal of prior righteousness). Ishmael blessed without covenant. See [[covenant|Covenant]], [[circumcision|Circumcision]].

**The Akedah (Gen 22)**: Primary frame for Hamilton: *hāʾĕlōhîm* **tested** Abraham (not demonic temptation); reader knows, Abraham does not. Real intensity: relinquishing “the one person who can perpetuate the promises of God,” not only a beloved son. Whole narrative (ram, stay of knife, subsequent promises) frames God as mercy + obedience — not v. 2 alone as cruelty. *yirʾat ʾĕlōhîm* = unreserved obedience yielding even the promised future; Yahweh-yireh names **God’s provision**, not Abraham’s obedience. 22:15–18 defended as original climax balancing 12:2–3. Resists making Isaac→Christ typology the interpretive center (Heb 11:17–19 resurrection faith and John 19:17 wood-bearing noted as reception, not the OT narrative’s center). See [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah: Interpretive Controversies]].

**Gen 50:20 — double intention**: Brothers *ḥăšaḇtem* evil; God *ḥăšābāh* good — same verb, neither cancels the other. Joseph refuses divine surrogate role. Theological climax of the Joseph *tôlĕdôt* and a load-bearing formulation of providence-with-agency for the whole book. See [[divine-providence|Divine Providence]].

## The Kabbalistic (Zoharic) Reading of Genesis

The [[zohar|Zohar]] reads Genesis at the **Sod** (mystical) level of [[pardes|PaRDeS]], treating the creation and patriarchal narratives not (only) as cosmology and history but as a coded account of the inner life of God — the self-unfolding of the ten [[sefirot|sefirot]] from [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]]. This is a fundamentally different mode from the grammatical-historical reading of [[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]] and the source-critical reading of the Documentary Hypothesis: it neither contests nor confirms the plain sense but claims to read *beneath* it. Key Zoharic re-readings of **Volume I**’s portion (Gen 1:1–11:32), grounded in the Pritzker Edition translation of Matt (Vol. I, 2004; fidelity reingest 2026-07-18; see [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol1|Matt Pritzker Vol I]] and [[zohar|The Zohar]]):

- **Genesis 1:1 — theogony, not cosmogony**: In the Zohar (Pritzker Vol. I, Matt; ~**1:15a**), “A spark of impenetrable darkness flashed within the concealed of the concealed, from the head of Infinity… of the mystery of Ein Sof…. a single, concealed, supernal point shone. Beyond that point, nothing is known, so it is called ראשית (Reshit), Beginning.” The point expands and builds a palace: “With this beginning, the unknown concealed one created the palace. This palace is called אלהים (Elohim), God. The secret is: … *Be-reshit bara Elohim*, **With beginning, created God** (Genesis 1:1).” *Elohim* is thus the **object**, not the subject, of the verb: “With *Beginning* (*reshit* = Ḥokhmah), [the unknown concealed one / Ein Sof] created *Elohim* (= Binah/palace).” The verse narrates the **emergence of the knowable God (the sefirot) from the unknowable Infinite**, not cosmogony. This stands in sharp contrast to the [[creation-ex-nihilo|*creatio ex nihilo*]] reading Hamilton defends: the Zohar describes **emanation** — though it preserves a “nothing” (*Ayin* / Concealed of all Concealed) as supernal source. Related Haqdamah move: *Mi* (Who) + *elleh* (these) → *Elohim*. See [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]], [[sefirot|Ten Sefirot]].
- **Tohu, bohu, darkness, and wind (Gen 1:2)**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I), the four are mapped to divine names — “Tohu—upon it rests the name שדי (Shaddai). Bohu—… צבאות (Tseva’ot)…. Darkness—… אלהים (Elohim). Wind—… יהוה (YHVH)” — and correlated with Elijah’s Horeb theophany (1 Kings 19: wind, earthquake, fire, “sound of sheer silence”).
- **“Let there be light” (Gen 1:3) and the luminaries**: Primordial light is treasured away for the righteous (shown to Adam “from one end of the world to the other,” later hidden). Day is the central pillar extending **Yesod** (Foundation, Vitality of the Worlds); Night is the female moon / Adonai / [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]. **Moon diminishment:** In the Zohar (Pritzker I), the moon initially “shared a single cleaving with the sun,” then “diminished herself, diminished her light”; *me’orot* (“lights,” Gen 1:14) is spelled deficiently as *me’erat* (“curse”), “to include the evil serpent who injected slime, causing separation.” Shells (including Lilith) rise after the kernel of light is hidden.
- **The second day and the firmament (Gen 1:6)**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I), “Here conflict was created through the left…. Out of that conflict… emerged Hell.” The third day (central pillar / Tif’eret) mediates left and right; “that it was good is not written of the second day.” This reading underlies the Zohar’s interpretation of the [[korah|Korah]] rebellion (left vs right; Moses as mediator).
- **Eden and the Fall (Gen 2–3) as cosmic divorce**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I), Rabbi Shim’on synthesizes: “Indeed, all is one. It was Samael appearing on a serpent, and the form of the serpent is Satan; all is one.” “When Adam sinned by eating from the tree… he caused a defect, **separating the Woman from Her Husband**. The fault of this defect stood out in the moon, until Israel stood at Mount Sinai… Once Israel sinned with the calf, She relapsed… the evil serpent prevailed.” On Gen 3:24 the Zohar asks who divorced whom: “Who drove out *Et*? Adam. Adam actually drove out *Et!*” (*Et* = [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]). Garments of light become garments of skin. Eve absorbs the serpent’s slime; dual paternity yields Cain from the impure side — “From the side of Cain emerge all the haunts of maleficent species, spirits, demons, and sorcerers” — and Abel from a still-incomplete compassionate side; Seth restores “in his likeness.” See [[adam|Adam]], [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]].
- **Sons of God / Flood generation (Gen 6:1–8)**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I), Uzza and Azael fall from their holy site, see the daughters of humans, and engender *nefilim*. Guilt is incomplete until they “spilled blood upon the earth fruitlessly” / “wasted their ways”: “one is only called evil if he wastes his way… empowering the impure spirit called evil… Shekhinah withdraws from the world.” Open, brazen sin “repels Shekhinah from earth.”
- **The Flood (Gen 6–9) as sexual/covenant crime and Destroyer-judgment**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I, Parashat Noaḥ), “they wasted their ways, so that the **upper waters and lower waters failed to unite fittingly**…. Who has ever ruined like this: male and female waters?” Punishment mirrors the crime: boiling water “peeling off their skin”; *springs of the great abyss* (lower) and *sluices of heaven* (upper). “Judgment never manifests… without that Destroyer surging through…. there was a flood, the Destroyer surging through that flood.” [[noah|Noah]] is “righteous below” corresponding to the Righteous One / Foundation of the world above (*ish ha-adamah*, husband of earth); the **ark** is the Ark of the Covenant with which he unites to sustain seed — entry only with Husband’s permission (“YHVH, husband of the ark”); he hides twelve months so the Destroyer’s gaze cannot overpower him. Marked **ambivalence**: “*in this generation*, precisely”; he “did not plead for compassion for the world and it perished,” so the Flood waters are named for him (Isa 54:9), unlike Moses who offered to be obliterated for Israel. Post-Flood world still incomplete: “earth did not escape the slime of the serpent until Israel stood at Mount Sinai.” Vineyard episode = failed probe of Adam’s sin, reopening “the breach of the world that had been closed”; Ham/Canaan as “scoria… primordial serpent.” Rainbow = covenant bow of the Righteous; forbidden to gaze “lest shame be exposed in Shekhinah.” See [[noah|Noah]].
- **Babel / holy tongue (Gen 11)**: In the Zohar (Pritzker I), journeying from *qedem* is departure from the Primordial One / united faith; the tower is “a name for ourselves—a deity to worship.” Builders shared “the holy tongue,” and “powers above know and recognize **only the holy tongue**. Once language was scrambled… their power weakened and fractured.”

*Patriarchal Genesis (Zohar Vol II — Lekh Lekha through Va-Yetse, Gen ~12–32; Pritzker II, Matt; fidelity reingest 2026-07-18; see [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol2|Matt Pritzker Vol II]]):*

The main running commentary of Pritzker **Volume II** covers five parashot on the patriarchal narratives (Zohar ~**1:77a–165b**). Claims below are from the Zohar body in Matt’s English; sefirotic labels are the wiki’s shorthand for the text’s own images (South/right, Night/left, sun–moon, Jubilee/Sabbatical, etc.).

#### Lekh Lekha (Gen 12:1–17:27; ~1:77a–97a)
- ***Lekh lekha* as self-refinement, not mere relocation** (Pritzker II, **1:78a**): “Go for yourself, to refine yourself, to perfect your rung… Go to yourself, to know yourself, to refine yourself.” Abraham tests the “conduits of the inhabited world”; leaves land, natal astrology, and father’s house. Four promises form “the throne of four legs, all comprised in Abraham” (right / left / middle / land of Israel) (**1:78b**).
- **Arousal below before above**: “Black light is not joined with white light until She is first aroused” (**1:77b–78a**). Negev = South = Abraham’s share (Ḥesed); two altars (revealed vs concealed rung) (**1:80a**).
- **Egypt as refinement** (**1:81b–83a**): Abraham not seduced like Adam/Noah; “If Abram had not gone down to Egypt and been refined there first, he would not have become the share of the blessed Holy One.” Shekhinah with [[sarah|Sarah]] (“my sister” = wisdom / Prov 7:4); chest radiance; ten plagues for Sarah prefigure Egypt. Descent without permission linked to four hundred years of enslavement (attributed Companion reading).
- **[[lot|Lot]] separation** (**1:84a–85a**): *mi-qedem* = from the Primordial One (parallel Babel); while Lot clung, Holy Spirit departed from Abraham; after parting it returns. Lot taken because Holy Spirit foresaw David from him.
- **[[melchizedek|Melchizedek]]** (**1:87a–87b**): *melekh shalem*, perfect king (esp. Yom Kippur when faces beam); alternatively lower world + “king of Salem” = supernal world — **two worlds as one**; bread and wine (“for She contains both”); priest of Right serving Most High; tenth as cluster/rung of faith.
- **[[circumcision|Circumcision]] as covenant of [[tsaddiq|Yesod]]** (**1:89a–96b**, load-bearing for the volume): “Before Abraham was circumcised, one rung spoke with him… *maḥazeh*…. Once he was circumcised, all those rungs rested upon this rung.” World created only through Covenant (Jer 33:25). “Abram does not engender; Abraham does engender” — Ishmael while still Abram (below); Isaac after name/covenant (above). Foreskin abandoned, sacred corona; Elijah at every *brit*; sign protects from Dumah/Hell if not inserted into foreign domain; *El Shaddai* completed when *yod* of the mark is revealed (**1:95b**). Name changes: Abram→Abraham and Sarai→Sarah each receive *he*; Isaac’s *yod* issues from their joined *he*s (**1:95b–96a**). See [[abraham|Abraham]], [[ishmael|Ishmael]], [[tsaddiq|Tsaddiq]].

#### Va-Yera (Gen 18:1–22:24; ~1:97b–120b)
- **Post-circumcision theophany** (**1:97b–98a**): Song 2:12 blossoms / time of pruning = circumcision; “YHVH appeared to him—to that rung who spoke with him”; voice joins speech. Heat of day = right / Ḥesed radiant. Three men = Abraham–Isaac–Jacob standing over the lower rung that sucks nourishment from them (**1:99a**); *Adonai* spelling = Shekhinah with three-color throne (**1:101b**).
- **Annunciation / opening of the tent** (**1:103a**): Sarah hears the *opening of the tent* = lower rung / opening of faith; first time she hears divine word. Isaac named for water↔fire joy from Gevurah side (**1:104a**).
- **Sodom as executed Din** (**1:105b–108a**): *I will go down* = compassion descending to judgment; *Va-YHVH* rain = Lower Court authorized from above. Abraham’s intercession (fifty→ten) is partial justice vs Moses who will not budge until “I forgive.” Lot saved by Abraham’s prior hospitality merit (“God remembered Abraham,” not Lot). Hospitality theurgy: escorting guests draws Shekhinah (**1:104b**). Hell’s dual water+fire paralleled in Flood and Sodom (**1:107b**).
- **Ishmael expulsion** (**1:118b–119a**): issues while Abraham uncircumcised; after Isaac, “son of Hagar” / scoria vs gold; *metsaḥeq* read as idolatry; seed named through Isaac alone.
- **Akedah — water vs fire** (**1:119a–120b**): “Elohim tested Abraham—not Isaac… previously Abraham had contained no judgment at all. Now water was embraced by fire.” *Et Abraham* = Isaac in low power until binding crowns him; “Then division became apparent: water versus fire.” “It was only so that division would manifest… until Jacob appeared and everything harmonized—triad of patriarchs completed, above and below arrayed.” Third day / place = Jacob’s rung seen “from afar” (Jacob unborn). Isaac willing (“the two of them walked on together”); sifted as incense; ram of twilight force. Double “Abraham! Abraham!” = incomplete then complete. See [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah]], [[isaac|Isaac]], [[jacob|Jacob]].

#### Ḥayyei Sarah (Gen 23:1–25:18; ~1:121b–134a)
- **Sarah’s years as supernal life** (**1:122b–123a**): “One hundred years—supernal, and twenty years—supernal, and seven years—supernal.” *Shanah* (100, 20) = singularity/unity; *shanim* (7) = separating into judgment and compassion while still one life abiding above. R. Ḥiyya: dies as soon as Isaac is bound (age 37 = *va-yihyu*); those thirty-seven years = “Sarah’s true life.” Death not by the serpent: “By supernal mystery… *be-Qiryat Arba*” / City of Four / *qeriyyat Shema* / Hebron joining David to the patriarchs (**1:125a**).
- **Cave of Machpelah** (**1:127a–129a**): Abraham sees Adam and Eve; gazing on Adam’s image opens the **Garden of Eden**; light and lamp; only the destined owner sees (Ephron sees darkness). Adam: “From now on—because of you—there is enduring ascension for me and the world!” *Ha-makhpelah* = **double *he* of the holy name** (Jerusalem above/below), not merely double cave. Field and cave “arose—rising, literally.”
- **Abraham “coming into days”** (**1:129a**): draws near rung by rung into “supernal days… mystery of faith”; blessed “with everything” from the ceaseless river site.
- **[[rebekah|Rebekah]] / Shekhinah return** (**1:133a**): “As long as Sarah existed, Shekhinah never departed from her. ## Composition and Source Criticism

The [[documentary-hypothesis|Documentary Hypothesis]] (Wellhausen) identifies four sources: J (Yahwist, c. 850 BCE; often Solomonic 960–930), E (Elohist, c. 850 BCE, northern, starts with patriarchs not creation), P (Priestly, 550–450 BCE; Wellhausen shifted P from earliest to latest), and D (Deuteronomist, confined to Deuteronomy). In Genesis, only J, E, and P are present. Hamilton (V1 Intro §III) reports classic criteria in descending significance: (1) divine names; (2) irreconcilable duplications (two creations, meshed flood, two Abraham covenants 15 & 17, two Hagar banishments, etc.); (3) anachronisms; (4) distinctive styles/ideologies. JEP distribution he reports: in 11:27–50:26 J predominates (~730 vv J, 336 E, 153 P); E absent from primeval history, first connected narrative contribution not until ch. 20; P as narrative only sparingly in four passages (1:1–2:4a; 6:1–9:29; 17:1–27; 23:1–20), major contribution genealogies and chronological framework.

**Hamilton’s positions as positions** (not wiki adjudication):
- An evangelical view of Scripture is **not** necessarily wedded to Mosaic authorship; “Composite is not a synonym for errant”; yet recent rhetorical/literary studies “tend to support the essential unity of Genesis.”
- Cassuto’s case against JEDP is “the most convincing”; Kitchen: DH anomalous in the ANE; multiedited Gilgamesh is empirically verifiable, multiedited Genesis remains hypothesis.
- Against Van Seters (Neo-Babylonian kerygmatic Abraham only): has not replaced Wellhausen consensus; growth/grandeur themes of JE unlikely in exilic decline.
- Against Rendtorff (scrap sources; six units; promises as late redaction): largely renames multilayered Genesis; stripping promises makes barren-heir narratives pointless.
- Welcomes Kikawada/Quinn (Gen 1–11 homogeneity), Rendsburg (purposeful structure; DH “untenable”), Radday/Shore (unity at word level).
- Body commentary repeatedly prefers literary unity over default doublets: Flood pairs as general→specific; YHWH/Elohim as thematic participants; Gen 7 epic resumption; 8:13–14 dual dry verbs as sequence/perspective; 8:20–22 + 9:1–17 as incipient→amplification; Table of Nations chiastic whole; Gen 15/17 one covenant (Hiphil *qûm* maintenance).
- On patriarchal historicity: reports Wellhausen’s irretrievability, Albright–Glueck–Speiser–Gordon archaeological case, and Van Seters/Thompson assault; best of Van Seters is exposing Nuzi parallelomania; failing Amorite Hypothesis is not falsifying Genesis (Sarna). Stakes if patriarchs nonexistent: covenant history and (including NT) Abraham as first link climaxing in Jesus, son of Abraham.

Conservative evangelical scholarship more broadly (e.g., Hamilton, [[peter-craigie|Craigie]]) engages the DH while arguing that compositeness does not imply errancy, and that the canonical text’s theological coherence is the primary object of interpretation. See also [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Hamilton 1–17]], [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|Hamilton 18–50]].

## Ancient Near Eastern Parallels

Genesis 1–11 draws on a world of shared Ancient Near Eastern literary tradition. Key parallels (Hamilton-attributed contrasts densified 2026-07-18):
- **Enuma Elish** (Babylonian): cosmological combat myth; Hamilton: Gen 1 creates by speech not combat; luminaries serve not served (avoids *šemeš*/*yārēaḥ*); *tannînim* are large creatures not combatants; anthropology of Gen 1 (regal humanity) “could not be wider apart” from man-as-afterthought servant of the gods; *tehôm* not personified Tiamat
- **Atrahasis Epic**: five-part structure paralleling Gen 1–11; Hamilton: Flood for moral rebellion vs noise/overpopulation; post-Flood Gen 9:1 “be fruitful” looks like conscious rejection of Atrahasis birth-control ending
- **Gilgamesh Epic** (Tablet XI): closest verbal flood parallels; Hamilton: moral motivation absent/vague in Gilgamesh; ark dimensions logical vs Utnapishtim’s cube; bird sequence more realistic; gods swarming “like flies” vs Yahweh’s soliloquy without hungry-god framing; multiedited Gilgamesh empirically verifiable, multiedited Genesis remains hypothesis
- **Ugaritic El tradition**: divine name and epithets for the God of the patriarchs
- **Sumerian King List**: structural parallel only (long pre-Flood lives → Flood → shorter lives); Hamilton: SKL = kingship/apotheosis vs Gen 5 = mankind never becomes god; open genealogies not age-of-earth arithmetic
- **Rainbow / warrior bow**: Hamilton: *qešet* radically reinterpreted from combat symbol to peace sign for God’s remembering

See [[genesis-ancient-near-east|Genesis and the Ancient Near East]].

## Manuscript Tradition

The Masoretic Text (MT) is the standard Hebrew tradition. The Septuagint (LXX) and Samaritan Pentateuch diverge significantly in the genealogical numbers of Gen 5 and 11. The Genesis Apocryphon (Dead Sea Scrolls) provides an Aramaic retelling. Targums Onkelos and Pseudo-Jonathan provide Aramaic translations with interpretive expansions.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks (selection)

- **Grammatical-historical / canonical evangelical** ([[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]], NICOT): holistic text; promise theology; ANE comparative without parallelomania; explicit NT appropriation sections; resists default source fragmentation. See [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot]], [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot]].
- **Source-critical / form-critical** (Wellhausen, Gunkel, Westermann, von Rad, Noth): J/E/P strands; oral *Sitz im Leben*; promise as theme or redaction.
- **Jewish peshat and midrashic** (Rashi; midrashim; Midrash ha-Ne’lam layer): plain sense plus expansive reading.
- **Kabbalistic Sod** ([[zohar|Zohar]] Pritzker I–III, X): sefirotic theogony/theurgy and soul drama — neither contests nor confirms the plain sense but claims to read beneath it (see section above).
- **Philosophical allegory** ([[maimonides|Maimonides]] on Gen 1–3 in the *Guide*).

His introduction addresses title, structure (*tôlĕdôt* as prospective titles; rejection of Wiseman–Harrison tablet colophons), composition (full engagement with DH, form criticism, Rendtorff, Van Seters, Kikawada/Quinn, Rendsburg), ANE parallels (Enuma Elish, Atrahasis, Gilgamesh, Ugaritic; moral Flood vs noise/overpopulation; anti-mythical polemic), and theological emphases (generation–degeneration–regeneration; promise as bridge; patriarchs unreproved; election by grace).
