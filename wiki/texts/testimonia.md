# Testimonia (4Q175)
**Sigla**: 4Q175 (= 4QTestimonia or 4QMessianic Testimonia)

The **Testimonia** (4Q175) is a one-column document from Qumran Cave 4 consisting of four biblical quotations presented without commentary. The quotations were selected for their **messianic** significance and together map the Qumran community's **three-figure** eschatological expectation: the **Prophet** like Moses, the **Royal/Star Messiah**, and the **Priestly Messiah**, followed by a closing excerpt from the Psalms of Joshua condemning the rebuilder of Jericho. It is one of the most economical and revealing Qumran documents — four quotations, no editorial comment, yet rich in implication.

## The Four Quotations

### Quotation 1 — Deuteronomy 5:28-29 + 18:18-19 (The Prophet)

> "A prophet I will raise up for them from among their brethren, like you [Moses], and I will put My words in his mouth and he shall speak to them all that I command him. And it shall come to pass that whoever will not hearken to My words which the prophet shall speak in My name, I will require it of him."

**The Prophet like Moses** (Deut 18:15-19) is the first of three figures. At Qumran this messianic Prophet is expected alongside but distinct from the two Messiahs — the **eschatological teacher of Torah** who will certify the final revelation of the Law. In the Damascus Document, the eschatological age is inaugurated by "a teacher of righteousness at the end of days" — possibly identified with this Mosaic Prophet.

### Quotation 2 — Numbers 24:15-17 (The Star and the Sceptre — Royal/Davidic Messiah)

> "The oracle of Balaam the son of Beor... I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near. **A star shall come forth from Jacob, and a sceptre shall rise from Israel**. He shall crush the temples of Moab and break down all the sons of Sheth."

**The Star of Jacob** is the Qumran shorthand for the **royal/Davidic Messiah**. The Damascus Document explicitly applies Num 24:17 to the "Messiah of Israel": "the star is the Interpreter of the Law... and the sceptre is the Prince of the whole congregation." Bar Kokhba's name ("Son of a Star") reflects the continued messianic use of Num 24:17 into the 2nd century CE.

### Quotation 3 — Deuteronomy 33:8-11 (The Priestly/Levitical Messiah)

> "And of Levi he said: Your Thummim and Urim belong to Your faithful one... They have observed Your word and kept Your covenant. They shall teach Jacob Your ordinances, and Israel Your law; they shall put incense before You and whole burnt offerings on Your altar."

**The Levitical/Priestly figure** whose mission is teaching Torah and maintaining Temple ritual. In the Qumran framework this corresponds to the **Messiah of Aaron** — the legitimate Zadokite priestly Messiah who takes precedence over the Davidic royal at the messianic banquet (see [[messianic-rule]]). The use of Moses' blessing of Levi deliberately invokes the priestly authority separate from kingship.

### Quotation 4 — Psalms of Joshua (Condemnation of Jericho's Rebuilder)

A citation from a text otherwise known as the **Psalms of Joshua** (4Q379 fr. 22 col. 2) — a non-canonical work known only from Qumran. It is a curse against the rebuilder of Jericho:

> "Cursed be the man who rebuilds this city! At the cost of his firstborn son shall he lay its foundation, and at the cost of his youngest son shall he set up its gates."

**Interpretation**: The Psalms of Joshua excerpt is applied to an unnamed historical figure — almost certainly Jonathan Maccabaeus (the "Wicked Priest") — and his son or successor, who are condemned as accursed rebuilders of Jerusalem (read as symbolic "Jericho"). The condemnation brackets the messianic hope: the Three Holy Ones are coming; the apostate ruling house is doomed.

## The Three-Figure Schema

The Testimonia presents the clearest evidence for the **three eschatological figures** expected at Qumran:

| Figure | Source text | Role |
|--------|-------------|------|
| The Prophet | Deut 18:18 | Mosaic teacher; certifies final Torah |
| The Star (Royal Messiah) | Num 24:17 | Davidic conqueror; the Messiah of Israel |
| The Priestly Messiah | Deut 33:8-11 | Levitical teacher-priest; the Messiah of Aaron |

This maps onto the formula in [[community-rule|1QS IX:10-11]]: "until the coming of the **Prophet** and the **Messiahs of Aaron and Israel**." Testimonia provides the three proof-texts.

## Hermeneutical Significance

The Testimonia is an early example of **anthological or florilegia exegesis** — proof-text collections organized around a theme. This genre of collecting biblical "testimonies" to demonstrate a theological point is found in early Christianity:
- The early church's collection of messianic proof-texts (testimonia) demonstrating Jesus as the Christ (a genre proposed by J. Rendel Harris)
- Matthew's formula quotations ("this was to fulfill what was written...")
- Hebrews' catena of Psalm citations (Heb 1:5-13)

Whether early Christian messianic proof-text collections depended directly on, or developed independently in parallel to, Qumran's testimonia genre is debated. Vermes notes the structural parallel without claiming direct dependence.

## Hanan Eshel's Identification of the Cursed Builder
VanderKam (2010) cites Hanan Eshel's proposal that the fourth quotation (the curse from the Psalms of Joshua against the rebuilder of Jericho) targets **John Hyrcanus** (high priest and ruler 134–104 BCE), who claimed all three offices (prophet, priest, king) and **built extensively in the Jericho area** — making the "rebuilder of Jericho" symbolism more literal than symbolic. This is distinct from the **Wicked Priest** identification of the main antagonist in the pesharim (who is usually identified with Jonathan or Simon Maccabaeus). If Eshel is correct, Testimonia may encompass two different Hasmonean opponents in a single document. See [[wicked-priest-identity]] and [[dead-sea-scrolls-vanderkam]].
