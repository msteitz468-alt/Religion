# Florilegium (4Q174)
**Sigla**: 4Q174 (= 4QFlorilegium or 4QMidrash on Eschatology)
**Also called**: Midrash on the Last Days

The **Florilegium** (4Q174) is a fragmentary thematic pesher from Qumran Cave 4. Its name, from the Latin for "anthology of flowers," reflects its structure: a sequence of biblical quotations linked by running commentary, organized around the eschatological themes of the **eternal sanctuary**, the **Davidic Messiah**, and the **suffering of the community in the last days**. It is the primary source for the **Sanctuary of Men** (*miqdash 'adam*) concept and offers a distinctive messianic exegesis of 2 Samuel 7 and Amos 9.

## Structure

The surviving fragments contain commentary on three biblical texts:

### Passage 1 — 2 Samuel 7:10-14 (the Davidic Covenant)

The Florilegium opens with a commentary on Nathan's oracle to David (2 Sam 7):

> "I will appoint a place for My people Israel and will plant them firm, so that they will dwell in their own place and be disturbed no more, and violent men will afflict them no more" (2 Sam 7:10).

**Interpretation**: "That is the house" — this refers to the eschatological sanctuary. The community glosses: opponents (*perîsîm*, perhaps Pharisees as "seekers of smooth things") and foreigners will not defile this place as they have the Jerusalem Temple.

> "Moreover the LORD declares to you that He will make you a house" (2 Sam 7:11b).

**Interpretation**: "That is, He will raise up for David a scion of David (*tsemach Dawid*) to stand with the Interpreter of the Law (*dorêsh ha-Torah*) who will arise in Zion in the last days."

This is the **dual messianic figure** cited from 2 Sam 7: a **Messiah of David** (royal/Davidic) standing alongside an **Interpreter of the Law** (= the Teacher or a priestly/legal figure). The Interpreter of the Law may be the same as the eschatological Prophet or a messianic priestly figure.

### Passage 2 — The Sanctuary of Men (*miqdash 'adam*)

The Florilegium contains the critical passage for the [[sanctuary-of-men|Sanctuary of Men]] concept:

> "He commanded that a Sanctuary of Adam (*miqdash 'adam*) be built for Himself, that there they may send up, like the smoke of incense, the works of the Law (*ma'asay ha-Torah*)."

This is the foundational text: the community itself is a **human sanctuary**, and the "works of the Law" (specific halakhic observances, the same phrase as in [[mmt|MMT]]) function as the **sacrificial offerings** — "like the smoke of incense." With the Jerusalem Temple defiled, the community's Torah-obedience replaces the Temple cult. The parallel to the [[mmt|MMT]]'s halakhic framework is explicit.

### Passage 3 — Amos 9:11 + Psalms 1-2

The text quotes and comments on Amos 9:11 ("I will raise up the booth of David that is fallen"), interpreting it as a messianic prophecy of the Davidic restoration. This is linked with Psalm 2:1 ("Why do the nations conspire") — interpreted as the battle at "the end of days" when the "elect of Israel" will stand against the **Kittim** (Romans).

## The *Tsemach Dawid* ("Scion / Shoot of David")

The Florilegium's use of *tsemach* (branch/shoot/scion) as a messianic title connects to Jeremiah 23:5, Zechariah 3:8, and 6:12, where "the Branch" (*tsemach*) is a messianic title. The Florilegium specifies: this Davidic scion will "stand with the Interpreter of the Law." The Interpreter of the Law (*dorêsh ha-Torah*) appears elsewhere in Qumran texts as a figure associated with authoritative legal interpretation — either the Teacher of Righteousness in his historical role, or a future messianic legal authority. See [[two-messiahs]].

## Hermeneutical Method: Thematic Pesher

The Florilegium uses a **thematic pesher** (as distinct from the **continuous pesher** of 1QpHab): rather than commenting on a single biblical book verse by verse, it gathers proof-texts from multiple books (Samuel, Amos, Psalms) around an eschatological theme. This is the Qumran technique Vermes calls "midrash" to distinguish it from the single-book pesharim. See [[pesher]].

## Significance

1. **Sanctuary of Men**: The *miqdash 'adam* formula — the community as human temple offering *works of the Law* — is the most explicit Qumran statement of the community-as-temple substitution. Cross-tradition parallel: NT [[eschatological-temple|eschatological temple]] concept (1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:21; 1 Pet 2:5).
2. **Two eschatological figures**: Davidic Messiah + Interpreter of the Law = the [[two-messiahs|Two Messiahs]] in their functional role.
3. **Works of the Law as offerings**: Explicit link between [[mmt|MMT]]'s *ma'asay ha-Torah* and cultic substitution — the same acts Paul contests in Galatians and Romans are, for Qumran, literally functioning as Temple sacrifice.
