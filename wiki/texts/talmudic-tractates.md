The [[mishna|Mishna]] and the corresponding [[talmud-bavli|Talmud]] are divided into six major orders, known as *Sedarim* (abbreviated as *Shas* - short for *Shisha Sedarim*, "Six Orders"). These orders are further divided into 63 tractates (*Masekhtot*).

The Babylonian Gemara covers 37 of these 63 tractates. The Jerusalem Talmud covers 39 tractates.

Below is the overarching structure. For detailed breakdowns of what each tractate contains, follow the links to the individual Orders.

## 1. [[seder-zeraim|Seder Zeraim]] (Seeds)
Deals primarily with agricultural laws, tithes, and blessings.
**11 Tractates:** Berakhot, Pe'ah, Demai, Kil'ayim, Shevi'it, Terumot, Ma'aserot, Ma'aser Sheni, Challah, Orlah, Bikkurim.
*(Only Berakhot has Babylonian Gemara).*

## 2. [[seder-moed|Seder Moed]] (Appointed Times)
Focuses on the Sabbath, festivals, and fast days.
**12 Tractates:** Shabbat, Eruvin, Pesachim, Shekalim, Yoma, Sukkah, Beitza, Rosh Hashanah, Ta'anit, Megillah, Moed Katan, Chagigah.
*(All but Shekalim have Babylonian Gemara).*

## 3. [[seder-nashim|Seder Nashim]] (Women)
Covers marriage, divorce, vows, and related family law.
**7 Tractates:** Yevamot, Ketubot, Nedarim, Nazir, Sotah, Gittin, Kiddushin.
*(All have Babylonian Gemara).*

## 4. [[seder-nezikin|Seder Nezikin]] (Damages)
Addresses civil and criminal law, court systems, and ethical guidelines.
**10 Tractates:** Bava Kamma, Bava Metzia, Bava Batra, Sanhedrin, Makkot, Shevuot, Eduyot, Avodah Zarah, Avot, Horayot.
*(All except Eduyot and Avot have Babylonian Gemara).*

## 5. [[seder-kodashim|Seder Kodashim]] (Holy Things)
Details the Temple service, sacrifices, dietary laws, and priestly duties.
**11 Tractates:** Zevachim, Menachot, Chullin, Bekhorot, Arakhin, Temurah, Keritot, Me'ilah, Tamid, Middot, Kinnim.
*(The first 9 have Babylonian Gemara).*

## 6. [[seder-tohorot|Seder Tohorot]] (Purities)
Examines the complex laws of ritual purity and impurity.
**12 Tractates:** Kelim, Oholot, Nega'im, Parah, Tohorot, Mikva'ot, Niddah, Makhshirin, Zavim, Tevul Yom, Yadayim, Uktzin.
*(Only Niddah has Babylonian Gemara).*
