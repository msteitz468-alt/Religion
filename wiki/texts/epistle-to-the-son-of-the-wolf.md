# Epistle to the Son of the Wolf

Persian/Arabic title: *Lawḥ-i-Ibn-i-Dhi'b*; also known as *Lawḥ-i-Shaykh* ('Tablet to the Shaykh').

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 11775–11804.*

## Overview

Bahá'u'lláh's last major work, composed c.1891 at [[bahji|Bahjí]], addressed to Shaykh Muḥammad-Taqí Iṣfaháni (Áqá Najafí), named by Bahá'u'lláh 'the Son of the Wolf', a prominent Iranian cleric who had persecuted the Bahá'ís (lines 11779–11786).

## Contents (lines 11787–11804)

Bahá'u'lláh:
- Called on the Shaykh to repent and beg God's forgiveness
- Declared his own mission to be God's cause
- Summarized some of his teachings and cited passages from his own earlier works
- Noted the transforming effect he had had on the Bábís (turning them from sedition to goodly deeds)
- Called for protection for his followers from religious persecution and martyrdom
- Presented biblical prophecies fulfilled by his coming
- Lamented the behaviour of [[subh-i-azal|Subh-i-Azal]] and his followers
- Called on the Bábí leader, Áqá Dawlatábádí, to investigate his cause

## Canon and Reception

Canonical for the Bahá'í Faith; recognized as Bahá'u'lláh's final major work and thus of particular significance.
