# Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (صحيح مسلم)

> **Text page.** The second most authoritative hadith collection in [[sunni-islam|Sunni Islam]].
> Compiled by **Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj al-Naysaburi** (202–261 AH / c. 817–875 CE). English
> translation by Abd-al-Hamid Siddiqui used for this ingest.

## Textual Overview

*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* ("The Authentic Collection of Muslim") is one of the **Kutub al-Sittah** (Six
Books) — the six canonical hadith collections authoritative for [[sunni-islam|Sunni Islam]]. It
is universally ranked second only to [[sahih-al-bukhari|Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī]] in Sunni hadith
scholarship. Imam Muslim is said to have evaluated approximately **300,000 ḥadīth** and selected
around **4,000** (by one counting; 7,190–7,500 by another, counting with repetitions) based on
stringent criteria of *isnād* (chain of transmission) verification.

Unlike Bukhari, who organized by legal theme, Muslim grouped traditions that share an *isnād*
together, which scholars consider methodologically distinctive. Muslim also prefaced the
collection with an important **introduction** (muqaddima) laying out his principles of hadith
criticism — a rare theoretical statement in hadith literature.

## Key Theological Themes

*(Expanded as Books are ingested. Books 1 complete; Books 2–43 pending.)*

### Book 1: Kitāb al-Īmān (Faith)

Book 1 is the theological heart of the collection. See [[iman|Īmān]] for the full concept page.

**The Jibrīl Hadith** (Chs. 1–2, Nos. 0001–0006): The collection opens with the foundational tripartite definition. The angel Jibrīl appears in human form and elicits from the Prophet precise definitions of: **Islām** (the five external practices), **Īmān** (interior belief in six articles: Allah, angels, Books, Messengers, Day of Judgment, Divine Decree), and **Iḥsān** ("That you worship Allah as if you are seeing Him, for though you don't see Him, He, verily, sees you"). The hadith closes with eschatological signs of the Last Hour.

**The Five Pillars** (Ch. 6): Three parallel transmissions (Nos. 0018–0021) from Abdullah b. Umar establish the canonical list: Shahāda, prayer, Zakāt, the Ramadan fast, pilgrimage. These constitute *al-Islām* (outward practice), distinct from *Īmān* (interior faith). See [[five-pillars|Five Pillars of Islam]].

**Salvation and the Shahāda** (Chs. 5–12): The minimum condition for entry into Paradise is sincere profession of the Shahāda. Abu Dharr reports the Prophet declaring that this affirmation grants Paradise "even if he committed adultery and theft" (Nos. 0171–0172) — a teaching deliberately withheld from broad public disclosure.

**The Islamic Christology Formula** (Ch. 11, No. 0043): A salvific declaration names Jesus as "servant and the son of His slave-girl and His word which He communicated to Mary and is His Spirit" — situating [[jesus|ʿĪsā]] within monotheistic confession rather than divinity.

**Īmān increases and decreases** (Chs. 21, 25): Major sins suspend the quality of belief during commission without nullifying it permanently. Commanding right and forbidding wrong is the minimum expression of faith (Ch. 21, No. 0079: "that is the least of faith"). Neglect of prayer is equated with unbelief (Ch. 36, No. 0146).

**Hypocrisy (*nifāq*)** (Ch. 26): Three or four signs of the hypocrite — lying, treachery in covenant, betrayal of trust, deviation in dispute — apply even to a professing, praying, fasting Muslim.

**Major Sins** (Chs. 38–39): *Shirk* (association with Allah) is the gravest sin, followed by killing one's child out of poverty-fear, then adultery with a neighbor's wife (Nos. 0156–0157). The seven "noxious things" include magic, unjust killing, consuming orphan property, usury, fleeing battle, and slandering chaste women (No. 0161).

**Recording of Deeds** (Ch. 60): A *hadith qudsī* (divine hadith): good intentions not acted upon earn one reward; good deeds earn ten to seven hundred; evil intentions not acted upon earn nothing; evil deeds earn one against the doer (Nos. 0233–0238).

**Intercession (*shafāʿa*)**: The Prophet will intercede for his *umma* on the Day of Judgment; the lowest in Paradise is described (Chs. 82–84). See [[shafaa|Shafāʿa]].

**Descent of ʿĪsā (Jesus)** (Ch. 72): Jesus son of Mary will descend and judge by the *sharīʿa* of Muhammad.

**Abu Talib** (Chs. 88–89): Muhammad's uncle dies refusing the Shahāda despite the Prophet's repeated urging; Qur'an 9:113 and 28:56 are cited as revealed in response. Intercession reduces but cannot remove his punishment.

## Hermeneutical Framework

*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* is the primary exemplar of **tafsīr biʾl-maʾthūr** (tradition-based
interpretation) applied to hadith science. Its method is:

1. **Isnād criticism** — evaluating chains of transmitters; Muslim's criteria are considered
   stricter than most but slightly less restrictive than Bukhārī's.
2. **Matn authentication** — where hadiths share an *isnād* group, Muslim collects them
   together, enabling comparison.
3. **Muqaddima** — the prefatory introduction is one of the earliest systematic statements of
   hadith methodology, addressing *ʿilm al-rijāl* (transmitter science).

## Canon and Reception

**Sunni Islam**: Universally canonical; the second of the Six Books; given the title *ṣaḥīḥ*
(authentic) by the tradition itself.

**Shia Islam**: Not part of the Shia hadith canon (the **Four Books**: *al-Kāfī*,
*Man lā Yaḥḍuruhu al-Faqīh*, *Tahdhīb al-Aḥkām*, *al-Istibṣār*). Some Shia scholars cite
it as a useful external witness but it carries no binding authority.

**Ibāḍī Islam**: Uses its own hadith literature; regards the Six Books as a Sunni construct.

## Key Interpretive Cruxes

1. **The Jibrīl ḥadīth** (Book 1): Defines the three-tier structure Islām/Īmān/Iḥsān. Later
   theology debates whether Iḥsān (*witnessing* God) is a third level of piety or the
   fulfillment of Īmān.
2. **Intercession (*shafāʿa*)**: The scope of prophetic intercession — who it covers, what
   sins it can erase — became a major intra-Sunni dispute and a Sunni/Muʿtazilī fault line.
3. **Incremental faith**: Whether Īmān admits of degrees was contested between Murjiʾa (who
   said Īmān is all-or-nothing) and the majority (who said it increases/decreases).
4. **The descent of ʿĪsā**: Traditions in Book 1 (Ch. 72) and Book 41 (*Fitan*) attest a
   Second Coming — interpreted literally in mainstream Sunni eschatology but read
   allegorically by some modernist scholars.
