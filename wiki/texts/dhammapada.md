The **Dhammapada** ("Path/Verses of the Dhamma") is the best-loved and most widely translated Buddhist
scripture — an anthology of **423 verses** in 26 chapters, drawn from the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha's]]
discourses and gathered in the *Khuddaka Nikaya* of the [[tipitaka|Pali Canon]]. Though formally part
of the Theravada canon, it is **revered across all Buddhist schools**, making it the nearest thing to
a shared Buddhist text (hence its place in the tradition page's minimal `canon_core`).

## Content

Pithy ethical and contemplative verses organized by theme — the Pairs (mind precedes all things:
"All that we are is the result of what we have thought"), Heedfulness, the Mind, Flowers, the Fool,
the Wise, the Arahant, the Thousands, Evil, Punishment, Old Age, the Self, the Path, and the Brahmana.
The verses compress the core teaching: actions and their fruits (*kamma*), the taming of the mind, the
ending of craving, and the marks of the awakened.

## Themes (and cross-tradition resonance)

The Dhammapada's wisdom-saying form invites comparison with the wisdom literature of other traditions —
[[proverbs|Proverbs]], [[ecclesiastes|Ecclesiastes]], the [[analects|Analects]] — a candidate
comparison page on cross-tradition gnomic wisdom. Its governing themes are
[[four-noble-truths|the path out of suffering]] and the disciplining of the mind.
