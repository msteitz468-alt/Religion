# Damascus Document (Sefer Brit Dammesek)

**Sigla**: CD (Cairo Damascus Document, MSS A and B from the Cairo Genizah); 4Q265–73 (4QDa–h); 5Q12; 6Q15

The Damascus Document is a foundational sectarian text in two parts — an **Exhortation** (historical and theological) and **Laws** (halakhah). It uniquely survived in two medieval manuscripts discovered in 1896 in the Cairo Genizah (MS A = 16 columns; MS B = 2 columns) before Qumran fragments confirmed it as a Qumran composition. Unlike the Community Rule (1QS), which governs the settled celibate community at Qumran, the Damascus Document addresses **camp communities** — married members living in towns throughout "the land of Damascus" (probably a symbolic reference to the region where the sect was active).

## Canon and Reception
Purely sectarian; unknown outside the sect until the Cairo Genizah discovery in 1896. The medieval copies attest to the text's preservation in Jewish communities for centuries after the sect's dissolution — possibly in a Karaite milieu, though the connection is not certain.

## Structure

### The Exhortation (Cols. I–VIII, XIX–XX)
The Exhortation opens with the community's **historical self-understanding**:

**"Plant of Aaron and Israel"**: God raised up a "remnant" from Israel, who "groped like blind men" for twenty years until God sent them the **Teacher of Righteousness** "to guide them in the way of His heart."

**The Wicked Priest and the Liar**: The Teacher is opposed by two figures — the **Wicked Priest** who persecuted him, and the **Spouter of Lies** (*mattif ha-kazav*) / **Man of Scoffing** who led many into apostasy with false teaching. The Spouter built "a city of vanity with blood and raised a congregation on deceit."

**The Covenant of Damascus**: The community is described as having entered a "new covenant in the land of Damascus" — a self-designation tied to Amos 5:27 (exile to "beyond Damascus") and the typology of Israel's wilderness sojourn. Those who keep this covenant will be saved at the final Visitation; those who apostatize are condemned.

**Three Nets of Belial (Col. IV)**: Belial ensnares Israel through three sins: fornication, riches, and pollution of the Temple. Specific targets: priests who marry a niece (forbidden at Qumran; permitted by rabbinic law); polygamy; remarriage after divorce.

**The Star and the Sceptre (Col. VII)**: Quotation of Numbers 24:17 ("A star shall come from Jacob and a sceptre shall arise from Israel") interpreted as: the **Star** = the Interpreter of the Law who went to Damascus; the **Sceptre** = the Prince of the whole congregation. This is messianic typology in the historical mode.

**MS B (Cols. XIX–XX)**: The second manuscript's version covers the same material differently and introduces the striking phrase: "from the day of the gathering in of the Teacher of the Community until the arising of the Messiah of Aaron and Israel." This establishes the teacher's death as a horizon and the coming of the Messiah(s) as the subsequent goal — the only Qumran text explicitly locating the Messiah's coming after the Teacher's death.

The "anointed one" from CD vi, 1 (4Q266 fr. 4): "God will set up a shepherd for His people and he will feed them in pastures" — a messianic allusion at the end of the Exhortation.

### The Laws (Cols. IX–XVI and 4QD)
The Laws govern the community's daily life in the camps:

**Purity regulations**: Detailed rules about menstrual impurity, corpse impurity, skin disease, discharge. Stricter than Pharisaic standards, following a priestly interpretation of Torah.

**Sabbath laws (Col. X–XI)**: Among the most stringent known from Second Temple Judaism: no carrying except specific items; no rescuing an animal from a pit on the Sabbath (contrast Matt. 12:11); the physician may not treat except in life-threatening emergency.

**Marriage and divorce**: One wife only; no divorce and remarriage; no marriage to a niece — contra Pharisaic/rabbinic practice; the prohibition is grounded in the principle that "the foundation of creation is male and female he created them" (Gen. 1:27, 5:2).

**Oaths and vows**: Complex rulings about oath-taking and vow-release.

**Judges and testimony**: Requirements for witnesses in capital cases.

**Camp organization**: Each camp has a **Mebaqqer** (Overseer/Guardian) who examines candidates, manages property, instructs members, and exercises pastoral care. The Mebaqqer must be between 30 and 50 years old and must know the language of all the peoples (perhaps: competent in Aramaic and the vernacular languages of the Diaspora).

## The "Book of Hagu" (*Sefer ha-Hagu*)
The laws require that every member study the "Book of Hagu" — an apparently authoritative text, probably the Community Rule or a related sectarian document, studied alongside the Torah.

## Key Theological Points
- **Historical pesher**: Current events are read as fulfillment of prophetic oracles. Amos, Isaiah, Ezekiel, and Numbers are cited as keys to the community's situation.
- **The Teacher after death**: CD XIX–XX implies the Teacher has died but the Messiah has not yet come — the community lives in the interim between teacher and messiah.
- **New Covenant**: The community describes itself as the heirs of a "new covenant" made in Damascus — an interpretation of Jeremiah 31:31-34 that anticipates Pauline and Christian new-covenant theology.
