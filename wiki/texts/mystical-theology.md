# Mystical Theology (*Peri Mystikes Theologias*)

The **Mystical Theology** of [[pseudo-dionysius|Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite]] is the
shortest work in the Dionysian corpus — five brief chapters addressed to "Timothy" — and the
locus classicus of Christian [[apophatic-theology|apophatic (negative) theology]]. Its central
move: direct Timothy to ascend beyond all things sensible, intellectual, affirmative, and even
negative, to union with "Him Who is above every essence and knowledge" through a "superessential
ray of the Divine Darkness." The work employs Moses' ascent into the dark cloud on Sinai
(Exod. 20:21; 24:15–18) as its central type.

## Structure and Contents

### Preface (Parker, lines 466–470)
Parker's preface to the Mystical Theology, comparing it to the ladder of Jacob (Gen. 28:12):
the angel ascending = the negative ("God is not matter, soul, mind…"); the angel descending =
the affirmative ("God is good, wise…").

### Caput I — The Divine Darkness; the Exhortation to Timothy

Caput I opens with a prayer addressed to the "Triad supernal, both super-God and super-good,
Guardian of the Theosophy of Christian men," asking to be directed to "the super-unknown and
super-brilliant and highest summit of the mystic Oracles, where the simple and absolute and
changeless mysteries of theology lie hidden within the super-luminous gloom of the silence"
(Section I). The exhortation to Timothy: "leave behind both sensible perceptions and intellectual
efforts, and all objects of sense and intelligence, and all things not being and being, and be
raised aloft unknowingly to the union, as far as attainable, with Him Who is above every essence
and knowledge. For by the resistless and absolute ecstasy in all purity, from thyself and all, thou
wilt be carried on high, to the superessential ray of the Divine darkness, when thou hast cast away
all, and become free from all" (Section I). Section II warns against the "uninitiated" who are
"entangled in things being, and fancy there is nothing superessentially above things being." The key
hermeneutical principle of the *Mystical Theology* is stated here: the negations do not stand in
opposition to the affirmations, but "far rather that It, which is above every abstraction and
definition, is above the privations" — God is not merely "not-finite" but above both limitation
and its negation.

### Caput II — Moses Ascending to the Luminous Darkness

Caput II opens with a prayer for the mystical ascent: "We pray to enter within the super-bright
gloom, and through not seeing and not knowing, to see and to know that the not to see nor to know
is itself the above sight and knowledge" (Section I). The method of abstraction is compared to
sculpture: "just as those who make a lifelike statue, by extracting all the encumbrances which have
been placed upon the clear view of the concealed, and by bringing to light, by the mere cutting away,
the genuine beauty concealed in it." Moses is the governing type: first purified and separated from
the crowd, he hears the trumpets and sees streaming lights, then goes "with the chosen priests" to
"the summit of the divine ascents" — but even there "he does not meet with Almighty God Himself,
but views not Him (for He is viewless) but the place where He is." Finally Moses is "freed from
them who are both seen and seeing, and enters into the gloom of the Agnosia; a gloom veritably
mystic, within which he closes all perceptions of knowledge and enters into the altogether impalpable
and unseen, being wholly of Him Who is beyond all, and of none, neither himself nor other; and by
inactivity of all knowledge, united in his better part to the altogether Unknown, and by knowing
nothing, knowing above mind." The paradox — "knowing nothing, knowing above mind" — is the
definition of *agnosia* as mystical *supra-knowledge*.

### Caput III — What Affirmative Theologies Assert; What Mystical Theology Denies

Caput III maps the Dionysian corpus as a hierarchy of discourse. The *Theological Outlines* covered
the Trinity, Paternity/Sonship, the Spirit, and the Incarnation — the most elevated positive
affirmations, expressed most briefly. The *Divine Names* covered Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, Power
and the full range of scriptural names. The *Symbolic Theology* (not preserved or not yet written)
covered sensible images — divine forms, appearances, body-parts, angers, griefs, carousals. The key
structural insight: "in proportion as we ascend to the higher, in such a degree the expressions are
circumscribed by the contemplations of the things intelligible... ascending from below to that which
is above, in proportion to the ascent, it is contracted, and after a complete ascent, it will become
wholly voiceless, and will be wholly united to the unutterable." The apophatic ascent moves in the
opposite direction: "by making the ascents from the lowest to the highest, we abstract everything"
— beginning with the least-like (sensible, material, negative qualities: "not given to debauch and
wrath") and ascending to what is most remote from God even among divine descriptions ("not expressed
nor conceived").

### Caput IV — The Sensible Things God Is Not

Caput IV executes the first level of negation — stripping away all sensible predicates: God "is
neither without being, nor without life — nor without reason, nor without mind, nor is a body — nor
has shape — nor form — nor quality, or quantity, or bulk — nor is in a place — nor is seen — nor
has sensible contact — nor perceives, nor is perceived, by the senses — nor has disorder and
confusion, as being vexed by earthly passions — nor is powerless, as being subject to casualties
of sense — nor is in need of light; — neither is It, nor has It, change, or decay, or division,
or deprivation, or flux, — or any other of the objects of sense." The form is deliberately rhythmic
and cumulative — a litany of sensory negation that exhausts the phenomenal world.

### Caput V — The Intelligible Things God Is Not; the Final Negation

Caput V executes the final and highest level of negation — stripping away all intelligible predicates,
including the most sublime divine names affirmed in the *Divine Names*. The ascending list: neither
soul nor mind; neither imagination, opinion, reason, nor conception; neither number, order, greatness,
littleness; neither equality nor inequality; neither similarity nor dissimilarity; neither standing
nor moving nor at rest; neither power nor light; neither life nor essence; neither eternity nor time;
neither science, truth, kingdom, nor wisdom; neither one, oneness, Deity, nor Goodness; "neither is
It Spirit according to our understanding; nor Sonship, nor Paternity"; "neither is It darkness, nor
light; nor error, nor truth; neither is there any definition at all of It, nor any abstraction."

The final sentence achieves maximum compression: "the all-perfect and uniform Cause of all is both
above every definition and the pre-eminence of Him, Who is absolutely freed from all, and beyond the
whole, is also above every abstraction." God is not only undefinable — God is also above the
*negation* of definition. Neither the cataphatic nor the apophatic captures the divine reality; the
[[mystical-theology|mystical silence]] (*siōpē*) that follows both is the only adequate "statement."
This is the locus classicus for the Dionysian position that the apophatic does not simply *negate*
the cataphatic but transcends the opposition of affirmation and negation alike.

## Key Theological Positions

1. **The Divine Darkness exceeds light, not merely light's absence.** "Super-luminous gloom" is not
   the darkness of ignorance but of excess — God's light is so intense that all finite eyes are
   darkened before it. This is the governing paradox of the work.

2. **Ascent requires total dispossession.** The soul must leave behind not only the senses but also
   intellectual operations, all positive names, and even the concept of negation itself, to achieve
   union "unknowingly" (*agnostōs*) with "Him Who is above every essence and knowledge."

3. **The negations are not opposed to the affirmations.** Both the cataphatic (*Divine Names*) and
   the apophatic (*Mystical Theology*) apply to God, but in different registers. The negations do
   not contradict the affirmations; they indicate that God "is above the privations." God transcends
   the opposition between affirmation and negation.

4. **Moses as the paradigm mystical theologian.** The ascent into the dark cloud on Sinai (Exod. 20:21;
   24:15–18) is the scriptural type for mystical theology: progressive purification, separation,
   ascent, and final union in the dark unknowing beyond all vision and knowledge.

5. **The apophatic logos diminishes toward silence.** As the Dionysian corpus moves from Symbolic
   Theology (most words) through Divine Names (fewer) to Mystical Theology (fewest), the text enacts
   the very contraction it describes. The Mystical Theology is the shortest work in the corpus by
   design; at the apex, "it will become wholly voiceless." See [[apophatic-theology|apophatic theology]].

## The Divine Darkness and Agnosia

The **Divine Darkness** (*skotos*) is Dionysius's term for the mode of encounter with God
that transcends both knowledge and ignorance. *Agnosia* (literally "unknowing") is not mere
ignorance but a "supra-knowledge" that arises when the mind has released all concepts,
including the concept of "unknowing" itself. Parker's preface glosses: "Agnosia is neither
ignorance nor knowledge intensified: but a supra-knowledge of Him, Who is above all things
known."

This motif became foundational for Western mysticism: [[cloud-of-unknowing|The Cloud of
Unknowing]] (14th c.) directly adopts the "divine darkness" imagery; John of the Cross's
"dark night" theology is influenced by the same current.

## Sharpe's Translation (1910) — A Second Witness

A. B. Sharpe's *[[sharpe-mysticism|Mysticism: Its True Nature and Value]]* (1910) appends an
independent English translation of all five chapters, useful for comparison with Parker. Sharpe's
renderings of the key phrases:

- **Ch. I** ("What the Divine Darkness Is"): the opening prayer asks to be led "to the summit of
  thy mystical oracles… where the simple and pure and unchangeable mysteries of theology are
  revealed in the darkness… **a darkness that shines brighter than light**." Timothy is told to
  "leave the senses, and the operations of the intellect, and all things sensible and
  intelligible… [to be] borne… into the **supernatural radiance of the divine darkness**." The
  Moses type ends: Moses "saw not Him… but **the place where He was**," and the initiate, "by
  understanding nothing… understand[s] after a manner above all intelligence."
- **Ch. II**: the sculptor analogy — we know God "by the abstraction of all that is natural; as
  those who would make a statue… abstract all the surrounding material… and by that abstraction
  alone reveal its hidden beauty." (Sharpe's footnote flags that this image is in [[plotinus|
  Plotinus]], *De Pulcritudine* vii, and is "adduced as an argument against" the apostolic
  authorship.) Mystical theology *subtracts* attributes from lowest to highest, where speculative
  theology *adds* them.
- **Ch. III**: ascending, "our speech is contracted… [until] we pass not merely into brevity of
  speech, but even into **absolute silence, and the negation of thought**." Affirmation is
  "hypothetical" (comparative): "Is not God more nearly life and goodness than air or a stone;
  must we not deny more fully that He is drunken or enraged, than that He can be spoken of or
  understood?"
- **Chs. IV–V**: the cumulative negations of sensible (IV) then intelligible (V) attributes,
  culminating: God "**can neither be affirmed nor denied**… for the perfect and sole cause of all
  is above all affirmation, and that which transcends all is above all subtraction."

Distinctive choices vs. Parker: Sharpe's chapter titles are fuller (Ch. II = "How to be united
with, and to give praise to Him who is the cause of all things and above all"), and his footnotes
attach a Thomistic apparatus (e.g. on "He is neither one nor unity… nor goodness," he glosses
with Aquinas: not "*Deus est Deitas*" but "*Deus est sua Deitas*"). See [[mysticism]] for how
Sharpe frames the work as the charter of supernatural mysticism.

## Rolt's Translation (1920) — A Third Witness

C. E. Rolt's *[[rolt-dionysius|Dionysius the Areopagite: On the Divine Names and the Mystical
Theology]]* (SPCK, 1920) supplies a third complete English version, markedly more fluent and
literary than Parker's. Rolt's distinctive renderings of the key passages:

- **Ch. I** ("**What is the Divine Gloom**"): the opening prayer asks to be guided "to that
  topmost height of mystic lore which exceedeth light and more than exceedeth knowledge, where
  the simple, absolute, and unchangeable mysteries of heavenly Truth lie hidden in the
  **dazzling obscurity of the secret Silence, outshining all brilliance with the intensity of
  their darkness**." Timothy is told to "leave the senses and the activities of the intellect…
  and all things in this world of nothingness, or in that world of being… [and be] led upwards
  to the **Ray of that divine Darkness** which exceedeth all existence." The negations
  "transcend… all positive and negative distinctions." Rolt's text cites "the blessed
  **Bartholomew**" on the Gospel as "vast and yet minute." The Moses type ends: he "beholds—not
  Him indeed (for He is invisible)—but **the place wherein He dwells**," plunging into "the
  **Darkness of Unknowing**."
- **Ch. II** (titled "How it is necessary to be united with and render praise to Him Who is the
  cause of all and above all"): the sculptor image — we praise God "by denying or removing all
  things that are—like as men who, carving a statue out of marble, remove all the impediments
  that hinder the clear perception of the latent image and by this mere removal display the
  hidden statue itself in its hidden beauty." The negative method ascends "from particular to
  universal conceptions," stripping off all qualities to reach "a naked knowledge of that
  Unknowing."
- **Ch. III**: names the earlier treatises **"Outlines of Divinity"** (= *Theological Outlines*)
  and **"Symbolic Divinity"** (= *Symbolic Theology*); as the soul soars upward "our language
  becomes restricted… reduced not merely to brevity of speech but even to absolute dumbness both
  of speech and thought… wholly united with Him Whom words cannot describe." Affirmation begins
  from the highest category, negation from the lowest: "truer to affirm that God is life and
  goodness than that He is air or stone, and truer to deny that drunkenness or fury can be
  attributed to Him…"
- **Chs. IV–V**: the cumulative negation of sensible (IV) then intelligible (V) attributes,
  culminating: the universal Cause "transcends all affirmation by being the perfect and unique
  Cause of all things, and transcends all negation by the pre-eminence of Its simple and
  absolute nature—free from every limitation and beyond them all."

For Rolt's philosophical reading of the apophatic ascent (Unknowing as a state *above* the
intellect, not Herbert Spencer's "Unknowable"; the Divine Darkness "dark through excess of
light"), see [[rolt-dionysius|Rolt's Introduction]].

## Canon and Reception

Not scripture in any tradition. Theological authority:
- **Authoritative for**: Catholic and Eastern Orthodox theology; the most cited of the
  Dionysian works in the Western mystical tradition
- **Disputed by**: Protestant tradition
- See [[pseudo-dionysius|Pseudo-Dionysius]] for authorship controversy
