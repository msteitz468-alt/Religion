# War Scroll (Milhamah)
**Hebrew title**: *Sefer Milhemet Bnei Or be-Vnei Hoshekh* — "Book of the War of the Sons of Light against the Sons of Darkness"
**Sigla**: 1QM; also 4Q491–6 (4QMa–f), 4Q285 (Book of War)

The War Scroll describes the **eschatological 40-year war** between the Sons of Light (the Qumran community and the tribes of Israel) and the Sons of Darkness (the Kittim/Romans and allied nations). It combines a **battle manual** (detailed military organization, weapons, formations, signals) with **liturgical texts** (prayers, hymns, priestly instructions). It is simultaneously a theological document about the nature of cosmic warfare and a quasi-practical manual for eschatological combat.

## Canon and Reception
Purely sectarian; no standing outside the Qumran community. The Roman military details reflect 1st c. BCE reality and have been used by scholars to date and contextualize the text.

## Structure and Contents

### The War Scenario (Cols. I–II)
The eschatological war lasts **40 years** (one for each year of Israel's wilderness wandering), divided into:
- Years 1–6: Wars against specific enemies (Kittim, Edom, Moab, Ammon, Philistia, Aram)
- Years 7–33: Remaining phases of war; Sabbath years are excluded (7 years of sabbath rest in 40)
- Final phases: Victory of Sons of Light; destruction of Belial's host

The war is waged on two levels simultaneously: **earthly** (historical armies) and **cosmic** (angelic hosts under Michael vs. Belial's angels).

### Military Organization (Cols. III–IX)
Detailed instructions for the battle formations:
- **Trumpets**: Different calls for advance, retreat, assembly, alarm — each inscribed with specific words
- **Standards and banners**: Each unit carries inscribed standards; the banners bear theological texts praising God's power
- **Formations**: Infantry formations resembling Roman legionary tactics — seven-battle array, cavalry support, shield-wall
- **Weapons**: Lances, swords, shields, bows — all with precise dimensions
- **Age requirements**: Warriors must be 25–30 minimum; boys, women, and the physically impure are excluded

The military detail is strikingly Roman in character, which Vermes uses to confirm the identification of the Kittim as Romans.

### Priestly Role in Battle (Cols. VII–IX)
Priests and Levites accompany the army in battle:
- Priests blow the trumpet calls from behind the battle line
- Priests perform prayers and blessings before each engagement
- **The pure condition of the camp** is strictly maintained — no woman, no lame, no blind, no sexually unclean person may enter the battle camp; God walks with the army and the holy angels are present

This is "holy war" theology at its most developed: the army of God must be ritually pure because God and the angels are visibly present in combat.

### Liturgy of War (Cols. X–XIX)
Extended prayers and hymns for the various phases:
- **Prayer of the Chief Priest** before battle: "Arise, O Hero! Lead Thy captives, O Glorious One! Gather Thy spoils, O Author of mighty deeds! Lay Thy hand on the neck of Thine enemies and Thy foot on the pile of the slain!"
- **The Seven-Battle Pattern**: The first three engagements are won by the Sons of Light; the next three by the Sons of Darkness — a pattern of apparent defeat that tests faith; then in the seventh and final battle the great hand of God intervenes decisively
- **Final victory hymn**: "Thou art God Most High, Lord of all, Ruler of all… Thou art God Most High, with Kings of all, and Prince of all princes"

### The Role of Michael
The War Scroll identifies **Michael** (Prince of Light, Prince of Angels) as the heavenly commander of the Sons of Light, parallel to Belial commanding the Sons of Darkness. This confirms the angelic-war cosmology behind the Two Spirits doctrine.

## Relationship to Other Texts
- **1QS / Two Spirits doctrine**: The War Scroll enacts the cosmic dualism of 1QS III–IV in military-eschatological form
- **11Q13 (Melchizedek)**: Melchizedek (= Michael) presides over the final judgment; the War Scroll's Michael is the same heavenly commander
- **4Q285 (Book of War)**: A related text that includes an important passage about the "Branch of David" (Isaiah 11:1) defeating the Kittim — possibly the Messiah of Israel leading the eschatological army in person
- **Revelation 12–19**: The War Scroll's cosmic war structure (heavenly battle, earthly battle, divine intervention, final victory) parallels the structure of Revelation's eschatological warfare, suggesting shared apocalyptic tradition
