# The Psalms (Psalter)

The Psalter (Hebrew *sēfer tehillîm*, “Book of Praises”; Greek *psalmoi* / *psaltērion*) is a collection of 150 poems — prayers for help, hymns, songs of trust and thanksgiving, instructional/Torah psalms, royal and enthronement poems, and liturgical units — arranged in five books. It is the largest book in the Hebrew Bible and the OT book most cited in the NT.

Primary modern commentary ingested at fidelity standard: [[declaisse-walford-psalms-nicot|deClaissé-Walford, Jacobson & Tanner, *The Book of Psalms* (NICOT, 2014)]]. Co-author overview: [[declaisse-walford-jacobson-tanner]].

## Textual Overview

### Title and character

[[declaisse-walford-jacobson-tanner|Jacobson]] (NICOT Introduction §I) notes that English “Psalm/Psalter” tracks Greek *psalmoi*/*psaltērion* (rendering Hebrew *mizmōr*, “song”), while the Masoretic title *tehillîm* is “praises” (abstract plural “praise”). Citing Limburg, he treats Hebrew “prayers” (*tepillôṯ*, cf. Ps 72:20) and “songs of praise” as two fundamental types. The volume opens with Luther’s “little Bible” framing and describes the Psalter as a “great choir of witnesses” rather than a single plot, epistle, or unified prophetic vision — expressing the full range of human emotion before God.

### Five-book structure

The Psalter is divided into five books by editorial doxologies (deClaissé-Walford, §IV):

| Book | Psalms | Closing doxology |
|---|---|---|
| Book One | 1–41 | 41:13 (“Amen and amen”) |
| Book Two | 42–72 | 72:18–19 (+ “The prayers of David, son of Jesse, are completed,” 72:20) |
| Book Three | 73–89 | 89:52 |
| Book Four | 90–106 | 106:48 |
| Book Five | 107–150 | 150:6 (entire Final Hallel as climax) |

Similar doxology wording and the restriction of “amen” to these closings suggest deliberate shaping. Midrash Tehillim on Ps 1 (reported by deClaissé-Walford): as Moses gave five books of law, David gave five Books of Psalms.

### Sub-collections

Preserved earlier collections (deClaissé-Walford §IV; Jacobson §II.B):

| Collection | Psalms | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Davidic | 3–14, 51–72, 108–10, 138–45 (and other *ledāwiḏ* units; 73 total) | Superscriptions; not original authorship labels per Jacobson |
| Korahite | 42–49, 84–85, 87–88 | Temple singer guild |
| Elohistic | 42–83 | Prefers *Elohim* over YHWH |
| Asaphite | 50, 73–83 | Temple musicians; Book Three core |
| Enthronement | 93, 95–99 (with debate over 94) | “YHWH reigns / is king” |
| Songs of Ascents | 120–134 | Pilgrimage liturgy |
| Egyptian Hallel | 113–118 | Passover |
| Hallelujah / Final Hallel | 111–118; 146–150 | *Halelû yāh* framing |

### Narrative elements

1. **Historical superscriptions** (thirteen MT units: Pss 3, 7, 18, 34, 51, 52, 54, 56, 57, 59, 60, 63, 142): Jacobson treats these as early interpretation, not compositional occasions; practically, all psalms are anonymous poem-prayers for later generations.
2. **Historical psalms** (78, 105, 106): recitals of Israel’s story as covenant testimony / confession.

## Major Themes / Narrative Summary

### The five-book story (canonical shape)

The central hermeneutical claim of [[declaisse-walford-jacobson-tanner|deClaissé-Walford]] (NICOT §IV): the five books **narrate ancient Israel’s history**, parallel to Samuel–Kings–Chronicles–Ezra–Nehemiah — “the story of the shaping of survival” for postexilic identity when temple/worship and **Yahweh as sovereign** (not a Davidic king) became the center of national life.

| Book | Narrative frame (deClaissé-Walford) | Dominant note |
|---|---|---|
| **One (1–41)** | Reign of David | ~59% lament; ʾašrê bookends (1 + 41); dual gateway Pss 1–2 |
| **Two (42–72)** | Toward Solomon | Korah + David (incl. historical superscriptions); aged-David reading of 71; Solomonic 72; “prayers of David… completed” |
| **Three (73–89)** | Divided kingdoms, destruction, crisis | Asaph/Korah; Ps 73 theodicy; Ps 88 unrelieved darkness; **Ps 89 hinge** (covenant renounced) |
| **Four (90–106)** | Exile identity | Only Mosaic psalm (90); Moses seven times in Book Four; enthronement 93–99; 105/106 twin; gather-us petition 106:47 |
| **Five (107–150)** | Return / new constitution | 107 answers 106:47; David reappears leading praise; Egyptian Hallel + 119 + Ascents; 145 kingship; Final Hallel 146–150 |

Lament→hymn movement (deClaissé-Walford statistics): Book 1 laments 59% / hymns 20%; Book 5 laments 23% / hymns 52%. David attributions fall from ~95% (Book 1) to ~6% (Book 3), then rise again in Book 5 (~32%).

### Psalm 1–2 gateway

Jacobson (Book One intro; Pss 1–2): dual introduction — **Torah piety** (Ps 1: *tôrâ* as “instruction,” not legalistic “law”; *hāgâ* as verbalized meditation) and **royal/YHWH sovereignty** (Ps 2: coronation; “You are my son” as metaphorical royal new birth, not adoption formula in Jacobson’s reading; post-monarchy messianic re-reading; NT application). Closing *ʾašrê* of 2:12 forms secondary inclusio with 1:1 (refuge).

### YHWH as king and *hesed*

Enthronement psalms answer the exile’s crisis by proclaiming **YHWH** (not a Davidic king) as king (Tanner, Book Four intro; Pss 93–99). *Hesed* is treated as an untranslated loanword in the NICOT volume (Jacobson §I): of 255 OT occurrences, **130 are in the Psalter** — character plus action, electing/delivering/sustaining Israel. See [[hesed]].

### Lament as necessary faith

The commentary treats prayers for help (individual/communal), including imprecation and unrelieved complaint, as theologically legitimate. Tanner on Ps 88: people suffer and die crying to a silent God; such speech is sacred Scripture. Imprecatory material (e.g. Pss 69, 109, 137:8–9) is not softened in the volume; reception includes McCann’s “non-violent act” framing (via Nancy on 109) and Zenger’s justice-for-victims reading (via Nancy on 137).

## Textual and Manuscript Tradition

From Jacobson §I (attributed):

- **Base text:** BHS / Codex Leningradensis (~1008 C.E.); overall quality “fair,” varying by psalm. BHQ Psalms and HU/Aleppo critical editions were not yet available for the 2014 volume (Aleppo lacks Pss 15:1–25:2).
- **Yarchin (~400 medieval MT Psalters):** semantic content (words/sentences) is stable, but poem *division* varies from **143 to 154** units; only ~**21%** match BHS’s 150-poem layout. Aleppo and Leningrad conjoin 114–115 (149 units). The traditional 150 stabilized with movable-type printing and the Rabbinic Bibles. The NICOT volume **honors** the 150-division while registering Yarchin’s caveat that this shape is an early modern editorial standardization, not the sole MS norm.
- **DSS / OG:** ≥39 DSS psalms MSS (Flint); order fluid especially for Pss 90+; Pss 1–89 more stable. LXX joins 9–10 and 114–115, adds Ps 151 “outside the number,” longer superscriptions.
- **Text-critical aim** of the volume: not the hypothetical pen-of-the-original-scribe text, but the text as it may have stood at the beginning of the Masoretic tradition (canonical texts of communities of faith; Childs/Waltke heuristic).

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

| Framework | How this wiki’s sources deploy it |
|---|---|
| **Canonical shape / editorial sequence** | deClaissé-Walford (building on Wilson *Editing of the Hebrew Psalter*, McCann *Shape and Shaping*): five-book narrative; sequence is hermeneutically load-bearing |
| **Poetry-first** | Jacobson/Tanner: meaning exists *in, with, and under* poetic language; parallelism multi-level (colon → twin psalms); no true meter; evocative language inseparable from theology |
| **Form criticism (background)** | Gunkel/Mowinckel/Gerstenberger (cult/life-setting stream) vs Westermann/Brueggemann (praise–petition; orientation–disorientation–reorientation). Volume leans second stream; resists reducing each psalm to a type |
| **Historical-critical / superscriptions** | Superscriptions = later editorial; *ledāwiḏ* not original King-David authorship; historical superscriptions = early exegesis |
| **Jewish reception** | Midrash Tehillim five-book / Moses parallel; liturgical use (Hallel, Ascents, Passover, etc.) |
| **Christian reception** | NT citations (esp. 2, 22, 110, 118); Christological/royal re-readings; penitential tradition (e.g. 51, 130) |

## Canon and Reception

- **Judaism:** Canonical among the Writings (*Ketuvim*); liturgical core (daily, festival, temple/synagogue); *tehillîm* as praise-telos of the collection.
- **Christianity:** Fully canonical in Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant canons; backbone of liturgy of the hours and hymnody; most-cited OT book in the NT.
- **Psalter as postexilic constitutive document** (deClaissé-Walford): answers “Who are we?” and “What are we to do?” under empire without a Davidic nation-state.

## Scholarship and Historiography

Positions as surveyed/held in the NICOT volume (not adjudicated in wiki voice):

- **Authorship:** Traditional Davidic association (ancient and expansive in LXX/Syriac/Qumran) vs critical practical anonymity; volume: *ledāwiḏ* likely originally not “authored by King David.”
- **Date of final form:** Among the latest OT books to stabilize; multiple “editions” at DSS/LXX period.
- **Theological center proposals** (Jacobson §VI survey): Brueggemann (dialectical), Mays (“YHWH reigns”), Creach (“refuge”), Wilson (sovereignty), McCann (justice + *hesed*), Jacobson (“YHWH is faithful”) — volume registers diversity without single adjudication.
- **Form vs final-form methods:** Childs (final form as transmitted whole) vs Sanders (final form *plus* recoverable community settings) both cited by deClaissé-Walford.

## Key interpretive cruxes

1. **Pss 1–2 as dual gateway** — torah instruction + royal messiah/refuge (Jacobson).
2. **Pss 9/10** — continuous imperfect acrostic (Jacobson).
3. **Ps 22** — Tanner: structure and NT passion reception; text-critical tentativeness at 22:16 (“like a lion” MT vs “pierced” LXX/DSS tradition).
4. **Ps 23** — majority “trust”; Tanner’s royal Shepherd-King reading vs Gerstenberger’s clan/individual; unrecoverable social setting.
5. **Ps 51** — historical superscription (Nathan/Bathsheba) as interpretive frame; penitence and *hesed* (deClaissé-Walford).
6. **Ps 72:20** — “prayers of David… completed” as editorial closure of Books 1–2 (Tanner).
7. **Ps 73** — sanctuary pivot for theodicy (Tanner / deClaissé-Walford).
8. **Ps 88** — only lament ending without trust/praise turn; God as cause of crisis (Tanner).
9. **Ps 89** — Psalter hinge: *hesed*/faithfulness/covenant praised then accused; 89:49 “Where is your hesed of old?” unanswered (Tanner). See [[davidic-covenant]].
10. **Book Four answer** — Moses envelope; YHWH-as-king enthronement without human king/David/Jerusalem at center (Tanner).
11. **Ps 110** — Melchizedek; NT dense citation; postexilic re-use (Nancy).
12. **Ps 119** — massive acrostic; eight torah-synonyms; torah as way to God / near-hypostasis (Freedman thesis reported) (Nancy).
13. **Ps 137:8–9** — infant-dashing imprecation extracted without softener; authors record justice-for-victims hermeneutics (Nancy via Zenger et al.).
14. **Ps 150** — absolute praise telos; every breathing thing (Nancy).
