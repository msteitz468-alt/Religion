# Dasam Granth

The "Book of the Tenth [Guru]" — a collection associated with **[[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind
Singh]]**, named the *Dasam* ("tenth") Granth to distinguish it from the *Ādi* ("first") Granth, the
[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]. Its canonical standing is the most contested in Sikhism: in
the eighteenth century it "shared with the Adi Granth the status of the eternal Guru… for a time, a
part of the Guru Granth," and 18th-century **Sarbat Khalsa** assemblies were held with both Granths
lying open; since then it "has dropped away" in status (McLeod, via agents 1, 2 ~L2006–2022).

*(Scaffolded 2026-06-27 from [[mcleod-sikhism|McLeod]]; the text itself is a later deep-ingest scope.)*

## Contents

Largely in **Braj Bhasha**, which few Sikhs can now read. Portions are in daily liturgical use as
*[[nam-simaran|gurbani]]* and recited at [[amrit-sanskar|amrit initiation]]:

- **[[jap-sahib|Jap Sahib]]** — 199 stanzas; an invocation cataloguing the divine qualities, its
  rhythm imitating sword-play; part of the morning [[nit-nem|Nit-nem]].
- **Ten Savayyas** — from the *Akāl Ustat*; appointed for daily recitation after [[japji|Japji]] and
  Jap.
- **Benatī Chaupaī** — recited in the evening **Sodar Rahirās** and at initiation.
- The **Zafar-nāma** ("Epistle of Victory") declares "that the Eternal One is just and that justice
  requires the sword to be drawn when order is threatened" (agent 4 ~L1973–1976) — the scriptural
  charter of the [[miri-piri|martial]] ethic.
- *Bachitar Natak* (a quasi-autobiographical narrative) and a large body of mythological/heroic
  narrative make up the bulk that is "largely ignored or of uncertain status."

## Canon and Reception (Canon Divergence)

- **Authoritative for [[sikhism|Sikhism]] / the [[khalsa|Khalsa]] in part** — Jap, Ten Savayyas, and
  Benatī Chaupaī are treated as *gurbani* and embedded in the Nit-nem and the initiation rite.
- **Disputed within mainstream Sikhism** for the bulk of the work; questions of authorship (how much
  is Gobind Singh's own) and of the heroic/mythological content keep its status unsettled.
- **Canonical for the [[namdhari|Namdharis]]**, who treat it as fully binding alongside the Adi Granth
  and obey it literally (agent 5 ~L15–18).
- **Given full honour by the [[nihang|Nihangs]]** (agent 4 ~L2080–2082).
- The ambiguity reaches the foundational moment: [[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind Singh]]'s terminal
  declaration vesting Guruship in "the Granth" does not specify whether the Adi Granth alone or both
  Granths were intended (agent 2 ~L1121–1125). See [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]].
