The **Pyramid Texts** are the **oldest known religious writings in the world** — funerary spells carved on
the inner walls of Old Kingdom royal pyramids (from c. 2400 BCE) to guarantee the dead **pharaoh's**
resurrection and ascent to the gods.

## Content

Hundreds of "utterances" (spells) intended to protect the king's body, reanimate him, and carry his soul up
to the sky to join **[[ra|Ra]]** among the imperishable stars, or to the realm of **[[osiris|Osiris]]**. They
preserve the earliest written forms of Egyptian theology — the [[heliopolitan-theology|Heliopolitan Ennead]],
the solar journey, and the king's identification with Osiris and the gods (including the startling
"**Cannibal Hymn**," in which the king devours the gods to absorb their power).

## Significance

The Pyramid Texts are the **source-stream** of all later Egyptian funerary literature — the Coffin Texts
(Middle Kingdom, for nobles) and the [[book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]] (New Kingdom, for everyone)
descend from them — and the foundation document of the Egyptian afterlife religion. Their afterlife was at
first **exclusively royal**, the king alone ascending to the gods.
