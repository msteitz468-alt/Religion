The **Bhagavad Gita** ("Song of the Lord") is the best-known Hindu scripture — 700 verses set within
the epic *Mahabharata*, the second of the **prasthana-trayi** ([[upanishads|Upanishads]], Gita,
[[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]]). Technically **smriti**, it functions as effectively canonical
scripture across [[hinduism|Hinduism]].

## The Dialogue

On the eve of battle, the warrior **Arjuna** refuses to fight kinsmen and turns to his charioteer,
**[[krishna|Krishna]]** — revealed as the supreme God in human form. Krishna's teaching synthesizes the
paths of Hindu spirituality:

- **Karma yoga** — disciplined action without attachment to its fruits (the Gita's signature ethic).
- **Jnana yoga** — the path of knowledge (the Upanishadic self/Brahman insight).
- **Bhakti yoga** — loving devotion and self-surrender to God, which the Gita exalts as supreme (ch.
  9–12; the cosmic theophany of ch. 11).

## Why Every Vedanta School Must Claim It

As part of the prasthana-trayi, the Gita is expounded by all three schools, and its very richness lets
each find itself there: [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] reads Krishna's "I am the Self in all beings" as
non-dual identity; [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] and [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] read its
intense theism and bhakti as a personal God really distinct from the devotee. One text,
[[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|three incompatible commentaries]].
