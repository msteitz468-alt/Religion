# Kitáb-i-Aqdas

*(Persian/Arabic: "The Most Holy Book")*

Bahá'u'lláh's book of holy law, composed in Arabic and completed around 1873 while he was still in the city of Akka (line 3381–3382). It was first published in Bombay in 1890/1 (line 3389). It was supplemented by various later writings and by Bahá'u'lláh's replies to a series of questions about it posed by his secretary, Zaynul-Muqarrabín, himself an expert in Islamic law (lines 3383–3388).

## Status and Significance

Shoghi Effendi described the Aqdas as the "Mother Book" of the Bahá'í dispensation; the "brightest emanation of the mind of Bahá'u'lláh"; and the charter of his "New World Order," foretold in the Bible in such phrases as the promised "new heaven" and "new earth" (lines 3427–3434).

Bahá'u'lláh stated that it was revealed in response to repeated requests from his followers for laws to follow (lines 3390–3393). For him, it is God's proof "unto all who are in heaven and all who are on earth" (line 3394–3395), his "unerring Balance" whereby all peoples of the world are tested, and its ordinances are "the mightiest stronghold for the protection of the world" (lines 3396–3402). True belief in God and his messenger is only complete by acceptance of what he has revealed and observance of his law (lines 3403–3404). "True liberty" consists in obedience to these laws (line 3422–3423).

## Contents

The laws partly follow the pattern of Islamic and Bábí holy law, and include (lines 3435–3472):

1. **Personal obligations towards God**: obligatory prayer, fasting, pilgrimage, zakát, Huqúqu'lláh, daily repetition of God's name.
2. **Personal status and contracts**: marriage, divorce, the age of maturity, inheritance, wills, endowments.
3. **Community life**: calendar, certain holy days, the Houses of Justice, the Mashriqu'l-Adhkár, education, burial.
4. **Prohibitions**: against adultery, pederasty, alcohol, drugs, asceticism, monasticism, begging, gambling, confession of sins, the kissing of hands, sedition, the slave trade, the use of insanitary Iranian public baths, overburdening animals.
5. **Exhortations**: to goodly deeds, cleanliness, detachment, trustworthiness, work, social order, to associate amicably with the followers of all religions, to promote the development of countries and cities, to adopt a single world language.
6. **Punishments** for crime and breaches of certain social laws.

Bahá'u'lláh also (lines 3473–3505):
- Abrogated Islamic teachings of ritual impurity, the Islamic prohibitions against music and the use of vessels of gold and silver, and the Bábí prohibition against marrying non-believers.
- Called upon the kings of the world to recognize him.
- Addressed specific rulers (Franz-Joseph, Wilhelm I), the presidents of the American republics, and the inhabitants of Istanbul and Tehran.
- Emphasized the infallibility of the Manifestations of God.
- Warned against pride and perverse interpretation.
- Directed the Bahá'ís to follow "Him Whom God hath purposed" amongst his sons after his death (the Covenant).
- Stated that there would be no further Manifestation of God for a thousand years.

## Translation History

An English translation was made around 1900 and circulated among early American Bahá'ís, and a literalistic non-Bahá'í translation was published in 1961. An official Bahá'í translation was long delayed; the Universal House of Justice published a Synopsis and Codification in 1973, and a full translation became available in 1992, accompanied by supplementary questions and answers and copious notes designed to help readers from a non-Islamic background (lines 3506–3524).

## Canon and Reception

Canonical for the Bahá'í Faith. The Aqdas is regarded as the supreme legal text of the Bahá'í dispensation, superseding both the Bayan of the Báb and Islamic holy law.
