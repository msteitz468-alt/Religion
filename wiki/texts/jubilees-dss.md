# Jubilees (Qumran Fragments)
**Sigla**: 4Q216–28 (4QJuba–h plus pseudo-Jubilees 4Q225–7 and 4Q228); also 1Q17–18, 2Q19–20, 3Q5; 11Q12

The **Book of Jubilees** survives in its complete form only in Ethiopic (Ge'ez) translation, where it is canonical for the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. The Qumran fragments — among the most numerous for any non-biblical book at Qumran (15+ copies) — confirm a **Hebrew original** and establish that Jubilees was treated as authoritative Scripture by the Qumran community. The Damascus Document explicitly cites Jubilees. The Qumran copies also provide several textual corrections to the Ethiopic translation.

## Canon and Reception

**Authoritative at Qumran**: The community's foundational solar calendar is the Jubilees calendar (364-day year organized by 7-week periods). CD explicitly treats Jubilees as Scripture. Multiple copies in multiple caves attest to its circulation and importance.

**Canonical in Ethiopia**: In the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition Jubilees is part of the broader biblical canon alongside 1 Enoch.

**Non-canonical elsewhere**: Not in the rabbinic Hebrew Bible or any Christian Old Testament except Ethiopia.

## Textual Corrections from Qumran

### 4Q219 and Abraham's Death
The Ethiopic text places Abraham's death in the **44th Jubilee**; 4Q219 reads the **43rd Jubilee**. This correction affects Jubilees' elaborate chronological framework for biblical history (every event is dated precisely by Jubilee, year, week, and day within a grand 49-year cycle structure).

### Other Corrections
The Qumran fragments confirm several readings against the Ethiopic where the translation appears to have smoothed over or altered the original Hebrew.

## 4Q225: The Akedah — Isaac's Willing Self-Sacrifice

The most theologically significant Qumran contribution is **4Q225 (pseudo-Jubilees a)**, which contains an account of the Akedah (Genesis 22) with a crucial development not explicitly in the biblical text:

> The angels are present at the sacrifice; **Mastema** (the prince of darkness, equivalent to Satan) has accused Abraham; in the Qumran version, **Isaac's willing participation in his own sacrifice is emphasized** — he is not simply a passive victim led to the altar but voluntarily offers himself.

This is the **earliest pre-Christian attestation** of the rabbinic tradition (later developed extensively in midrash) that Isaac understood what was happening and willingly offered himself as the sacrifice. The tradition is:
1. Pre-Christian: the Qumran fragments are dated to the 2nd–1st c. BCE
2. Parallel to but independent from the New Testament's use of the Akedah as a type of Christ's willing self-offering
3. Later developed in rabbinic midrash (*Bereshit Rabbah*, *Leviticus Rabbah*) and in early Christian theology (Augustine, Origen)

The Qumran Isaac is an active participant in atonement, not merely its passive object. This precedes and partially shapes Christian Atonement theology's emphasis on Christ's willing self-offering.

**Mastema's role**: The prince of darkness (Mastema) accusation of Abraham is also found in the full Jubilees text — Mastema is the one who challenges God to test Abraham (paralleling Satan in the Prologue of Job). The Qumran version emphasizes this accusatory function.

## Hermeneutical Framework

Jubilees is the paradigm case of **"rewritten Bible"** at Qumran. Vermes's definition is exact: "The work itself is a midrashic retelling of the story of Genesis (and the beginning of Exodus) in the form of a revelation conveyed by angels to Moses."

Three interpretive commitments follow from that sentence:

**Retelling, not commenting.** Jubilees does not stand outside Genesis and explain it; it re-narrates it. There is no lemma, no citation formula, no interpretive distance — the exegesis is carried by the retelling itself.

**A revealed chronological framework.** The Hebrew title Vermes records is *Book of the Divisions of the Times* — "repeated also in other 4Q fragments, a title already known from the Damascus Document (XVI, 3)." Biblical narrative is redistributed into jubilee periods, so that chronology becomes the organizing interpretive grid. That the Damascus Document already cites the work under this title, and that 4Q228 quotes it as authority — "For thus is written in the Divisions [of times]" — shows Jubilees functioning as scripture-like authority within the sect.

**Angelic dictation as the authority claim.** The revelation is mediated by angels to Moses, which places Jubilees' version of Genesis on the same footing as the Sinai revelation rather than subordinate to it.

**Gap-filling.** Where Genesis is terse, Jubilees and its associated Qumran material supply motive and dialogue. The Akedah in 4Q225 dramatizes a heavenly tribunal absent from Genesis 22 — the angels of Mastemah anticipating that Abraham "will be found unfaithful [to God]" while holy angels weep — and gives Isaac the words "T[ie me well]." Vermes notes these features "display close parallels to the post-biblical representation of the Akedah... anticipating features known from the Palestinian Targums," and that 4Q225 is "the earliest (pre-Christian) evidence for the rabbinic story of Isaac's voluntary self-sacrifice."

**On the calendar:** the 364-day solar reckoning is central to Jubilees as a whole, but the Qumran Jubilees fragments in the ingested range do not themselves argue it. The embedded Enoch fragment (4Q227) does tie Enoch's astronomical writing to preserving the righteous from error — he "wrote all the ... heaven and the ways of its host and the [mon]ths ... [th]at the ri[ghteous] may not stray."

## The Jubilees Calendar
Jubilees is the primary literary source for the **364-day solar calendar** that became the Qumran community's foundational commitment:
- 52 weeks of 7 days = 364 days exactly
- All festivals fall on the same weekday every year
- Passover always Wednesday; Shavuot always Sunday
- The year is divided into four seasons of 13 weeks each, plus 4 intercalary days

The calendar is not merely a practical matter but a theological one: Jubilees presents it as the **divinely ordained celestial order**, given to Moses by the Angel of the Presence. Using any other calendar (the lunar calendar of Jerusalem) is a cosmic transgression.

## The Jubilees Cosmology
Jubilees presents an expanded historical narrative from Creation through Sinai with:
- **Angelic mediation**: The Torah is dictated by the "Angel of the Presence" to Moses, not given directly by God; this elevates the heavenly origin of the Torah
- **Mastema / Satan**: A specific angelic adversary who tests humans with God's permission
- **Watchers**: The fallen angel tradition (from 1 Enoch) is incorporated into the narrative of Genesis 6
- **Israel's election**: History is divided into Jubilee periods demonstrating Israel's covenantal relationship

## Relationship to the Qumran Community
Jubilees was foundational for the Qumran community's:
- **Solar calendar** (the primary cause of separation from the Temple)
- **Angel cosmology** (heavenly warfare; Mastema as adversary)
- **Halakha** (Jubilees' strict Torah interpretation prefigures Qumran stringency)
- **Historical self-understanding** (the community's history is interpreted within the Jubilee chronological framework of the Damascus Document)
