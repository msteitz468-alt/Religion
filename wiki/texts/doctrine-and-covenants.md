# The Doctrine and Covenants

The **Doctrine and Covenants** (D&C) is the second of the Latter-day Saint "standard works" — a numbered collection of **modern revelations**, mostly given through [[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]], on church governance, doctrine, and practice. Unlike the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] (an ancient narrative), the D&C is a record of **contemporary revelation**, and it is the clearest expression of Mormonism's **[[continuing-revelation|open, additive canon]]**.

## Canon and Reception

| Standing | Community |
|----------|-----------|
| **Canonical** | [[latter-day-saints|LDS Church]] — living, sectioned corpus; later Official Declarations may be printed with it (not in the public-domain edition ingested here) |
| **Separate growing corpus** | [[community-of-christ|Community of Christ]] maintains its *own* D&C with different numbering and continuing sections from its prophet-presidents — graded here as deuterocanonical *relation* to the shared Smith-era material |
| **Rejected** | Historic Christian canons |

**Structural distinctive:** the D&C can and has been *added to* (e.g. §137–138; Official Declarations 1–2 in modern LDS printings). That open-canon principle is the taxonomy stress test of the wiki's `canon_scope` design alongside the Book of Mormon.

**Edition note (this ingest):** sacred-texts public-domain text containing **§§1–138**. **Official Declarations 1 (1890) and 2 (1978) are absent** from this file — note that gap when citing modern LDS practice (end of plural marriage; priesthood ban lift).

## Structure and Historical Arc

The D&C is not a continuous narrative but a chronological deposit of revelations — yet read in order, the sections track the church's own turbulent story across two decades, moving with it from New York to Ohio to Missouri to Illinois and past the founder's death. Following that arc is the best way to make sense of an otherwise disjointed corpus.

The **founding** sections (roughly §§2–23, 1823–1830) cluster around the coming-forth of the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] and the birth of the church: the early angelic promises, the crisis of the lost 116-page manuscript (§3, §10), the restoration of the Aaronic Priesthood (§13), and the articles and covenants that formally organized the church on 6 April 1830 (§20–21). With the move to **Ohio and the first designation of Zion** (1831), the Lord's own preface to the whole collection (§1) is joined by the law of consecration and a moral code (§42), the naming of Independence, Missouri as the center place of Zion (§57), and the principle of agency in governance — "it is not meet that I should command in all things" (§58).

The **Kirtland years of high doctrine** (1832–1833) produce the corpus's most expansive theology in quick succession: the vision of the three **degrees of glory** (§76), the oath and covenant of the priesthood (§84), the "olive leaf" on the light of Christ and the school of the prophets (§88), the **Word of Wisdom** health code (§89), and the teaching that intelligence is eternal and uncreated (§93). As persecution then mounts in **Missouri** (1833–1836), the revelations turn practical and pained — just war and the inspired Constitution (§98), an explanation of why Zion has been allowed to suffer (§101), the ordering of priesthood quorums (§107) — before culminating in the Kirtland Temple's dedicatory prayer (§109) and the return of Moses, Elias, and Elijah bearing their priesthood keys (§110).

Driven west to **Far West and then into Liberty Jail** (1838–1839), Joseph Smith receives the law of tithing (§119–120) and, from prison, the meditations on priesthood exercised only "by persuasion, by long-suffering… and by love unfeigned" (§121–123). In **Nauvoo** (1841–1843) the most distinctive temple theology arrives: baptism for the dead (§124, 127–128), the corporeal Godhead (§130), and celestial and plural marriage (§131–132). The collection then closes past the **martyrdom and succession**: John Taylor's account of the killings at Carthage (§135), Brigham Young's organization of the westbound Camp of Israel (§136), and two later visions of the afterlife — Joseph's glimpse of the celestial kingdom (§137) and Joseph F. Smith's 1918 vision of the redemption of the dead (§138).

The section-by-section payloads behind this arc are mapped thematically below.

## Key Sections (doctrinal map)

### Authority and church order
- **§1** — Lord's preface: "mine authority, and the authority of my servants"; the only true and living church *with which he is well pleased, speaking unto the church collectively and not individually* (1:30); what the Lord has spoken he has spoken (1:38).
- **§20** — Articles and covenants; church organized 6 April 1830; offices; baptism and sacrament liturgies; justification and sanctification; fall-from-grace warning.
- **§13** — Aaronic Priesthood restoration (John the Baptist): keys of ministering of angels, gospel of repentance, immersion baptism.
- **§27** — Sacrament; chain of keys (Moroni → Elias → John → Elijah → patriarchs → Peter/James/John).
- **§107** — Melchizedek and Aaronic structure; quorum equality; Adamic lineage; Adam-ondi-Ahman / Michael.

### Plan, cosmology, afterlife
- **§19** — "Endless" / "Eternal" as divine names; infinite suffering ("bleed at every pore"); Martin Harris and the printing of the Book of Mormon.
- **§29** — Spiritual and temporal creation; agency; third part of hosts; Fall; apocalypse; little children redeemed.
- **§76** — **Vision of the three degrees of glory** (celestial / terrestrial / telestial), after translating John 5:29 with Sidney Rigdon. Testimony: "He lives!" Worlds created by the Only Begotten. **Sons of perdition** alone unredeemed after wrath. Celestial: baptism, Holy Spirit of promise, "they are gods, even the sons of God," glory of the sun. Terrestrial: honorable, not valiant, glory of the moon. Telestial: delayed redemption, glory of stars, cannot dwell where God and Christ dwell "worlds without end."
- **§88** — "Olive leaf": light of Christ fills the immensity of space; soul = spirit + body; law of each kingdom; school of the prophets; resurrection/eschaton sequence.
- **§93** — Expansion of the Johannine prologue: grace for grace; "truth is knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come"; intelligence was not created; spirit and element inseparably connected receive fulness of joy.
- **§130–131** — Father and Son have bodies of flesh and bones; Holy Ghost a personage of Spirit; three degrees *within* the celestial kingdom; highest requires the **new and everlasting covenant of marriage**; spirit is refined matter.
- **§137–138** — Joseph's vision of the celestial kingdom (Alvin saved by desire of heart; children before accountability); Joseph F. Smith's 1918 vision of the **redemption of the dead** and organized preaching in the spirit world.

### Economy, health, gathering
- **§38, §42, §51, §78, §82** — "If ye are not one ye are not mine"; law of consecration / stewardships / storehouse; equal in earthly things or cannot be equal in heavenly; "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say" (82:10).
- **§57–59** — Independence, Missouri as center place of Zion; temple lot; free agency ("not meet that I should command in all things"); sabbath and fulness of the earth.
- **§89** — **Word of Wisdom**: "not by commandment or constraint"; wine/strong drink, tobacco, hot drinks restricted; herbs, grain, flesh sparingly; promise of health and that the destroying angel will pass by.
- **§119–120** — Law of tithing (surplus then tenth of interest; disposition council).

### Temple, dead, marriage
- **§109–110** — Kirtland Temple dedicatory prayer; acceptance by Jehovah; keys of Moses (gathering), Elias (Abrahamic gospel), Elijah (turning hearts).
- **§124, §127–128** — Nauvoo temple; **baptism for the dead** with recorder system; books of judgment; **welding link** between fathers and children; "their salvation is necessary and essential to our salvation… neither can we without our dead be made perfect" (128:15); Malachi 4 and 1 Cor 15:29 appropriated.
- **§132** — **New and everlasting covenant** of marriage; sealing by the Holy Spirit of promise under a single earthly key-holder; unsealed marriage ends at death (angels without exaltation); sealed celestial marriage → "continuation of the seeds forever" and "shall they be gods." The section also claims divine command for **plural marriage** on the model of Abraham/Hagar, with instructions concerning Emma Smith and the "law of Sarah." *Recorded as the text's claims — not adjudicated; see [[plural-marriage-lds]].*

### Suffering, government, martyrdom
- **§98, §101** — Renounce war / three-strike peace ethic; Constitution inspired; Zion's delays as theodicy for transgression; importune ladder.
- **§121–123** — Liberty Jail: many called, few chosen; "No power or influence can or ought to be maintained by virtue of the priesthood, only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by love unfeigned" (121:41); experience for good; document persecutions.
- **§134** — Declaration on governments and laws.
- **§135** — John Taylor on the martyrdom of Joseph and Hyrum Smith (Carthage, 27 June 1844): Joseph "has done more, save Jesus only, for the salvation of men in this world, than any other man that ever lived in it" (135:3).
- **§136** — Brigham Young: Camp of Israel organization after the exodus from Nauvoo.

## Major Themes

1. **Living prophetic authority** — servant voice = Lord's voice when moved upon (§1, §68).
2. **Open canon / continuing revelation** — corpus grows; mysteries line upon line.
3. **Graded afterlife and exaltation** — three kingdoms (§76); degrees within celestial (§131); godhood language (§76:58; §132:20).
4. **Priesthood keys and temple** — Aaronic/Melchizedek; Kirtland/Nauvoo; baptism for the dead; sealing.
5. **Zion as place and order** — Independence center; consecration; stakes; gathering.
6. **Embodied Godhead** — distinct personages; Father and Son corporeal (§130); non-Nicene framing relative to creedal Christianity.

## Hermeneutical Method

- **Revelatory present tense** — God speaks in first person to a named prophet and audience.
- **Scripture expansion** — John 5:29 → §76; John 1 → §93; Matthew 24 → related material in PGP; Malachi 4 → Elijah keys and welding link; 1 Cor 15:29 → baptism for the dead.
- **Canonical self-authentication** — §67 challenge to produce a commandment equal to the least in the Book of Commandments.
- **Keys over text** — living key-holder can seal/bind; later sections reinterpret earlier practice (e.g. Word of Wisdom's later binding force is reception history beyond §89's "not by commandment").
