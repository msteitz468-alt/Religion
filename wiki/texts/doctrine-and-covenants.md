The **Doctrine and Covenants** (D&C) is the second of the Latter-day Saint "standard works" — a
collection of **modern revelations**, mostly given through [[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]], on church
governance, doctrine, and practice. Unlike the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] (an ancient narrative),
the D&C is a record of **contemporary revelation**, and it is the clearest expression of Mormonism's
**open canon**: it has been *added to* over time (and differs between the LDS and Community of Christ
editions).

## Content

Numbered "sections" of revelation: the organization of the church and priesthood, the law of consecration,
the Word of Wisdom (the health code), temple and ordinance instructions, eschatology, and the distinctive
later doctrines (the degrees of glory, eternal marriage, and — historically — plural marriage, later
rescinded by Official Declaration 1, 1890).

## Canon Scope

Like the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]], `canonical_for: [latter-day-saints]`,
`deuterocanonical_for: [community-of-christ]` (which maintains its *own* growing D&C), and `rejected_by`
the historic Christian canons. Its **open, additive** character is the structural feature that most
distinguishes the Mormon canon from every other in the wiki — continuing revelation as a canon principle.
