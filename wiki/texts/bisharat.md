# Bishárát ("Glad-Tidings")

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from lines 7933–7997 unless noted.*

## Overview

Bishárát (Persian: "Glad-Tidings") is a Persian tablet of [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] revealed at [[bahji|Bahjí]]. It is also called the "Tablet of the Call" (*Lawh-i-Nidá'*). In it Bahá'u'lláh announces fifteen "glad-tidings" (*bishárát*) (lines 7933–7938).

## Contents: The Fifteen Glad-Tidings

**(1) Abolitions of Islamic and Babi laws and practices:**
- No. 1: Abolition of holy war (*jihád*) (line 7940–7941)
- No. 2: Abolition of restrictions on association between believers and followers of other religions (lines 7941–7943)
- No. 7: Abolition of restrictions on how believers should dress and wear their hair (line 7944)
- No. 10: Abolition of the Babi law regarding destruction of books (line 7946–7947)
- No. 11: What subjects believers might study — freedom of inquiry (line 7945–7946; see also Knowledge)
- No. 14: Abolition of the Shi'i encouragement to make special journeys to visit the graves of the dead (line 7949–7951)

**(2) Instructions regarding relations to secular powers:**
- No. 4: Bahá'ís should love and serve any king who protects their community (lines 7955–7956)
- No. 5: Wherever they reside, Bahá'ís should be loyal, honest, and truthful towards the government (lines 7957–7960)

**(3) Governance:**
- No. 13: Government of human affairs is entrusted to the (Universal) House of Justice; acts of worship are to be observed according to God's book (the Aqdas) (lines 7963–7967)

**(4) Instructions to world rulers:**
- No. 3: Choose a single language and script to be taught universally to all schoolchildren (lines 7969–7971)
- No. 5: Conversion of weapons of war into instruments of reconstruction (lines 7972–7973)
- No. 6: Establishment of the Lesser Peace (lines 7973–7974)
- No. 15: Ideal form of government is a combination of republicanism with kingship (constitutional monarchy) (lines 7975–7979)

**(5) Social injunctions:**
- No. 2: Associate with followers of other religions "in a spirit of friendliness and fellowship" (lines 7982–7984)
- No. 8: Christian monks and priests should abandon seclusion and live in the "open world," working, marrying, and having children (lines 7987–7990)
- No. 9: Confession of sins should be made to God, not to other people (lines 7991–7993)
- No. 12: Every person should have an occupation; work is a form of worship; idleness and begging are condemned (lines 7993–7996)
