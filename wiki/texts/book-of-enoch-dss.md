# Book of Enoch — Qumran Fragments
**See also**: [[1-enoch]] (main text page)
**Sigla**: 4Q201–212 (4QEna–f + Astronomical Enoch 4Q208-11 + 4Q212 Enoch and his Letter); 4Q530–3 (Book of Giants)

The Qumran cave discoveries produced **Aramaic fragments of multiple sections** of what we know as 1 Enoch — the composite work preserved in its complete form in Ethiopic. The Qumran finds confirm the Aramaic (original or near-original) language of the text, resolve several textual questions, and reveal that the **Book of Parables** (1 Enoch 37–71, the "Son of Man" section) was **not present at Qumran**. They also substantially expand our knowledge of the **Astronomical Book** (1 Enoch 72–82) and confirm the **Book of Giants** as a related Enochic text.

## Canon and Reception

**Authoritative at Qumran**: Multiple copies of multiple Enoch sections confirm Enochic literature was authoritative for the community. The Watchers narrative (1 Enoch 1–36) underlies the Qumran Two Spirits doctrine and angelology. Enoch's angelic cosmology is foundational for the community's worldview.

**Canonical in Ethiopia**: 1 Enoch is in the Ethiopian Orthodox biblical canon.

**Cited in Jude (NT)**: Jude 14-15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 as prophecy. Not otherwise canonical in any Christian tradition.

## The Aramaic Originals

The Qumran fragments confirm that the Enoch material was originally composed in **Aramaic** (or in Aramaic/Hebrew for different sections), not in the Ethiopic in which the complete text was preserved. The Aramaic of the Qumran fragments is literary and careful, suggesting a sophisticated scribal tradition.

## Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 1–36): 4Q201-2, 4Q204-7

**The Watchers Narrative**: 200 fallen angels (Watchers/*Irin*) descend to earth, take human wives (Gen. 6:1-4), and corrupt humanity. The Qumran fragments preserve portions of the narrative including:

**The Names of the 20 Chiefs** (4Q201 fr. 4; 4Q204):
The leader of the Watchers is **Shemihazah** (*Shemihazah* = "my name is what he saw"). The full list of 20 leaders is preserved:
1. Shemihazah (head)
2. Araqib / Araqael
3. Ramaël
4. Kokabël (star-god)
5. Tamaël
6. Ramaël
7. Daniël
8. Ezeqeel
9. Baraqiyal
10. Asael (**= Azazel**; taught humans to make weapons and use cosmetics)
11. Armaros
12. Batariel
13. Ananel
14. Zaqiel
15. Shamsiel (sun-god)
16. Sahariel (moon-god)
17. Tummiel
18. Turiel
19. Yomiel
20. Sariel

The specific identification of **Asael = Azazel** is important: in the biblical scapegoat ritual (Leviticus 16), the goat "for Azazel" is the sin-bearer. The Enochic tradition identified Azazel/Asael as the fallen angel who taught humans the arts of war and cosmetics — thus the scapegoat sent into the wilderness carries the sins that Azazel introduced. The Ages of Creation (4Q180) also interprets the 70 weeks of years as including a period defined by Azazel and the fallen angels.

**The Noah Birth Narrative**: A fragmentary account (4Q204 fr. 1 col. XXII; overlaps with 1 Enoch 106-7) describes the miraculous birth of Noah — he emerges glowing "like the sun," causing his father Lamech to fear he is fathered by an angel (the same scene elaborated in the Genesis Apocryphon 1QapGen cols. I-V). Enoch is consulted; he confirms Noah is of natural parentage but that great things are destined for him.

## Absence of the Book of Parables (1 Enoch 37–71)

The **Book of Parables** — the section containing the "Son of Man" figure ("that Son of Man who was with the Head of Days," 1 Enoch 46:1–2) — is **not attested in any Qumran fragment**. This is significant:
- All other major sections of 1 Enoch are found at Qumran
- The Book of Parables contains the most developed pre-Christian "Son of Man" speculation
- Its absence from Qumran raises questions about its date: possibly composed after the Qumran period (1st c. CE?) or simply not found yet

Vermes notes this absence without extensive commentary; it remains a major open question in Enoch scholarship.

## Astronomical Book (4Q208-11)

The Qumran fragments of the Astronomical Book are **more developed than the Ethiopic version** — they preserve more detailed astronomical tables and observations. The Astronomical Book describes the movements of the sun and moon and the lengths of days across the year, providing the cosmological basis for the 364-day solar calendar. Its Qumran version represents an earlier, more technically elaborate form of the text than what survived in Ethiopia.

## Book of Giants (4Q530-3)

A separate but related Enochic text not preserved in the Ethiopic 1 Enoch. The Book of Giants expands the Watchers narrative by focusing on the fate of the **giants** born from the union of Watchers and women. Key content from the Qumran fragments:

**The Giants' Dreams**: The giants have disturbing dreams that require interpretation. Two giants — **Ohiyah** and **Hahyah** (sons of Shemihazah) — dream of divine destruction and judgment. They cannot interpret the dreams themselves.

**Mahawai as Messenger**: A figure named Mahawai acts as messenger between the giants and **Enoch**. He flies to Enoch (who is in a transcendent realm) to ask for the dream interpretations.

**Ohiyah's Dream**: He sees trees being cut down by angels — a symbolic vision of the judgment against the giants.

**Enoch's Interpretation**: Enoch interprets the dreams as predicting the destruction of the giants in the coming flood-judgment.

**Gilgamesh Named**: One of the fragments contains the name **Gilgamesh** — the Mesopotamian hero — as one of the giants. This is remarkable: a Jewish text preserves Gilgamesh as a named participant in the Enochic Watchers narrative. The Book of Giants later circulated among the **Manichaeans** (Mani incorporated it into his mythology), confirming its wide circulation in late antiquity.

## Hermeneutical Framework

**Pseudepigraphic revelation as the authority claim.** Enoch speaks in the first person as a recipient of angelic instruction and a reader of heavenly writing: "I know the mysteries [of the Lord which] the holy ones have explained and showed me and which I read [in] the heavenly [tablets]" (4Q204). The visionary journey form reinforces it — "I was shown other mountains... I passed to the Paradise of righteousness" (4Q206) — as does his being shown the pits where souls wait "until the day of their judgement." Authority rests on eyewitness cosmic travel and privileged access to celestial records, not on exegesis of a received text.

**Narrative expansion of Genesis 6:1–4.** The terse biblical notice of the "sons of God" and the Nephilim is developed into a full Watchers narrative with a named hierarchy — twenty chiefs headed by Shemihazah, including Asael, Baraqel, Kokabel and Hermoni (4Q201 1) — who "took for themselves wives from all those whom they chose... and to teach them sorcery and magic," and whose union produces giants. The related 4Q180 gives an alternative naming with Azazel at the centre; Vermes describes that text's subject as "the myth of the fallen angels and the daughters of men, which is based on Genesis vi, 1–4, and fully developed in 1 Enoch." Expansion here is generative rather than explanatory: it supplies an aetiology of evil that Genesis withholds.

**Calendrical reasoning in the Astronomical Book.** The Qumran recension is, per Vermes, "considerably longer than the Ethiopic." The surviving material tracks lunar waxing and waning in sevenths against the sun's passage through "gates" — "the sun comple[tes] the passage through all the sections (?) of the first gate and recommences to go in and come out" (4Q209 7). This is descriptive astronomical reckoning; the ingested range contains no explicit argument for the 364-day year and no polemic against a lunar calendar, so the connection to the sect's solar calendar should not be read out of this text alone.

**Textual divergence from the Ethiopic — a hermeneutically loaded absence.** Vermes: the Qumran copies are "attested by, but not strictly identical to, the Ethiopic... the astronomical section is more developed... while **the Book of Parables (chaps. XXXVII–LXXI) with its Son of Man speculation is completely lacking at Qumran**." Because the Parables carry Enoch's Son of Man material, its absence bears directly on debates over pre-Christian Son of Man expectation. Vermes is methodologically strict about not filling the gap: he declines to translate Milik's retroversions, printing only "those which make sense in themselves." See the "Absence of the Book of Parables" section below, and [[1-enoch]].

## Significance of the Qumran Enoch Material
1. **Aramaic original confirmed**: The Enochic texts are originally Aramaic Second Temple compositions, not translations from Ethiopic
2. **Calendar basis**: The Astronomical Book provides the cosmological foundation for the 364-day calendar
3. **Watchers tradition**: The full list of fallen angel chiefs and their corrupting activities underlies Qumran angelology and demonology
4. **No Book of Parables**: The Son of Man speculation most relevant to NT Christology is absent — a datum for the dating debate
5. **Book of Giants — Gilgamesh**: A Jewish text naming the Mesopotamian hero; confirms cross-cultural transmission of ancient Near Eastern traditions through Jewish apocalyptic
