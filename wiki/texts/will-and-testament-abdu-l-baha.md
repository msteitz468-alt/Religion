# Will and Testament of 'Abdu'l-Bahá

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Overview

'Abdu'l-Bahá's Will is a lengthy document written in three parts over a seven-year period (1901–8) during the early period of his ministry. He wrote it in his own hand and addressed it to [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] (lines 32463–32469). Its contents were not known by the Bahá'ís until after 'Abdu'l-Bahá's death (28 November 1921), and it was apparently not read to Shoghi Effendi himself until "a few days" after his arrival in Haifa (29 December; lines 32470–32477).

## Contents

The Will (lines 32478–32519):
1. **Identified Shoghi Effendi** as the "primal" and "chosen" branch (Aghsán), appointed him as the first in a projected line of Guardians of the Cause and head of the as yet unelected [[universal-house-of-justice|Universal House of Justice]], and directed all Bahá'ís to turn to him.
2. **Assured** both the Guardian and the House of divine protection and guidance. All were to obey them; those who disputed with them disputed with God.
3. **Declared** that Muhammad-'Alí (called "the Center of Sedition" and "the focal Center of Hate") had "passed out from under the shadow of the Cause" due to his covenant-breaking, implicitly setting aside the original successorship established by Bahá'u'lláh in his Book of the Covenant.
4. **Instructed** Bahá'ís to teach, manifest spiritual qualities, associate lovingly with all peoples, be loyal to their rulers, avoid contention, and shun Covenant-breakers.
5. **Described** the duties and manner of election of the Universal House of Justice, and the obligations of the [[hands-of-the-cause|Hands of the Cause]].

The Will was first read officially on 3 January 1922; copies were sent to Bahá'ís in the East. Shoghi Effendi prepared an English translation, though copies were not widely circulated in the West at first (lines 32521–32527).

## Reception

The provisions were almost universally accepted by the Bahá'ís. The exceptions were Ruth [[white-ruth|White]] and a few Western followers, who challenged the Will's authenticity — a charge rebutted by those who read Persian and were familiar with 'Abdu'l-Bahá's handwriting, including opponents of Shoghi Effendi (lines 32528–32534). Shoghi Effendi addressed some of the issues raised by this challenge in the first two of his [[world-order-of-bahaullah|World Order of Bahá'u'lláh]] letters (lines 32535–32539). Elsewhere he described the Will as the "Charter" for a new World Order (GPB 325–6; WOB 143–4; lines 32540–32544).

## Significance

The Will and Testament is the founding document of the Bahá'í Administrative Order as it exists today. It established the Guardianship of Shoghi Effendi and defined the relationship between the Guardian and the Universal House of Justice. Without it, neither institution could claim Bahá'í textual authorization.
