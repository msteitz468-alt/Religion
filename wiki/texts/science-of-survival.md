**Science of Survival** (1951), by **[[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]**, is the second major foundational
book of the [[scientology|Dianetics/Scientology]] movement, written in the year between [[dianetics|
Dianetics]] (1950) and the founding of the Church. Its great contribution is the **[[tone-scale|Tone
Scale]]** and the accompanying **Hubbard Chart of Human Evaluation**.

## Teaching

The book systematizes the prediction of human behavior. Built around the principle that the dynamic
urge of all life is **to survive** ("survive!"), it presents the **[[tone-scale|Tone Scale]]** — a graded
scale of **emotional tones** (from death and apathy at the bottom, up through grief, fear, anger,
antagonism, boredom, to conservatism, cheerfulness, and **enthusiasm**, and higher) that is held to
correlate with a person's ethics, health, sanity, reliability, and behavior. By placing a person on the
scale (their "chronic tone level"), one can — Hubbard claims — **predict** how they will behave across
many columns of life. The large fold-out **Chart of Human Evaluation** is the book's centerpiece.

## Place in the Corpus

With [[dianetics|Dianetics]], Science of Survival belongs to the **early, ostensibly secular** phase of
Hubbard's work (before the explicit [[thetan|thetan]] doctrine and the religion). Within Scientology it is
"Source" — scripture to be applied as **standard tech** — and the Tone Scale remains a central practical
tool throughout the later system.

## Reception

Mainstream psychology does not accept the Tone Scale as a validated predictive instrument; the wiki's
secondary sources ([[reitman-inside-scientology|Reitman]], [[shelton-a-to-xenu|Shelton]]) treat it
critically. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
