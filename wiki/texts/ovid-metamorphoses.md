**Ovid's *Metamorphoses*** (c. 8 CE) is the single greatest **repository of Greco-Roman myth** — a Latin
epic in 15 books narrating the world's history "from the first beginning" to Ovid's own day through a chain
of **transformation** stories (gods and mortals changed into animals, plants, stars, and stones).

## Content and Religious Value

Though a literary masterpiece rather than a cult text, the *Metamorphoses* is the form in which most
classical mythology reached the later West: the loves and punishments of [[zeus|Jupiter]], Apollo and
Daphne, Phaethon, Persephone, Pygmalion, Pyramus and Thisbe, and hundreds more. It presents the
[[interpretatio-romana|Roman gods under their Greek myths]] and is the chief source for the **personalities
and stories** of the pantheon as later European culture knew them.

## Significance

The decisive channel of classical mythology into medieval and Renaissance Europe; for the wiki, the broad
narrative companion to [[hesiod-theogony|Hesiod's]] genealogy and [[homer|Homer's]] epics.
