# Seven Valleys (*Haft Vádí*)

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Overview

The *Seven Valleys* is a well-known mystical treatise by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]], described by [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] as his greatest mystical work (*God Passes By*, p. 140). It was composed in Baghdad some time after Bahá'u'lláh's return from Kurdistan (1856) in reply to a letter by Shaykh Muhíyu'd-dín, a Qádirí Sufi and sometime religious judge (*qádí*) in the town of Khániqín (lines 28314–28323).

## Structure: The Seven Valleys

The text describes the seven stages ("valleys") of the journey of the seeker's soul towards God, using the framework established by the great 12th/13th-century mystical poet Shaykh Farídu'd-dín 'Attár in his *Conference of the Birds* (*Mantiqu't-tayr*). The valleys are:

1. **Search** (*talab*): characterized by patient quest for the divine beloved
2. **Love** (*'ishq*): with its passion of pain and ecstasy
3. **Knowledge** (*ma'rifat*): with its certitude and understanding of inner truth
4. **Unity** (*tawḥíd*): in which the seeker transcends the world of limitation, and is able to see the reality of things as they really are
5. **Contentment** (*istighnáʼ*): in which the mystic sees the beauty of God in everything, and has burnt away all other veils
6. **Wonderment** (*ḥayrat*): with its bewilderment with the myriad of divine truths that can now be understood
7. **True poverty** (*faqr-i-ḥaqíqí*) and "absolute nothingness" (*faná-yi-kullí*): characterized by a dying from self and a living in God

(Lines 28324–28349.)

## Hermeneutical Framework

The text exemplifies a Sufi-inflected mystical hermeneutic — Bahá'u'lláh engages directly with the Persian mystical poetic tradition ('Attár, Rúmí, Háfiz, Sa'dí are all cited) while reorienting it around his own spiritual claims. The framework is *neyartha/nitartha* in spirit — the apparent Sufi content is re-read as pointing toward the Manifestation of God. This is the *commentary-as-transformation* mode: engaging the reader in the mystical journey while redirecting its destination.

## Relationship to the Four Valleys

The *Seven Valleys* is frequently paired with the *Four Valleys* (*Chahár Vádí*), another Baghdad-period mystical treatise by Bahá'u'lláh, though the *Seven Valleys* is the more prominent of the two.

## Canon and Reception

Canonical scripture for Bahá'ís. Widely read and valued as an introduction to Bahá'í mystical theology. An English translation is published in the volume *The Seven Valleys and the Four Valleys*.
