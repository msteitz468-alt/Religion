# Habakkuk Commentary (Pesher Habakkuk)
**Sigla**: 1QpHab

The most extensively preserved and theologically richest of the **pesharim** (singular: *pesher*), discovered in Cave 1 in 1947. It provides a running interpretive commentary on Habakkuk 1–2 (chaps. 1 and 2 only; Hab. 3 is not included), treating every clause of the prophetic text as a coded reference to the community's own history. The Habakkuk Commentary is the primary source for the community's understanding of the **Teacher of Righteousness**, the **Wicked Priest**, the **Kittim**, and the **Liar/Spouter of Lies**.

## Canon and Reception
Purely sectarian; no standing in any other tradition. Its value is historical and hermeneutical — it documents the Qumran community's self-interpretation and its application of prophetic scripture to immediate historical experience.

## The Pesher Method
Each biblical verse is quoted and then interpreted with the formula "its interpretation (*pesher*) concerns…" The community treats Habakkuk as a prophet who did not know the meaning of his own words; God revealed the full meaning **to the Teacher of Righteousness** alone, who is the divinely authorized interpreter of all prophecy. The fulfillment is happening now, in the community's own time.

## Key Interpretations

### The Teacher of Righteousness (Cols. I–II)
*Habakkuk 1:4 — "The wicked man compasses the righteous"*:
> "The wicked man is the Wicked Priest and the righteous man is the Teacher of Righteousness."

*Habakkuk 1:5 — "Look among the nations"*:
> "This concerns the traitors with the Man of the Lie, who did not listen to the words of the Teacher of Righteousness from the mouth of God."

*Habakkuk 2:2 — "That he who runs may read it"*:
> "This concerns the Teacher of Righteousness, **to whom God made known all the mysteries of the words of His servants the Prophets**."

This is the foundational claim: the Teacher is the singular authorized interpreter of all prophecy. His interpretation is not scholarly opinion but divinely imparted revelation.

### The Liar / Spouter of Lies (Cols. II–V)
The "House of Absalom" (*bet Avshalom*) — a group that "did not assist the Teacher of Righteousness against the Man of Lies, who rejected the Law in the midst of their whole congregation." These are people who were close to the Teacher but failed to support him when the Liar opposed him.

The Liar/Spouter of Lies:
- "Led many astray in order to build his city of vanity with blood and to raise a congregation on deceit"
- Made his followers work without wages — "weary them in service of falsehood"
- His fate: he "shall receive the same reward as the Wicked Priest"

### The Kittim (Cols. II–VI)
*Habakkuk 1:6 — "that bitter and hasty nation"*:
> "This concerns the Kittim who are quick and valiant in war, causing many to perish."

The Kittim are unmistakably the **Romans** on multiple specific grounds cited by Vermes:
1. They "worship their military standards" (*nishmat kelev*, their military standards that they sacrifice to)
2. Their cavalry use **greaves** — a detail Vermes links to equipment introduced in Julius Caesar's cavalry
3. They "come from the islands of the sea" (the Mediterranean)
4. Their officers ("rulers") come one after another and destroy the land
5. Their generals receive tribute from Gentile cities

### The Wicked Priest (Cols. VII–XII)
The longest and most specific section. Multiple accusations:

**Origin**: "The Wicked Priest who was called by the name of truth when he first arose, but when he ruled over Israel his heart became proud and he abandoned God and betrayed the precepts for the sake of riches."

This precisely fits Jonathan Maccabaeus (ruled 153–143 BCE), who began as a legitimate resistance leader but accepted the high priesthood from a Seleucid king (Alexander Balas, 152 BCE), thereby becoming — in the community's view — an illegitimate priest.

**Attack on the Teacher on the Day of Atonement**:
> "He pursued the Teacher of Righteousness to the house of his exile that he might confuse him with his venomous fury. And at the time appointed for rest, for the Day of Atonement, he appeared before them to confuse them and cause them to stumble on the Day of Fasting, their Sabbath of repose."

The "house of exile" is the community's settlement; the Wicked Priest violated the community's Day of Atonement (which fell on a different date per the solar calendar) by physically confronting them.

**Robbing the poor**:
> "He robbed and amassed the riches of the men of violence who rebelled against God, and he took the wealth of the peoples, heaping sinful iniquity upon himself. And he lived in the ways of abominations amidst every unclean defilement."

**Fate of the Wicked Priest**:
> "God delivered him into the hands of his enemies because of the iniquity committed against the Teacher of Righteousness and the men of his Council, in order to humble him by the stroke of suffering, causing him to vanish away in bitterness of soul, for having acted wickedly against His elect."

This death by enemies' hands matches Jonathan Maccabaeus, who was captured and killed by Tryphon (143 BCE).

**"Lebanon" and "the beasts"** (Hab. 2:17):
> "Its interpretation: Lebanon is the Council of the Community; and the beasts are the simple of Judah who keep the Law."

Lebanon = community; the beasts destroyed by Lebanon's devastation = the simple faithful whose suffering the Wicked Priest caused.

### "The Righteous Shall Live by His Faith" (Col. VIII)
*Habakkuk 2:4 — "The righteous man shall live by his faith/faithfulness" (*emunato*)*:
> "This concerns all those who observe the Law in the House of Judah, whom God will deliver from the House of Judgement because of their suffering and because of their **faith in the Teacher of Righteousness**."

This is the Qumran interpretation: *emunah* = **faithfulness to the Teacher of Righteousness**, not abstract faith in God. The righteous are saved through their loyalty to the Teacher's interpretation of the Law. Paul cites this same Habakkuk text (Rom. 1:17; Gal. 3:11) with a strikingly different interpretation (*faith in Christ*), suggesting common exegetical tradition applied to different figures.

### The Spouter of Lies and the Cup (Cols. XI–XII)
*Habakkuk 2:15–16 — "Woe to him who gives his neighbours drink… the cup of the Lord's right hand"*:
> "This concerns the Wicked Priest who pursued the Teacher of Righteousness to the house of his exile… and drank, causing revelers to be drunken… the cup of the wrath of God will swallow him up."

## Historical Significance
The Habakkuk Commentary is the primary documentary witness to:
1. The community's understanding of the Teacher as uniquely authorized interpreter
2. The identity and career of the Wicked Priest (Jonathan Maccabaeus per Vermes)
3. The community's Kittim = Romans identification
4. The community's dualistic, eschatological self-understanding
5. The Qumran interpretation of "righteous by faith" — faith as loyalty to a specific inspired teacher
