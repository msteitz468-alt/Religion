# The First Letter of Peter

## Textual Overview

A circular **Diaspora letter** addressed "to the elect, foreigners of the dispersion of Pontus,
Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia" (1:1) — the Christians of northern Asia Minor — written to
encourage believers suffering social hostility for their faith. Often called "the most condensed NT
résumé of the Christian faith and of the conduct it inspires" (Clowney, cited by Jobes); Luther
ranked it among the "noblest books in the New Testament," on a par with Romans and John. Its
governing concept is Christian identity as **"foreigners and resident aliens"** (*parepidēmoi* and
*paroikoi*, 2:11) in a hostile world, and its governing ethic is that **it is better to suffer than
to sin**, following the pattern of the suffering and vindicated Christ (2:21). The letter's stated
purpose, in Jobes's reading of the closing, is: "This is the true grace of God. Stand firm in it!"
(5:12) — apostolic confirmation that the letter's teaching and the suffering-as-calling it describes
are the true grace, amid doubt under social pressure.

[[karen-jobes|Karen H. Jobes]]'s BECNT commentary (2005; wiki source page
[[jobes-1-peter-becnt|Jobes, *1 Peter* BECNT]]) makes three distinctive contributions: (1) a **new
historical theory** — the recipients were converted elsewhere (probably Rome) and *displaced to Asia
Minor by Roman colonization*, rather than evangelized in situ; (2) a sustained reading of 1 Peter
against its **Septuagint** matrix (Peter applies OT passage *contexts*, not proof-texts); and (3) a
**syntax-critical** argument that the Greek shows "bilingual interference" consistent with a
Semitic-speaking author — undercutting the standard "Greek too good for a fisherman" objection to
Petrine authorship. See [[karen-jobes|Jobes]], [[peter-apostle|Simon Peter]], and
[[1-peter-authorship|1 Peter: Authorship, Date, and the Colonization Theory]].

**Genre and unity (Jobes).** Jobes treats the work as unified **personal correspondence** in Diaspora-
letter form (closest parallel: Epistle of Jeremiah; cf. 2 Macc. 1:1–9; 2 Bar. 78–87) — a recognized
spiritual authority urging a holy way of life on a scattered people. She (with the current consensus)
rejects older source-critical theories that 1 Peter was a **baptismal homily/paschal liturgy** later
framed as a letter (baptism is mentioned only once, 3:21, and is not discourse-prominent) and that
4:11 marks a seam between two documents (the doxology closes a unit of thought; persecution intensity
after 4:12 is exaggerated). Primary literary source: the **Greek OT (LXX)**; secondarily oral
Christian tradition (Schutter via Jobes). Claims that it embeds pre-formed hymns/creeds (notably
2:21–25; 3:18–22) are judged unprovable — the material is too thoroughly integrated; 2:21–24 is
adequately accounted for from Isa 53 LXX.

**Structure (Jobes's outline of the discourse).**

- **I. 1:1–2** — Greeting to the Christian Diaspora; election shaped as foreknowledge of the Father,
  consecration of the Spirit, purpose in obedience and sprinkling of Christ's blood (Exod 24
  covenant language redefined)
- **II. 1:3–2:10** — *Reassurance for God's people*: 1:3–12 as rhetorical exordium (one long Greek
  sentence: new birth into living hope; joy amid trials; advantage over prophets and angels); 1:13–2:3
  "be what you are" (hope, holiness, love, crave pure spiritual milk); 2:4–10 identity climax (living
  stones / spiritual house; "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation," 2:9)
- **III. 2:11–4:11** — *Live godly lives as God's people*: lifestyle evangelism (2:11–12); submit to
  pagan authority (2:13–17); household code with Christ as Servant paradigm (2:18–3:7); inner
  community virtues (3:8–12); unjust suffering for the name (3:13–4:11), including spirits in prison
  (3:18–22) and gospel-to-the-dead (4:6)
- **IV. 4:12–5:11** — *Consolation for the suffering flock*: fiery ordeal; judgment beginning with
  God's house; elders as shepherds; resist the devil; trust the Creator
- **V. 5:12–14** — Closing: Silvanus, "she in Babylon chosen with you," Mark; true grace / stand
  fast; kiss of love; peace (structural inclusio with 1:1–2)

**Recipients and situation (Jobes).** Five regions (~129,000 sq. mi., Elliott) predominantly outside
Paul's attested mission field (Spirit bars Bithynia, Acts 16:7). Modern consensus: predominantly
Gentile readers (1:18; 4:3); Jobes: ethnicity "makes little difference" — Peter addresses them
indiscriminately *from within the traditions of biblical Israel*, assuming LXX literacy. Suffering:
not state-sponsored martyrdom policy but *sporadic, local, unofficial social hostility* — slander,
malicious talk, false accusation, honor/status loss (1:6; 2:12; 3:9, 16; 4:4, 14–16). "Fiery ordeal"
(4:12) = testing/smelting image (Seneca; Sir 2; OT refining), not Nero's literal fires. Region list
at 1:1 is more likely the author's **mental map** than a proven sequential courier itinerary.

## Major Themes / Narrative Summary

*(Attributed to Jobes's seven-point "Major Themes and Theology" and related body exegesis.)*

1. **Foreigners and resident aliens.** Controlling self-understanding: Christians as *visiting
   foreigners and resident aliens* (*parepidēmoi*, *paroikoi*) whose new allegiance marks them off
   from society. Diaspora (1:1) // Babylon (5:13) inclusio joins readers to Israel's exile frame;
   foreignness is established by election (Goppelt via Jobes). Literal colonization displacement may
   have powered the metaphor; after circulation, figurative sense predominated. See
   [[christian-exile-diaspora|Christian Exile / Diaspora Identity]].
2. **Suffering for the name.** Destiny of Christ = destiny of the Christian; a godless world rejects
   believers for the same reasons it rejected Christ. Church as *alternate society / colony* of
   brotherly love. Suffering carefully delimited to persecution for bearing Christ's name (4:14–16),
   though trust-in-God principles may apply more broadly. Principle: better to suffer than to sin
   (3:17; 4:1–2).
3. **God.** Theocentric *and* christocentric. Divine **fatherhood** controlling (Father of our Lord
   Jesus Christ, 1:3; Father of believers by new birth); merciful (1:3; 2:10) but impartial **Judge**
   of every life (1:17); judgment "begins with the household of God" (4:17); Creator/sovereign who
   calls to eternal glory (5:10).
4. **Christ.** Focus not primarily preexistence or Synoptic teaching but *rejection unto death*:
   basis of new covenant (1:1–3), price of redemption (1:18–19), substitutionary sin-bearing (2:24),
   reconciliation (3:18) — and **resurrection and ascension** as victory over all powers (3:18–22).
   Shepherd/Overseer (2:25); living Cornerstone vs stumbling stone (2:4–8).
5. **Salvation.** Goal of faith (1:9); benefits of new birth **present**, but full *sōtēria* "ready
   to be revealed at the last time" (1:5) — inaugurated eschatology; trials test genuineness of faith
   (1:7). Deliverance from foreigner-status dissonance into undissonant existence with God.
6. **Eschatology.** Alpha: new birth via resurrection; omega: final judgment and glory. "The end of
   all things is near" (4:7) = urgency of living in the last stage of redemptive history, not a failed
   timetable (Jobes: no more or less true today than when Peter wrote). Present suffering can itself
   be God's judgment process beginning with his house; Spirit of glory rests on the insulted (4:14).
7. **Early, creative theology.** Jobes (with Schlatter, R. P. Martin, Marshall) stresses that 1 Peter
   is *not* a footnote to Paul but independently creative and "antiquated" in the good sense — close
   to the crucifixion. Three distinctive NT contributions Jobes credits to Peter: (1) explicit
   **identification of Jesus with the Isaiah 53 Suffering Servant** (2:21–24 follows *passion*
   sequence, not Isa 53 order — see [[servant-songs|Servant Songs]]); (2) **spirits in prison /
   cosmic victory** of resurrection-ascension (3:18–22); (3) **living stones / spiritual house**
   with Christ as living cornerstone (2:4–8) — theocentric temple image (unlike Pauline bride/body).

### Narrative frameworks and typology (Jobes)

1. **Passion as paradigm (2:21–25).** Concentrated passion sequence modeling silent endurance of
   unjust suffering; creative interweave of Isa 53 LXX (not proof-text order); *hypogrammon*
   (children's letter-tracing pattern) = closest of copies. Corporate Servant language of Isa 41
   extended to Christians after identifying Jesus with the individual Servant of 53.
2. **Enoch–Noah tradition (3:19–20).** Jobes reads "spirits in prison" against 1 Enoch Watchers /
   Gen 6 background: fallen angels (and/or spirits of their offspring) associated with Noah's day.
   Ascension proclamation of victory/condemnation, not *descensus* or postmortem gospel. Flood ↔
   baptism typology: few (eight) saved through water // baptism now saves through the resurrection.
   Asia Minor Noah prominence (Apamea Kibotos, indigenous flood traditions, Sibylline Oracles)
   underwrites cultural intelligibility. See [[noah|Noah]], [[enoch|Enoch]].
3. **Israel identity language for the church (2:4–10).** Unabashed application of Exod 19 / Isa 43 /
   Hos 2 descriptors (chosen race, royal priesthood, holy nation, people for special possession;
   once not a people / now people of God) — "total appropriation of the language of Israel"
   (Achtemeier via Jobes), distinct from Hebrews prefiguration, Pauline typology, Matthean
   fulfillment formulas.
4. **Ps 34 (33 LXX) as ethical matrix.** Diaspora-contextualized Greek psalm (*paroikia* sojournings)
   grounds ethics (allusions at 1:17–21; quote at 2:3; extended at 3:10–12) much as Isa 53 grounds
   Christology.

## Textual and Manuscript Tradition

*(What Jobes's commentary supplies; she is not a text-critical monograph.)*

- **Greek text base.** Jobes works within the Nestle-Aland critical tradition (NA27-era apparatus
  conventions of BECNT: half-brackets for variants; shading for unit summaries). She does not offer a
  full stemma or MS history of 1 Peter.
- **Variants she adjudicates (examples from the re-ingest digests).**
  - **3:18 *epathen* ("suffered") vs *apethanen* ("died"):** strong MS support for "died," but Jobes
    with majority critical commentators prefers **"suffered"** as original — better fits discourse
    (Christ as paradigm of unjust suffering; *paschō* 12× in the letter); scribes may have changed to
    "died" in veneration for the crucifixion.
  - **4:14 longer reading** ("blasphemed by them but glorified by you"): Jobes sides with **NA27**
    (and most English versions except NKJV) that the expansion is secondary — earliest best MSS omit;
    weak Isa 52:5 "allusion" for originality.
  - **1:21 *pistous* vs *pisteuontes*:** critics prefer adjective *pistous* as original (unusual form
    scribes smooth to participle); sense still "who trust through him in God," with faithfulness
    overtone.
  - **2:3 *chrēstos* / *Christos* play:** possible one-vowel wordplay on "the Lord is good" generated
    MS variants reading *Christos*.
  - **3:19 Bowyer emendation** (*Enōch kai* for *en hō kai*): almost universally rejected; no MS
    evidence — Jobes agrees, while still using Enoch tradition as background.
- **Language character of the Greek (syntax excursus).** Jobes's quantitative syntax criticism
  (adapted from R. A. Martin's criteria; earlier form *BBR* 13.2 [2003]) finds **Semitic bilingual
  interference** on multiple criteria (prepositions, genitive personal pronouns, attributive-adjective
  position, dative with *en*) even with LXX quotations removed. With quotes included, S-number
  **0.28**. Profile: more Semitic than Polybius or Josephus's mastery; comparable to 1 Thessalonians
  (S-number 0.37); less "native-proficient" than Hebrews 5–9. Major conclusion: author whose **first
  language was not Greek** — undercuts "Greek too good for a Semitic speaker," without by itself
  proving Petrine authorship or naming an amanuensis.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

How 1 Peter is read in this wiki's principal commentary (Jobes), and how Jobes says *Peter* reads
Scripture:

1. **LXX-matrix method (Jobes's second stated contribution).** "It was the ancient Greek translation
   of the Old Testament (OT) that formed the scriptural context in which Peter wrote. Peter does not
   proof-text… but applies the context of the passage as it occurs in the LXX." Inventory examples
   from the re-ingest: Lev 19:2 exact at 1:16; Isa 40:6–8 with large co-text at 1:24–25; Isa 52:3 +
   Ps 34 (33 LXX) for redemption; Ps 33 LXX taste (2:3) and ethics (3:10–12); stone complex Isa 28 /
   Ps 118 / Isa 8 at 2:6–8; Exod 19 / Isa 43 / Hos 2 at 2:9–10; Isa 8:12–13 at 3:14–15 (Christ
   identified with Yahweh of the quotation); Isa 53 creative interweave at 2:21–25; Isa 11:2 at 4:14.
2. **Peter's own OT hermeneutic (1:10–12).** Same Spirit of Christ revealed to prophets the
   sufferings-then-glories of Messiah; prophets ministered "not for themselves but for you"; unity of
   OT prophecy and gospel grounds direct ethical application of OT to Christian readers without
   separate Jewish/Gentile address. Differentiated Torah application: **continuity of authority and
   principle** (be holy as God is holy) with **discontinuity of Holiness-Code particulars**
   (post-resurrection, post-Pentecost, different place).
3. **Diaspora-letter form.** Jewish genre of spiritual authority writing to scattered people under
   uncongenial society (Ep. Jer.; 2 Macc 1; 2 Bar. 78–87; Nestle parallel to Gamaliel II Diaspora
   letters' "peace multiplied"). Greeting and closing frame personal correspondence.
4. **Differentiated Christ-and-culture engagement (Volf via Jobes).** Neither Elliott's sectarian
   resistance nor Balch's straightforward accommodation, but piece-meal accommodating / rejecting /
   subverting / transforming. Household code is the prime example. See
   [[1-peter-christ-and-culture|Christ and Culture in 1 Peter]].
5. **BECNT evangelical-exegetical method.** Series aims (Yarbrough/Stein): scholarly depth with
   pastoral exposition; paragraph as thought unit; critical research without abandoning long-standing
   views for fashion; trustworthiness and essential unity of Scripture. Jobes: structure follows
   biblical text; three distinct contributions (colonization theory; LXX matrix; bilingual syntax).

## Canon and Reception

- **Canon status.** Universally received as NT Scripture among the major Christian traditions tracked
  in this wiki (`canon_scope.canonical_for`: catholicism, eastern-orthodoxy, protestantism). No
  significant modern confessional rejection.
- **Luther (via Jobes).** Ranks 1 Peter among the "noblest books in the New Testament," a "paragon of
  excellence" with Romans and John — containing all necessary for a Christian to know.
- **Early and modern reception notes Jobes records.** Ancient exegetes mostly took the audience as
  Jewish converts (exceptions: Augustine, Jerome); Calvin continued the Jewish-convert tradition.
  Modern consensus: Gentile majority. Rejection of Petrine authorship is relatively recent
  (19th/early 20th c.: von Soden, Gunkel, Knopf, Loisy, Windisch, Renan, Harnack); earlier tradition
  uncontested; continued support from Weiss, Zahn, Lightfoot, Hort, and many moderns favoring
  amanuensis under Peter's direction. Oecumenius (via Selwyn): teaching letter to strengthen
  converts. Global pastoral reception Jobes notes: popular under persecution contexts (Halle
  post-WWII; former Yugoslavia; Muslim Indonesia; Bantu [Wendland]; US sanctuary movement [Elliott]).
- **Royal priesthood reception.** Reformation "priesthood of all believers" sometimes over-read as
  clerical-authority leveling; Jobes: Peter's 2:9 thought is collective obedience/holiness and
  mediating God-in-Christ to the nations, not ecclesiastical power-sharing.

## Scholarship and Historiography

### Authorship and date (positions as positions)

The letter "indisputably claims" to be from "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ" (1:1). **Modern
critical majority** (Achtemeier, Elliott, Goppelt, Boring, and many others): pseudonymous work by a
"Petrine group in Rome" c. AD 75–95, accurately representing Peter's thought. **Amanuensis under
Peter's direction** (Davids, Grudem, Gundry, Michaels with hesitation, Selwyn, and many others):
composition within Peter's lifetime with secretarial help. **Jobes:** case against Petrine
authorship "no longer appears… overwhelming"; constructive program (colonization theory + bilingual
syntax + LXX method + genre of personal correspondence) reopens early Petrine or at least
lifetime-linked authorship. Possible windows with Mark and Silvanus: late 40s/early 50s and early–mid
60s (Rome). Date: possibly early 50s, possibly mid-60s before Neronian martyrdom (~64–67); irenic
2:13–17 and optative "even if you should suffer" (3:14) easier **before** Nero's watershed.

**Four classic anti-Petrine arguments and Jobes's counters** (full debate:
[[1-peter-authorship|1 Peter: Authorship, Date, and the Colonization Theory]]):

1. **Greek too good** — undercut by syntax interference (non-native L2 profile); rhetorical polish
   real but not Hebrews-level; formal training unproven.
2. **Persecution / Sitz** — nature of hostility undatable (sporadic verbal/social, not Trajanic
   policy); "fiery ordeal" not Nero's fires.
3. **Church structure** — elders and *episkopountes* as activity, not 2nd-c. monarchical bishop;
   evidence once used for late date "points in the opposite direction."
4. **Pauline dependence** — affinities via shared (esp. Roman) tradition, not literary dependence;
   if so Pauline and pseudonymous, why attribute to Peter?

Further: **Babylon** not necessarily post-70 apocalyptic code; **pseudonymity** accepted in
wisdom/apocalyptic but not clearly for *personal correspondence* (3 Corinthians censure; Galen;
Metzger on forgery). Jobes admits colonization evidence is "meager" and the letter "makes no direct
reference" to deportation — possibilities and probabilities, offered as more specific than pure
*in situ* conversion assumptions.

### Recipients: colonization theory (Jobes's distinctive historical proposal)

Shared false assumption (Jobes): readers were native Asia Minor converts. Alternative: converted
elsewhere (probably Rome), associated with Peter, displaced as *peregrini*/*parepidēmoi* into Claudian
colonies in **all five** regions named in 1:1 (Andrapa/Neoclaudiopolis; Claudiconium; Archelais;
Claudiolaodicea; Bithynium-Claudiopolis, etc.). Claudius expulsion of Jews over "Chrestus"
(Suetonius; Acts 18:2) could have included Christians still viewed as a Jewish sect. Theory explains
exile framing, LXX literacy of Gentile readers, Greek as common language, mild early persecution,
lifestyle evangelism vs overt proselytizing that may have provoked Roman expulsion — while Jobes
admits lack of direct textual reference as greatest weakness.

### Genre, unity, sources (scholarly history)

Older German source criticism bipartitioned at 4:11 / intensified persecution (Streeter; Preisker /
Perdelwitz baptismal homily; Cross paschal liturgy). Current consensus (Jobes concurs): **original
unity**. Primary source LXX; more oral than written Christian sources (Schutter). Preexisting
hymn/creed fragments unprovable (Elliott: no convincing fixed creed for 3:18–22).

## Key interpretive cruxes

**1. Christ and the "spirits in prison"; baptism (3:18–22).** The most disputed passage (Luther: "I
still do not know for sure what the apostle meant"). Jobes follows the post-Dalton (1965) majority
line: three participles (put to death — made alive — *went*) trace death → resurrection →
**ascension**; resumptive *poreutheis* at 3:22 shows the "going and proclaiming" of 3:19 is Christ's
*ascension victory proclamation*, not *descensus ad inferos*. Verb is *poreuomai* not *katabainō*; no
Hades/Tartarus/Sheol; *phylakē* not NT term for place of human dead. "Spirits" (*pneumata*) =
**fallen angels / demonic spirits** of the Noah-generation tradition (1 Enoch Watchers / Gen 6), not
unqualified deceased human souls (*psychē*). Message = victory/condemnation confirming "day of the
great conclusion," not a postmortem gospel offer (*euangelizomai* not used). Noah–baptism typology
(3:20–21): baptism "now saves" **through the resurrection**, not as external washing of moral filth
but as **pledge** (*eperōtēma*) of a good conscience to God. Jobes rejects: (a) descensus +
postmortem conversion (Clement, Origen, Goppelt); (b) Augustine/Grudem "Christ preached through
Noah"; (c) Bowyer *Enōch* emendation. Pastoral function: evil powers behind hostility already subject
(3:22). See [[spirits-in-prison-1-peter-3|Christ and the Spirits in Prison]].

**2. "The gospel preached to the dead" (4:6).** Jobes rejects any identity with 3:19 and any
postmortem-second-chance reading. Differences: Christ is *content* of preaching (passive
*euangelizomai*) not the proclaimer (*kēryssō*); audience *nekrois* ≠ *pneumata*; 4:6 is one group
already judged *sarki* by humans and alive *pneumati* by God. "The dead" = those in 4:5's merism who
heard the gospel while alive but are *now dead*. Point: physical death exempts no one from judgment
and nullifies neither gospel promises nor warnings. Most contemporary interpreters (Achtemeier,
Dalton, Davids, Elliott, Michaels, etc.) no longer associate 4:6 with 3:19. See
[[spirits-in-prison-1-peter-3|the controversy page]].

**3. The household code (2:13–3:7).** Submission "to every human creation/institution" (2:13), then
slaves (2:18–25), wives (3:1–6), husbands (3:7). Jobes within *Christ and culture*: neither sectarian
withdrawal (Elliott) nor straightforward assimilation (Balch) but **differentiated, selective**
engagement (Volf) / "selective acculturation" (Balch's later phrase) — upholds *and* subverts. Slaves
are *paradigmatic for all Christians* as slaves of God; Jesus as Isa 53 Servant is the only NT
passage that identifies Jesus with the Suffering Servant this explicitly and extensively. Peter
directly addresses slaves and wives as free moral agents (unprecedented vs Aristotle/Plutarch). On
wives: suffering in view is **primarily verbal abuse and loss of social standing**, *not* physical
domestic violence — "nothing in this passage… would either sanction the abuse of wives or suggest
that women should continue to submit themselves to that kind of treatment"; 3:7 (husbands "according
to knowledge"; coheirs; "weaker vessel" physical/social) "delicately prohibits domestic violence in
the Christian household." Positive 2:13–17 toward emperor/governors supports earlier dating. See
[[1-peter-christ-and-culture|Christ and Culture in 1 Peter]].

**4. "Babylon" (5:13) and Silvanus (5:12).** Modern consensus: "Babylon" **refers** to Rome; Jobes:
*not* as Revelation's apocalyptic evil city (1 Peter is not apocalyptic; positive toward the state).
Functions as closing **inclusio** with Diaspora (1:1); shared exile status; "the only thing wrong with
Babylon is that it is not home" (Michaels via Jobes). Kelly: may not designate specific locality at
all; Jobes: Rome as composition place "leans much more heavily on tradition than on exegesis." "She
… chosen with you": majority = local Christian community (preferred elision *adelphotēs* from 5:9
over unused *ekklēsia*); not Peter's wife (against Bigg); Mark = John Mark, traditional interpreter.
**Silvanus:** *dia Silouanou … egrapsa* more likely **courier** idiom (papyri; Ignatius; Acts 15:23)
than amanuensis; amanuensis still possible "on other grounds"; traditional Silvanus-as-scribe solution
was driven by the Greek-quality assumption the syntax excursus reopens. Probably Paul's companion
Silas (Latinized form).

**5. Pure spiritual milk (*logikon gala*, 2:2).** Modern near-consensus: milk = word of God
(preaching/Scripture). **Jobes (with Calvin, Hort, Selwyn, Beare, Michaels):** rejects narrow
"word-milk"; *logikos* = true to the nature of the new eschatological reality established by the
resurrection; referent = **the Lord / divine grace** that sustains new birth (Ps 33:9 LXX "taste that
the Lord is good" as immediate control, not only 1:23–25). Craving mode = putting off vices (2:1);
*adolon* not primarily "inerrant Scripture" slogan. Hearing the word remains vital, but milk is not
limited to more sermons.

**6. "Appointed" to stumble (2:8).** Jobes follows a Michaels-type reading: one divine appointment of
Christ as stone with twofold result (honor for believers; stumbling for rejecters). Election can be
argued from 1 Peter; **reprobation is not** addressed here as a corollary. Disobedience is not forever
exclusion — lifestyle evangelism still aims to win unbelievers (2:12; 3:1).

## From Jobes BECNT re-ingest (2026-07-18)

Fidelity notes from the full-commentary body re-ingest (digests R1–R8); all claims remain attributed to
Jobes. Source page: [[jobes-1-peter-becnt|Jobes, *1 Peter* (BECNT)]].

- **Discourse structure.** Jobes's five-part outline (I greeting → II reassurance 1:3–2:10 → III
  godly lives 2:11–4:11 → IV consolation 4:12–5:11 → V closing 5:12–14) now frames this page's
  Textual Overview; 1:3–12 is one long Greek sentence and rhetorical exordium without explicit
  imperatives; 1:13–2:3 is four aorist imperatives (hope, holiness, love, crave milk).
- **Pure spiritual milk (2:2).** Jobes's minority/critical position against modern "milk = word of
  God" consensus: *logikon gala* = sustenance true to new-creation reality; crave the Lord/grace
  (Ps 33 LXX matrix), mode of craving = putting off community-destroying vices.
- **Silvanus as courier preference.** *dia Silouanou … egrapsa* read primarily as courier idiom;
  amanuensis not established by 5:12 alone and must be decided on other grounds; syntax excursus
  reopens the Greek-quality assumptions that traditionally drove the Silvanus-as-scribe solution.
- **Syntax undercutting the Greek-quality objection.** Quantitative profile (Martin criteria / Jobes
  S-number): Semitic interference in prepositions, genitive pronouns, attributive adjectives, dative
  with *en*; non-native Greek author; does **not** by itself prove Peter wrote the letter or that
  Silvanus/1 Thessalonians share an author.
- **Colonization theory** retained with Jobes's own admitted weakness (no direct textual reference).
- **Isa 53 Servant Christology** as Peter's distinctive NT contribution (only explicit extensive
  passion-Servant identification); order follows passion sequence.
- **3:18–22 / 4:6** upgraded with Dalton-line ascension-proclamation detail, Enoch-Noah / Asia Minor
  Noah background, baptism-as-pledge, and explicit severance of 4:6 from 3:19.
- **Household code:** uphold-and-subvert; verbal-abuse scope for wives' suffering; 3:7 as delicate
  prohibition of domestic violence — not a counsel to remain under physical abuse.

## Relation to Other Books

- **Paul (esp. Romans, Ephesians)** — real affinities, but Jobes (with most) attributes them to
  shared early Christian tradition (perhaps Roman), not literary dependence; 1 Peter is no
  "deutero-Pauline" derivative.
- **Isaiah 53** — the substructure of Peter's Christology (2:21–25). See [[servant-songs|Servant
  Songs]].
- **The Johannine Epistles / Revelation** — also addressed to Asia Minor late in the century, but
  with very different (heresy-conscious, anti-Roman) tone — a foil that, for Jobes, points to 1
  Peter's earlier date.
- **2 Peter** — shares the apostolic name but is widely held to be by a different hand; not covered
  here.

## Commentators

- [[karen-jobes|Karen H. Jobes]] — BECNT (Baker Academic, 2005); source page
  [[jobes-1-peter-becnt|Jobes, *1 Peter* BECNT]]; colonization theory; LXX-as-matrix; syntax
  criticism / bilingual interference; favors reconsideration of Petrine (amanuensis) authorship;
  Westmont College; Septuagint specialist
- J. Ramsey Michaels (WBC, 1988) — major critical-evangelical commentary; cautious on authorship
- Paul J. Achtemeier (Hermeneia, 1996) — the standard critical commentary; pseudonymous; persecution
  as unofficial social hostility; Jobes's dialogue partner on Greek quality and "no Semitic influence"
- John H. Elliott (AB, 2000; *A Home for the Homeless*, 1981) — sociological reading; *paroikoi* as
  literal disenfranchised rural poor; sectarian-resistance model (Jobes rejects poverty reconstruction
  partly via wives' adornment and public benefaction)
- David L. Balch (*Let Wives Be Submissive*, 1981) — acculturation/apologetic reading of the
  household code (the foil to Elliott); later "selective acculturation" language Jobes cites with Volf
- Leonhard Goppelt (Eng. 1993) — defends a *descensus* / postmortem reading of 3:19 and 4:6 (Jobes's
  main dialogue partner against on those cruxes)
- Wayne Grudem (TNTC, 1988) — "Christ preached through Noah" reading of 3:19
- Edward G. Selwyn (1946/1958); Peter H. Davids (NICNT, 1990); I. Howard Marshall (1991)
