The **Brahma Sutras** (Vedanta Sutras) are the third of the **prasthana-trayi** and the **systematic**
foundation of [[hinduism|Vedanta]]: ~555 terse aphorisms (*sutras*) attributed to **Badarayana** that
organize and reconcile the teachings of the [[upanishads|Upanishads]] into a coherent doctrine of
[[brahman|Brahman]].

## Why It Is the Battleground of Vedanta

The sutras are so compressed as to be **unintelligible without a commentary** — and that is exactly why
they became the decisive text. To *found a school of Vedanta is to write a bhashya (commentary) on the
Brahma Sutras.* The three classical commentaries on this one text are the three schools:

- [[shankara|Shankara]]'s *Sharira-bhashya* → [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] (non-dualism)
- [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]]'s *Sri-bhashya* → [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] (qualified
  non-dualism)
- [[madhva|Madhva]]'s commentary → [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] (dualism)

The very first sutras set the stage: *athato brahma jijnasa* ("now, therefore, the inquiry into
Brahman") and *janmady asya yatah* ("[Brahman is] that from which the origin etc. of this [world
proceed]"). Each commentator extracts a different metaphysics from the same words — the wiki's prime
exhibit of [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|one canon, irreconcilable readings]].
