**Ovid's *Fasti*** is a poem on the **Roman religious calendar** — six surviving books, one per month
(January–June) — explaining the **festivals, rites, and the gods** honored on each day. It is the **single
best literary source** on the practice of [[roman-religion|Roman religion]].

## Content

For each festival Ovid gives its myth, its ritual, and the origin of its customs — the rites of
[[janus|Janus]] (January), the Lupercalia and the Parentalia (February, honoring the dead and the
[[lares-and-penates|Lares]]), the Megalensia of [[magna-mater-cybele|Cybele]], the cult of [[vesta|Vesta]],
the festivals of [[ares|Mars]], [[aphrodite|Venus]], and [[fortuna|Fortuna]], and much else. Where
[[ovid-metamorphoses|the *Metamorphoses*]] gives the myths, the *Fasti* gives the **religion as practiced**.

## Significance

An indispensable window onto Roman **orthopraxy** — the calendar, the priesthoods, the household and state
rites — and a major source for the wiki's [[roman-state-cult|state-cult]] and [[roman-household-religion|
household-religion]] pages.
