# The Incoherence of the Philosophers (Tahafut al-Falasifa)

The **Tahafut al-Falasifa** ("The Incoherence / Precipitance of the Philosophers") is the
celebrated polemical treatise by **[[al-ghazali|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]]** (d. 1111), composed
in 1095, in which he subjects the metaphysics of the Islamic Aristotelian–Neoplatonist
philosophers (*[[falsafa|falasifa]]*) — above all **[[avicenna|Ibn Sina / Avicenna]]** and
**[[al-farabi|al-Farabi]]** — to a systematic internal critique. Ghazali's aim is not to
defend [[kalam|Ash'arite theology]] with its own proofs but to show, on the philosophers'
*own* logical and demonstrative standards, that their metaphysical conclusions are not
demonstratively proven. The companion volume *Maqasid al-Falasifa* ("The Aims of the
Philosophers") expounds the doctrines neutrally; the *Tahafut* then demolishes them.

Edition ingested: the parallel English–Arabic text translated, introduced, and annotated by
**Michael E. Marmura** (2nd ed., Brigham Young University Press, 2000).

## Structure: Twenty Discussions

The work refutes twenty philosophical theses across twenty *masa'il* ("Discussions"):
sixteen on metaphysics (*ilahiyyat*) and four on natural science (*tabi'iyyat*). The pivotal
ones are the **First** (the world's past eternity), the **Seventeenth** (causality and
miracles — Ghazali's critique of necessary causal connection), and the **Twentieth** (bodily
resurrection).

## The Three Charges of Unbelief (takfir)

In the Conclusion, Ghazali declares the philosophers **infidels (*kafir*)** on exactly three
doctrines, on which their teaching "does not agree with Islam in any respect":

1. the **past eternity of the world** (and that all substances are pre-eternal);
2. that **God's knowledge does not encompass temporal particulars**;
3. the **denial of bodily resurrection**.

On all other contested points (e.g. the divine attributes, causal necessity) he holds their
errors to be *heretical innovation* (*bid'a*) shared with one or another Islamic sect (notably
the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]]), not outright unbelief. This selective *[[takfir|takfir]]* is the
single most consequential verdict in the book. See [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]] and
[[averroes|Averroes]]'s rejoinder, the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahafut al-Tahafut]]*.

## Hermeneutical Framing

The *Tahafut* is a work of **dialectical theology (*[[kalam|kalam]]*)** deploying the
philosophers' own **Aristotelian logic / demonstration (*burhan*)** against them — Ghazali
insisting logic is a neutral instrument, not the property of the *falasifa*. The Zahir/Batin
axis appears in his charge that the philosophers reduce scriptural promises (resurrection,
paradise) to *batin* allegory for the masses. Track the **occasionalist** reading of causation
(Discussion 17) as the hermeneutical key to miracle and prophetic veracity.

## Key Interpretive Cruxes

- **Discussion 1** — whether a temporal world can proceed from an eternal God; the philosophers'
  "why not sooner?" argument and Ghazali's reply via divine will. See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
- **Discussion 17** — whether the connection between cause and effect is *necessary*; Ghazali's
  famous denial ("the burning of cotton at contact with fire"), grounding [[occasionalism]] and
  the possibility of [[prophetic-miracle|miracles]].
- **Discussion 20** — bodily vs. purely spiritual resurrection. See [[bodily-resurrection]].

## Canon and Reception

The *Tahafut* is not scripture but an **authoritative theological classic** for
[[sunni-islam|Sunni]] (especially [[asharism|Ash'arite]]) orthodoxy. It was answered point by
point by **[[averroes|Ibn Rushd / Averroes]]** in the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahafut
al-Tahafut]]* ("The Incoherence of the Incoherence"), defending the *[[falsafa]]*. The exchange
became the defining set-piece of the medieval reason-versus-revelation debate in Islam.
