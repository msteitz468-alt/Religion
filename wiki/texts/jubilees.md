The **Book of Jubilees** is a 2nd-century BCE retelling of [[genesis|Genesis]] and the first part of
[[exodus|Exodus]] — "Little Genesis" — presented as a revelation given to [[moses|Moses]] on Sinai by the
"Angel of the Presence." It rewrites the patriarchal narratives to ground later Jewish law and a
distinctive calendar in primordial history.

## Distinctive Features

- **The 364-day solar calendar** — Jubilees insists on a 364-day year (so festivals always fall on the
  same weekday), polemicizing against the lunar calendar — the same calendar that divided the
  [[essenes|Essene]]/Qumran community from the Jerusalem Temple.
- **Chronology by "jubilees"** — history is periodized into 49-year units, projecting the law backward:
  the patriarchs already keep the Sabbath, festivals, and purity rules.
- **Halakhic stringency and angelology** — expanded angels and demons (Mastema), and a hard line on
  intermarriage and Gentile separation.

## Canon Scope

Like [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]], Jubilees is **canonical only in the Ethiopian churches**
(`canonical_for: [ethiopian-orthodoxy]`), was **authoritative at [[essenes|Qumran]]** (Hebrew copies
found there), and is **rejected** by the rabbinic, Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant canons — a clear
pseudepigraphal canon-split parallel to 1 Enoch.

## Significance

Jubilees is a prime exhibit of "**rewritten Bible**," a major Second Temple genre, and a key source for
the Qumran community's calendar and halakha — back-filling the [[essenes|Essenes]] sect's expansive-canon
profile.
