The **Enuma Elish** ("When on High," its opening words) is the **Babylonian epic of creation** — seven
clay tablets recounting how the world was made and how **[[marduk|Marduk]]** became king of the gods. It is
the central theological text of [[babylonian-religion|Babylonian religion]] and a key comparandum for
[[genesis|Genesis 1]] (see [[genesis-ancient-near-east|the ANE parallels]]).

## The Story (in detail)

**The primeval waters.** Before sky or earth, there were only two primordial beings whose waters mingled:
**[[apsu|Apsu]]** (the sweet fresh water) and **[[tiamat|Tiamat]]** (the salt sea, a chaos-dragon). From them
the younger gods were born — generation upon generation, noisy and restless.

**The first murder.** The clamor of the young gods so disturbed **Apsu** that he plotted to **destroy them**
all so he could rest. Warned of this, the clever god **[[enki|Ea (Enki)]]** cast a spell, **killed Apsu**, and
built his dwelling on the slain god's body. There Ea's son **[[marduk|Marduk]]** was born, mightiest of the
gods.

**Tiamat's war.** Enraged at Apsu's death (and goaded by the other gods), **[[tiamat|Tiamat]]** resolved on
revenge. She spawned an **army of eleven monsters** — serpents, dragons, scorpion-men — and set her new
consort **Kingu** at their head, giving him the **Tablet of Destinies**. The terrified gods could find no
champion — until young **Marduk** agreed to fight, on one condition: that if he won, he be made **supreme
king of the gods**, his word law. They agreed.

**The battle and the making of the world.** Armed with winds, a bow, and the storm, Marduk faced Tiamat in
single combat. He **caught her in his net**, drove the winds into her open mouth so she could not close it,
and **shot an arrow that split her heart**. Then he **split her vast corpse in two "like a shellfish"** —
making the **sky** from one half and the **earth** from the other, fixing the stars, the moon, and the sun in
their courses, and ordering the calendar.

**Humanity and Babylon.** To free the gods from labor, Marduk had **Kingu executed**, and from his **blood**
Ea **fashioned humankind** — created to **serve the gods** and do their work. The grateful gods built Marduk
his city and temple, **Babylon**, and proclaimed his **fifty names**, the sum of all divine powers.

## Significance

The Enuma Elish presents creation as the **victory of order (Marduk) over watery chaos (Tiamat)** and
humanity as the gods' **servant-laborers** — both motifs (the chaos-waters, the divine ordering of sky and
luminaries, the close-of-creation rest) widely compared with [[genesis|Genesis 1]], though the theology is
sharply different. Recited at the Babylonian **New Year (Akitu)** to re-found cosmic order.
