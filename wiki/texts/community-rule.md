# Community Rule (Serekh ha-Yahad)
**Sigla**: 1QS; also 4Q255–64 (Serekh copies a–j), 5Q11

The Serekh ha-Yahad ("Rule of the Community") is the foundational constitutional document of the Qumran sect, governing the life of the **yahad** — the settled community at Qumran. Found as a complete scroll in Cave 1 in 1947, it is one of the seven original Dead Sea Scrolls. Multiple recensions from Cave 4 preserve an earlier form in which the "sons of Zadok" do not yet appear as the supreme authority, suggesting the text developed over time.

## Canon and Reception
The Community Rule is a purely **sectarian text** — it has authority within the Essene yahad alone. It was not known to rabbinic Judaism, early Christianity, or any other tradition until the 1947 discovery. It is not canonical for any extant community. Its significance is entirely historical and comparative.

## Structure and Contents

### Covenant Renewal Ceremony (Cols. I–III)
The scroll opens with the annual covenant renewal rite. The Levites recite Israel's sins; the congregation confesses and receives blessing (Sons of Light) or curse (Sons of Darkness). Blessings and curses follow the Deuteronomic covenant pattern. The ceremony is performed "as long as Belial rules."

### Two Spirits Doctrine (Col. III–IV)
The most famous theological passage: God "created man to govern the world, and appointed for him two spirits in which to walk until the time of His visitation: the spirits of truth and of falsehood." Every human being is determined from creation to belong to the dominion of the Prince of Light or the Angel of Darkness. Fate is fixed; the proportions of light and darkness in an individual can vary but the fundamental allegiance is predestined. At the end of days God will destroy the spirit of falsehood forever and purify the Sons of Light.

Key terms:
- **Prince of Light / Angel of Truth** — head of the Sons of Light
- **Angel of Darkness** — head of the Sons of Darkness; causes even Sons of Light to stumble
- **Spirit of Truth** — operates in Sons of Light: humility, patience, wisdom, love, zeal for law
- **Spirit of Falsehood** — operates in Sons of Darkness: greed, wickedness, deceit, pride

This is the earliest full statement of the **Two Spirits doctrine** — a thoroughgoing predestinarian dualism within Jewish monotheism.

### Community Organization (Cols. V–VII)
Entry procedures for new members:
1. Voluntary profession of commitment to the Community Rule
2. First year: examination; may not touch communal food or property
3. Second year: examination by the Many; may touch communal food but not communal drink
4. Third year: full membership; property absorbed into the common fund

Annual examination: every member is ranked annually "according to his understanding and his deeds."

**The Many** (*ha-Rabbim*): the general assembly with supreme authority in community matters. The **Overseer/Guardian** (*Mebaqqer*) and the **Priests** (sons of Zadok, in the revised version) function as executive authority.

**Penal Code** (Col. VII): punishments for offenses — reduction in food ration, exclusion from communal meals, permanent expulsion for apostasy. Offenses include: speaking the Name carelessly; lying about property; insulting a colleague; spitting in assembly; laughing inappropriately.

### Council of the Community (Cols. VIII–IX)
A **Council of twelve men and three priests** is mandated as the inner core — they shall "preserve faith in the Land" and "make atonement for sin by the practice of justice and by suffering the sorrows of affliction." Their existence constitutes the community as a **"Sanctuary of Men"** (*miqdash Adam*): the community itself replaces the defiled Jerusalem Temple; prayer and Torah study replace sacrifices.

This council is described as "a Most Holy Dwelling for Aaron" and "a House of Truth in Israel." They are "an agreeable offering" and a "sweet fragrance" — explicitly sacrificial language applied to communal life and obedience.

### The Hymn of the Maskil (Cols. X–XI)
A concluding hymn praising God's predestinarian sovereignty: "From the source of His righteousness is my justification… He will draw me near by His grace… No flesh can be justified." God's righteousness provides atonement; the Teacher/Maskil counts on divine grace rather than human merit.

## Hermeneutical Method
The Community Rule operates primarily with **peshat**-style legal application of biblical norms, but within a framework shaped by **pesher** theology — the community understands itself as the eschatological remnant living in the prophetically foretold age of testing. The Two Spirits doctrine draws on Qumran cosmology rather than any single biblical proof-text.

## Key Theological Themes
- **Predestination**: Human destiny is fixed by divine choice before creation; the community does not convert sinners but rather identifies those God has already chosen
- **Communal holiness**: The community as living temple replaces sacrifice; the collective is the sacred space
- **Solar calendar**: Implied throughout; the covenant year organizes communal life
- **Eschatological urgency**: The community exists in the era of Belial's dominion; they await the Final Visitation

## Relationship to Other Texts
- **Damascus Document (CD)**: The companion text governing camp communities outside Qumran; shares legal material but addresses married members
- **1QSa (Messianic Rule)**: Annex describing life of the congregation at the end of days, including the messianic banquet
- **1QSb (Blessings)**: Annex containing ritual blessings for the Two Messiahs
- **4Q174 (Florilegium)**: Develops the "Sanctuary of Men" concept explicitly from 2 Samuel 7:10
- **4Q246**: The predestinarian ethos of the Serekh contrasts with the apocalyptic messianism of the "Son of God" fragment

## Cave 4 Textual Variants (VanderKam)
VanderKam (2010) notes that **4Q259 (copy e)** of the Community Rule — one of the Cave 4 recensions — differs significantly from the Cave 1 copy (1QS):
- **Lacks the Two Spirits section** (1QS III–IV), suggesting this doctrinally foundational passage was an insertion into the main text rather than an original element
- **Lacks the Two Messiahs formula** ("until the coming of the Prophet and the Messiahs of Aaron and Israel," 1QS 9.11), replaced by different content

These variants confirm that the Community Rule was a **living document** revised over the community's history. The theological weight placed on 1QS 9.11 for the [[two-messiahs|Two Messiahs]] doctrine is qualified by this evidence: the formula does not appear in all versions of the text.
