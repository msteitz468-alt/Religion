# The Dawn-Breakers (Táríkh-i-Nabíl)

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 10341–10365.*

## Overview

An early narrative of Bábí history, composed by [[mulla-husayn|Nabíl-i-A'ẓam]] (Muḥammad-i-Zarandí) in AH 1305 / 1887–8. The Persian original remains in manuscript only. [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]'s English translation was published in 1932 under the title *The Dawn-Breakers*, with an introduction by [[george-townshend|George Townshend]] (lines 10343–10364).

## Historical value

Although written long after the events described, the work relies heavily on the memoirs of surviving early Bábís (including the author himself), making it an important source for early Bábí history (lines 10347–10351).

## Impact

The English translation had an enormous impact on Western Bahá'ís' understanding of their religion, firmly linking it to Bábism and to the heroic acts and martyrdoms of the Bábís (lines 10353–10356). Shoghi Effendi specifically intended the book to inspire readers to greater dedication and self-sacrifice (lines 10357–10358).

## Canon and Reception

Not scripture in the strict sense, but authoritative historical source recommended by both Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice for deepening (line 10501–10503). Shoghi Effendi directed Bahá'ís to study it alongside the Quran as part of their religious education.
