## Overview

The *Acaranga* (Sanskrit: "Behaviour," the full title is *Acaranga Sutra*) is the **first of the twelve *angas*** (limbs) of the [[jain-agamas|Svetambara canon]] and is among the oldest surviving texts of the Jain tradition. Its very position as the first *anga* reflects its fundamental status for the tradition.

## Contents

The *Acaranga* contains:
- **Doctrinal statements** about the nature of reality, concerning particularly the soul and action
- **A biography of [[mahavira]]** — one of the earliest biographical accounts of the last fordmaker
- **Injunctions about monastic discipline**

The text is particularly important as a witness to the question of early Jain monastic practice regarding nudity. Key passages cited in Dundas's account:

- ***AS* 1.6.2.3**: Describes lack of clothes "as being in full conformity with Jain doctrine" (line 65–66).
- ***AS* 1.6.3.1–2**: Refers to the difficulties experienced by the naked monk and to the fact that he does not need to beg for or repair clothes (lines 66–68).
- ***AS* 1.8.5**: Advises the monk only to "restrict himself in the wearing of clothes through not possessing too many garments and to be either very lightly clad or completely naked during summer as a form of penance" (lines 69–73).

These passages show "options about the wearing of clothes by monks" existed at an early stage, with some passages endorsing naked asceticism as the norm and others permitting restricted clothing. This textual ambiguity became a central point in the [[svetambara]]–[[digambara]] dispute over dress.

## Textual Character

The *Acaranga* is textually complex. Walther Schubring demonstrated that "the ancient first book of the first limb of the canon, the *Acaranga*, was in origin a kind of mosaic, often consisting of mere fragments of verses and disjointed bits of prose, which had been transformed into, if not a seamless whole, at least a continuum" (lines 1135–1139). The medieval commentarial tradition completely failed to grasp this composite origin, which suggests the transformation occurred at one of the scriptural recitations.

A chapter is also lost: "the seventh chapter of the first book of the *Acaranga*" is "last heard of in the hagiography of the teacher Vajra being used as a magic spell to gain the power of flight (*AvNiry* 769)" (lines 1143–1145).

## Canon and Reception

The *Acaranga* is the foundational limb of the [[jain-agamas|Svetambara canon]], canonical for all [[svetambara|Svetambara]] sub-sects (Murtipujaka, [[sthanakvasi]], and Terapanthi). The [[digambara|Digambaras]] reject the Svetambara agama as a whole and do not regard the *Acaranga* as authoritative, though their abstract enumeration of the *anga* texts is identical to the Svetambara list (the Digambaras claim all these texts no longer survive in authentic form).

The Svetambara canonical commentator Silanka wrote on the *Acaranga* and is cited by Dundas on the relationship between knowledge and behaviour (*AS* 1.9.4.17).
