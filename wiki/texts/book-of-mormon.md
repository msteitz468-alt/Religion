The **Book of Mormon** is the founding scripture of the [[latter-day-saints|Latter-day Saint movement]],
published by **[[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]]** in 1830 and subtitled "Another Testament of Jesus Christ."
It is the wiki's **headline `canon_scope` case** — the text whose split standing motivated the whole
structured-canon design.

## The Canon-Scope Stress Test

One book, **three different standings** plus a narrative claim that resists the buckets:

- **`canonical_for: [latter-day-saints]`** — full scripture, one of the four "standard works," alongside
  the Bible, the [[doctrine-and-covenants|D&C]], and the [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great Price]].
- **`deuterocanonical_for: [community-of-christ]`** — the Community of Christ (formerly RLDS) and some
  other Restoration branches use it but grade it as secondary/optional rather than co-equal with the Bible.
- **`rejected_by: [catholicism, eastern-orthodoxy, protestantism]`** — not scripture for any historic
  Christian body.
- **The narrative claim** — the Book of Mormon **presents itself as a continuation of the biblical
  narrative** (the record of Israelite migrants to the Americas and a post-resurrection visit of Christ to
  them). Per CLAUDE.md, that *continuation* claim is a **reception fact recorded here in the body**, not a
  canon bucket — the buckets record only canonical *standing*, per community.

## Content

A religious history of peoples said to have migrated from Jerusalem to the Americas (c. 600 BCE), narrated
by the prophet-historian **Mormon** and his son **Moroni** on golden plates. Its theological climax is the
appearance of the resurrected **Jesus Christ** to these peoples (3 Nephi), re-teaching the Sermon on the
Mount. It is organized into "books" (1–2 Nephi, Jacob, Mosiah, Alma, Helaman, 3–4 Nephi, Mormon, Ether,
Moroni) and is heavily inter-textual with the KJV [[isaiah|Isaiah]].

## Historicity and Controversy

Its origin (divine translation vs. 19th-century composition) and its claims about pre-Columbian Israelite
peoples are rejected by mainstream archaeology, linguistics, and genetics, and accepted on faith by
believers — a live boundary in the "are the Latter-day Saints Christian?" debate (see
[[latter-day-saints|the sect page]]). Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
