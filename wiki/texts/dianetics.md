**Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health**, by **[[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]** (1950), is the
foundational text of [[scientology|Scientology]] — the book that launched the movement before the religion
itself was organized. Originally presented as a **secular self-help psychotherapy**, it became part of the
Scientology scriptural corpus ("Source").

## Teaching

Dianetics proposes that the mind has two parts: the rational **analytical mind** and the **reactive mind**,
which records **[[engram|engrams]]** — complete impressions of moments of pain, unconsciousness, or
emotional trauma (including prenatal experiences). These engrams, replayed unconsciously, are said to be the
hidden source of irrational behavior, psychosomatic illness, and unhappiness. The therapy of **[[auditing|
auditing]]** ("Dianetic reverie") returns a person to these incidents to discharge them; one who has cleared
the reactive mind becomes a **"Clear."** The book promises dramatic gains in intelligence, health, and
well-being.

## From Self-Help to Scripture

Published in 1950 to mass-market success (and immediate rejection by the medical and psychological
establishment), Dianetics was reframed within a few years as the entry level of a **religious** path:
Hubbard extended it "upward" with the doctrine of the immortal [[thetan|thetan]] and past lives, founding
the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] (1953–54). Within Scientology the book is scripture,
and its procedures must be applied as **"standard tech."**

## Reception

Mainstream science and medicine reject Dianetics' claims as unsupported; the secondary literature in the
wiki ([[reitman-inside-scientology|Reitman]], [[malko-the-now-religion|Malko]], [[shelton-a-to-xenu|
Shelton]]) treats its origins and reception critically. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction
Protocol).
