# Fusus al-Hikam (The Bezels of Wisdom)

The **Fusus al-Hikam** ("The Bezels of Wisdom," also rendered *The Seals of Wisdom*) is the
late, compact masterwork of **[[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]]** (d. 638/1240) — the single most influential
and most controversial book of speculative [[sufism|Sufism]]. Where his vast *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya*
("The Meccan Openings") is encyclopedic, the *Fusus* distills his metaphysics into twenty-seven dense
chapters, each a "bezel" (*fass* — the setting that holds the jewel of a signet ring) of a particular
**divine wisdom** as it is manifested in the "word" (*kalima*) of a particular **prophet**.

## A Book Received, Not Composed

Ibn Arabi opens by claiming he did not author the book but **received it from the Prophet Muhammad in
a vision** at Damascus, who handed it to him with the command to deliver it to mankind. The claim is
itself a doctrinal statement — the book presents itself as a disclosure (*kashf*) of the inner
realities of prophecy, not as personal speculation — and it set the terms of both the work's
authority among Sufis and its scandal among the jurists.

## Structure: Twenty-Seven Prophetic Bezels

Each chapter pairs a prophet with the facet of divine wisdom his existence most perfectly
displays — so that prophetic history becomes a gallery of the Names of God refracted through human
loci. The opening **bezel of [[adam|Adam]]** ("the wisdom of divinity") sets out the doctrine of the
**[[al-insan-al-kamil|Perfect Man]]**: Adam as God's vicegerent (*khalifa*), the polished mirror in
which the Real contemplates its own Names, the microcosm summing up the whole cosmos, by whom the
world is "guarded." The sequence runs through Seth, Noah, Idris (Enoch), Abraham, Isaac, Ishmael,
Joseph, Hud, Salih, Shu'ayb, Lot, Ezra ('Uzayr), Jesus, Solomon, [[david|David]], Jonah, Job, John
the Baptist (Yahya), Zachariah, Elias, Luqman, [[aaron|Aaron]], [[moses|Moses]], Khalid, and closes
with the **bezel of Muhammad** ("the wisdom of singularity"), the comprehensive locus uniting all the
preceding wisdoms — the **Muhammadan Reality** (*al-haqiqa al-Muhammadiyya*).

## Major Doctrines

- **[[wahdat-al-wujud|Wahdat al-wujud]] ("the unity of being")** — the cardinal Akbarian teaching
  (the term is later, systematized by Ibn Arabi's school): there is in reality only one Being, the
  Real (*al-Haqq*), of which all existents are self-disclosures (*tajalliyat*) and "fixed essences"
  (*a'yan thabita*); the cosmos is God's perpetual self-manifestation through his Names.
- **[[al-insan-al-kamil|The Perfect Man]] (*al-insan al-kamil*)** — the human being as the
  all-comprehensive mirror of the divine Names, the *isthmus* (*barzakh*) between the Real and
  creation, the reason the cosmos was made and is sustained; realized supremely in the prophets and
  in Muhammad.
- **The balance of [[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih and tashbih]]** — the **bezel of Noah** is the locus
  classicus: to assert only God's incomparable transcendence (*tanzih*, the way of [[mutazilism|the
  rationalists]]) or only his immanent likeness (*tashbih*) is one-sided; the gnostic holds both at
  once. This is the precise hinge on which Ibn Arabi diverges from the Mu'tazilite reading of the
  same God.
- **The God created in belief** — each knower worships the Lord formed in his own capacity to
  receive him; the perfect knower recognizes God in every form of belief while binding himself to
  none.

## Canon and Reception

The *Fusus* is **authoritative scripture-adjacent reading within [[sufism|Sufism]]**, especially the
**Akbarian school** that grew from Ibn Arabi's student and son-in-law **Sadr al-Din al-Qunawi** at
Konya, and it shaped Persian, Ottoman, and Indian mysticism for centuries (a vast commentary
tradition surrounds it). It is **not scripture** for any community — the Qur'an and hadith remain the
canon — and it was fiercely **contested by jurists and theologians**: the Hanbali Ibn Taymiyya and
many others condemned *wahdat al-wujud* as monism or veiled unbelief, and several chapters drew
specific fire (the suggestion that Pharaoh died a believer; the apparent dignifying of the idol-cults
named in Noah's bezel). Within the wiki the conflicting valuations — venerated Sufi classic vs.
condemned heresy — are preserved side by side and **not adjudicated** (Contradiction Protocol); see
[[sufism|Sufism]] and [[wahdat-al-wujud|wahdat al-wujud]].

## Interpretive Cruxes

- **The Noah bezel** (*tanzih* vs. *tashbih*) — the programmatic statement that pure transcendence is
  as much a "veil" as pure likeness.
- **The Adam bezel** — the Perfect Man / mirror doctrine, the seed of the whole book.
- **The Muhammad bezel** — the Muhammadan Reality as the comprehensive Name uniting all wisdoms.
- **The reception cruxes** — the faith of Pharaoh; whether *wahdat al-wujud* is pantheism,
  panentheism, or a non-ontological "unity of witnessing" (*wahdat al-shuhud*, the corrective offered
  later by the Naqshbandi Ahmad Sirhindi).
