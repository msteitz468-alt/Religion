The **Daodejing** ("Classic of the Way and Virtue") is the foundational text of [[daoism|Daoism]] and,
after the Bible, among the most translated books in the world — 81 short, gnomic, often paradoxical
chapters traditionally attributed to **[[laozi|Laozi]]**.

## The Opening and the Whole

Its first line sets the tone of the entire tradition: **"The Dao that can be told is not the eternal
Dao; the name that can be named is not the eternal name."** The text praises the **[[dao|Dao]]** as the
nameless source; commends **[[wu-wei|wu-wei]]** (non-action / effortless action), softness over
hardness, emptiness, the "uncarved block," and the lowly valley and water as images of power; and
applies all of this to **sage rulership** (govern by not-interfering).

## One Text, Two Transformative Readings

The Daodejing is the wiki's case of a single classic read in two registers — the "commentary-as-
transformation" framework:

- **[[wang-bi|Wang Bi]]** (226–249) reads it **philosophically** (*xuanxue*): the Dao as **wu**
  (non-being), the metaphysical root of all being.
- **[[heshang-gong|Heshang Gong]]** reads it for **longevity cultivation**: its images decode the
  adept's inner body and *qi*, a manual for [[religious-daoism|attaining immortality]].

See [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|the controversy]]. Its `canon_scope` is shared
(`canonical_for: [daoism]`); only the reading divides.
