The **Homeric Hymns** are a collection of 33 archaic Greek **hymns to individual gods**, in Homeric epic
style (hence the name; not actually by Homer). Each celebrates a deity's birth, powers, and chief myth, and
they are major sources for several cults.

## Key Hymns

- **To [[demeter|Demeter]]** — the abduction of [[persephone|Persephone]] and the founding charter of the
  [[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]] ("happy is he who has seen these things").
- **To [[apollo|Apollo]]** — his birth on Delos and the founding of the Delphic oracle.
- **To [[hermes|Hermes]]** — the infant trickster who invents the lyre and steals Apollo's cattle.
- **To [[aphrodite|Aphrodite]]** — her power over gods and men (the Anchises episode).
- Plus shorter hymns to Dionysus, Pan, Ares, Athena, and others.

## Significance

The hymns preserve the **birth-myths and cult aetiologies** of the major gods in their own right, and are
the primary text behind the Eleusinian Mysteries.
