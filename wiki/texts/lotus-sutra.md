The **Lotus Sutra** (*Saddharma Pundarika*) is among the most influential [[mahayana|Mahayana]]
scriptures, foundational for the Tiantai/Tendai, Nichiren, and (with others) East Asian Buddhism
generally. Its great themes are **upaya** (skillful means) and the **One Vehicle**.

## Central Teachings

- **The One Vehicle (*ekayana*)** — the apparently separate paths (arhat, *pratyekabuddha*,
  bodhisattva) are ultimately a single vehicle to buddhahood; the earlier "three vehicles" were a
  provisional teaching.
- **Skillful means (*upaya*)** — dramatized in the famous parables (the **burning house**, the
  **prodigal son**, the **rain cloud**): the Buddha adapts his teaching to his hearers' capacities, a
  doctrine with direct hermeneutical force (it grounds the [[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]]
  ranking of scriptures).
- **The eternal Buddha** — Shakyamuni's life and death are themselves *upaya*; his true buddhahood is
  beyond time.

## Why it is Canon-Divergent

`canonical_for: [mahayana, vajrayana]`, `rejected_by: [theravada]`. The Lotus's claim that the early
*arhat* path was provisional is precisely the Mahayana move that Theravada rejects — the Lotus
*narrates* the canon dispute from the Mahayana side.

## Hermeneutics

The Lotus reads itself as **[[neyartha-nitartha|nitartha]]** (the definitive, final teaching) and is
the classic warrant for **upaya** as an interpretive principle — different teachings for different
capacities, all converging on the One Vehicle.
