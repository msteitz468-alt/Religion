# Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi)

The **Fēngshén Yǎnyì** 封神演義 — "**The Investiture of the Gods**" or "Creation of the Gods" — is a **Ming
vernacular novel** (late 16th c.) that serves as the closest thing [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk
religion]] has to a **charter-myth for the [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]**. A landmark of the
**shénmó** ("gods and demons") genre, it dramatizes how the gods of the pantheon **received their offices**.

## The story

Set at the fall of the tyrannical last **Shang** king (corrupted by the fox-spirit **Daji**) and the rise of
the **Zhou**, the novel narrates a vast cosmic war in which **Daoist immortals, magicians, and gods take
opposing sides**. Its hinge is the doctrine of **investiture**: by the authority of the supreme deities, the
**heroes and immortals slain in the war — of both sides — are enrolled, by name and office, into the
hierarchy of Heaven**. Death in the war is not an end but a **bureaucratic appointment**: the dead are
**apotheosized into the 365 gods** of the celestial administration. The novel thus explains, in narrative form,
the folk-religious conviction that **gods are deified humans holding heavenly office**.

## Key figures

- **[[nezha|Nezha]]** — the rebellious child-god (the "Third Prince"), who returns his flesh and bones to his
  parents and is reborn from a lotus; a hugely popular deity.
- **Li Jing**, the **[[celestial-bureaucracy|Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King]]** (Guardian of the Gate of Heaven),
  Nezha's father.
- **Jiang Ziya**, the strategist who **performs the investiture**, conferring the divine offices.

## Significance

Together with *Journey to the West* (the legend of **[[sun-wukong|Sun Wukong]]**), the *Fengshen Yanyi* is one
of the two **great mythological novels** that function as popular canon for the gods of China — read,
performed as opera, and drawn on by temple cult. [[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's compilation]] retells
several of its episodes ("The Guardian of the Gate of Heaven," "A Battle of the Gods").
