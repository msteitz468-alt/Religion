# Sharpe, *Mysticism: Its True Nature and Value* (1910)

**Source:** `raw/scholarship/Dionysius the Areopagite: Mysticism Theology and Letters.md` (OCR of the 1910
second edition; the running title of the original is *Mysticism: Its True Nature and Value*).

A. B. Sharpe was an English Roman Catholic priest-scholar. The book is a **Thomistic apologia
for the reality of supernatural mysticism**, written against the early-twentieth-century
tendency (William James, idealist philosophy, comparative-religion reductionism) to dissolve
mysticism into psychology or pantheism. Sharpe's governing distinction is between **natural**
and **supernatural** mysticism; his constructive aim is to show that genuine (supernatural)
mystical experience is *empirical* — a real, immediate cognition of God — yet fully compatible
with scholastic epistemology and not reducible to pathology or to any "cosmic consciousness."

The book closes with Sharpe's own English **translation of the Dionysian *Mystical Theology***
(5 chapters) and of **Letters I and II to Caius and Letter V to Dorotheus**, making it a
secondary witness to those primary texts alongside Parker's
translation. See [[mystical-theology|Mystical Theology]] and [[letters-of-dionysius|Letters]].

Provenance note: the title under which this file was supplied ("Dionysius the Areopagite:
Mysticism Theology and Letters") reflects the bundled Dionysian translations; the work itself
is Sharpe's monograph on mysticism, of which the translations form Chapter XII and the
appended letters.

## Why It Matters / What It Adds

- Supplies the wiki's first sustained **theory of mysticism as such** (vs. the existing
  topic-specific pages on [[apophatic-theology]], [[negative-theology]], [[theophany]],
  [[beatific-vision]], [[perennialism]]). See the new [[mysticism]] concept page.
- A **Catholic/Thomistic** counter-reading of [[plotinus|Plotinus]] and of [[pseudo-dionysius|
  Dionysius]] — claiming the Christian mystics for orthodoxy while conceding the Neoplatonic
  debt.
- An early **critique of William James's** *Varieties of Religious Experience* (Appendix I).
- A second translation of the [[mystical-theology|Mystical Theology]] and selected
  [[letters-of-dionysius|Letters]], useful for comparison with Parker.

## Scope Plan (ordered)

This is a single bounded monograph; scope = the whole work, read in full. Partitioned for
parallel extraction:

1. **Introduction** (uses of "mystical"; the congenital confusion of magic/science/theology;
   the education of the race; Christian mysticism still entangled). Lines ~361–1253.
2. **Chs. I–V** — the core theory: Two Ideas of Mysticism; Supernatural Mysticism; the Nature,
   Object, and Psychology of Mystical Experience. Lines ~1254–4755.
3. **Chs. VI–X** — Evil; Immanence and Transcendence; Plotinus; Heretical Mystics; Mysticism,
   Philosophy and Religion. Lines ~4755–7223.
4. **Ch. XI–Appendices** — Dionysius (history/authorship); the *Mystical Theology* translation;
   Letters I, II, V; Appendix I (Reality of Mystical Experience / critique of James); Appendix
   II (Notes). Lines ~7223–8962.
