# Bhikkhu Bodhi, *In the Buddha's Words* (2005)

A thematic anthology of discourses (*suttas*) from the **[[tipitaka|Pali Nikāyas]]**,
selected, translated, and introduced by **[[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bhikkhu Bodhi]]**. It is the
standard one-volume entry to the early Buddhist canon and the wiki's primary window onto
the **early/Theravada** stratum of the teaching, prior to the [[mahayana|Mahayana]]
developments.

## What the Book Is

Rather than reproducing a single text, Bodhi extracts discourses from across the five
Nikāyas and arranges them into **ten thematic chapters**, each prefaced by a substantial
introduction (Buddhadharma notes that these introductions "strung together would
themselves serve as a beautiful and accessible overview of the Dhamma"). The architecture
is itself the argument: it reconstructs the **gradual training** the canon presupposes.

The ten chapters:

1. **The Human Condition** — *[[dukkha|dukkha]]*, aging, sickness, death, the round of
   [[samsara|saṃsāra]].
2. **The Bringer of Light** — the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]]'s appearance, biography, and
   role as discoverer (not inventor) of the path.
3. **Approaching the Dhamma** — faith, inquiry, the *Kālāma Sutta*'s call to test the
   teaching.
4. **The Happiness Visible in This Present Life** — lay ethics: family, marriage,
   livelihood, wealth, friendship.
5. **The Way to a Fortunate Rebirth** — *[[karma|kamma]]*, merit, generosity, the heavens
   and hells; the "mundane right view" oriented to a good [[samsara|rebirth]].
6. **Deepening One's Perspective on the World** — the danger in sensual pleasures, the
   limits of the mundane path.
7. **The Path to Liberation** — the **[[noble-eightfold-path|Noble Eightfold Path]]** and
   the [[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]] as the "supramundane" right view.
8. **Mastering the Mind** — meditation: the hindrances, *samādhi*, the *jhānas*.
9. **Shining the Light of Wisdom** — insight (*paññā*): the [[anatta|aggregates and
   non-self]], **[[dependent-origination|dependent origination]]**, impermanence.
10. **The Planes of Realization** — the stages of awakening (stream-enterer,
    once-returner, non-returner, **arahant**) and **[[nirvana|Nibbāna]]**.

## Key Interpretive Claims

- **Two right views, one path.** Bodhi stresses the canon's distinction between a
  *mundane* right view (affirming [[karma|kamma]], [[samsara|rebirth]], and the moral
  fruitfulness of action — chapters IV–V) and a *supramundane* right view (the
  [[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]] aiming at liberation — chapter VII). The
  Buddha's teaching is graduated, not flat.
- **The Nikāyas as a "single cohesive collection."** Against the view that the early texts
  are a disordered patchwork, Bodhi argues the discourses form a coherent system once their
  pedagogical sequence is recovered.
- **Rebirth and kamma are integral.** The "fortunate rebirth" chapter is not optional
  cosmology but the doctrinal substrate of the path; Bodhi resists secularized readings
  that excise it.

## Significance for the Wiki

This source anchors the early-Buddhist concept cluster that the wiki previously referenced
without grounding: **[[nirvana|Nibbāna]]**, the **[[noble-eightfold-path|Noble Eightfold
Path]]**, **[[samsara|saṃsāra]]**, and **[[karma|kamma]]**. It supplements the
[[theravada|Theravada]] and [[tipitaka|Pali Canon]] pages with the canon's own thematic
self-organization and supplies the **stages of awakening** vocabulary.
