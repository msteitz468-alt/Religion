# Crosby & Skilton, *The Bodhicaryāvatāra*

The standard accessible scholarly English translation of **[[shantideva|Śāntideva]]**'s
**[[bodhicaryavatara|Bodhicaryāvatāra]]**, by the Oxford/Bristol Sanskritists **Kate Crosby** and
**Andrew Skilton**, with a substantial **General Introduction by Paul Williams** (the leading
Anglophone authority on Mahayana doctrine) and per-chapter introductions and annotation.

## Approach

The translators render the verse text into clear English prose, prefacing each of the ten chapters
with a discussion of its structure and issues, and flag the **two recensions** of the text (the
shorter Dūnhuáng version and the longer canonical recension). Williams's General Introduction sets the
poem in its [[mahayana|Mahayana]] and [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] context and is itself a compact
statement of the tradition's ethics and metaphysics.

## What It Adds to the Wiki

- A full, reliable text of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* — previously present in the wiki only via the older
  public-domain Barnett translation and a stub commentator page.
- **Williams on the philosophy of emptiness** (chapter 9): emptiness is "a very specific sort of
  negation" — the negation of *inherent existence*, not the denial that things exist; "only
  emptinesses are ultimate truths"; and crucially **emptiness is itself empty** — "if we think of
  ultimate reality as an Absolute, the really inherently Real… we need to let go of that grasping as
  well." This grounds the wiki's [[sunyata#emptiness-of-emptiness|emptiness-of-emptiness]] treatment
  and ties Śāntideva firmly to [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] (the "Middling"
  way between reifying existence and nihilistic non-existence).
- **The two types of [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]]** (1.15–16): the *Mind resolved on Awakening* and the
  *Mind proceeding toward Awakening*.
- A clear account of the **[[exchange-of-self-and-other|equalizing and exchanging of self and
  other]]** (ch. 8) and the **altruism argument** from [[anatta|nonself]], including Williams's
  citation of the **Dalai Lama**: "The only purpose of my existence is to be used by others and to
  serve others."

## Hermeneutical Frameworks Tracked

- **Prāsaṅgika [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** ([[two-truths|two truths]];
  [[neyartha-nitartha|definitive]] emptiness).
- The **bodhisattva-path / *pāramitā*** framework as the structure of the poem.
- Modern **philological + Indo-Tibetan doctrinal** scholarship (Williams's *Mahāyāna Buddhism: The
  Doctrinal Foundations*).
