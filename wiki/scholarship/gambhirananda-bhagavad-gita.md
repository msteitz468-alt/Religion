# Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya (trans. Gambhirananda)

The earliest and most authoritative surviving commentary on the **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]** —
[[shankara|Śaṅkara's]] **Gītā-bhāṣya** — in [[swami-gambhirananda|Swami Gambhirananda's]] literal
English translation. This is the Gita read as a treatise on **liberating knowledge of the Self**, not
as a manual of devotion or activism, and it is the single most influential interpretation in the text's
history.

## Śaṅkara's Reading of the Gita

**The Gita's purpose (the *avataraṇikā*/introduction).** Śaṅkara opens by dividing the Vedic religion
into two *dharmas*: **pravṛtti** (engagement — action, ritual, the householder's duties), which yields
worldly prosperity and continued rebirth, and **nivṛtti** (withdrawal — knowledge and renunciation),
which alone yields **liberation (mokṣa)**. The Gita, he argues, was spoken to set forth the second.
Arjuna's grief is the paradigm of the bound soul: it springs from the false ideas "I am the doer" and
"these are mine," rooted in **ignorance (avidyā)**, the engine of [[samsara|saṃsāra]]. Krishna's cure
is not better action but **knowledge of the Self**.

**He begins at 2.10.** Famously, Śaṅkara passes over chapter 1 and the first nine verses of chapter 2
without comment and begins his *bhāṣya* only at **2.10** — treating the narrative frame as preamble and
the philosophy proper as starting with Krishna's teaching "You grieve for those not to be grieved for"
(2.11). The Self is **unborn, unchanging, actionless** (*akartṛ*): "it neither slays nor is slain"
(2.19–20) is read as literal metaphysics — the Self can no more act than space can burn.

**Knowledge alone liberates.** The bhāṣya's controlling thesis, stated in the introduction:
*liberation comes "from nothing other than the knowledge of the Self which is preceded by the
renunciation of all duties."* Action ([[karma-yoga|karma-yoga]]) cannot itself produce liberation; at
most it **purifies the mind** (*sattva-śuddhi*) and qualifies one to receive knowledge. Hence Śaṅkara
**rejects the combination of knowledge and action** (*jñāna-karma-samuccaya*) as a means to mokṣa — the
position he attributes to an opponent (the *Vṛttikāra*) in his very introduction. See
[[jnana-karma-samuccaya|the controversy]].

**Two grades of teaching.** What looks in the Gita like an exaltation of devotion and engaged action
Śaṅkara reads as **provisional** — addressed to the not-yet-qualified, leading them toward the purity
in which the definitive teaching (the actionless Self, *nirguṇa* [[brahman|Brahman]]) can land. This is
the [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] application of [[two-truths|levels of truth]] to the Gita itself.

## Why This Source Matters to the Wiki

- It supplies the **Advaita reading of the Gita** to stand beside the Advaita reading of the
  [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]], completing Śaṅkara's coverage of the **prasthāna-trayī** in the wiki.
- It grounds the wiki's sharpest Hindu **hermeneutical controversy** — whether the Gita teaches that
  knowledge *alone* liberates, or knowledge *with* action ([[jnana-karma-samuccaya|jñāna-karma-samuccaya]]).
- It is one of three commentaries on the Gita now ingested at very different ends of the interpretive
  spectrum: Śaṅkara's non-dual *jñāna*, the Vaiṣṇava *bhakti* reading of [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]], and the
  esoteric Śaiva reading of [[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta]] (see
  [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|one text, rival commentaries]]).
