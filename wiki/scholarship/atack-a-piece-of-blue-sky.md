# *A Piece of Blue Sky*: Scientology, Dianetics, and L. Ron Hubbard Exposed — Jon Atack (1990)

## Source Overview

**Jon Atack** spent **seven years in Scientology** (rising to OT5, the highest level he describes), then left and spent **six years researching** this book before its 1990 publication. It is the first major insider critical biography of Hubbard and comprehensive organizational history of Scientology, predating Russell Miller's *Bare-Faced Messiah* (1987) in scope and surpassing it in organizational detail.

**Methodology**: approximately 700 pages of documents; 150+ interview sources; the **Armstrong case transcripts** (28 volumes, ~10,000 pages); personal participation in the movement; Freedom of Information Act requests; court records from the United States, United Kingdom, and elsewhere. Atack is explicitly critical — this is not neutral scholarship — but he documents his claims extensively and supplies primary-source citations throughout.

Atack was a target of Church legal action before publication: in May 1989, **New Era Publications** (Scientology's publishing arm) sued his publisher for copyright infringement and obtained a court order to review the manuscript pre-publication; a January 1990 injunction was overturned on appeal, and three appeals judges unanimously upheld all 121 complained passages.

## Structure

- Part 1: Hubbard's early life and pulp fiction career (to 1949)
- Part 2: Dianetics and the founding of Scientology (1950–1954)
- Part 3: Scientology's doctrine and organizational development (1954–1966)
- Part 4: The Sea Organization and Hubbard's decline (1966–1977)
- Part 5: The Guardian's Office and Operation Snow White (1966–1977)
- Part 6: Hubbard's final years; Miscavige's rise (1977–1986)
- Part 7–9: The post-Hubbard Church; Fair Game, Ethics, and scripture
- Epilogue; Bibliography; Abbreviations

## Key Claims and Evidence

### Hubbard's Fabricated Biography
- Military service record: fired on Mexican territorial waters (investigated); assessed "not qualified for command"; the Church's "Notice of Separation" document (showing decorations and combat record) independently identified as a forgery by two St. Louis archivists — phantom signatory "Howard D. Thompson," font and medal anachronisms.
- Academic credentials: failed nuclear physics at GWU; PhD from Sequoia University (unaccredited correspondence school).
- Atack finds the gap between the Church's hagiographic mythology and the documents is a defining feature of the movement.

### The "Religion Angle" (1952–53)
- **April 10, 1953 letter to Helen O'Brien**: "Perhaps we could better our income by... the creation of a religious fellowship... It is not necessary to prove any specific doctrine... It is necessary to make money." Atack locates this as the pivotal document showing the religion angle was primarily strategic (FDA pressure, Purcell bankruptcy, medical-claim liability).

### OT Materials and Compartmentation
- Extensive discussion of **OT III** (the Xenu incident, 75 million years ago), the **"Incident I"** and **"Incident II"** cosmology, the body thetans (BTs), and the NOTs auditing process (developed by [[david-mayo|David Mayo]] in collaboration with Hubbard, September 1978).
- **Revolt in the Stars** screenplay: Xenu (+ Chi, Galactic Minister of Police; Chu, President of the Galactic Interplanetary Bank; Rawl, the hero/Hubbard-character) — reveals OT3 content in fictional form; written by Hubbard.
- Compartmentation: materials graded from "Broad Public Issue" (BPI) through confidential to secret; no single individual saw all of it; the Tech, Admin, and GO streams were kept largely separate.
- "Hubbard was not only a science fiction writer, but also a competent hypnotist. A very competent hypnotist."

### The Guardian's Office
- Established 1966; described as a "private CIA"; at peak: up to 5,000 operatives.
- **Snow White Program**: **Guardian Order 732, April 1973**, authored by Hubbard personally (not, as the Church claimed, an independent GO initiative). Systematic infiltration of the IRS, FBI, Interpol, and other agencies; resulted in the **July 7, 1977 FBI raids** (134 agents; 28,000 documents seized).
- **Operation Freakout** vs. journalist Paulette Cooper: framing for bomb threats; indictment before Church involvement was exposed.
- **Operation Meismer**: GO attempt to place a spy inside the IRS before Snow White.
- **B-1 files**: the most sensitive GO intelligence materials, available only to those who passed stringent filters.

### Financial Structure
- **OTC (Operation & Transport Company)**: incorporated Panama, January 1, 1968; 98 of 100 shares owned by Hubbard — the corporate vehicle through which Hubbard controlled Sea Org assets personally.
- **Religious Research Foundation (RRF)**: incorporated Liberia, August 1973; mechanism for siphoning Church funds to Hubbard tax-free.
- **Author Services Incorporated (ASI)**: for-profit company managing Hubbard's copyrights; de facto Church management under Miscavige.
- Hubbard's "Governing Policy" for finance (March 1972): every point says "MAKE MONEY."
- ASI target: $85 million "Christmas present" to Hubbard (1982).
- **Church of Spiritual Technology (CST)**: assets over $500 million (per Washington DC tax case records); dug a 350-foot tunnel into a New Mexico mesa to preserve Hubbard's writings in nuclear-war-proof conditions; additional storage facilities near Los Angeles and in northern California.

### Hubbard's Personal Conduct and Death
- **Letters to wives**: Atack cites letters confirming Hubbard used drugs during the OT3 research period (North Africa, 1966).
- **Motorcycle accident**, Tenerife, November 1973: broken arm and ribs; refused doctor; the **RPF** was ordered and introduced while Hubbard was recovering.
- **Hubbard's flight to Creston**: February/March 1980, with Pat and Annie Broeker; the "All Clear Unit" (headed by Miscavige) was tasked with clearing the legal situation for his return.
- **Death**: January 24, 1986, "Whispering Winds" ranch, Creston, California; hydroxyzine (anti-anxiety/anti-stroke medication) found in blood; coroner sought autopsy but a religious-objection document (reportedly signed only four days before death) was presented; inquest not held.
- Atack's psychological assessment: Frank Gerbode (psychiatrist) assessed Hubbard as "manic with paranoid tendencies"; Atack applies Cleckley's psychopath checklist extensively; notes Bolitho's *Twelve Against the Gods* (adventurers who overreached) was Hubbard's favorite book since 1952 and argues he modeled himself on it.
- 1938 letter to first wife Polly: Hubbard expressed ambition to equal Napoleon, Genghis Khan, and Alexander the Great.

### Sea Organization
- **Chain-locker punishments** (1968): children as young as 3–5 years old confined in chain lockers; one boy held 30 nights.
- **Overboarding** at Corfu (1968): Class VIII course filming; Hubbard filmed overboardings as "instant Ethics."
- **CMO (Commodore's Messenger Organization)**: children recruited as personal messengers; Tonja Burden testified at age 13–18 she received $2.50–$17.20/week and received no education.
- **RPF** (introduced 1973); **RPF's RPF** (1974).
- **La Quinta film studio** (1977–78): RPFers working in 117°F desert heat; Hartwell family testimony.
- **Clearwater Hearings**, May 1982: Casey Kelly income figures ($400K–$2.3M/week at Flag); David Ray on RPF conditions; George Meister on his daughter Susan's death.
- **Happy Valley** (Indian reservation, 1982): executives ran around poles 5 hours/day; Vicki Aznaran escaped April 9, 1987 by walking onto the Sobova Indian Reservation, rescued by Native Americans.
- **Watchdog Committee** created April 1979; 14-year-old Gulliver Smithers serving as CMO UK executive.

### Miscavige's Rise
- Joined Sea Org at 16 (1976); Chief Cinematographer at 17; "just over five feet tall and asthmatic."
- Confronted Mary Sue Hubbard May 1981; dismantled GO by August 1981.
- Chairman of ASI (for-profit company); later Inspector General of RTC.
- **RTC incorporated January 1, 1982** — Atack: David Mayo signed incorporation documents but claims terms were altered after he signed.
- The Broekers (Pat and Annie): Aznaran testimony describes a **schism in February 1987** between Miscavige and the Broekers over possession of "upper level Holy Scriptures."

### Armstrong Case
- **Gerry Armstrong** saved 20 boxes of Hubbard archive from the "Jaws" shredder at Gilman Hot Springs.
- Armstrong case: **Church of Scientology of California v. Gerald Armstrong**, Superior Court LA County, Case No. C 420153.
- **Judge Paul Breckenridge ruling, June 22, 1984**: found Hubbard "schizophrenic and paranoid," a "pathological liar"; described the Church as "corrupt"; awarded Armstrong the right to retain documents.
- Armstrong settled December 1986 for $800,000.

### Court Rulings Against Scientology
- **Justice Latey ruling** (Re B & G Wards, High Court, London, July 23, 1984): Church of Scientology "corrupt, sinister, and dangerous."
- **Christofferson-Tichbourne case** (Oregon, 1985): $39M jury award; mistrial declared by Judge Londer.
- **Wollersheim case** (Los Angeles, July 1986): $30M jury; reduced to $2.5M on appeal 1989.

### "Acceptable Truth" and Compartmentation
- Hubbard's PR maxim: statements tailored to the "reality" of a given audience; recorded in *The Management Series 1970–1974*, p. 116. In the GO, became "plausible denial." In practice: lying.
- "Responsibilities of Leaders" / "Bolivar" Policy Letter: seven maxims for retention of power, including: "to live in the shadow of a power you must gather and USE enough power to hold your own," and "the dull thud of one of his enemies in the dark, or the glorious blaze of the whole enemy camp as a birthday surprise."

## Atack's Evaluative Framework

Atack writes as an ex-member who retains deep familiarity with the literature while applying a critical journalistic and psychological lens. He does not adjudicate theological questions but treats Scientology's claims as empirically testable and finds them consistently false. He draws on:
- Psychological typologies: Cleckley's psychopath checklist; Berne's transactional analysis (used in cult mind-control literature)
- Comparative cult literature: Conway & Siegelman (*Snapping*); Robert Lifton (*Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism*); Eric Hoffer (*The True Believer*)
- Legal record: Armstrong case, Wollersheim, Christofferson-Tichbourne, Latey
- Primary documents obtained from the Armstrong archive and FBI FOIA releases

**Hermeneutical note**: Atack reads Scientology texts historically-critically — he treats them as products of a specific person at specific times for specific purposes, rather than as scripture. This is the opposite of the "standard tech"/"Source" hermeneutic within the movement.
