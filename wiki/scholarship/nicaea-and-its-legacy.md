# Nicaea and Its Legacy (Lewis Ayres, 2004)

**Source:** `raw/scholarship/Nicaea and Its Legacy An Approach to Fourth-Century Trinitarian Theology (Lewis Ayres) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).txt` (OCR/epub-derived plain text, 22,666 lines; body lines 405–20483, then bibliography and index).

## Overview

Lewis Ayres's study is a revisionist account of the fourth-century Trinitarian
controversies and their resolution. Its central methodological argument is that the
traditional narrative — an "Arian" heresy opposed by "Nicene" orthodoxy, settled at the
[[arian-controversy|Council of Nicaea (325)]] — distorts the history. Ayres argues:

1. **"Arianism" is a polemical construct.** There was no coherent "Arian" party or
   movement descended from [[arius|Arius]]; the label was forged (especially by
   [[athanasius|Athanasius]] after c. 340) to bind together diverse theologians. The book
   prefers to speak of overlapping **theological trajectories**.
2. **Nicaea (325) did not settle anything in 325.** *Homoousios* was not a rallying cry for
   decades; there was no single "Nicene theology" in 325. The creed's authority was
   *constructed* over two generations.
3. **The real outcome is "[[pro-nicene-theology|pro-Nicene theology]]"** — a shared set of
   theological grammars and strategies (not merely the creed's words) that coalesced
   c. 360–380 in figures such as [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil]], [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory
   Nazianzen]], [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]], [[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary]],
   [[ambrose|Ambrose]], [[marius-victorinus|Marius Victorinus]], and [[augustine|Augustine]].
4. **East and West are not opposed.** Ayres attacks the "de Régnon paradigm" (East starts
   from the three persons, West from the one essence) as a modern misreading; Augustine
   belongs squarely within pro-Nicene theology.

The book is read against earlier emphasis on the creed and conciliar politics; its
contribution to this wiki is the **trajectories framework**, the deconstruction of
"Arianism," and the constructive account of pro-Nicene grammar (inseparable operation,
divine simplicity, the partial/analogical character of theological language, participation,
the ordering of Christology and anthropology to contemplation).

## Hermeneutical Frame

Ayres works as a historical theologian: he reconstructs *how* fourth-century authors read
Scripture and deployed philosophical resources, insisting that pro-Nicene theology is
inseparable from a particular practice of scriptural reading and a "culture" or *habitus* of
the theological life. He resists both the "decline-from-Hellenization" thesis and triumphalist
confessional narratives.
