# Siderits & Katsura, *Nāgārjuna's Middle Way*

A complete English translation of the 449 verses of [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s
**[[mulamadhyamakakarika|Mūlamadhyamakakārikā]]** with a **running philosophical commentary**, by
Mark **Siderits** (analytic philosopher of Buddhism, the leading exponent of "fusion" / analytic
Buddhist philosophy) and Shōryū **Katsura** (philologist of Indian Buddhist logic). It is the
standard scholarly English entry point to the MMK.

## Method

Rather than translate any single Indian commentary, the translators construct their elucidation from
**all four classical commentaries** — the anonymous *Akutobhayā*, Buddhapālita's *Madhyamakavṛtti*,
**Bhāviveka**'s *Prajñāpradīpa*, and **Candrakīrti**'s *Prasannapadā* — using them to supply the
bracketed material the compressed *kārikā* verses omit and to identify which opponent's thesis each
chapter refutes. They deliberately **keep their own interpretive preferences in check**: "it would
be presumptuous… to suppose that we knew better than they what Nāgārjuna really meant." (Siderits's
own well-known "semantic non-dualist" reading is largely held back here.) The base text is the La
Vallée Poussin edition, corrected against Ye 2011.

## What It Adds to the Wiki

- A clear reconstruction of the **[[abhidharma|Abhidharma]] presuppositions** Nāgārjuna targets
  (two truths; dharmas as the only ultimate reals; [[svabhava|svabhāva]]; the conventional reducible
  to the ultimate) — the framework that makes MMK's moves intelligible.
- A taxonomy of Nāgārjuna's recurring **argument patterns** — infinite regress, "neither identical
  nor distinct," the three times, irreflexivity, nonreciprocity — and the key point that there can be
  **no positive "master argument"** for universal emptiness, only *reductio*.
- Careful readings of the pivotal chapters: **ch. 1** (the fourfold refutation of arising), **ch. 18**
  (the graded self/nonself teaching as skillful means), **ch. 24** (the [[two-truths|two truths]] and
  the answer to the nihilism charge; 24.18's "dependent origination = emptiness = the middle path";
  the [[sunyata#emptiness-of-emptiness|emptiness of emptiness]]), and **ch. 25** (the non-distinction
  of [[mulamadhyamakakarika|saṃsāra and nirvāṇa]]).

## Hermeneutical Frameworks Tracked

- **Commentarial-lineage** interpretation (the four Indian commentaries; the Svātantrika/Prāsaṅgika
  divergence they seeded).
- **[[neyartha-nitartha|Neyārtha / nītārtha]]** (interpretable vs. definitive) ranking — emptiness as
  the definitive meaning.
- Modern **analytic / comparative** philosophy of religion as the reading lens.
