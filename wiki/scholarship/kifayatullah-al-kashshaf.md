# Kifayatullah, *Al-Kashshaf: Al-Zamakhshari's Mu'tazilite Exegesis of the Qur'an*

A scholarly monograph reconstructing how [[al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] (d. 538/1144) embedded the
[[mutazilism|Mu'tazilite]] theological system into his celebrated Qur'an commentary **al-Kashshaf**.
It is the wiki's source for the al-Zamakhshari, Mu'tazilism, and *tafsir bi'l-ra'y* pages.

## Organizing Approach

The study is in two parts. **Part I** treats the man and the book: al-Zamakhshari's biography, the
transmission and manuscript history of *al-Kashshaf*, and his **methodology of tafsir** (rhetoric,
*i'jaz*, grammar, variant readings, and the *ta'wil* of anthropomorphic verses). **Part II** maps the
commentary onto the **five Mu'tazilite principles** (*al-usul al-khamsa*) one chapter at a time —
*tawhid*, *'adl* (justice), the promise and the threat, the intermediate position, and enjoining
right — analyzing the specific verses at which al-Zamakhshari's exegesis turns doctrinal, especially
the anthropomorphisms and the createdness of the Qur'an.

## What It Adds to the Wiki

1. **A worked case of the Contradiction Protocol in reception history**: a confessedly heterodox
   commentary that the orthodoxy condemning it could not discard, and so preserved by **expurgation**
   — al-Baydawi's sanitized *Anwar al-tanzil*, Ibn al-Munayyir's refuting marginalia *al-Intisaf*.
2. **The mechanics of *tafsir bi'l-ra'y***: how reason-based exegesis actually operates — not as
   free opinion, but as doctrine argued out of grammar, rhetoric, and *ta'wil*.
3. **The Mu'tazilite/Ash'arite hinge in concrete exegesis**: the contrast between al-Zamakhshari
   (reason governs where text and proof conflict) and his Ash'arite abridger al-Baydawi (revelation
   has priority), tracked verse by verse.
4. **The created-Qur'an flashpoint**: the report of the commentary's original opening — "Praise be to
   God who *created* (*khalaqa*) the Qur'an" — and its later softening to "*sent down* (*anzala*)."

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

Squarely the Islamic [[tafsir|tafsir]] framework — the **bi'l-ra'y** (reason-based) mode against
**bi'l-ma'thur** (tradition-based), and the **[[allegorical-reading|ta'wil]]** reading of the
anthropomorphic (*mutashabih*) verses demanded by Mu'tazilite **[[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih]]**.
