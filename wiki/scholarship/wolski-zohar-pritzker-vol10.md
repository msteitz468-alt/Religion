## What the source is

Volume X presents the ***Midrash ha-Ne’lam*** (“the Concealed Midrash,” or “the Midrash of the Concealed”) **on the Torah** — recognized by Zohar scholars as the **earliest layer** of the Zoharic literary corpus (Wolski preface). It is **not** the main running Torah commentary of Vols. I–IX (Matt): sefirotic symbolism is virtually absent (except parts of Shemot); the Companions epic around Rashbi is not the frame; instead: philosophical allegory, proto-kabbalistic midrash, and a diverse tannaitic/amoraic cast including Masters of Mishnah as forerunners of the fellowship.

## Volume / work synthesis

Volume X is the **earliest audible register** of the Zoharic library in the Pritzker set: not the Companions’ sefirotic theurgy of Vols. I–IX, but a concealed midrash that already has the “Zoharic eye.” Be-Reshit builds a philosophical-cosmic creation (letter *he*, 32 paths, 42, Abyss pillars) and a graded soul (intellectual *neshamah*, Body of Souls, storehouses). Noaḥ and Lekh Lekha map the soul’s geography (seven openings; Abram=soul / Sarai=body / Lot=evil impulse). Va-Yera and Ḥayyei Sarah stage death, Hell, and resurrection as allegory of the soul’s career, with Dumah/Ephron as the register-keeping “Bookkeeper” complex and Metatron as bone-leavening servant. Toledot–Va-Yetse densify resurrection body, heart/liver Jacob–Esau, refined Laban, and the ladder’s multiple readings. Shemot is the volume’s sefirotic spike (Gevurah, crowns, Shekhinah garden, prince of Egypt). Ki Tetse and the three Addenda close with reincarnation, Shema cooling Hell, the dog attribute, and the path of life.

What this reingest **adds** is folio-anchored fidelity for material first filed 2026-06-07 without ledgers; a dedicated source page; densification of the patriarchal soul allegory and afterlife apparatus on [[zoharic-afterlife|Zoharic Afterlife]] and [[allegorical-reading|Allegorical Reading]]; and first proper wiring of MhN Shemot / Ki Tetse into [[exodus|Exodus]] and [[deuteronomy|Deuteronomy]].

Tensions held: MhN vs main-body Genesis (Vols. I–III) on the same narratives; *Zohar Ḥadash* as “new” in print title vs earliest material (Wolski/Hecker); Scholem’s younger de León vs earlier-date arguments (positions, not adjudicated); philosophical Hebrew allegory vs Aramaic proto-kabbalah as polyphony rather than forced unity.
