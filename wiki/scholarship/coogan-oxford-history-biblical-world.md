# The Oxford History of the Biblical World

## Overview

A multi-author scholarly history of the world in which the biblical texts were
produced, edited by Michael D. Coogan. It runs from the prehistory of the ancient
Near East through the Bronze and Iron Ages, the Israelite monarchy, the Assyrian and
Babylonian conquests, the Persian and Hellenistic periods, and into the Roman period
and the Jesus movement. Each chapter is written by a specialist in the relevant
period and region.

This ingest focused specifically on the volume's value for **groups/** and
**locations/** pages — the peoples, tribes, nations, empires, cities, and sites that
form the human and geographical backdrop of the Hebrew Bible. The volume is
unusually strong for exactly the material the wiki had been missing: the
pre-Israelite ANE world (Canaanites, Amorites, Hurrians, Hyksos), the emergence of
Israel and the Philistines, and the imperial powers (Assyria, Babylon, Persia) that
deported and restored Judah.

## Hermeneutical Approach

Thoroughly **historical-critical and archaeological**. The contributors treat the
biblical narrative as a *dependent variable* whose historical value is weighed
against — not above — extrabiblical texts and material remains. Characteristic moves:

- **Source criticism / Documentary Hypothesis**: J, E, P, D as distinct strands;
  the Deuteronomic History (Joshua–2 Kings) as a 7th–6th c. composition. See
  [[documentary-hypothesis]], [[deuteronomy-authorship]].
- **Archaeology as primary evidence** where texts are absent or theological: e.g.,
  settlement surveys for the emergence of Israel; destruction layers for the conquest;
  Sennacherib's Lachish reliefs for the 701 campaign.
- **Distinguishing textual claims from critical reconstruction** without flattening —
  the volume regularly records both the biblical account and the archaeological
  picture where they diverge (Exodus historicity, conquest model, the "empty land").

## Chapters Drawn On (this ingest)

- **Prologue + Ch. 1 (Pitard), "Before Israel: Syria-Palestine in the Bronze Age"** —
  Canaanites, Amorites, Hurrians/Mitanni, Hyksos, Apiru; Ugarit, Ebla, Mari, Hazor,
  Megiddo, Byblos, Shechem, Jerusalem (Amarna); the alphabet; El and Canaanite religion.
- **Ch. 2 (Redmount), "Bitter Lives: Israel in and out of Egypt"** — Exodus
  historicity; Egypt and the Nile Delta; Goshen, Pithom, Rameses; Sinai; Shasu;
  the Merneptah ("Israel") Stela; New Kingdom empire and the Sea Peoples.
- **Ch. 3 (Stager), "Forging an Identity: The Emergence of Ancient Israel"** — the
  three models (conquest / pastoral-nomad / peasants' revolt) plus ruralization;
  the Israelites as a rural subset of Canaanite culture; Midianites/Kenites and
  Yahwism; the Philistine pentapolis and Sea Peoples migration.
- **Ch. 7 (M. Cogan), "Into Exile: From the Assyrian Conquest of Israel to the Fall
  of Babylon"** — Assyrian and Babylonian empires; fall of Samaria (720) and Jerusalem
  (586); Hezekiah, Sennacherib, Manasseh, Josiah, Zedekiah; the deportations; Edom's
  betrayal.
- **Ch. 8 (Leith), "Israel among the Nations: The Persian Period"** — Cyrus, Darius,
  the satrapy of Abar Nahara; Yehud; the return and Second Temple; Samarians and the
  roots of the Samaritans; Idumea/Edomites; Phoenician dominance of the coast.

## Notable Claims / Tensions Recorded

- **No independent extrabiblical witness to the Exodus** exists; the Merneptah Stela
  (c. 1209 BCE) is the earliest mention of "Israel," as a *people* (not a city-state),
  and is *not* recorded in the Bible. Feeds [[herem-canaanite-conquest]] and a
  potential Exodus-historicity controversy.
- **The "unified conquest" of Joshua fails archaeologically** at most sites (Jericho
  and Ai unoccupied in the relevant period; only Hazor and Bethel fit). Early Israel
  looks like a *rural subset of Canaanite culture*, not an external invader — a direct
  tension with the Deuteronomic Historian. See [[herem-canaanite-conquest]],
  [[israelites]], [[jericho]].
- **Yahwism may have Midianite/Kenite antecedents** (the "Shasu-land of Yahweh" in
  Egyptian lists; the mountain of God in Arabia, not Sinai). See [[midianites]],
  [[divine-name]].
- **The Samarians were Yahweh-worshipers**, not the syncretistic foreigners of
  2 Kings 17; Samaritanism is "more derivation than deviation" from Second Temple
  Judaism. See [[samaritans]].
