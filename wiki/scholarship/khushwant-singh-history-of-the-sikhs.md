# A History of the Sikhs — Khushwant Singh (OUP, 2004)

The standard narrative **political and social history** of the Sikhs, by the journalist and
historian **Khushwant Singh** (1915–2014). First published by Princeton in 1963; the OUP second
edition (2004) is in two volumes. **Volume 1 (1469–1838)** covers the religious origins and the
rise of Sikh political power: from [[guru-nanak|Nanak]] through the ten Gurus, the founding of the
[[khalsa|Khalsa]], [[banda-bahadur|Banda Bahadur]], the era of persecution and the *misls*, and the
kingdom of [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]. Singh writes for a general audience but bases the account
on Persian, Gurmukhi, and English archival sources. This is **Scope 2** of the Sikhism corpus plan
(see [[mcleod-sikhism]]); it supplies the historical-narrative dimension that complements McLeod's
thematic survey.

## Structure (Volume 1 contents)

Chapters: (1) The Sikh Homeland; (2) Birth of Sikhism — Nanak; (3) Building of the Sikh Church —
Angad to Arjan and the Granth; (4) The Call to Arms — Hargobind; (5) From the Pacifist Sikh to the
Militant Khalsa — Tegh Bahadur, Gobind Singh, the Khalsa; (6) The Rise and Fall of Banda Bahadur;
(7) Persecution of the Sikhs and the Reorganization of the Khalsa Army; (8) Ahmed Shah Abdali and the
Sikhs; (9) From the Indus to the Ganges; (10) Rise of the Sukerchakia Misl; (11) Maharajah of the
Punjab; (12) Suzerain of Malwa; (13) British Annexation of Malwa (Treaty of Lahore/Amritsar, 1809);
(14) Consolidation of the Punjab; (15) Extinction of Afghan Power; (16) Europeanization of the Army;
(17) Dreams of Sindh and the Sea; (18) Across the Himalayas to Tibet. Followed by appendices
(documents, genealogies), bibliography, and index.

## Scope Plan — Khushwant Singh Vol. 2 (1839–2004) (single pass, six extraction ranges)

Volume 2 body (raw lines 281–14435: 24 chapters in five Parts + Epilogue) is ingested in one pass,
partitioned into six disjoint line-ranges for parallel extraction. Appendices (1–6: Cultural Heritage,
Anglo-Sikh war documents, Anandpur Sahib Resolution text, genealogies; lines 14436–end), bibliography
and index are apparatus — consulted for reference, not read as primary content. The volume covers the
collapse of the Sikh kingdom, British annexation, the Singh Sabha and Akali reform movements, the
Gurdwara Reform movement, Partition, the Punjabi Suba, and the 1980s crisis (Bhindranwale, Operation
Blue Star, Indira Gandhi's assassination, the 1984 anti-Sikh violence, Khalistan).

- **D — 6513–8782**: Gurdwara Reform / Rise of the Akalis; Constitutional Reforms and the Sikhs; Sikhs and World War II; Prelude to Partition.

- **F — 11251–14435**: Anandpur Sahib Resolution; Fatal Miscalculation; Assassination and After (Operation Blue Star, Indira Gandhi); Elections and the Accord; Foreign Connections and Khalistan; Epilogue (to 2004).
