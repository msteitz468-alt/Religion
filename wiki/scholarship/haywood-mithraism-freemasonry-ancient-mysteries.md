# Haywood, *Mithraism: Freemasonry and the Ancient Mysteries*

> **Source page.** A short **Masonic essay** by Brother H. L. Haywood examining the popular
> thesis that modern Freemasonry descends from the ancient Mystery cults, using **Mithraism**
> as the representative test case. Source: `raw/Mithraism Freemasonry and the ancient mysteries (H. L. Haywood) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).txt`

## Overview

A popular, non-academic Masonic essay in six short sections: (I) Mithra's rise from Iranian
sky-god to "first-class god"; (II) the cult's entry into Rome via Mithridates' scattered armies
and its spread through soldiers, merchants, and emperors; (III) the "Mithraic theory of things"
(cosmology, soul's planetary ascent, the bull-slaying myth); (IV) the alleged resemblances to
**Freemasonry** (all-male membership, seven grades, initiation, "lodge"-like mithraea, charity,
calling one another "brother").

Haywood leans heavily on **Franz Cumont** and is **skeptical** of the strong descent thesis
associated with **Albert Pike** ("Freemasonry is the modern heir of the Ancient Mysteries") and
Sir Samuel Dill's phrase "a sacred Freemasonry": he concedes startling *resemblances* but holds
the parallels are largely superficial/external and that direct descent cannot be established.

**Caveats for fidelity:** the essay is dated, popularizing, and at points imprecise — e.g. it
attributes the **taurobolium** (a bull's-blood baptism properly associated with
[[magna-mater-cybele|Magna Mater/Cybele]]) to Mithraism, and traces a speculative transmission
chain Mithraism → [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]] → Paulicianism → medieval dualist sects → faint
echoes in Masonic ritual. These are recorded as **Haywood's claims**, not established fact.
