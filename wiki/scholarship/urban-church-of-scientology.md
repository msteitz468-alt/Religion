# The Church of Scientology: A History of a New Religion

**Author**: Hugh B. Urban (Ohio State University, History of Religions)
**Publisher**: Princeton University Press, 2011

## Summary

Urban's is the most rigorous academic history of Scientology available. It follows Scientology's development **decade by decade from the 1940s through 2010**, with a sustained central thesis: Scientology's contested journey to recognition as a "religion" in the United States is itself a key chapter in how the category *religion* has been defined, contested, and institutionalized in American law, culture, and politics over the past sixty years.

Urban employs a double hermeneutic — **hermeneutics of respect** (taking Scientology seriously as a lived religion for its members) and **hermeneutics of suspicion** (maintaining critical distance from both the Church's self-presentation and from government/anticult overreach). The approach draws on Bruce Lincoln's definition of religion as **discourse claiming transcendent authority**, Talal Asad's **genealogies of religion** (the category is not neutral but historically produced), Jonathan Z. Smith on "religion" as a scholarly construct with no fixed essence, and David Chidester on religion as contested in American popular culture.

## Chapter-by-Chapter Content

### Introduction: The World's Most Controversial New Religion

- Urban's position at OSU — own experience teaching Scientology and receiving legal threats from Moxon & Kobrin (the church's law firm)
- The "simulacrum of religion" critique from colleagues vs. Urban's methodological agnosticism
- Global variation: recognized in Spain and Australia; refused recognition in UK and Ireland; France convicted it of fraud (2009); Germany regards it as antidemocratic/totalitarian
- Framework: Lincoln, Asad, J. Z. Smith
- The "dual bind" of secrecy: the scholar cannot fully respect privacy *and* fully expose; neither total respect nor total suspicion alone is adequate

### Chapter 1: L. Ron Hubbard — American Entrepreneur, Spiritual Bricoleur

- Hubbard (1911–1986): born Tilden, Nebraska; son of navy officer Harry Ross Hubbard
- Church's **hagiographic mythology** (Dorthe Refslund Christensen's term) vs. critics' counternarrative
- **Fabricated credentials**: failed nuclear physics at GWU; PhD from unaccredited "Sequoia University"; war record — investigated for firing on Mexican waters, "not qualified for command"
- **Armstrong case (1984)**: Judge Paul Breckenridge: Hubbard a "pathological liar… motivated by egotism, greed, avarice, lust for power, and vindictiveness"
- **Pulp fiction career**: 70,000–100,000 words/month; pseudonyms including "Rene Lafayette"; fiction as laboratory for ideas about mind and reality
- **"Ole Doc Methuselah" series** (1947–50): Soldiers of Light + crossed rods symbol → precursor to Sea Org uniform and Scientology cross
- **"Typewriter in the Sky"** (1940): the god-like power of the author to create and destroy universes → foreshadows the thetan doctrine
- **"Excalibur" manuscript** (1938): near-death experience under anesthetic → "SURVIVE" as the fundamental command of life
- **Parsons/Crowley "Babalon Working"** (1946): Jack Parsons (JPL rocket scientist, OTO member), Hubbard as "Scribe" channeling Babalon; Aleister Crowley called Hubbard a "confidence trickster"; Hubbard allegedly absconded with Parsons's girlfriend Betty and $20,970.80 life savings; Church claimed he was on a "military mission to break up black magic"
- **Dianetics** (May 1950, *Astounding Science Fiction*): analytical mind / reactive mind / engrams / auditing / Clear; 28 weeks on NYT bestseller list
- **E-meter**: derived from Volney Mathison's device; Hubbard-Mathison split 1954; "sees all, knows all, is never wrong"; tomato plant experiment
- **Aldous Huxley** personally audited by Hubbard; **William S. Burroughs** became Clear #1163

### Chapter 2: Scientology, Inc. — Becoming a "Religion" in the 1950s

- Dianetics → Scientology transition (1952–54): financial collapse, Purcell bankruptcy, past-lives controversy, FDA pressure on medical claims
- **April 10, 1953 letter to Helen O'Brien**: the **"religion angle"** — religion chosen explicitly for business and legal protection ("It is not necessary to prove any specific doctrine"; avoids medical regulations, taxation, government scrutiny)
- **December 1953**: Church of American Science, Church of Scientology, Church of Spiritual Engineering incorporated in Camden, NJ
- "Scientology" term — ironically coined (negatively) by Allen Upward in 1910 as "pseudoscientific babble"
- **Thetan** as immortal self; MEST (Matter, Energy, Space, Time) universe as "trap of idiotic simplicity"; Gnostic parallels noted (though Hubbard had no deep Gnostic knowledge)
- **Past lives**: *A History of Man* (1952) — "cold-blooded account of your last sixty trillion years"; clam memories
- **Space opera cosmology**: Invader Forces, Marcab Confederacy, Arsclycans; "Earth is a sort of prison"
- **Exteriorization**: "Be three feet back of your head"; Grand Tour of the Universe; parallels to astral projection (Muldoon, Crowley's *Magick*)
- **Operating Thetan**: "ability to create own universe"; "beyond God"
- **Eastern religions turn** (mid-1950s): Veda as earliest ancestor; dhyana = Scientology; Hubbard's "Am I Metteyya?" poem (1955–56 Buddhist convention); claims Hinayana Buddhist lineage; but rejects nirvana — thetan retains individuality
- **Stephen Kent**: Hubbard made Eastern religion claims precisely when facing FDA/medical scrutiny
- **Bryan Wilson**: "precision of Gautama Buddha with the urgent productive practicality of Henry Ford"

### Chapter 3: A Cold War Religion — Scientology, Secrecy, and Security in the 1950s–60s

- Dianetics: "the only race that matters is between Scientology and the atomic bomb"; anti-radiation drug Dianezene (FDA seizure 1958)
- Hubbard's **anti-Communist letters** to Hoover's FBI; identified ex-wife's lover as Communist
- **Alleged Soviet brainwashing manual** (1955): Urban and FBI conclude Hubbard likely authored it himself
- **OT levels** (from July 1966): increasingly esoteric; "fatal to the unprepared"
- **OT III / Xenu narrative**: Galactic Confederacy; 75 million years ago; Xenu/Xemu; hydrogen bombs in volcanoes; body thetans; "Wall of Fire"; documented via Wollersheim (1985) and Fishman (1993) court records; *Revolt in the Stars* screenplay (1977) = same narrative. Hubbard's security check "Have you ever enslaved a population?" and "Did you come to Earth for evil purposes?"
- **Fair Game** (1965): "May be deprived of property or injured by any means … may be tricked, sued or lied to or destroyed." Officially canceled 1968 for PR reasons; practice continued
- **Guardian's Office** (formed March 1966; Mary Sue Hubbard director; Jane Kember Guardian Worldwide from 1969): "private CIA"; up to 5,000 covert agents
- **Operation Italian Fog** (vs. Clearwater mayor Gabe Cazares)
- **Operation Freakout** (vs. Paulette Cooper — *The Scandal of Scientology*, 1971): attempted frame for terrorism; threat against Henry Kissinger
- **CIA psychic research**: Harold Puthoff (OT VII), Ingo Swann (OT IV), Pat Price (OT IV) — "remote viewing"; Stanford Research Institute program funded by CIA until 1995

### Chapter 4: The "Cult of All Cults"? — Scientology and the Cult Wars of the 1970s–80s

- **Sea Org** (formed 1968): billion-year contracts; naval uniforms; "last ditch stand against total destruction"
- **Rehabilitation Project Force (RPF)** (1973): punitive labor unit; RPF's RPF (1974); "roach-infested cargo holds" (Armstrong) vs. "monastic retreat" (Melton, York, Pentikäinen)
- **Brainwashing debate**: Stephen Kent (yes) vs. Catherine Wessinger/Dawson (no)
- **Corporate structure**: "like a Rubik's Cube" (Vaughn Young); CSI (1981), CST (1982), RTC; described as "deviant business"
- Finance policy letter 1972: "A. Make Money … J. Make Money … K. Make More Money"
- **Bridge to Total Freedom**: minimum $300,000–$400,000 to complete all levels; 1993 total assets: $398 million
- **Messianic Project** (late 1970s): Nancy Many's account; surveys to market Hubbard as next messiah (like Christ/Mohammed/Buddha); inspired by Al Ries/Jack Trout "positioning" marketing theory; apparently dropped early 1980s
- **Celebrity Centre** (1969, LA): Project Celebrity (1955) — Hubbard targeted celebrities as "quarry"; Tom Cruise (joined 1986), John Travolta (1975)
- *Battlefield Earth* (2000): universally panned; Roger Ebert: "something historic, a film that for decades will be the punch line"
- **Anderson Report** (Victoria, Australia, 1965): "Scientology is evil"; Justice Latey (UK): "Mr. Hubbard is a charlatan and worse"
- *Time* "Cult of Greed" (Richard Behar, 1991): $416M libel suit; dismissed 1996; *Time* spent $3.7M in defense
- **CAN → New CAN**: Garry Scarff testimony about order to kill Cynthia Kisser; Jason Scott/Rick Ross deprogramming case; CAN driven to bankruptcy; bought for $20,000 by Scientologist Stephen Hayes; now "New Cult Awareness Network" controlled by Scientology

### Chapter 5: "The War" and the Triumph of Scientology — IRS Battle and Tax Exemption

- Tax-exempt 1956 → revoked 1967 (IRS found money flowing personally to Hubbard); 26-year war
- April 10, 1953 O'Brien letter: "religion angle" for legal/tax protection
- 1962 policy letter "Religion": "Scientology 1970 is being planned on a religious organization basis … entirely a matter for accountants and solicitors"
- March 1966: transferred all HASI assets to CSC to avoid scrutiny; "Parliaments don't attack religions"
- **Operation Snow White** (planned 1973): Michael Meisner, Gerald Wolfe ("Silver") placed inside IRS; 10-foot stack/30,000 pages stolen; IRS Chief Counsel Office bugged
- **July 7, 1977 FBI raids**: 134 agents; crowbars, battering rams; 200,000 documents seized; Jane Kember and Mary Sue Hubbard convicted and imprisoned; Hubbard named unindicted co-conspirator; spent remaining years in hiding
- 2,700 simultaneous lawsuits vs. IRS at peak; private investigators surveilling IRS officials
- **October 1991**: Miscavige and Rathbun held unscheduled meeting with IRS Commissioner Fred Goldberg
- **October 1, 1993**: tax-exempt status granted; $12.5M settlement (secret until *WSJ* leak 1997); IRS dropped audits of 13 entities; Miscavige: "The war is over!"
- **IRS 13-point criteria** for a "church" (published 1977, the same year as Snow White arrests): distinct legal existence, recognized creed, ecclesiastical government, formal doctrine, distinct religious history, ordained ministers, literature, places of worship, regular congregations, regular services, Sunday schools, minister schools
- **Urban's thesis**: Scientology's battle with the IRS was a key episode in how "religion" was *constructed* and *defined* in 20th-century American law

### Chapter 6: Secrets, Security, and Cyberspace — Scientology's Wars on the Internet

- **RTC** (1982): guardian of Scientology IP; David Miscavige chairman of the board
- **Wollersheim case** (1980–2002): $8.6M final settlement; OT materials entered as court exhibits; LA Times published them (November 1985)
- **FACTNet** (Fight Against Coercive Tactics Network, 1993): August 1995 federal court raid; Judge Kane (September 1995): fair use, NOT trade secrets; "public interest is best served by the free exchange of ideas"
- **Fishman affidavit**: Steven Fishman convicted of mail fraud; submitted 69 pages of OT materials in trial; OT VIII text claims Jesus "a lover of young boys" and identifies Hubbard as both Maitreya AND "anti-Christ/Lucifer"; RTC initially claimed copyright, then called it a "forgery"
- **Netherlands Supreme Court** (December 2005): OT materials legal to publish (Karin Spaink case)
- **Google / Xenu.net** (2002): Scientology demanded removal of critical site; chilling effects notice still shown
- **Wikipedia ban** (May 2009): 10-0 vote to ban all Scientology IP addresses for "sock-puppeting"
- **Anonymous / Project Chanology** (January 2008): triggered by Tom Cruise video leak; "Message to Scientology" video; global Guy Fawkes mask protests; "Operation Sea Arrrgh"; 19-year-old NJ man pleaded guilty to DDoS
- **Chef Xenu** (Urban's former OSU student): received Moxon & Kobrin letter (October 2005) demanding removal of materials; became active Anonymous member; Columbus protests
- Urban's argument: Scientology's information wars are losing badly in the Internet age; "Nobody can sue the Internet"

### Conclusion: New Religions, Freedom, and Privacy in the Post-9/11 World

- **Hubbard's death** (January 24, 1986): Blue Bird motor home at "Emanuel Camp," Creston, CA; long unkempt hair and fingernails; hydroxyzine in system; Church described it as voluntary departure to "research the next level"; Gold Base mansion still maintained for his return
- **David Miscavige's rise**: allegations of internal coup ousting Mayo, Broeker, and others vs. Church's official narrative
- **Membership figures**: ARIS survey: US membership fell from 55,000 (2001) to 25,000 (2008); Rathbun: "the Titanic hitting the iceberg"
- **Lisa McPherson** (died Clearwater, 1995): thromboembolism + dehydration; charges dropped June 2000 after medical examiner amended report; remains a touchstone for critics
- **OSA**: replaced Guardian's Office post-1977; now uses private investigators as cut-outs
- **Concluding theoretical argument**: Who gets to define religion? The debate involves academics, courts, IRS, journalists, Anonymous, and believers alike. Using Bruce Lincoln: Scientology is a self-conscious attempt to *make* a religion — to deploy religious discourse to claim transcendent authority. Stephen Kent's alternative: Scientology is a "multifaceted transnational" of which religion is only one component. Urban's own position: the more important question is not "Is it a religion?" but "Who decides, and what's at stake?" — because the stakes include millions in taxes, international legal recognition, and constitutional protections

## Urban's Theoretical Framework

Urban is a **history of religions** scholar in the tradition of Bruce Lincoln. Key theoretical moves:

1. **Religion as discourse claiming transcendent authority** (Lincoln, *Holy Terrors*): Scientology has worked relentlessly to make precisely this claim through every available medium.
2. **Genealogies of religion** (Asad): The category "religion" is historically produced, not neutral. Our current definition was shaped by Protestant-Christian assumptions and has been contested and renegotiated since — Scientology's battles are part of this ongoing negotiation.
3. **"Religion" has no essence, only more or fewer competing definitions** (J. Z. Smith, 50+ definitions): the *real* question is who gets to decide, and by what processes.
4. **Hermeneutics of suspicion + respect** (adapting Ricoeur): neither total credulity nor total suspicion is adequate for studying new religious movements. The scholar must hold both simultaneously.
