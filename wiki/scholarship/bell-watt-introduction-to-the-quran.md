# Bell & Watt, *Introduction to the Qur'an*

The standard mid-twentieth-century **Western historical-critical introduction** to the
[[quran|Qur'an]]: Richard **Bell**'s 1953 *Introduction to the Qur'ān*, thoroughly revised by W.
Montgomery **[[william-montgomery-watt|Watt]]** (1970) for the Edinburgh *Islamic Surveys*. It is the
wiki's anchor for the **critical account of the Qur'an's composition and text history**, the
Meccan/Medinan chronology, Qur'anic style, and the doctrine of abrogation — and the foil against
which the traditionalist counter-history of **[[al-azami-history-of-the-quranic-text|Al-Azami]]**
(ingested alongside it) is defined.

## Organizing Approach

A textbook in eleven chapters, working from outside in: the **historical context** (late-antique
Arabia between Byzantium and Sasanian Persia; Muhammad's career); **Muhammad's prophetic experience**
(the nature of revelation, *wahy*); the **history of the text** (the "collections" under Abu Bakr and
Uthman); the **external form** (suras, verses, the mysterious letters, the "dramatic form" of divine
speech); **features of style** (rhyme, *saj'*, didactic forms); the **shaping of the Qur'an**
(abrogation, evidences of revision, and **Bell's hypothesis** of written documents reused on their
versos); the **chronology** of the suras; the **names** of the book; its **doctrines**; **Muslim
scholarship** (tafsir, kalam); and **Western study** of the Qur'an.

## Key Positions

- **The collection**: Bell & Watt judge the tradition of an **Abu Bakr "collection"** to be largely
  unhistorical (a back-projection to avoid crediting the disliked **Uthman**), while accepting that
  the **Uthmanic recension** (under caliph Uthman, 644–56, via **Zayd ibn Thabit**, privileging the
  Quraysh dialect) was the first authoritative text, with prior codices destroyed. They argue
  Muhammad himself had already ordered much of the material into proto-suras.
- **Authenticity — a moderate critical verdict**: crucially, "modern study of the Qur'an has **not**
  in fact raised any serious question of its authenticity"; the Uthmanic revision "was honestly
  carried out, and reproduced … what Muhammad had delivered." The style is "almost unmistakable."
  This is a *conservative* conclusion on authenticity even as the collection-traditions are treated
  skeptically — a nuance the wiki preserves against the assumption that all Western scholarship
  "attacks" the text.
- **Revision and abrogation**: the Qur'an itself attests substitution of verses (Q 2:106; 16:101;
  13:39; 87:6f.), formalized by Muslims as **[[naskh|naskh]]** (*an-nasikh wa-l-mansukh*). Bell goes
  further, positing editorial **revision** by Muhammad and reused document-scraps to explain rough
  seams — *Bell's hypothesis*, much debated and not generally accepted.
- **The Satanic Verses** (Q 22:52; sura 53): read as the type case of a passage "proclaimed … then
  regarded as satanic and … no longer regarded as belonging to the Qur'an."

## Hermeneutical Framework

**Modern historical-critical** method applied to the Qur'an — source, form, and redaction-critical
analysis of the kind developed for the Bible — explicitly the German *Geschichte des Qorāns* lineage
(Nöldeke, Schwally) extended into English. Stands opposite both Muslim *tafsir* and the
traditionalist apologetics of [[al-azami-history-of-the-quranic-text|Al-Azami]].
