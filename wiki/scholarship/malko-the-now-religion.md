# Malko, *Scientology: The Now Religion*

*Scientology: The Now Religion* (1970), by **George Malko**, is an **early** critical study of
[[scientology|Scientology]] — valuable precisely because it captures the movement **near its origins**,
during [[l-ron-hubbard|Hubbard's]] own lifetime and active leadership, before later developments and the
Church's mature media management.

## Scope and Value

Covers the rise of the "Now Religion," a portrait of "**Ron**," the emergence of [[dianetics|Dianetics]] and
then [[scientology|Scientology]], the "techniques, drills, and processes" ([[auditing|auditing]]), and
"**ethics**." It opens with **John McMaster**, promoted as "the world's first **Clear**." As a period
source it documents the movement's self-presentation and practices c. 1970 and the early controversies.

> Note: as an early popular work it has been partly **superseded** by later, better-documented histories
> (e.g. [[reitman-inside-scientology|Reitman]]); the wiki treats it as a **primary-era witness** rather than
> a definitive account.

## Hermeneutical Framework

Journalistic and critical, contemporaneous with its subject — useful for tracking how Scientology looked and
described itself at an early stage.
