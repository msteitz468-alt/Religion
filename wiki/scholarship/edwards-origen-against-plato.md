# Origen against Plato — Mark Julian Edwards (2002)

Mark Julian Edwards (Christ Church, Oxford; series editor for Ashgate Studies in Philosophy & Theology in Late Antiquity) challenges the longstanding (ancient and modern) portrayal of Origen (c. 185–254) as the figure chiefly responsible for "contaminating" biblical theology with pagan (Platonic) philosophy. Instead, Edwards argues, Origen set out to construct a *Christian* philosophy precisely *in order to preserve* theology from the infiltration of pagan thought. Examining the question of philosophical influence on Christian thought, he contends that scholars have often leapt to unjustified conclusions based simply on common vocabulary or parallel development, without sufficient attention to Origen's own writings, 3rd-century standards, and the controlling role of ecclesiastical tradition and Scripture.

The book advances new interpretations of the early Christian systems commonly called 'Gnostic' and of the Doctrine of the Trinity in Origen's 'Platonist' teacher Clement of Alexandria. Its conclusion: Origen's hermeneutics, eschatology, cosmology, and Trinitarian theology are all related to his understanding of human nature, which is *radically opposed* to that of Platonism.

This is a targeted study in the series' approach: concentrate on the arguments of a single (or small group of) Christian writer(s) from AD 100–600, compare/contrast with pagan contemporaries addressing similar questions, attend to political/cultural/social milieu leading to convergence or divergence, set out pagan and Christian teachings clearly/systematically to highlight true originality, and estimate value for modern times.

## Structure and Key Arguments

**Introduction**: The title is deliberately surprising. Origen has a justified (if exaggerated) reputation as founder of philosophical theology; his Alexandrian birth, association with Platonists/Pythagoreans (per Porphyry), and "foreign" thoughts/style in his works seem to confirm Platonist influence (e.g., *On First Principles*, astral souls, unity of creatures + preexistence of soul). But crude positions have never been held by specialists. Bigg's *Christian Platonists of Alexandria* perpetuates the assumption. Researches (Simonetti, Crouzel) clarify Origen as Churchman first, availing himself of philosophy in service of exegesis and defence of tradition. Trigg makes him almost Protestant (Bible alone); Alviar/Laporte catholic (sacraments, contemplative prayer). Even critics (Hanson on allegory) prioritize exposure of errors over causes. At turn of 21st c., detractors are fewer/wiser, but an essay like this is still needed to preserve Origen from the "libels of his friends" (e.g., Pope John Paul II's encyclical praising him for adopting Platonic philosophy to counter Celsus and construct early Christian theology—contradicted by Origen's own animadversions on philosophy in *Against Celsus*, one of his latest works, and cross-references showing its late date).

Ancient jury convicted Origen of excessive fondness for Greek *paideia*. Post-254 reputation sea-change; open file for three centuries with contradictory impeachments. Pre-320: subordination of Son (no voice yet raised against it in East); gravest charge connascent with Father (denying offspring) or mere emanation ("Platonic" for moderns). Post-325 (Nicaea homoousios): convicted of opposite extreme (Son so inferior he did not know Father, member of created order). 4th-c. "Arianizers" branded Judaizers despite NT appeals; modern NT students find Son's inferiority hard to deny, yet historians imply it would never have occurred to Christians without overhearing Platonists on "second god."

Soul origin/destiny: 309+ allegations of denying fleshly resurrection and eternal punishment; exchanging hell for transmigration (misfortunes = prior sins); even (per Methodius) souls imprisoned in beasts like most impious Platonists. Origen allegedly taught preexistence (fall from blessedness via fatigue with God-contemplation, arrogance). Methodius/Epiphanius repeat (Epiphanius, despite knowing spiritual body survival, vilifies as denying resurrection). Jerome (under Epiphanius' spell) revives charges of all punishment reformative/temporary (devil savable; saints may fall). Both (despite own allegorizing) charge Origen with excessive allegory, jettisoning literal sense/biblical body for chimerical "spiritual" exegesis. Justinian (551/553): 11/15 counts against Origen (preexistence of souls, monstrous apokatastasis/restoration; creation only of incorporeal intelligences falling to bodies/stars/demons; sun/moon/stars rational but fallen; twofold race of demons; Nous demiourgos older than world creating via intelligence not Trinity; Christ pitying falls, passing classes, taking flesh; Christ as Nous by knowledge of Monad not Divine Logos; post-resurrection ethereal/spherical bodies; judgment = destruction of body to immaterial; heavenly powers/men/devil/evil spirits united with Word, Christ's Kingdom ends; Christ not different from other reasonable beings, all at God's right hand as in preexistence). Origen's name not attached (judges scrupulous; he and advocates protested adulteration of writings). Modern sketches of Origen's "Platonism" rely more on these anathemas than his pen. (Perceval translation quoted.)

**Chapter 1: Origen among Christians, Jews and Gnostics** — Christian and Jew (Alexandria church; Christian heterodoxy in Alexandria; Christological considerations; concluding remarks on Origen). Detailed on milieu: not straightforward "Platonist" contamination; Origen's 'Hebrew' master (Jewish Christian, not legalist Ebionite type; construed Isaiah vision as Trinity hint but not reducing Christ to angel); permeability of apocryphal/canonical in Clement's time (Peter's Gospel used but later fishery for heretics); Origen more scrupulous. Gnostics as context for heterodoxy; Origen's Christology measured against 3rd-c. needs of orthodoxy.

**Chapter 2: The God of Origen and the Gods of Plato** — Platonism and the Name of God; Studying Philosophy in Alexandria; God, Philosophy and Revelation; The Divine Creator; Christ as Logos; The Trinity, Ousia and Hypostasis. Origen's God as mind (not distinctive; Stoics/Aristotle also); rejects Stoic material/fire immanence (passible, mutable); agrees with Platonists on noetic substance but does not commit to all substance noetic (God may be superior to substance/mind; only God comprehends his being). Creator: matter made by God or nonexistent (prefers latter; banishes matter from cosmology). Time: moving image of eternity (Plato) but arche/beginning = Christ the Second Person (coeval with time). Subordination: Son servitor of Father (catholic/apostolic in his day; majority pre-Nicene more extreme; NT itself shows Son's inferiority in places). Not modelled on incarnate life as if "immanent = economic Trinity" platitude; had God not become incarnate, we would not know him as Trinity (differs from Platonists' logical abstraction from being's properties). Son of God (created, palpable, unique) shares epithets with Timaeus cosmos but is not any Platonist's god.

**Chapter 3: The Doctrine of the Soul in Origen** — Did Origen Believe in the Pre-existence of the Soul? (Interlude on preexistence of Christ's soul; does *On First Principles* contradict itself?; A Pilgrimage of Souls; Image, Likeness and the Fashioning of Saints; Eschatology and Mysticism). Challenges caricatures (fatigue from contemplation, transmigration to beasts, etc.); ties to his (anti-Platonic) anthropology.

**Chapter 4: The Interpretation of Scripture** — Notes Toward a Definition of Allegory; Origen and the Word of God. Against modern complaint of falsifying scripture by belated/capricious allegory overruling authorial intent. Frequency of "machinations" measures sanctity (Sanday). Philo pioneer (Alexandrian Judaism as book-religion); Origen measured by 3rd-c. orthodox needs, not modern Biblical scholarship or assumed "long traditions."

**Conclusion**: Mysticism, Platonism, Jewish Literalism; Word and Sacrament; Objections and Replies. Reiterates radical opposition in human nature/anthropology as key to differences in hermeneutics, eschatology, cosmology, Trinitarian theology. Origen Churchman using philosophy in service, not contaminator; against libels of friends and enemies.

## Relation to Existing Wiki (Stead/Wolfson cluster) and Additions

This directly complements the recent Stead (*Philosophy in Christian Antiquity*) and Wolfson (*Philosophy of the Church Fathers*) ingests on patristic philosophy/Trinity/Logos/Incarnation. Stead: concise overview, background Greek philosophy (Middle Platonists/Philo bridge), topical on God/Logos/substance, strong Augustine, semantic caution on "Christian philosophy" label. Wolfson: exhaustive reconstruction of Fathers' "Christian version of Greek philosophy" (Philo as foundation; allegory classifications; single/double faith; Trinity as 3-in-1 relative unity [rule or substratum/ousia/hypostasis]; Incarnation as 2-in-1 via predominance/perichoresis analogies; heresies as logical from same problems). Edwards: targeted counter to over-Platonizing narrative (common in ancient heresiologists like Methodius/Epiphanius/Justinian and moderns); Origen constructs *Christian* philosophy *to preserve from* pagan thought; common terms/vocabulary/parallels insufficient (must attend to his writings, 3rd-c. standards, biblical/ecclesial control); radical opposition in anthropology/human nature drives differences (hermeneutics, eschatology, cosmology, Trinity); new readings of Gnostics and Clement's Trinity. Challenges "contamination" (John Paul II cited as example of "libels of friends"); Origen measured by own cross-references (e.g., *Against Celsus* late) and animadversions on philosophy.

Specific contributions:
- Origen as Churchman first (exegesis/tradition defence; philosophy servant).
- Anthropology as key (radically anti-Platonic; ties all major areas).
- Allegory: definition, Philo pioneer, frequency = sanctity measure; Origen not excessive by 3rd-c. orthodox needs.
- God: mind (not distinctive); rejects Stoic immanence/fire (passible); may be superior to substance/mind; creator (matter by God or nonexistent); time/arche = Christ; subordination (catholic in his day; NT itself has it; differs from Platonist abstraction).
- Soul/preexistence: challenges caricatures (fatigue, transmigration to beasts); interlude on Christ's soul; pilgrimage, image/likeness, eschatology/mysticism tied to anti-Platonic anthropology.
- Trinity: context of subordination charges (pre-/post-Nicaea shifts); not "Platonic emanation"; had God not incarnate, unknown as Trinity.
- Gnostics/Clement: new interpretations.
- Method: against leaping from vocabulary/parallels; measure by own writings + contemporary standards (not modern or assumed traditions).
