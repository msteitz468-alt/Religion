# Rose, *Zoroastrianism: An Introduction*

Jenny **Rose** (Associate Professor of Religion, Claremont Graduate University; also
Stanford), a specialist in the **European reception of Zoroaster** (*The Image of
Zoroaster*, 2000), wrote this survey for the I.B. Tauris *Introductions to Religion*
series. It is, with [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]] and
[[skjaervo-spirit-of-zoroastrianism|Skjærvø]], one of the wiki's anchor sources on
[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]]. Where Boyce reconstructs the religion **doctrinally**
(the *yasna*, the theology, the cosmology), Rose narrates it **historically and
geographically** — tracing the faith across three Persian empires, Central Asia, Islamic
Iran, the Parsi settlement of India, and the modern global diaspora — and **methodically
foregrounds the problem of evidence** at each stage.

## Organizing Approach

Rose's distinctive method is **chronological-geographical and reception-critical**. She
opens and closes with *lived* Zoroastrianism (gahanbars in Yazd, a *navjote* in
Pennsylvania), and insists that "Zoroastrianism" is not a fixed essence but a tradition
read differently in each time and place — including by the very outsiders (Greeks,
classical authors, European Orientalists, Christian missionaries) whose lens has shaped
both scholarship and Zoroastrian self-understanding. She repeatedly distinguishes:

- the **Old Avestan** religion of the [[gathas|Gathas]] (a mobile-pastoralist,
  Indo-Iranian milieu, dated with the Rig Veda to the mid-2nd millennium BCE);
- the **Young Avestan** development (the *yashts*, the *Videvdad*, the rise of the
  [[mitra|yazatas]]);
- the **religion of the Ancient Persians** (Achaemenid inscriptions — close to, but not
  identical with, the Avesta);
- the **codified "orthodox" Zoroastrianism** of late-Sasanian and early-Islamic priestly
  books — which she argues may be a relatively *late*, narrow systematization
  (*poryotkesh*) of a much broader lived religion.

## What It Adds to the Wiki

1. **The historical sweep.** A continuous narrative — Achaemenid → Parthian → Sasanian →
   Sogdian/Central Asian → Islamic-era Iranian → Parsi Indian → modern diaspora — that
   Boyce's thematic treatment leaves implicit. This grounds the
   [[zoroastrianism|tradition page's]] historical-development and diaspora sections.
2. **The [[persians|Ancient Persian]] religion question.** Rose treats the asha/*arta*
   vs. *drauga* ethic of Darius, the [[xwarenah|*xwarenah*]] of kingship, the Avestan
   personal names in the empire, the origin of "[[paradise]]" (*pairidaeza* → Greek
   *paradeisos*), Cyrus as a "good-ruler" cipher, and the Anahita/Mithra image-cult under
   Artaxerxes II — and the Deutero-Isaiah parallels.
3. **The [[parsis|Parsi]] story in full** — the *Qesse-ye Sanjan* migration, the Iran
   Shah fire, the calendar disputes, the Wilson missionary controversy, Haug's
   Gatha-only "ethical monotheism," and the reform/traditionalist split.
4. **Modern lived religion and diaspora** — the "Who is a Zoroastrian?" debate,
   conversion, patrilineality (the 1908 Bombay *obiter dicta*), and the global
   "community without borders."
5. **Philological precision** on the Gathic vocabulary (*manah*, *manyu*, *manthran*,
   *spenta*, *asha*, *daena*) and on the **reception of [[zarathustra|Zarathushtra]]**
   himself (from Gathic *manthran* to Middle Persian mythic prophet to Nietzsche's
   cipher).

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

- **Historical-critical and reception-critical**: every datum is weighed against the
  bias of its source (Herodotus and the Greeks; Sasanian royal propaganda; Christian and
  Muslim polemic; European Orientalism).
- **Comparative Indo-Iranian philology**: the Gathas read against the **Rig Veda**
  (asha/*rta*, [[mitra|Mithra/Mitra]], [[yama|Yima/Yama]], *haoma*/*soma*, *yasna*/*yajna*).
- **Lived-religion / insider–outsider**: normative praxis (prayer, *kusti*, *gahanbar*,
  charity) as the living core, set against textual and doctrinal reconstruction; explicit
  reflexivity about her own "outsider" status.
- **The contested category of "dualism"**: Rose frames Zoroastrian "dualism" as a
  **vertical good/evil split**, not a mind/matter one, and tracks how the
  monotheism-vs-dualism question was forced on Parsis by Christian missionaries (Wilson)
  and answered by Haug's Gatha-only reading — a live intra-tradition fault line.

## Relation to Other Sources

Rose stands in Boyce's literary tradition (so Almut Hintze) but is **more cautious about
the strong influence-on-Judaism thesis** and **more attentive to the constructed,
contested, modern character** of "Zoroastrianism." Her stress that codified orthodoxy may
be a late priestly systematization sits in productive tension with Boyce's confidence in
a deep, continuous doctrinal core — a difference recorded here without adjudication
(Contradiction Protocol).
