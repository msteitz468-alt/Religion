## Overview

A short scholarly volume (the first in the *Garrett Theological Studies* series) reporting an
American team's fieldwork at **Ostia**. It is structured as linked essays moving from the
material remains of the Ostian mithraea outward to the social environment, then inward to
Mithraic theology (dualism), liturgy, and finally a confessional/historical comparison with
early Christianity. Its standing references are the foundational works of **Franz Cumont**,
the *Scavi di Ostia* excavation reports (Becatti), and Vermaseren's *Corpus*.

## Key Contributions

- **The Ostian mithraea catalogued** (Groh): fourteen sanctuaries, their typical cave-form
  (rear entry, central aisle, side *podia*, altar+*bema*+tauroctonos), and the finding that
  **no developmental sequence** can be reconstructed across ~150 years — feeds [[ostia|Ostia]]
  and [[mithraism|Mithraism]].
- **The social environment** (Schreiber): a cult of **slaves, freedmen, and dock-workers** with
  no attested magistrate; an **even city-wide distribution unmatched by any other Ostian cult**;
  a 2nd→3rd-c. shift from private to public premises that may have eroded its arcaneness.
- **The taurobolium correction**: the bull's-blood baptism belonged to
  [[magna-mater-cybele|Magna Mater]], not Mithras; the Mithraic bull-slaying ([[taurobolium|see]])
  had become **symbolic** by the time it reached Ostia.
- **Mithraic dualism** redefined (Laeuchli) as primarily **social/cultic**, not metaphysical —
  feeds [[mithraic-dualism]].
- **The liturgy** reconstructed (Stewardson & Saunders): the **cult meal** as central rite,
  Pater-as-Mithra and Heliodromus-as-Sol, the seven planetary grades — feeds [[mithraism|Mithraism]].
- **"Christ and Mithra"** (Laeuchli): the **fourfold method** for weighing parallels and the
  argument for fundamental difference (message + community) — feeds
  [[christianity-mithraism-parallels|the controversy page]].
