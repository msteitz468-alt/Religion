# How Jesus Became God (Bart D. Ehrman, 2014)

**Source:** `raw/scholarship/How Jesus Became God The Exaltation of a Jewish Preacher From Galilee (Bart D. Ehrman) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).txt`

A popular-level but scholarly historical-critical account of how Jesus — in
Ehrman's reconstruction a lower-class apocalyptic Jewish preacher from Galilee
who never called himself God — came to be confessed as God incarnate, "of one
Being with the Father," by the time of Nicaea (325 CE). Ehrman's thesis is
**developmental and evolutionary**: there was no single moment but a gradual
exaltation, beginning with the disciples' belief in the resurrection, and the
earliest Christologies were "from below" (a human being exalted to divine
status) before "from above" (incarnation) Christologies developed. He situates
the question against ancient assumptions — pagan and Jewish — in which the
divide between divine and human was not an absolute wall but a continuum
populated by gradations of divinity.

## Thesis and Method

- **Historical, not theological.** Ehrman writes as a historian (and former
  evangelical, now agnostic), bracketing the question of whether Jesus *was*
  God to ask how and when his followers came to *say* he was.
- **Christology as a spectrum, developing over time.** Earliest views exalted
  the human Jesus to divine status *at* the resurrection (exaltation
  Christology, "from below"); these were progressively pushed back — to the
  baptism, to the birth, to pre-existence and incarnation ("from above") —
  culminating in the Logos Christology of John and, later, Nicene orthodoxy.
- **Ancient divinity is graded.** Both the Greco-Roman and Jewish worlds had
  ways of conceiving humans who became divine and divine beings who became
  human; the question "how could Jesus be God and there still be one God?" is
  answered differently in each milieu.
