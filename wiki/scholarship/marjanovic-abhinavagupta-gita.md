# Abhinavagupta's Gītārtha-saṃgraha (trans. Marjanovic)

[[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta's]] commentary on the **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]** — the
**[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaiva]]** reading of the text — in Boris Marjanovic's English translation.
It is the wiki's fourth ingested Gita commentary and the one most unlike the others: a tantric,
non-Vedantic reading that finds in the Gita a secret teaching "more esoteric than Vedanta."

## What Makes This Commentary Distinctive

**It is a *saṃgraha*, not a *bhāṣya*.** Abhinavagupta does not gloss every verse. He comments **only
where a hidden (esoteric) meaning is at stake**, skips verses whose surface meaning is plain, and titles
the work *Gītārtha-saṃgraha* ("a gathering of the [essential] meaning of the Gita"). His stated purpose
(maṅgala verse 5): earlier commentators expounded the Gita extensively but left "hidden meanings out of
sight"; his task is to bring those to light. He uses a **Kashmiri recension** of the Gita that differs in
many places from the standard text.

**Knowledge and action are one.** Where [[shankara|Śaṅkara]] subordinates action to liberating knowledge,
Abhinavagupta (following the earlier Kashmiri commentator **Ramakantha**) holds knowledge (*dṛk*) and
action (*kriyā*) to be the **two inseparable powers of the one conscious Lord** — "not two distinct
entities, but combined create a whole" (on 5.14). His image: as the potter's movement of stick and wheel
is nothing but the potter's own nature, action is never separable from the consciousness that is its
ground. This is a *samuccaya* grounded in Śaiva metaphysics, not Vedic ritualism — see
[[jnana-karma-samuccaya|the controversy]].

**Esoteric (tantric) decoding.** Abhinavagupta reads the Gita's yogic and ritual language as veiled
**Trika practice**:

- The breath-control verses (4.29–30, *prāṇa* offered into *apāna* and vice versa) become a **guru–
  disciple initiation**: the disciple's inhaling breath is made to enter the guru's exhaling breath, so
  that "both guru and disciple attain liberation."
- The Gita's "sacrifice" becomes an **offering into the fire of one-pointedness**, glossed through the
  *Vijñāna Bhairava*.

**Goal: recognition, not absorption.** The Gita's aim, for Abhinavagupta, is the
**[[pratyabhijna|recognition]]** that the self is already Śiva — the all-inclusive consciousness "where
there is no state, within or without, in which He does not shine" (8.11). He states outright that
Krishna's teaching is **"more esoteric than Vedanta"** (*guhyāt vedāntād api guhyam*, 18.63), explicable
neither by dry Sāṃkhya nor by Vedanta but only by supreme non-duality (*paramādvaita*).

## Why This Source Matters to the Wiki

- It establishes the **[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Shaivism]]** sect page and the
  [[pratyabhijna|Pratyabhijñā]] concept.
- It adds a fourth, non-Vedantic column to [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the comparison of readings of
  the same canon]] — showing that the Gita was claimed even by traditions that owed it no allegiance.
- It is a vivid case of **esoteric/inner-meaning hermeneutics** ([[allegorical-reading|allegory and
  inner meaning]]) — directly comparable to Sufi *bāṭin*, Kabbalistic *sod*, and Christian allegory.
