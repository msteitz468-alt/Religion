# A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith

Peter Smith (2000), Oneworld Publications, Oxford. ~380 pages + thematic index + bibliography. Part of the Oneworld "Concise Encyclopedia" series (companion volumes cover Christianity, Judaism, Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism). Smith is a sociologist and historian at Mahidol University, Thailand, and a specialist in Bahá'í Studies; his fuller scholarly treatment is *The Babi and Bahá'í Religions* (Cambridge University Press).

The work is an alphabetically-organized reference encyclopedia covering all aspects of the Bahá'í Faith: major figures (Bahá'u'lláh, the Báb, 'Abdu'l-Bahá, Shoghi Effendi, and dozens of secondary figures); canonical texts (Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Kitáb-i-Íqán, Hidden Words, Bayán, etc.); doctrinal and theological concepts; administrative institutions; historical events; locations; groups; and international developments. The text is OCR-converted from PDF and contains OCR artifacts throughout; transliteration follows the system used in Bahá'í publications (based on the Tenth International Congress of Orientalists, 1894).

**Source note:** The text is OCR'd from the published PDF. OCR artifacts are present throughout but the content is readable. Diacriticals are often corrupted or missing.

**Scope:** This encyclopedia covers the full Babi-Bahá'í tradition comprehensively at an introductory-to-intermediate scholarly level. Body entries run from approximately line 930 (first entry: 'Abbás Núrí, Mírzá Buzurg) through approximately line 33,900 (end of main text; bibliography begins c.33,900).
