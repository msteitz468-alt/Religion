# Walpola Rahula, *What the Buddha Taught* (1959/1974)

The classic short exposition of core Buddhist doctrine by the Sri Lankan scholar-monk
**[[walpola-rahula|Walpola Rahula]]**, structured around the
**[[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]]** and grounded throughout in the
[[tipitaka|Pali Canon]]. With Demiéville's foreword and an appendix of Rahula's own
translations (the first sermon, the *Mettā Sutta*, the *Maṅgala Sutta*, passages on
[[anatta|non-self]] and mindfulness), it is the wiki's primary systematic statement of
the early teaching.

## Structure (eight chapters)

1. **The Buddhist Attitude of Mind** — the Buddha as a human teacher, not a savior; *come
   and see* (*ehipassika*); the *Kālāma Sutta*'s rejection of blind authority; doubt as
   legitimate; tolerance.
2. **The First Noble Truth: Dukkha** — the analysis of [[dukkha|dukkha]] (not "pessimism"):
   its three aspects (ordinary suffering, suffering due to change, suffering as
   conditioned states) and its definition via the **[[five-aggregates|five aggregates of
   clinging]]**.
3. **The Second Noble Truth: Samudaya** — the *arising* of dukkha in craving (*taṇhā*); the
   four nutriments; **[[karma|karma]] and [[samsara|rebirth]]** without a transmigrating
   self.
4. **The Third Noble Truth: Nirodha** — *cessation*: **[[nirvana|Nirvāṇa]]**. Rahula's
   insistence that nirvana is **not negative** and **not a result** produced by the path;
   nirvana as "Absolute Truth"; the limits of language; "if there is no Self, who realizes
   Nirvāṇa?"
5. **The Fourth Noble Truth: Magga** — the **[[noble-eightfold-path|Noble Eightfold Path]]**
   and the three trainings (ethical conduct, mental discipline, wisdom).
6. **The Doctrine of No-Soul: Anatta** — Rahula's celebrated chapter: there is no
   permanent *ātman*; the self is a convenient designation for the changing aggregates;
   how rebirth and moral responsibility work without a soul. (See [[anatta|Anatta]].)
7. **Meditation, or Mental Culture: Bhāvanā** — the *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta*; mindfulness
   (*sati*) of breathing, body, feelings, mind; insight (*vipassanā*) as the heart of
   Buddhist meditation, against the misconception of meditation as escapism. (See
   [[mindfulness|mindfulness]].)
8. **What the Buddha Taught and the World Today** — the Dhamma as applicable to lay and
   social life, not only the monastery; Buddhism and engagement.

## Key Interpretive Claims

- **Dukkha is realism, not pessimism.** Rahula resists the popular reading of the First
  Truth as gloom: it is a clear-eyed diagnosis whose point is the *cure*.
- **Nirvana is not annihilation and not a reward.** Against both nihilist and "heaven"
  misreadings, he holds nirvana to be the unconditioned reality realized when craving
  ceases — neither produced by the path nor a place one goes.
- **No-self is the linchpin.** The anatta chapter argues the whole edifice ([[dukkha|the
  Truths]], [[karma|karma]], [[samsara|rebirth]], [[nirvana|nirvana]]) coheres *only*
  without a permanent self — directly opposing the Hindu *ātman* the Buddha rejected.
- **The vehicles agree on essentials.** Rahula reads [[theravada|Theravada]] and
  [[mahayana|Mahayana]] as fundamentally continuous in doctrine.

## Significance for the Wiki

Supplies the systematic doctrinal backbone behind the Pali-Canon pages, and anchors three
previously-ungrounded concept pages — **[[dukkha|dukkha]]**, the **[[five-aggregates|five
aggregates]]**, and the **[[three-marks-of-existence|three marks of existence]]** — while
deepening [[anatta|anatta]] and [[nirvana|nirvana]] from a primary, confessional Theravada
standpoint (complementing [[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bodhi]]'s anthology).
