# The Jains — Paul Dundas (2nd ed., 2002)

Source: `raw/texts/The Jains (Paul Dundas) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).txt`

The standard scholarly single-volume survey of the Jain religion in English, written for
university students of Indian religions and members of the Jain community. Dundas (University of
Edinburgh) combines textual, historical, doctrinal, and ethnographic material across the whole
sweep of Jainism — from the *tirthankaras* and the historical Mahavira through the
Digambara/Svetambara schism, scripture, doctrine, medieval history, ascetic and lay practice,
the doctrine of many-pointedness (*anekantavada*), and modern reform movements and the diaspora.

This is the wiki's **foundational ingest for Jainism** — it establishes the [[jainism|Jainism]]
tradition page and the core sect, figure, text, concept, and location pages.

## Hermeneutical framing

Jain interpretation operates through the **naya** (standpoint/perspective) doctrine and the
**nikshepa** (modes of analysis), with the foundational Jain epistemic claim of
**[[anekantavada|anekantavada]]** (non-one-sidedness / many-pointedness) and its expressions
**[[syadvada|syadvada]]** (the "in-some-way" predication) and the **sevenfold predication**
(*saptabhangi*). Scriptural authority is graded by the **anga/non-anga** structure of the
Svetambara canon, contested by the Digambaras (who hold the original canon lost). Track also the
**neyartha/nitartha**-like distinction and the Digambara mystical reading associated with
[[kundakunda|Kundakunda]] (the *vyavahara*/*nishchaya* — conventional vs. absolute — standpoints).

Notes, Bibliography, and Index (reference apparatus). Ingested as one work, partitioned across
deployed subagents by chapter range:

1. **Introduction + Ch. 1 "The Fordmakers"** — Jain identity, Western views; the *tirthankaras*,
   the historical Mahavira, Parshva, Makkhali Gosala, early teachings.
2. **Ch. 2 "The Digambaras and the Svetambaras" + Ch. 3 "Scriptures"** — the schism; women's
   deliverance; the canon, the lost Purvas, the Kalpasutra, Digambara scriptures.
3. **Ch. 4 "Doctrine"** — omniscience, the *loka*, fundamental entities (*jiva*/*ajiva*), karma,
   rebirth, deliverance, Kundakunda's mysticism, God.
4. **Ch. 5 "History: from early times to the late medieval period"** — Mathura, south India,
   Jain kingship, sects, *bhattaraka*s, gacchas, relations with Muslims.
5. **Ch. 6 "The Ascetic" + Ch. 7 "The Lay Person" (incl. Worship/Pilgrimage)** — the *gunasthana*,
   the Great Vows, asceticism, *sallekhana*; lay life, *puja*, festivals, holy places.
6. **Ch. 8 "Jain relativism" + Ch. 9 "Recent developments"** — *anekantavada*, relations with
   Hindus/Buddhists; Lonka, Sthanakvasis, Terapanth, Srimad Rajacandra, Kanji Svami Panth, diaspora.
