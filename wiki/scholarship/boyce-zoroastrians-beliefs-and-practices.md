# Boyce, *Zoroastrians: Their Religious Beliefs and Practices*

Mary **Boyce** (1920–2006), professor of Iranian Studies at SOAS and the dominant
twentieth-century scholar of Zoroastrianism, wrote this single-volume survey as the standard
accessible introduction to the faith. It traces the religion from its prehistoric
**Indo-Iranian** roots, through the reform of [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]], the three Iranian
empires, the Islamic conquest, and the survival of the **Parsis** of India and the
Zoroastrians of Iran into the modern day. For this wiki it is the anchor source grounding
the deep but previously page-less Zoroastrian doctrinal vocabulary —
[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]], [[asha-druj|asha vs. druj]], the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]],
[[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]], the [[chinvat-bridge|Chinvat Bridge]], the
[[the-three-times|Three Times]], [[fravashi|the fravashis]], and the [[saoshyant|Saoshyant]].

## Central Theses

1. **Zoroastrianism is the oldest of the revealed world-religions**, and Boyce argues it "had
   more influence on mankind, directly and indirectly, than any other single faith" — supplying
   to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam (and to a range of Gnostic currents) the cluster of an
   individual **judgment after death**, **heaven and hell**, **bodily resurrection**, a **general
   Last Judgment**, and **life everlasting**. The strong-influence claim is Boyce's; this wiki
   records it alongside the more cautious framing of
   [[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]] without overclaiming.

2. **Reform, not invention from nothing.** Zoroaster was himself a **priest** of the old
   Indo-Iranian religion. Boyce reconstructs that older stratum by triangulating the most archaic
   Avestan material against the **Rigveda** and the Brahmanic ritual texts: the libation
   (*zaothra*), the fire-cult (Iranian **Atar** / Vedic **Agni**), the pressed
   [[soma|*haoma*/*soma*]] sacrifice, the cosmic order **asha** (Vedic **rta**), and the two
   ethical "lords" (*ahura*/*asura*) **[[varuna|Varuna]]** (Apam Napat) and **Mithra**, against
   the amoral warrior-god **Indra**. The prophet's originality lay in what he did *with* this
   inheritance.

3. **Strict ethical dualism with a guaranteed good end.** Boyce reads the Gathas (esp. Yasna 30)
   as teaching **two uncreated primal spirits** — the wholly good [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] and
   the wholly malign Hostile Spirit, [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] — who made an original choice
   between good and evil. Crucially the dualism is **temporally bounded and asymmetric**: evil is
   real and uncreated, but world-history is a finite battlefield that **Ahura Mazda is certain to
   win**, ending in [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]], the "making wonderful."

4. **The yasna as the seedbed of the theology.** Boyce's distinctive interpretive move is to
   derive Zoroaster's abstractions from his **meditation on the daily ritual**: pondering the
   *yasna* and its seven creations, the priest-prophet apprehended within each physical thing an
   "immaterial presence," yielding the [[amesha-spentas|heptad of Holy Immortals]] and the
   doctrine of the two states of existence — *menog* (spiritual) and *getig* (material) — set out
   in [[the-three-times|the Three Times]].

5. **Continuity of living practice.** A hallmark of Boyce's scholarship (grounded in her year of
   fieldwork among the Zoroastrians of Yazd) is the insistence that **living Parsi and Iranian
   observance preserves genuinely ancient features** — the *kusti*/*sudreh* (sacred cord and
   shirt), the five daily prayers, the seven seasonal feasts (*gahambars*) and **No Ruz**, the
   purity laws, and the exposure of the dead. She reads ritual as primary evidence, not mere
   survival.

## What It Adds to the Wiki

- **Anchors the dangling doctrinal vocabulary.** Pages now exist for the terms the
  comparative-eschatology and Sasanian-Judaism material had been invoking without grounding.
- **Supplies the Indo-Iranian comparative frame** that connects Zoroastrian material to the
  [[rig-veda|Vedic]] pages ([[varuna|Varuna]], [[soma|Soma/Haoma]], [[yama|Yama]]/Yima, *rta*),
  reinforcing [[the-combat-myth|the combat myth]] and [[axial-age|Axial Age]] discussions.
- **Frames the strong-influence thesis as a named scholarly position** (Boyce's), to be held in
  tension with cautious comparativism per the Contradiction Protocol.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

Boyce works as a **philologist-historian of religion**: comparative Indo-Iranian reconstruction
(Avestan ↔ Vedic), close reading of the Gathic dialect, and the use of **living ritual** as
evidence for ancient belief. She distinguishes Zoroaster's own message (the Gathas) from later
**Younger Avestan** and **Pahlavi** (Zand, [[bundahishn|Bundahishn]], Denkard) development — the
same Gathas-vs.-tradition layering the [[gathas|Gathas page]] depends on.
