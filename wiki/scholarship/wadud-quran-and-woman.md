# Wadud, *Qur'an and Woman*

Amina Wadud's *Qur'an and Woman* (Malaysia 1992; expanded Oxford ed. 1999) is the
foundational work of **gender-inclusive [[tafsir|tafsir]]** — a rereading of the
[[quran|Qur'an]] "from a woman's perspective" that Wadud claims was "nearly unprecedented
throughout fourteen centuries of Islamic thought." It is the methodological charter of what
became [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] and of Wadud's own activist "gender jihad."

## Organizing Thesis

The book's wager is sharp: take the Qur'an itself — not *fiqh*, not custom, not the *hadith*,
not the accumulated *tafsir* tradition — as "the ultimate criteria available within the
Islamic intellectual legacy," and read it without "centuries of historical androcentric
reading and Arabo-Islamic cultural predilections." Wadud reports that the more she did so,
the more she was "affirmed that in Islam a female person was intended to be primordially,
cosmologically, eschatologically, spiritually, and morally a full human being, equal to all
who accepted Allah as Lord." The inequality Muslim women experience is therefore a *reading*
imposed on the text, not a teaching of it. The key analytic move is to treat **gender as a
category of thought**, not merely a "subject for discourse" — and to expose that classical
Islamic thought built "the normative human" from the male person, relegating woman to
"subject without agency."

## Methodology: A Hermeneutics of Tawhid

Wadud's hermeneutic has two named components.

1. **The double movement (after [[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur Rahman]]).** Every Qur'anic passage
   was "revealed... in a specific time in history and within certain general and particular
   circumstances." Interpretation moves *from the present situation to Qur'anic times, then
   back to the present*: one extracts the moral *principle* a verse expressed within
   seventh-century Arabia (the *particular*), then re-applies that principle (the
   *universal*) to present circumstances. The Arabic language and the social specifics of
   the *occasion of revelation* are the historically bound vehicle, not the eternal cargo.
2. **The hermeneutics of [[tawhid|tawhid]] (holistic, against the atomistic).** Against the
   **atomistic** method of "almost all traditional exegesis" — which proceeds one verse at a
   time from al-Fatiha to al-Nas with no thematic principle linking them — Wadud reads the
   Qur'an as a coherent whole whose unity (*tawhid*) "permeates all its parts." Each
   gender-relevant verse is analysed (a) in its immediate context; (b) against discussions
   of the same topic elsewhere; (c) in the light of similar language and syntax used
   elsewhere; (d) under "overriding Qur'anic principles"; and (e) within the Qur'anic
   *Weltanschauung* (world-view). This is an elaboration of the classical principle of
   *tafsir al-Qur'an bi al-Qur'an* (interpreting the Qur'an by the Qur'an).

She borrows the linguistic frame of **"prior text"**: every exegete brings a world-view and a
set of pre-textual assumptions; this is unavoidable and "neither good nor bad," but it
becomes illegitimate "when one individual reader... asserts that his or her reading is the
*only* possible or permissible one." Naming the androcentric prior text of the inherited
*tafsir* is half her argument.

She also classifies interpretations of woman into three kinds — **'traditional'** (the
atomistic classical/modern *tafasir*, written almost entirely by men), **reactive** (modern
responses, often to Western critique, that argue *about* women without re-reading the text),
and **holistic** (her own: gender as a category, the whole text in view).

## Key Readings (what it adds to the wiki)

- **Egalitarian creation.** The Qur'an creates humankind from a single *nafs* (self/soul) and
  its *zawj* (mate, pair) — *min nafsin wahidatin* (Q 4:1) — with **no rib, no derivative or
  secondary creation of the woman, and no naming of "Eve" (*Hawwa*) at all**; nor does the
  Qur'an assign Eve special blame for the Garden lapse (both are tempted; in Q 20 it is
  *Adam* who forgets). The "phenomenon of pairs" (*zawj*) is itself a sign of *tawhid*: all
  created things are paired, therefore the uncreated Creator alone is unpaired and One. See
  [[adam|Adam (Islamic reception)]].
- **Eschatological equity.** Each *nafs* is recompensed strictly "in accordance to what it
  earns," male or female alike (Q 3:195; 4:32). Wadud reads the **[[hur-al-ayn|hur al-'ayn]]**
  (the fair-eyed companions of Paradise) as a *Makkan-period*, culturally specific incentive
  pitched to the desires of the *Jahili* Arab male — "impossible to believe the Qur'an
  intends white women with large eyes to represent a single universal description of beauty
  for all humankind." Later (Madinan) passages use the gender-neutral *zawj* (companion/mate)
  for the believers' reward; she rejects the common conflation of *zawj* with *huri* and the
  "multiple virgins" reading as a reduction of the highest reality to "a single ethnocentric
  world-view."
- **The "controversy" verses** (chapter 4) — her most cited contribution; see the dedicated
  controversy page [[quran-4-34-male-authority|Q 4:34 and male authority]]:
  - **[[qiwama|Darajah]]** (Q 2:228, "men have a *degree* above them"): restricted by its
    context (divorce — the unilateral male right of repudiation), *not* a universal hierarchy
    of all men over all women.
  - **[[qiwama|Faddala]]** ("preference," Q 4:34): the verse reads *ba'd* over *ba'd* ("*some*
    of them over *some*"), never "men over women" as classes; the only material preference the
    Qur'an actually specifies is the double inheritance share (Q 4:7), itself reciprocal with
    the male's maintenance duty — so "men as a class are not *qawwamun* over women as a class."
  - **[[qiwama|Qiwama]]** ("men are *qawwamuna 'ala* women," Q 4:34): conditioned by the
    particle *bi-* on two contingent conditions — *faddala* and material support; if either
    fails, a man is not *qawwam*. She reads *qiwama* as a *responsibility* (especially toward
    the childbearing woman), not a rank, and extends it beyond the merely material.
  - **[[qiwama|Nushuz]] and *daraba*** (the "wife-beating" clause): since *nushuz* is used of
    **both** husbands (Q 4:128) and wives (Q 4:34), it cannot mean "disobedience to the
    husband" but "marital disorder" (Sayyid Qutb's reading, which she adopts). The three
    steps (verbal resolution → separate beds → *daraba*) are a graduated, mutually-applicable
    de-escalation; *daraba* (Lane, *Lisan al-'Arab*) does not necessarily mean violent
    striking, and stands against the intensive *darraba*; in the context of pre-Islamic
    violence against women the verse is read as a **restriction**, not a license. *Qanitat*
    ("devout," used of men and women toward God) is mistranslated "obedient [to the
    husband]."

## Hermeneutical Frameworks Tracked

- Modern **thematic / holistic** Qur'anic interpretation (Fazlur Rahman; Mustansir Mir on
  Islahi's *nazm*) against the classical **atomistic** *tafsir*.
- **Tafsir bi'l-ra'y** of a reformist, principle-extracting kind — explicitly an act of
  *ijtihad* over inherited consensus.
- Engagement with **Sayyid Qutb**'s *Fi Zilal al-Qur'an* (adopted on *nushuz*/*qanitat*;
  resisted on *qiwama* as family hierarchy).

## Reception and Contradiction (recorded, not adjudicated)

Wadud reports being attacked as "Western" and "feminist" (both used "in a reductionist
manner... to mean anti-Islam"). Her readings of Q 4:34 and of *qiwama* are **directly
contested** by traditionalist exegesis (and by Qutb on the family-hierarchy point); per the
[Contradiction Protocol] both sides are carried on
[[quran-4-34-male-authority|the controversy page]] without adjudication. Her later activism
(notably leading a mixed-gender Friday prayer in 2005) intensified the dispute over her
standing; that belongs to her [[amina-wadud|commentator page]] and to
[[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]].
