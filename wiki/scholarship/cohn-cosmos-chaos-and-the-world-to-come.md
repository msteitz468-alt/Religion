# Cohn, *Cosmos, Chaos and the World to Come: The Ancient Roots of Apocalyptic Faith*

A landmark study by the historian **Norman Cohn** (author of *The Pursuit of the Millennium*) tracing the
**origins of apocalyptic faith** — the belief in a coming, final triumph of good over evil and the perfecting
of the world. Cohn's question: was it really the Jews and Christians who first expected "a single, final
consummation," and did ancient peoples really all imagine time as endlessly cyclical? His answer reframes the
whole story and synthesizes much of what this wiki covers across the ancient pantheons.

## The Thesis

**1. The archaic "static cosmos" and the combat myth.** Across the ancient Near East — [[egyptian-religion|
Egypt]], [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamia]], [[vedic-religion|Vedic India]], [[canaanites|Canaan]], and
[[judaism|early Israel]] — peoples shared a common vision: a **divinely appointed order, established for human
benefit, that would never fundamentally change.** That order was perpetually defended against the forces of
**chaos** through the **[[the-combat-myth|combat myth]]** — a champion god slaying a chaos-monster:
[[marduk|Marduk]] vs. [[tiamat|Tiamat]], [[ninurta|Ninurta]] vs. Anzu, [[indra|Indra]] vs. [[vritra|Vritra]],
Baal vs. Yamm, and the Egyptian defense of **[[maat|ma'at]]** against *isfet* and the serpent
[[apep|Apophis]]. Order could be threatened and restored, but the world was **essentially stable** — "as
things have been, they remain."

**2. Zoroaster's revolution.** **[[zarathustra|Zoroaster]]** broke this frame. He recast the cosmos as a
**battlefield in linear time**, moving toward a definite **end**: the assault of [[asha-druj|Angra
Mainyu]] on the order (*asha*) of [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] will not go on forever but will culminate in
"**the making wonderful**" (**[[frashokereti|Frashokereti]]**) — the final defeat of evil, the **resurrection
of the dead**, a universal **judgment**, and a **world made perfect and changeless forever**. With it came
**ethical cosmic dualism**, individual post-mortem judgment at the **Chinvat Bridge** (and, Cohn argues, the
invention of **hell**), and a future savior, the **Saoshyant**. This — not a cyclical eternal return — is, for
Cohn, the **origin of apocalyptic eschatology**.

**3. The Syro-Palestinian crucible.** Israel began with the same static worldview (the order of **mishpat,
tsedeq, shalom**), but under the pressure of exile and foreign domination — and in plausible contact with
Persian ideas during the [[persians|Achaemenid]] period — it produced the **[[apocalyptic-literature|
apocalyptic]]** vision of [[daniel|Daniel]], [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]], and [[jubilees|Jubilees]], where for the
first time a supernatural personification of opposition to God (Mastema/the **[[satan|Devil]]**) appears, and
history drives toward a final consummation. From there the apocalyptic hope passed into [[christianity|
Christianity]] and [[islam|Islam]].

## Significance for the Wiki

Cohn is a **keystone synthesis** linking material the wiki built separately: the ANE [[the-combat-myth|combat
myths]] of the new ancient-pantheon pages, the [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] eschatology, and the
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|Zoroastrian-influence]] question. His distinctive claim — the
shift from a **static defended cosmos** to a **linear history with an end** — is recorded here as a major,
well-argued position (the influence question itself remaining contested per the Contradiction Protocol).
