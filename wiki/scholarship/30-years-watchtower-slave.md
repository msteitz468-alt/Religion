# 30 Years a Watchtower Slave

William J. Schnell's memoir of his thirty years inside the [[jehovahs-witnesses|Jehovah's Witness]] organization — from his entry into the Magdeburg Bethel on August 18, 1924, through his quiet departure from a Kingdom Hall in 1953 and his final vow to expose the Organization publicly. Schnell was an American-born former Bible Student who spent his formative young adulthood in Germany (1914–1927), served as a Bethel worker in Magdeburg, a Pioneer, a Company servant in Manhattan, and finally as Zone servant for Northeastern Ohio and Northwestern Pennsylvania under Judge Rutherford. He was directly involved in engineering the organizational methods he later repudiates.

The work is a primary document in two senses: (1) as an eyewitness account of WTS internal operations from 1924 to 1953, and (2) as a specimen of the counter-JW evangelical apologetics genre that emerged in the 1950s. Schnell writes explicitly as a Christian calling other Christians to resist the "Watchtower Theocracy." His account is hostile throughout; the anti-WTS argument is never bracketed, and his claims about WTS internal motivations and strategies are those of a participant-turned-opponent. The wiki records his account without adjudication per the Contradiction Protocol.

## Chapter Summary and Key Content

### Part I — The Bible Student Era and the German Years (Chapters 1–5)

**Personal Background**: Schnell born 1905 in Jersey City, NJ; taken to Germany May 1914; stranded by WWI. Conversion experience at 12 (1917) attending a Lutheran Sunday school class. Association with the Berlin Bible Students' *Ecclesia* from ~1921; water baptized November 1921. The Berlin Ecclesia is characterized as autonomous, spirit-led, elder-governed, marked by "fellowshipping and Christian love" — a contrasting baseline against which Schnell measures all subsequent developments.

**Rutherford's Takeover**: Upon Russell's death (1916), legal counsel Rutherford maneuvered shareholder proxies to become WTS president over Russell's own succession instructions. He used his 1918 arrest and imprisonment (for antiwar editorial policy in the *Watchtower*) to manufacture the image of martyrdom, obscuring how he came to power and silencing internal opposition by creating an external enemy (organized Christendom). After exoneration, the aura of "God's chosen servant" never left him.

**The Organizational Pivot (1919–1925)**:
- *Fall of Babylon the Great* pamphlet (1919): attacked organized Christendom as "Babylon the Great" — the first step of separating Bible Students from all other Christian bodies
- *Millions Now Living Shall Never Die* (1920): promised the Kingdom's arrival; first mass expectation-raising campaign
- Cedar Point Convention (1922): "Advertise, Advertise, Advertise the King and the Kingdom" — reframed witnessing as a mass bookselling advertising campaign
- *The Harp of God* (1922): first WTS-owned printing; 35-cent book produced for 4 cents (U.S.) or 1 Reichsmark produced for 12 pfennigs (Germany); enormous profit margins concealed behind the claim of "preaching work"
- *Watchtower* article "Birth of a Nation" (1925): established the class structure — Faithful and Wise Servant Class over Mordecai-Naomi, Ruth-Esther, and Jonadab classes
- *Watchtower* article "Covenant or Sacrifice: Which?" (1925): declared individual character development ("character building") a waste of time; introduced the Kingdom Publisher model — time reporting on standardized forms, quota fulfillment, book placement counts submitted to Brooklyn

**Magdeburg Bethel (1924–1927)**: Schnell enters Bethel August 18, 1924, age 19. Describes:
- Printing one million copies of *The Harp of God* in German, working around the clock seven days a week
- Rutherford's spring 1925 visit: the "beard incident" (Director ordered to shave his Russell-style beard in exchange for a printing press)
- Magdeburg Convention (1925): 15,000 attendees; Rutherford's dramatic speech recounting his prison vow — "If thou wilt get me out of here, I will never cease to expose Christendom" — and pivoting from end-of-world expectation to vision of a world organization. Schnell notes Rutherford's gesture of feeding 15,000 with one hot dog each: "most attendants remembered the one free hot dog and potato salad" while forgetting everything else they had surrendered
- Spy networks and dossier system maintained on all Bethel workers; information used as leverage; Director lived in luxury (silk shirts, first-class travel, chauffeur-driven Mercedes) while workers earned 15 Reichsmark/month
- "Kadaver gehorsam" (corpse-like obedience) demanded; term came from German military usage

**"Sifting" (1924–1927)**: Independent-minded Bible Students systematically removed:
- Service Directors appointed by Society over elected Elders; Service Directors' mail came from Brooklyn, not from congregations; by 1927 Elders fully displaced
- Schnell, age 21, sent to split a 175-person congregation that refused to accept a Service Director; he walks 8 members out and forms a competing congregation with WTS-bussed reinforcements
- Schnell identifies Germany as the "blueprint" and proving ground for all subsequent American organizational strategy
- Growing personal disgust; Schnell applies for U.S. passport; returns to America June 1927

### Part II — The American Years: Pioneers and Organizational Construction (Chapters 6–8)

**Pioneers (1931–1937)**: Schnell observes America ~3 years behind Germany organizationally. The Great Depression (1929) is described as a "godsend" — vast pools of anxious, newly insecure people available for recruitment. Pioneer service becomes the primary recruitment and training corps. Schnell joins his family's Pioneer group in 1933 (Georgia). Pioneer economics: books bought at 8 cents, sold at 35 cents (4 free books for every 1 sold under Rutherford's bonus system); barter for produce, batteries, and automobile radiators in rural areas during the Depression; no WTS financial support despite requiring quota performance. By 1935, books increasingly refused — public catching on.

**Doctrinal Construction (1922–1938)**: Schnell summarizes the theological architecture built during these years:

| Book | Year | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| *The Harp of God* | 1922 | Initial publishing vehicle; new doctrinal frame |
| *Deliverance* | 1926 | Christendom as "Egypt"; Rutherford as Moses-figure |
| *Light I and II* | 1928 | Revelation reinterpreted; "locusts" = Witness witnessing parties |
| *Government* | 1928 | Blueprint for coming Theocratic dictatorship |
| *Life* | 1929 | Used Jewish return to Palestine as proof of nearness of end; discarded a year later |
| *Vindication I–III* | 1932 | Attacks on Christianity; consolidated Watchtower claims |
| *Preservation* | 1932 | Doctrinal basis for class structure |
| *Preparation* | 1933 | Jonadab/"Great Multitude" class; Gibeonite typology |
| *Riches* | 1934 | Summarizes new class doctrine; motivational |
| *Enemies* | ~1937 | "Religion is a snare and a racket"; attacks on Catholic and Protestant clergy |
| *Children* | 1940 | Story of "John and Eunice" — discouraged marriage before Armageddon; later withdrawn |
| *Religion* | 1940 | Extended attack on all organized religion; "instrument of the Devil" |
| *Neutrality* | 1941 | Basis for conscientious objector claims in WWII |

**Class System**: The WTS's three-tier class architecture (Schnell's term for the successive structures):
- Tier 1 (1879–1916): Charles T. Russell's individual leadership
- Tier 2 (1919–1942): Rutherford's dictatorship; established the Faithful and Wise Servant Class, class system, and Theocracy of 1938
- Tier 3 (1942–"2942"): Consortium/Board of Directors; New York corporation; perpetuating itself through charter member appointments

**The Name "Jehovah's Witnesses" (1931)**: At the Columbus, Ohio Convention of 1931, the name was given as "the Penny" (Matthew 20:1–16 parable). The 12 years since 1919 were equated with the 12 hours of the parable; the name was the equal reward given to all laborers (Remnant and Jonadabs alike). The name change simultaneously shed the "Bible Students" identity and rejected the name of Jesus Christ as organizationally inconvenient for proselytizing non-Christians.

**"Spiritual Israel"**: From 1930 onward, WTS appropriated all OT prophecies to itself, treating JWs as "spiritual Israelites." Schnell identifies this as the moment the organization abandoned Christianity structurally: "They failed to realize that in thus seeking to shed all connection with Christ they were also branding themselves as opposers of Him."

**Martyrdom-with-Purpose Strategy**: Schnell details the deliberate engineering of arrests:
- Sunday witnessing parties in New Jersey's exclusive communities, specifically to provoke arrests
- Knowing they had freedom-of-press protection under the First Amendment, the Society deliberately pursued freedom-of-religion framing instead — to generate martyrdom narrative and public sympathy
- "Religion Is a Snare and a Racket" sandwich-sign campaigns deliberately incited mob violence to create the spectacle of persecution
- *Lovell v. City of Griffin* (1938): Supreme Court case granting First Amendment protection to JW literature distribution; the Society exploited the ruling but had strategically engineered the arrests for years beforehand
- *Martin v. City of Struthers*: Schnell himself engineered this case from within Ohio Zone; SCOTUS struck down Struthers' doorbell-ringing ordinance

### Part III — The Theocracy of 1938 and Zone Work (Chapters 10–13)

**Seven-Step Indoctrination Program** (described in detail by Schnell as something he helped create):
1. Place books (any method)
2. Back call (monthly minimum; "person of goodwill" recorded in files)
3. Home Book Study (weekly; WTS books as text, not the Bible; first direct indoctrination step)
4. Area Book Study (Friday group meeting; "conference style"; seasonal "debunkers" for Christmas, Easter, etc.; grievances exploited to wean from prior associations)
5. Kingdom Hall / Watchtower Study (Sunday; induction into salvation-through-Organization message; salvation-by-Christ replaced with "city of refuge" concept)
6. Service Meeting (training in quota-filling, report-slip completion; "Mark of the Beast on the right hand")
7. Baptism (public initiation into Theocracy; individuality "buried"; official entry as Kingdom Publisher)

Schnell notes only 6.5% Scripture content in WTS books, with 93.5% WTS verbiage; and that he personally baptized **463 people** into the Organization across his years of service.

**Phonograph**: Introduced as a door-opener after radio stations declined to sell airtime to JWs; Judge Rutherford's 6-minute speeches played at doors under the cover of "preaching" rather than selling. Schnell identifies this as a tactical pivot that whitewashed the bookselling character of the work.

**Testimony Card**: A pre-printed card describing the current book offer; officially to help Publishers present books; actually served to prevent individual Scriptural engagement (which might reveal ignorance) and to create uniformity. JW conscientious objector claimants during WWII often couldn't find basic books of the Bible: "You mean to tell me that you are a minister and cannot tell me where to find this passage of Scripture?" — "We do not use this method of preaching. We use a testimony card."

**Atlantic City (1938)**: Schnell selected as "patsy" to defy Mayor White on the Boardwalk; arrested after 10 words before 25,000 spectators; 17 Publishers arrested including women and children; jailed overnight; reflects in his cell: "I knew that I had not come to preach the gospel but to create an issue for my Theocratic masters."

**Zone Work (1938–1941)**: As Zone servant for NE Ohio/NW Pennsylvania, Schnell:
- Broke up companies, installed Jonadabs over independent-minded Remnant class members
- Received 22-page dossier letter from Rutherford demanding a Yes/No answer on compliance
- Engineered Hubbard, Ohio picketing campaign (sandwich signs; mass arrests; civil damage suits; eventual permanent injunction)
- Engineered *Martin v. Struthers* case, carried to SCOTUS
- Zone servant work terminated November 30, 1941 — one week before Pearl Harbor

**Postwar Global Expansion**: New York incorporation (Watchtower Bible and Tract Society, Inc.) designed to perpetuate American control; charter member system (6–8 per state, Board-appointed and Board-dismissible); American passport exploited as "open sesame" for postwar missionary expansion; Gilead Bible College established at South Lansing, NY; Theocratic Ministry Course introduced to address JWs' appalling Biblical ignorance exposed by WWII draft board hearings.

### Part IV — Exit and Final Analysis (Chapters 14–18)

**WTS Attacks on Christendom**: Schnell compiles the WTS's own published rhetoric against Catholicism, Protestantism, and the clergy — and identifies the precise organizational behaviors the WTS condemned in others while practicing itself (fear-based recruitment, organizational hierarchy, collecting money through "works," hierarchical authority structures, building "houses" across the earth). He describes this as the WTS "using as their blueprint this list of accusations of the Roman Catholics." The attacks on clergy are traced to Rutherford's personal grudge from his 1917–1919 arrest and imprisonment, which he blamed on clergy influence.

**Personal Liberation**: 
- February 15, 1951: fired from Pioneer list for publishing the *Book News* newsletter and selling non-WTS Bible versions through W.J. Schnell Co. (founded 1941); twenty-one years of full-time service "blotted out in one night"
- Subsequent JW boycott of his book business; anonymous phone calls; surveillance; threatened bankruptcy; physical heart attack; alcoholism
- April 1952: all-night prayer; vow to God to expose the Organization if delivered from his fears and addiction; emergence from both simultaneously described as miraculous
- 1953: quietly walked out of Kingdom Hall, never returned
- August 1954, exactly 30 years after entering Magdeburg Bethel, announced the "Converted Jehovah's Witness Expositor" newsletter

**Hermeneutical Analysis**: Schnell's consistent critique of WTS biblical interpretation:
- Matthew 24:45–52: "faithful and wise servant" = class (WTS); "evil servant" = individual (any critic) — he calls this "the double standard of interpretation evident throughout all the books and booklets and magazines"
- Regular structure of WTS argument: (1) establish an incontrovertible historical fact; (2) associate it with Babylon/Satan; (3) add a fragment of Scripture; (4) present the desired conclusion as following necessarily
- The Gibeonite typology (Josh. 9–10) for the Jonadab class: "It really makes sense — if you are not squeamish about misusing Scripture"
- 6.5% Scripture content in WTS books, used disconnectedly, embedded in 93.5% "Watchtower verbiage"

## Source Classification and Limitations

This is a memoir by a hostile former insider, written and published 1956 as an evangelical counter-apologetic. Limitations:
1. **Hostility**: Schnell's anti-WTS conclusion was fixed before the book was written (by vow); his interpretations of WTS *motives* (e.g., that the entire 1922 campaign was designed from the beginning to maximize book revenue and subjugate members) are attributions of intent that cannot be verified from this source alone
2. **Reliability of memory**: Events from 1924–1941 are recalled from 15–30 years' distance; specific conversations, the precise wording of Rutherford's speeches, internal meeting details — all are reconstructions
3. **Apologetic frame**: The book is structured as a conversion narrative and warning; the contrast between the "early Bible Students" (good) and the WTS Theocracy (evil) is stark to the point of caricature
4. **Value as insider testimony**: Despite these limitations, Schnell's organizational details — the class system, the timing of doctrinal shifts, the mechanics of the seven-step program, the Zone servant role, specific court cases — are corroborated by external WTS history and are among the earliest detailed insider accounts published
