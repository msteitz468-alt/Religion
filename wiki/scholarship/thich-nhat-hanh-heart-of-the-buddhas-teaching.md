# Thich Nhat Hanh, *The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching* (1998)

A systematic yet practice-oriented exposition of core doctrine by the Vietnamese
**[[zen|Zen]]** master **[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]**, translated from the
Vietnamese *Trái Tim Của Bụt*. It covers the same doctrinal ground as
[[rahula-what-the-buddha-taught|Rahula]] and [[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bodhi]] but from a modern
[[mahayana|Mahayana]]/Zen and **Engaged-Buddhist** standpoint, and it openly *revises*
several received formulas.

## Structure

- **Part One — The Four Noble Truths**: the [[four-noble-truths|Four Truths]] in their
  "three turnings and twelve aspects"; suffering re-read as workable, not a verdict on life.
- **Part Two — The Noble Eightfold Path**: each factor of the
  [[noble-eightfold-path|Eightfold Path]] as a present-moment practice (Right View … Right
  Concentration), with **Right Mindfulness** ([[mindfulness|mindfulness]]) at the center.
- **Part Three — Other Basic Buddhist Teachings**: the [[two-truths|Two Truths]], the
  **Three Dharma Seals**, the Three Doors of Liberation, the [[five-aggregates|Five
  Aggregates]], the links of [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]] / interbeing.

## Key Interpretive Claims

- **Mindfulness as the heart of practice.** *Shamatha* (stopping, calming) and the practice
  of mindful breathing, walking, and "deep looking" are the engine of the whole path; the
  Buddha posts mindfulness as a **sentinel at each sense door**. Mindfulness must also be
  **collective** — a social and ecological practice, not only private serenity (Engaged
  Buddhism).
- **Interbeing.** Thich Nhat Hanh's signature term [[interbeing|interbeing]] reframes
  [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]] and [[sunyata|emptiness]]: nothing has a
  separate self; everything "inter-is" with everything else.
- **A critique of "Life is suffering."** He argues the rote slogan over-generalizes the
  First Truth; suffering is to be *embraced and understood* ("Our suffering is holy if we
  look deeply into it"), not used to declare existence worthless.
- **Craving is not the *sole* cause.** The standard "craving causes suffering" names only
  the first item on the list of afflictions (*kleshas*); anger, ignorance, suspicion,
  arrogance, and wrong views cause suffering too.
- **The Three Dharma Seals — nirvana, not suffering.** His most pointed revision: the
  authentic three universal marks are **impermanence, nonself, and
  [[nirvana|nirvana]]** — NOT impermanence, nonself, and suffering. Impermanence and
  nonself are universal *marks of all things*; suffering is **not** (a table is impermanent
  and selfless but not "suffering"). He invokes the Northern *Saṃyukta Āgama* and
  [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] for nirvana as the third Seal. This **directly contradicts** the
  standard list recorded (from [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]]) on the
  [[three-marks-of-existence|three marks]] page — staged as
  [[three-dharma-seals-is-suffering-universal|a controversy]], unadjudicated.
