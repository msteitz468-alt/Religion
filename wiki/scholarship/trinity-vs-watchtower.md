# The Trinity vs. the Watchtower

A short evangelical apologetic in five chapters by D. S. O'Cleary (no biographical details provided). The work frames the Jehovah's Witnesses as a re-emergence of the **Arian controversy of the fourth century** — "resurrected and renamed under the guise of the Jehovah's Witnesses and The Watchtower Bible and Tract Society." It surveys Trinitarian doctrine, JW history and beliefs, JW anti-Trinitarian arguments, JW teaching methods, and offers a practical Christian response.

This is a **secondary synthesis** citing standard evangelical and mainstream Trinitarian theologians (McGrath, Grudem, Grenz, Torrance, Berkhof, etc.) alongside JW Society publications; it draws no original research but usefully documents JW positions from official WTS sources (cited Watchtower issues, WTS books) alongside counter-arguments. Its main contributions to the wiki are two doctrinal data points and one legal testimony not previously recorded.

## Chapter Summary

### Chapter 1 — The Holy Trinity

A survey of Trinitarian doctrine development:

- **NT evidence**: plural *Elohim* (Gen 1:26; 3:22; 11:7; Isa 6:8); Shema as backdrop; Luke 1:35 (Incarnation); Matt 28:19 (baptismal formula); 2 Cor 13:14 (benediction)
- **Key terminological history**: Athenagoras (second century, first non-Christian-world articulation); **Tertullian** (first coined *personae* for the Trinity; the Roman actor's mask as the source of the theatrical image; *tres personae en una substantia*); Origen (subordination of Son to Father, Spirit to both — ground Arius developed)
- **Arian controversy**: Arius (Son as creature, not co-eternal; developed from Origen's subordinationism); **Athanasius** (*homoousios*; only the Incarnational perspective makes Son appear lesser; co-eternity of Son); Cappadocians (*tres hypostases en mia ousia*)
- **Later development**: Augustine (*De Trinitate*; "lover, loved, and love" Trinity analogy; filioque derivation; Son alone is the Word, Spirit alone is the Gift); Aquinas and Augustine on "subsistent relations" rather than persons; Boethius (*person = individual substance of a rational nature*); Barth (neo-modal Trinitarianism; Trinity as construct of faith, not explicit NT statement); Zizioulas (*personae* as eschatological concept and interpersonal communion)
- **Filioque**: Western addition of "and from the Son" (*filioque*) to the Nicene Creed — adopted in the Mass of 1014 (Pope Leo IX); Eastern objection (implies Holy Spirit as "Grandson of the Father")
- **Fatherhood of God**: *Abba* as intimate Aramaic term; gender-neutral Spirit (*pneuma/ruach*); adoptive parent metaphor
- **Son of God**: *logos* as John's primary title (appears 330 times in NT); pre-existence established by Early Church to avoid subordinationism; *morphē* (Phil 2:5) = "the essential nature" not "likeness"; *begotten, not made* (C. S. Lewis via Jewett)
- **Holy Spirit**: *ru'ach* (OT, feminine) → *pneuma* (NT, neuter) → *parakletos* (John's preferred term, masculine); Qumran *Testament of Judah* as possible parakletos background; Basil of Caesarea on the Spirit's limitless intelligence

### Chapter 2 — The Jehovah's Witnesses (History, Belief, Practice, Doctrine)

Significant content beyond what the wiki already contains:

**Russell's origin**: Raised Presbyterian; joined Congregational Church at 15; doubted predestination and eternal hell; became agnostic; converted through a Second Adventist Bible study group in 1870; organized his own study group.

**Pyramidology** (not previously in wiki): Russell's *Studies in the Scriptures*, Vol. 3 used the Great Pyramid at Giza as "the Bible in Stone" — the *Watchtower* magazine of March 1911 (also the magazine of 1911 in the bibliography lists it) stated that measuring the "grand gallery" in inches foretold specific prophetic dates (e.g., the bride of Christ assembled by 1910). Russell also claimed Melchizedek used Egyptian labour to build the pyramid. After Russell's death, **Rutherford repudiated the pyramidology** as deception by Satan — "the pyramid had been constructed under the guidance of Satan."

**The 144,000 / two-class Christianity** (not previously in wiki): The "anointed class" (144,000) was officially declared **closed in 1935** — "God had stopped calling people to the Elect as the 144,000 places had been filled." This produced a two-class Christian economy:
- **Anointed class** (144,000): those who will spend eternity in heaven; the spiritual leaders of the Witnesses; must be "born again"; participants in the Lord's Supper
- **Great Crowd** (everyone else): will inhabit the restored Earth throughout eternity; need not be "born again"; cannot share in the communion Bread or Cup; not in the New Covenant; not justified through faith in Jesus Christ per JW soteriology

O'Cleary notes the theological problem: as surviving anointed-class members die, new men not born before 1935 have been added to the Governing Body — which requires either deselecting prior members or admitting the 1935 closure was not absolute.

**WTS as sole interpreter**: *Watchtower* of 15 August 1981 warned followers that reading the Bible alone would lead them into apostate doctrines; the Society alone can interpret it. This is confirmed by **the Douglas Walsh trial (1954)**: in a Scottish court, WTS Secretary-Treasurer **Grant Suiter** admitted under oath that a Jehovah's Witness *cannot* understand the Scriptures apart from the Society's publications — directly contradicting the Society's own public claims.

### Chapter 3 — JW Teaching on the Trinity

JW anti-Trinitarian arguments from WTS publications (*Should You Believe in the Trinity*, 1989; others):

- The Trinity originated in Babylon c. 2200 BCE with Cush/Semiramis/Nimrod; replicated in Hindu Brahma/Vishnu/Siva and Egyptian Isis/Nephthys/Osiris — therefore Satanic
- The Bible does not explicitly teach the Trinity; Barth is quoted (out of context, per O'Cleary) as saying the NT lacks explicit Trinitarian equality
- The *ante-Nicene Fathers* (Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Hippolytus) are cited by the WTS as supporting a subordinate or created pre-human Jesus; Tertullian's "there was a time when the Son was not" is cited to deny the Son's eternity
- The Athanasian Creed was not written by Athanasius (true — O'Cleary concedes this)
- The Trinity is a product of Greek philosophy (Platonic), not the Hebrew mind
- JW Christology: Jesus = the Archangel Michael, created first; at the Incarnation, Michael's "life force" was sent to become the child in Mary's womb; after resurrection, Michael re-assumed existence as Jesus Christ; the resurrection body (with nail wounds) belongs to "another deceased person"
- Holy Spirit = God's "active force" (not a person); not capitalised in JW literature

### Chapter 4 — JW Teaching Methods

- Door-to-door proselytizing (46% of converts attracted this way)
- "Sheep/goat" classification at first contact; ongoing record cards
- "Back calls" system to follow up interested persons
- Meetings for prospects only — strangers not invited; incorrect answers tactfully silenced
- Emphasis on the Society as God's exclusive channel; submission to the Society replaces individual salvation
- *Watchtower* of 15 September 1910: studying the Bible alone leads to spiritual darkness; studying Society publications alone leads to "the light" within two years
- The Society's *Knowledge That Leads to Everlasting Life* (1995) and *Let God Be True* (1952) used as the primary study materials in the back-call sequence

### Chapter 5 — A Christian Response

- **John 1:1 argument**: O'Cleary cites **Colwell's Rule** (E. C. Colwell, 1933) — in predicate-nominative constructions where the predicate noun precedes the verb, the article is regularly omitted without making the noun indefinite; "the Word was God" is grammatically correct. William Barclay called the NWT's "a god" rendering "a deliberately distorted translation of truth" demonstrating "intellectual dishonesty." Randolph O. Yeager: "only sophomores in Greek grammar will translate 'the Word was a god'."
- **NWT internal inconsistency**: Thomas's confession in John 20:28 — "My Lord and my God" — is retained in the NWT with *theos* having the article (ho theos mou); the Society explains it as an exclamation rather than declaration
- **Practical advice**: Do not attack the Trinity directly (JWs are intensely trained for Trinity debates); instead focus on the Society's **false prophecies** (dated Armageddon predictions, 1910/1925/1975 expectations) to undermine the WTS's authority claim; use personal testimony; then show Jesus is not the Archangel Michael
- **Zechariah 11–12 proof**: the "pierced" servant sold for 30 silver pieces — fulfilled in Jesus per John 19:37 — identifies Jesus with Jehovah God in OT prophecy

## Source Classification and Limitations

1. **Apologetic frame**: This is evangelical counter-JW apologetics written from a convinced Trinitarian position; it does not present JW arguments charitably or seek their strongest form
2. **No author credentials**: D. S. O'Cleary provides no institutional affiliation or credentials; the work reads as a theological dissertation or extended essay, not a scholarly monograph
3. **Secondary throughout**: The Trinity chapters are textbook summaries citing McGrath, Grudem, Grenz, Torrance; there is no original exegetical or historical argument
4. **Value**: Best used for its documentation of WTS's official published positions (from cited *Watchtower* issues and WTS books) and for the three specific factual items identified above (pyramidology, 1935 closure, Walsh trial)
5. **Contradiction Protocol**: O'Cleary's characterizations of JW motives and WTS claims are not adjudicated here; they are recorded as his position
