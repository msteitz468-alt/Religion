# A Shorter Summa (Kreeft, 1993)

*A Shorter Summa* is a pedagogical anthology edited by Peter J. Kreeft, selecting and
annotating the principal philosophical passages of [[summa-theologica|Aquinas's *Summa
Theologiae*]] for general readers and undergraduate students. It is itself a condensation
of Kreeft's earlier *Summa of the Summa* (Ignatius, 1990), which excerpted ~500 pages from
the original ~3000. The 1920 Dominican Province English translation is used throughout.
Published by Ignatius Press in 1993.

This source adds nothing to the wiki's knowledge of Aquinas's own text (the *Summa* is
ingested in full across four raw files), but it contributes: Kreeft's selection criterion
and the seven-domain map of Aquinas's philosophical core; his three introductory essays;
a 45-term Thomistic glossary; and 59 numbered endnotes providing pedagogical explication
of specific articles. These are the principal sources for the [[peter-kreeft]] commentator page.

---

## Kreeft's Selection Criterion

Kreeft's inclusion rule is twofold: a passage must be (1) **intrinsically important** and
(2) **non-technical enough to be intelligible to modern readers**. Where an important passage
is technical, it is retained but accompanied by explanatory footnotes. All of Part III
(Christ, sacraments, eschatology) is deliberately **omitted** as revealed theology rather
than philosophical theology: "I also omitted all topics suitable for theology but not
philosophy classes, i.e., the revealed as distinct from the rational, philosophical
theology." Many objections are also omitted as "no longer live options."

## Structure: Seven Philosophical Domains

Kreeft organizes the selected articles into seven topical sections:

| Section | Topic | ST Loci |
|---------|-------|---------|
| I | **Methodology: Theology as a Science** — sacred doctrine vs. philosophy; the fourfold sense | I, QQ. 1 (AA. 1, 10) |
| II | **Proofs for the Existence of God** — the Five Ways | I, Q. 2 (AA. 1–3) |
| III | **The Nature of God** — simplicity, omnipresence, immutability, divine names, knowledge, truth, will, love, providence | I, QQ. 3, 8, 9, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20, 22 |
| IV | **Cosmology: Creation and Providence** — eternity of the world; creation from nothing | I, Q. 46 (AA. 1–2) |
| V | **Anthropology: Body and Soul** — incorruptibility of the soul; union of soul and body | I, QQ. 75, 76 |
| VI | **Epistemology and Psychology** — intellect vs. will; free will; abstraction; intelligible species | I, QQ. 82, 83, 84, 85 |
| VII | **Ethics** — happiness; the good; evil; cardinal virtues; theological virtues; law (eternal, natural, human, divine); natural law | I, Q. 5; I, Q. 48; I-II, QQ. 2, 61, 62, 91, 94 |

This structure closely maps Part I (*Prima Pars*) and a slice of Part I-II (*Prima Secundae*),
with Part II-II and Part III entirely absent. The selection reflects a **philosophy of religion**
framing: the sections that reason can treat without dependence on revealed premises are those
selected.

## Editorial Apparatus

### The Three Introductory Essays

**I. On St. Thomas**: Eight reasons Kreeft gives for Aquinas's greatness — truth, common sense,
practicality, clarity, profundity, orthodoxy, medievalism, and modernity. Kreeft argues Aquinas is
"certainly the greatest philosopher for the two thousand years between Aristotle and Descartes"
and that he executed seven essential syntheses: (1) faith and reason, (2) biblical and classical
heritages, (3) clarity and profundity, (4) common sense and technical sophistication, (5) theory
and practice, (6) intuitive vision and rigorous logic, (7) unity and multiplicity. See [[peter-kreeft]].

**II. On the Summa Theologica**: Kreeft explains the *disputatio* format (the five parts of
each Article), describes the *exitus–redditus* architecture ("God is the ontological heart
that pumps the blood of being through the arteries of creation into the body of the universe"),
and recommends grounding in Aristotelian logic before reading. He characterizes the Summa's
"choppy" atomistic style as intentional pedagogy: "Mommy cuts Baby's meat into bite-sized
chunks." He notes the difference between a *Summa* (structured debate, inductively as well
as deductively based) and a Cartesian closed system.

**III. On This Book**: Kreeft identifies four features that distinguish this anthology: (a) it
uses Aquinas's own words, not paraphrase; (b) it uses the old Dominican translation rather
than later interpretive paraphrases; (c) it confines itself to the *Summa* rather than ranging
across Aquinas's other works; and (d) it has copious explanatory footnotes (*explicate texti*).

### The Glossary

A ~45-term Thomistic–Aristotelian lexicon placed at the front as "a plain, dull door to a
great, golden mansion." Notable definitions capturing Kreeft's philosophical emphasis:

- **analogical**: "partly the same and partly different, neither univocal (wholly the same) nor
  equivocal (wholly different)" — bridging how divine attributes can be meaningfully attributed
  to God and creatures.
- **being**: threefold: (1A) *ens* (entity, substance), (1B) *esse* (the act of existing),
  (1C) *essentia* (essence, what a thing is).
- **matter**: distinguished sharply from modern usage — "not a thing but a metaphysical
  principle or aspect of things, which together with form explains change, as the actualization
  (in-form-ing) of potency (matter)."
- **nominal**: "Nominalism is the theory that universal terms like 'justice' or 'man' are only
  names, not real essences; it is perhaps the most pervasive and destructive error in modern
  philosophy."
- **transcendental**: "universally common to all things. The five transcendental properties of
  all being are: something, one, true, good, beautiful."
- **truth**: "conformity of the mind to real things."

### The 59 Endnotes

Kreeft's footnotes fall into two types: some **explain** difficult or technical ideas; others
**highlight** philosophical importance. Philosophically substantive notes include:

- **Section I, note 8**: The astronomer-physicist analogy (sacred science and philosophy can prove the
  same conclusion from different angles) as a model of faith-reason harmony.
- **Section III, note 24**: The medieval world-view diagram — "human science as reading God's art
  ('God wrote two books, nature and scripture')" — set against modern positivistic-materialistic
  empiricism.
- **Section III, note 27**: The Euthyphro dilemma applied to divine will — Kreeft frames the problem
  as avoiding both arbitrariness and heteronomy of God's will, which Aquinas resolves by grounding
  the divine will in the divine nature/intellect.
- **Section III, note 29**: "God does not fall in love for the same reason water does not get wet."
  God is pure act, without potentiality, and therefore uncaused.
- **Section III, note 30**: Vice and virtue have an **ontological dimension**: "we diminish our being
  when we sin and augment it by the virtues."
- **Section IV, note 1**: The **Big Bang** as modern scientific confirmation of the compatibility
  of creation with a temporal beginning — "the 'Big Bang' theory, which gives the world a temporal
  beginning, fits in nicely with the idea of creation of time (rather than creation in time)."
- **Section IV, note 3**: Aquinas's distinction between *creation* (of existence) and *change*
  (of state) — "a distinction Aristotle did not use, for, not knowing that the world was created,
  he never wondered why it existed but took its existence for granted and wondered only about its
  nature and properties." This awareness of the existence/essence distinction is posed as a
  distinctively Christian philosophical contribution.
- **Section V, note 6**: Classical/medieval explanation is from lower to higher (matter explained
  by form, body by soul); modern reductionism is the reverse ("thought is only cerebral
  biochemistry, love is only lust, man is only a clever ape"). Kreeft traces modern reductionism
  to **Ockham's Razor and Nominalism** in the fourteenth century.
- **Section VI, note 1**: The will-vs.-intellect question as the crux of the faith-reason synthesis:
  "for Plato and Aristotle the central, deepest, and highest part of a man is his mind and its
  knowledge, while in the Bible it is the heart, or will, and its love which are primary."

## Relation to the Full Summa Ingest

The *Summa Theologiae* is already ingested in full across four passes (Parts I, I-II, II-II, III +
Supplement); see the Coverage Ledger on [[summa-theologica]]. This anthology adds:
- No new textual coverage of Aquinas's own arguments.
- A **canonical selection** of the philosophical core as identified by a leading Neo-Thomist
  popularizer — useful for understanding how modern Catholic intellectual culture frames the
  *philosophia perennis* vs. the theological portions.
- A **pedagogical glossary** of Thomistic terms.
- Kreeft's **interpretive positions** as a modern expositor and defender of Thomism.
