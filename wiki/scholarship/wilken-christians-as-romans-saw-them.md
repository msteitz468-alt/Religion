# The Christians as the Romans Saw Them — Robert Louis Wilken

**Source:** `raw/scholarship/The Christians as the Romans Saw Them (Robert Louis Wilken) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).txt`
(8,138 lines; 2nd ed. 2003, with a new preface)

A study of the emergence of Christianity in the Roman world **from the perspective of its Greek and
Roman observers** — not the apologists' self-presentation but the pagan gaze. Wilken reconstructs how
educated outsiders (a provincial governor, a physician, two philosophers, an emperor) actually saw the
new movement: first as an obscure private association and *superstitio*, later as a serious
intellectual and religious rival. A central thesis is **revisionist**: Rome was not irreligious or
gratuitously cruel, and the pagan critics were often perceptive, raising objections (to creation *ex
nihilo*, bodily resurrection, the incarnation, Christianity's apostasy from Judaism, its corrosion of
the social order) that Christian thinkers were forced to answer. The book is organized around named
observers.
