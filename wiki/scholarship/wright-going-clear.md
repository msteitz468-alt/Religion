# Going Clear: Scientology, Hollywood, and the Prison of Belief

**Lawrence Wright** (Alfred A. Knopf, 2013). Investigative journalism expanded from Wright's New Yorker profile "The Apostate: Paul Haggis vs. the Church of Scientology" (Feb. 14, 2011). Based on hundreds of interviews, court documents, and archival sources; fact-checked through a landmark nine-month process that generated 971 questions and 48 three-ring binders of church responses.

## Coverage Ledger

Read in full: Introduction, Chapters 1–11, Epilogue, Acknowledgments and Note on Sources, Notes (full), Bibliography, Index. **Complete ingest.**

## Methodology

Investigative journalism + ex-member testimony + documentary/court record. Primary narrative frame: **Paul Haggis**, the Academy Award-winning writer-director (*Crash*, *Million Dollar Baby*) who spent 34 years in Scientology before resigning publicly in August 2009. Haggis functions as the book's "witness" whose trajectory — entry, disillusionment, exit — organizes each phase of the wider institutional history.

Significant witnesses: Marc Headley, Claire Headley, Marty Rathbun, Mike Rinder, Tom De Vocht, Amy Scobee, Hana Eltringham Whitfield, Tommy Davis, Jessica Feshbach, Karen de la Carriere, Jason Beghe, John Brousseau, Gary Morehead, Stephen "Sarge" Pfauth, Jim Dincalci, Spanky Taylor, among many others.

Robert Jay Lifton (*Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism*, 1961) provided an evaluative framework applied to Scientology; Lifton read the manuscript and confirmed its application.

Wright's primary methodological constraint, noted in the Acknowledgments: the Church never made David Miscavige or senior executives available for interview; many were, as Wright later learned, sequestered in "the Hole."

## Key Contributions to the Wiki

### On L. Ron Hubbard (Chapters 1–3, Notes)
- **Parsons/Crowley connection** corroborated and detailed: "Babalon Working" (early 1946); Hubbard as Parsons's "Scribe"; alleged theft of $20,970 and Betty Northrup; Crowley's warning to Parsons
- **Military record forgery**: two St. Louis archivists formally pronounced the Church's "Notice of Separation" document a forgery — phantom signatory "Howard D. Thompson," font inconsistencies, medals that did not exist until after Hubbard's service
- **Medical/psychological record**: "paranoid personality" diagnosis, "manic depressive" assessment; Veterans Administration records showing no combat wounds; "malignant narcissism" diagnosis
- **Hubbard's "Admissions" (Affirmations)**: private self-help document (church disputes authenticity) in which Hubbard describes personal shortcomings and instructs himself to overcome them, including sexual issues; authenticated by content overlap with correspondence
- **Quentin Hubbard's death** (October 1976): found unconscious in Las Vegas; died two months later; found with semen in rectum; ruled accidental; Hubbard's reaction: "That little shit—he's done it to me again"
- **Final years**: living in Blue Bird motor home at Creston ranch; six weeks before death told "Sarge" Pfauth he had "failed" and was leaving; Pfauth built a Tesla-coil/E-Meter "suicide machine" at Hubbard's request to "blow" a body thetan; it blew up the E-Meter; Hubbard died January 24, 1986 of a stroke (hydroxyzine in system)
- **Between-lives doctrine**: Hubbard taught the thetan experiences implantation at "Heaven" between lives (explicitly described as a 43-trillion-year-old electronic implant station); this is why Scientology aims to free the thetan from the cycle

### On Tom Cruise (Chapters 6, 8–9, Epilogue)
- Introduced to Scientology 1986 by Mimi Rogers; Rogers's father Philip Spickler confirms; Rogers denies
- OT III completion at Gold Base; audited by Marty Rathbun (sessions secretly filmed by Miscavige)
- Cruise's connection gave Miscavige enormous leverage: "Every minute of every hour someone reaches for LRH technology, simply because they know Tom Cruise is a Scientologist" (Miscavige at 2004 IAS event)
- **Freedom Medal of Valor** awarded October 2004 at Freewinds
- Church resources used on Cruise's behalf: house renovations, Mimi Rogers divorce, Nicole Kidman management, arranged girlfriend (Nazanin Boniadi)
- **Nazanin Boniadi episode** (November 2004–January 2005): security-checked one month, $20,000 Rodeo Drive wardrobe, met Cruise at NY Org, lived in his house, dismissed after insulting Miscavige at a restaurant; forced to scrub toilets and do menial labor for revealing the relationship
- Church assisted Cruise–Kidman marriage; Miscavige was best man at Telluride wedding December 1990; assisted in Nicole Kidman's management and eventual divorce

### On the IRS Settlement (Chapter 7)
- October 8, 1993: Miscavige announced "The war is over!" at IAS event
- Settled for $12.5 million vs. the claimed $1 billion liability
- Secured complete tax exemption for all Scientology entities
- Wright reports that Miscavige and Rathbun walked unannounced into IRS headquarters and negotiated directly; full terms remain classified under IRS privacy rules
- The price included church agreeing to stop all litigation against IRS and individual IRS officials
- Critics (ex-IRS officials) alleged the church's surveillance/private investigation of IRS employees created coercive conditions

### Comparative Religion Analysis (Epilogue)
Wright compares Scientology to: early Mormonism (Joseph Smith's documented fraud vs. religion's growth), Christian Science (alternative healing + "scientific" claims), the Amish (enforced conformity, disconnection analogs), Branch Davidians, Jonestown, Aum Shinrikyo. Frames Scientology within the broader American tradition of religious innovation and persecution. Wright's conclusion: Scientology's legal standing as a religion is settled (IRS 1993; US courts); the more important question is whether it uses that protection to cover human-rights abuses.

Lifton's eight criteria for thought reform applied in Chapter 4; Wright quotes Lifton directly on "milieu control," "sacred science," "thought-terminating clichés," and "apparent converts."
