# A Short History of the Bahá'í Faith

Peter Smith (1995), Oneworld Publications, Oxford. 172 pages. Part of the Oneworld "Short History" series. Smith is a sociologist and world historian at Mahidol University, Thailand; his fuller treatment is *The Babi and Bahá'í Religions* (referenced in preface).

The book offers a concise academic narrative of the Bahá'í Faith from its roots in mid-nineteenth-century Iranian Shi'ism through the Babi movement to the contemporary Bahá'í world community (~5 million adherents as of 1995). Smith explicitly brackets "faith-dimension" and theological interpretation, presenting a sociological-historical account.

## Key Claims and Contributions

- The Bahá'í Faith emerged from Babism, which itself arose within Twelver Shi'ism in mid-nineteenth-century Iran.
- The Báb (Siyyid 'Alí-Muḥammad, 1817–1850) and Bahá'u'lláh (Mírzá Ḥusayn-'Alí Núrí, 1817–1892) are both regarded by Bahá'ís as divine Manifestations; Bahá'ís also include Krishna, Zoroaster, the Buddha, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muḥammad in the list.
- Smith stresses that calling the Bahá'í Faith "a Muslim sect" is as inaccurate as calling second-century Christianity "a Jewish sect."
- The Bahá'í Administrative Order (local and national Spiritual Assemblies, Universal House of Justice) is a distinctive institutional innovation with no close parallel in Islam.
