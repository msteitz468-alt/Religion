## Overview

The *Visuddhimagga* ("Path of Purification") is the most authoritative non-canonical text of
[[theravada|Theravada]] Buddhism, composed by [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]] in 5th-century Sri Lanka at
the Mahavihara monastery. It is a comprehensive manual of Theravada doctrine and meditation practice,
organized around the **threefold training**: virtue (*sīla*), concentration (*samādhi*), and
understanding/wisdom (*paññā*). The work synthesizes the scattered meditation instructions of the Pali
Canon into a single ordered system and codifies Theravada Abhidhamma analysis of mind and matter.
The opening verse (I.1) encapsulates the whole:

> "When a wise man, established well in virtue, / Develops consciousness and understanding, / Then as
> a bhikkhu ardent and sagacious, / He succeeds in disentangling this tangle."

## Structure

### Part I — Virtue (*Sīla*, Chs. I–II)

**Ch. I: Description of Virtue (*Sīla-niddesa*)** — Comprehensive treatment of the Theravada
monastic code. Virtue is both the foundation for the higher practices and the "soil" from which
concentration and understanding grow. Covers the types of virtue (individual, recited, restraint of
sense faculties, livelihood, reflection on requisites), their grades (inferior/medium/superior), and
their purification. The five *khandha* of virtue + concentration + wisdom + liberation +
knowledge-of-liberation-and-vision are presented as the formal structure of the path.

**Ch. II: Ascetic Practices (*Dhutaṅga-niddesa*)** — The thirteen optional austerities for
bhikkhus (wearing rag robes, eating only alms, living under a tree, etc.), which "shake off"
(*dhuta*) defilements associated with sensual comfort. Not required but recommended as supports for
the temperament that benefits from simplicity and renunciation.

### Part II — Concentration (*Samādhi*, Chs. III–XIII)

**Ch. III: Taking a Meditation Subject** — The theoretical framework for the whole of Part II. The
meditator identifies a **temperament** (*carita*): greedy, hating, deluded, faithful, intelligent, or
speculative. Forty **meditation subjects** (*kammaṭṭhāna*) are catalogued with which temperaments
they suit. Key distinction: **access concentration** (*upacāra-samādhi*) — achieved proximity to
*jhāna* but not absorbed — vs. **absorption concentration** (*appanā-samādhi*) — full absorption.
The distinction between **serenity** (*samatha*) and **insight** (*vipassanā*) is established: the
forty subjects can be developed for serenity up to the four material and four immaterial jhānas; a
subset can produce direct-knowledges (*abhiññā*). Only insight leads to liberation.

**Chs. IV–VII: The Kasinas, Foulness, and Recollections** — Detailed instruction on ten *kasina*
objects (earth, water, fire, air, blue, yellow, red, white, light, space); ten kinds of foulness
(corpse meditations); mindfulness of the body (*kāyagatā-sati*, in thirty-two parts); ten
recollections (Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, deities, mindfulness of death, body,
breathing, peace); contemplation of the four elements; mindfulness of in-and-out breathing
(*ānāpānasati*) — analyzed in great detail as the most highly recommended subject. All produce
access concentration; the kasinas and breathing produce *jhāna*.

**Ch. VIII: The Four Brahmaviharas (*Appamāññā*)** — Loving-kindness (*mettā*), compassion (*karuṇā*),
sympathetic joy (*muditā*), equanimity (*upekkhā*). Full meditative cultivation with the specific
phrases and the "near enemies" (love → sentimentality; compassion → grief; joy → excitement;
equanimity → indifference). The brahmaviharas produce *jhāna* (the first three up to the third
*jhāna* in the fivefold reckoning; equanimity only the fourth). Loving-kindness analyzed in eleven
benefits (A XI 16).

**Ch. IX: The Immaterial States (*Arūpa*)** — Four immaterial jhānas: base of boundless space (*ākāsānañcāyatana*), base of boundless consciousness (*viññāṇañcāyatana*), base of nothingness (*ākiñcaññāyatana*), base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (*nevasaññānāsaññāyatana*). Detailed accounts of entry, mastery (*vasiṭā*, five kinds: adverting, attaining, resolving, emerging, reviewing), and the phenomenology of each.

**Ch. X: Description of Concentration** — Systematic account of the four material jhānas in the
fivefold counting (splitting the first jhāna into two — with and without applied thought). Each jhāna
defined by its **factors** (applied thought, sustained thought, joy, pleasure, equanimity,
one-pointedness) and the progressive abandonment of factors as the jhāna deepen. The distinction
between jhāna factors, path factors, and enlightenment factors mapped here for Part III.

**Chs. XI–XIII: Direct-Knowledges (*Abhiññā*)** — Five mundane supernormal powers: supernormal
powers (*iddhividha*), divine ear (*dibbasota*), mind-reading (*cetopariyañāṇa*), recollection of
past lives (*pubbenivāsānussati*), divine eye (*dibbacakkhu*). Plus the sixth, supramundane:
**knowledge of destruction of cankers** (*āsavakkhayañāṇa*) = Arahantship. Practical instruction
for cultivating each, with canonical narratives.

### Part III — Understanding / Wisdom (*Paññā*, Chs. XIV–XXIII)

**Ch. XIV: Description of the Aggregates (*Khandha-niddesa*)** — The five aggregates (materiality,
feeling, perception, formations, consciousness) analyzed in full Abhidhamma detail. Materiality
broken into derived vs. primary, types by origin (kamma-born, consciousness-born, temperature-born,
nutriment-born). Consciousness broken into 89 (or 121) types. The analysis here is the
basis for the insight meditation in Chs. XVIII–XXI.

**Ch. XV: Bases and Elements (*Āyatana/Dhātu*)** — The twelve sense bases (six internal: eye, ear,
nose, tongue, body, mind; six external: visible form, sound, odor, flavor, tangible object, mental
objects) and eighteen elements (*dhātu*). The sense-door process explained in detail as basis for
the path.

**Ch. XVI: Faculties and Truths (*Indriya/Sacca*)** — The twenty-two faculties (faith, energy,
mindfulness, concentration, understanding, and others including the three supramundane faculties
of one who will know, of one knowing, of one who has known). The **Four Noble Truths** analyzed at
length — suffering defined (in brief: the five aggregates as objects of clinging), origin (craving,
in three forms), cessation (Nibbāna), path (the Noble Eightfold Path).

**Ch. XVII: Dependent Origination (*Paṭicca-samuppāda*)** — The most technically dense chapter
before the insight sequence. The **twelve nidānas** analyzed in both forward (arising) and reverse
(cessation) order, with each link unpacked: ignorance → formations → consciousness → name-and-form
→ six bases → contact → feeling → craving → clinging → becoming → birth → ageing-and-death. The
round traverses three lifetimes (past → present → future), with kamma and its results as the causal
mechanism. Buddhaghosa's resolution of the apparent infinity regress (*regressus ad infinitum*):
the wheel revolves as long as ignorance and craving persist; "for there is no other beginning
discernible." Key controversy: whether name-and-form and consciousness are mutually dependent or
sequential.

**Ch. XVIII: Purification of View (*Diṭṭhi-visuddhi*)** — The first of five "purifications of
understanding" (the second and third purifications being virtue and concentration in Parts I–II).
The meditator resolves what seems to be a "self" into bare mentality-materiality (*nāma-rūpa*) by
delimiting the four primary material elements by the **characteristic method** (hardness, fluidity,
heat, motion) and the **function method**, and by identifying the arising of mentality from
materiality at the six sense doors. This dissolves the illusion of a persisting experiencer.

**Ch. XIX: Purification by Overcoming Doubt** — The meditator traces the causal conditions behind
the arising of mentality-materiality (past kamma, past ignorance and craving), seeing "in the past
there was only name-and-form, not a being"; extends understanding to past lives and future lives.
This directly uproots the *causal skeptic* view.

**Ch. XX: Purification by Knowledge and Vision of What Is and Is Not the Path** — Comprehension by
groups (*kalāpa-sammasana*): the meditator groups all mentality-materiality into impermanent/painful/
not-self, extending comprehension across all eleven instances (past/future/present × internal/external ×
gross/subtle × inferior/superior × far/near). The **ten imperfections of insight** (*vipassanupakkilesa*)
arise at this stage in a meditator on the right course: illumination, knowledge, rapturous happiness,
tranquillity, bliss, resolution, exertion, assurance, equanimity, and attachment to these as subtle
defilements. The meditator recognizes these are not the path and continues.

**Ch. XXI: Purification by Knowledge and Vision of the Way (*Paṭipadā-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi*)** —
The core insight sequence, comprising **eight insight knowledges** plus conformity (ninth):

1. **Knowledge of rise and fall** (free from the ten imperfections)
2. **Knowledge of dissolution** — mindfulness brought only to cessation/breakup; culminating point of impermanence
3. **Knowledge of appearance as terror** — formations in all kinds of becoming appear as great terror
4. **Knowledge of danger** — no asylum, shelter, or refuge in any becoming; non-arising is safety; non-arising is Nibbāna
5. **Knowledge of dispassion** — mind tends/inclines/leans toward "Non-arising is safety"
6. **Knowledge of desire for deliverance** — desirous of being delivered from whole field of formations
7. **Knowledge of reflection** — again discerns three characteristics; foul aspect emphasized; snake-charmer simile for "contriving means to deliverance"
8. **Knowledge of equanimity about formations** — abandons both terror and delight; indifferent and neutral; divorced-wife simile; land-finding crow simile

Then: three "triple gateway" liberations (signless, desireless, void); seven kinds of noble persons
(faith devotee through liberated by understanding) dependent on which characteristic is predominant
at emergence; insight leading to emergence (equanimity about formations + conformity + change-of-lineage).
Twelve similes for the whole sequence (fruit bat, black snake, house, oxen, ghoul, child, hunger, thirst,
cold, heat, darkness, poison). Discussion of how equanimity about formations governs differences
in path factors, jhāna factors, mode of progress (difficult/easy, sluggish/swift), and kind of
liberation.

**Ch. XXII: Purification by Knowledge and Vision (*Ñāṇadassana-visuddhi*)** — The supramundane
paths and fruits. **Change-of-lineage knowledge** (intermediate, unassignable to either the sixth or
seventh purification) takes Nibbāna as object for the first time; conformity knowledges dispel the
murk of defilements hiding the truths, while change-of-lineage actually sees Nibbāna but cannot
dispel defilements. Path knowledge follows without pausing, piercing and exploding the masses of
greed, hate, and delusion never pierced before. Detailed account of the four paths and fruits
(stream-entry, once-return, non-return, Arahantship) and their associated noble persons (stream-enterer
through Arahant). Thorough analysis of the thirty-seven states partaking of enlightenment; the four
functions of path knowledge (full-understanding, abandoning, realizing, developing) exercised
simultaneously in one moment; the three kinds of abandoning (suppressing, substitution of opposites,
cutting off); and the sixteen ways the four truths are penetrated simultaneously.

**Ch. XXIII: Benefits of Developing Understanding** — Four main benefits: (A) removal of various
defilements; (B) experience of the taste of the noble fruit; (C) ability to attain the attainment
of cessation (*nirodha-samāpatti*); (D) achievement of worthiness to receive gifts. The attainment
of cessation analyzed in eleven questions: it is the non-occurrence of consciousness and its
concomitants; only non-returners and Arahants with the eight attainments can attain it; it requires
two powers (serenity and insight), tranquilization of three formations (verbal, bodily, mental),
sixteen kinds of exercise of knowledge, and nine kinds of exercise of concentration; it lasts as
determined (up to seven days); it requires prior resolution on non-damage to others' property,
Community's waiting, Master's summons, and limit of duration; the preparatory task (striving with
both serenity and insight through progressive jhānas to the base of nothingness, then the fourfold
preparatory task) is required.

**Conclusion** — Buddhaghosa's closing colophon names his patron (Elder Saṅghapāla of the
Mahavihara), affirms the work is "almost free from errors and from flaws after collating all the
expositions of all these meanings classed as virtue and so on stated in the commentarial system of
the five Nikāyas," and prays that it may continue to show the way to purity "for just as long as
this world / Shall last that name 'Enlightened One.'" The Pali text comprises fifty-eight
recitation sections. A Sinhalese-manuscript colophon expresses hope for seeing Metteyya (the future
Buddha).

## Key Doctrinal Contributions

**Seven purifications** (*satta-visuddhi*) — the path organized as a sequence:
(1) Purification of virtue → (2) Purification of mind (= concentration) → (3) Purification of
view → (4) Purification by overcoming doubt → (5) Purification by knowledge and vision of what is
and is not the path → (6) Purification by knowledge and vision of the way → (7) Purification by
knowledge and vision (= the supramundane paths). Based on the Rathavinīta Sutta (MN 24).

**The forty meditation subjects** (*kammaṭṭhāna*) and their allocation to temperament — the
Visuddhimagga's most-cited practical schema.

**The Abhidhamma analysis of materiality** — four types by origin (kamma, consciousness, temperature,
nutriment); the material octad (*aṭṭhaka*) as the minimal unit of material existence. The
life-continuum (*bhavaṅga*) as the substrate of personal continuity between moments of active
consciousness.

**The cognitive series** (*vīthi*) — the moment-by-moment analysis of how a sense-door or mind-door
process unfolds: life-continuum → adverting → five-door → receiving → investigating → determining
→ seven impulsions → two registration consciousnesses → life-continuum. Path arises at one of the
middle impulsions, with change-of-lineage preceding it.

**The attainment of cessation** (*nirodha-samāpatti*) — progressive suppression of consciousness
through the material and immaterial jhānas, culminating in the complete cessation of consciousness
and mental concomitants; available only to non-returners and Arahants with the eight attainments;
not death (bodily formations, heat, life span remain).

## Hermeneutical Notes

Buddhaghosa's method throughout is **commentarial-harmonizing**: he gathers the scattered instruction
of the Pali suttas and Abhidhamma, resolves apparent tensions by careful definition and taxonomy, and
builds a single coherent system. The Paṭisambhidāmagga (*Paṭis*) is his most-cited canonical source
for the detailed insight sequence (Chs. XVIII–XXII). Disputes within the Mahavihara tradition
(Chs. XXI.112–116 on the three elders' theories) are recorded without adjudication. The Suttanta
method (allowing three liberations) and the Abhidhamma method (allowing only two) are both recorded
with the technical reason for their divergence explained.
