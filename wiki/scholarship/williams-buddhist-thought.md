# Williams, Tribe & Wynne, *Buddhist Thought* (2000/2012)

A scholarly survey of the **Indian** Buddhist intellectual tradition — from the
[[gautama-buddha|Buddha]] to the tantras — by **[[paul-williams|Paul Williams]]** (the early
teaching, Mahayana, Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, Buddha-nature) with **Alexander Wynne** (origins
and meditation) and **Anthony Tribe** (the tantric *Mantranaya*). It is the wiki's
**academic-Buddhology** anchor, set beside the practitioner expositions of
[[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bodhi]], [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]], and [[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]].

## Coverage

- **The Buddha and the early teaching** — the hagiography as illustration of the Dharma; the
  "undeclared questions" and the parables of the **arrow** and the **raft** (the teaching is
  pragmatic and soteriological, but still held to be *objectively true*); the
  [[four-noble-truths|Four Truths]]; **not-Self** via the *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta*;
  [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]]; rebirth without a Self (the "A → B"
  causal analysis).
- **[[abhidharma|Abhidharma]]** — the program of redescribing experience "with maximum
  possible exactitude" into momentary *dharmas* and their causal relations.
- **Mahāyāna origins** — "the biggest internal development and division within Buddhism"; a
  matter of *vision and motivation* (the [[bodhisattva|bodhisattva ideal]]) rather than a
  schism; its diversity and openness to ongoing revelation.
- **[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** — [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s emptiness; the two truths;
  the nihilism objection.
- **[[yogacara|Yogācāra]]** — "mind-only"; the **three natures**; emptiness redefined as the
  dependent substratum's being empty of *subject–object duality*; Yogācāra as the antidote
  to a nihilist reading of Madhyamaka.
- **[[buddha-nature|Buddha-nature (tathāgatagarbha)]]** — the factor in each being enabling
  buddhahood; the startling use of *ātman* ("Self") for it in some sūtras.
- **Buddhas and bodhisattvas** — the multiple "bodies" (*kāya*) of the Buddha; the great
  celestial bodhisattvas (Maitreya, Avalokiteśvara, Tārā, Mañjuśrī).
- **Mantranaya / tantra** (Tribe) — mantra, visualization, *maṇḍala*, and the *sādhana* as
  the "ritualization of metaphysics."

## Key Interpretive Claims

- **Not-Self means no Self at all.** Williams argues against the soft reading that the Buddha
  "only showed what is *not* the Self" while leaving room for a transcendent Self: had the
  Buddha accepted a Self behind the [[five-aggregates|aggregates]] he would have said so
  plainly, as the [[upanishads|Upaniṣads]] do. The *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* gives only
  denials; liberation comes from *letting go*, with "no need to postulate a Self." (Supports
  the [[anatta|anatta]] page.)
- **Yogācāra is anti-nihilist, not idealist-for-its-own-sake.** Its "Mind" posits a real
  dependent substratum precisely so that emptiness does not collapse into "there is
  nothing" — a different solution from [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]'s.
- **Buddha-nature is soteriology, not ontology.** The *tathāgatagarbha* literature is about
  *encouragement* — every being can become a Buddha — and its near-*ātman* language is to
  be read in that light, a perennial interpretive problem.

## Significance for the Wiki

Anchors two long-referenced but pageless topics — the **[[yogacara|Yogācāra]]** school and
**[[buddha-nature|Buddha-nature]]** — completing the map of the great Indian Mahayana
philosophical schools (Madhyamaka + Yogācāra), and deepens [[abhidharma|Abhidharma]],
[[anatta|not-Self]], and the [[mahayana|Mahayana]] pantheon from a rigorous academic
standpoint.
