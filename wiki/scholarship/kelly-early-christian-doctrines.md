## Overview

The standard one-volume survey of the development of Christian doctrine from the
sub-apostolic age to the Council of Chalcedon (451) and shortly after. J. N. D. Kelly
(1909–1997), Principal of St Edmund Hall, Oxford, and Patristic Studies lecturer, traces
doctrinal evolution thematically rather than author-by-author: the Trinity, Christology,
sin and grace, the work of Christ, ecclesiology, the sacraments, and eschatology, each
followed from the Apostolic Fathers through the great fourth- and fifth-century settlements.
A descriptive historical work (Kelly deliberately abstains from adjudicating "the
fundamental doctrinal issues" or assessing the impact of Hellenism), prized for its
sober summary of patristic positions with dense primary-source citation.

This source is **largely an extension of existing wiki pages** on patristic Christianity
(see [[trinity]], [[logos-christology]], [[incarnation]], [[arian-controversy]],
[[chalcedonian-definition]], [[origen]], [[athanasius]], [[augustine]], etc.) rather than a
generator of many new pages. File lean: extend before creating.

## Scope Plan

The work is partitioned into four scopes, one per Part, worked in order:

1. **Scope 1 — Part I: Prolegomena** (chs. I–III, lines ~377–3289). The background
   (Roman-empire religion, Graeco-Roman philosophy, Neo-Platonism, Judaism, Gnosticism);
   Tradition and Scripture (the rule of faith, Irenaeus and Tertullian, the appeal to the
   Fathers); the Holy Scriptures (OT, NT canon, inspiration, unity of the Testaments,
   typology/allegory, the Antiochene reaction).
2. **Scope 2 — Part II: The Pre-Nicene Theology** (chs. IV–VIII, lines ~3290–8725). The
   divine Triad; third-century Trinitarianism (Monarchianism, Hippolytus, Tertullian,
   Clement, Origen); the beginnings of Christology; man and his redemption; the Christian
   community (ecclesiology, early sacraments, penitential discipline).
3. **Scope 3 — Part III: From Nicaea to Chalcedon** (chs. IX–XVI, lines ~8726–17933). The
   Nicene crisis and Arius; the doctrine of the Trinity (homoousion, the Cappadocians,
   Augustine); fourth-century Christology (Apollinarianism, Antiochene/Alexandrian); the
   Christological settlement (Nestorius, Cyril, Eutyches, Leo, Chalcedon); fallen man and
   grace (Pelagius, Augustine, original sin, predestination); Christ's saving work;
   Christ's mystical body (ecclesiology, the Roman primacy); the later sacraments.
4. **Scope 4 — Part IV: Epilogue** (ch. XVII, lines ~17934–~19150). The Christian hope /
   eschatology (the tension in eschatology, Origen, resurrection of the body, parousia and
   judgment, life everlasting).

## Hermeneutical Framing

Kelly writes as a historical theologian in the descriptive Anglican patristics tradition.
He tracks the **Christian Quadriga in formation** — especially the patristic tension
between [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]] and the [[figurative-interpretation|Antiochene literal-historical (theoria)]] reading — and the gradual hardening of the
**rule of faith** ([[rule-of-faith]]) and creedal norms. He is careful to date developments
and to distinguish what a Father actually held from later orthodox retrojection.

### Scope 1 — Prolegomena (chs. I–III, read in full 2026-06-28)

- **Ch. I (Background)** largely maps onto existing, already-rich wiki pages — [[middle-platonists|Middle Platonism]], [[neoplatonism]]/[[plotinus]], Stoicism, [[philo-of-alexandria]], [[gnosticism]]/[[gnostics]], [[demiurge]], [[pleroma]], [[emanation]], [[mystery-cults]], [[manichaeism]] — so it was filed lean (no new background pages). Kelly's distinctive framing: the East–West theological temperament split and the Constantine/Nicaea (325) dividing line; the Stoic/[[philo-of-alexandria|Philonic]] *logos endiathetos / logos prophorikos* distinction underlying later [[logos-christology|Logos Christology]]; the four shared Gnostic tenets (radical dualism, a fallen Demiurge as world-maker = the OT God, a divine spark in the elite, descending mediator) with redemption *by knowledge*.
- **Ch. II (Tradition and Scripture)** is the major new contribution. Kelly's thesis: for the Fathers Scripture and tradition are "complementary authorities… coincident in content," not two separate sources (the two-source reading is anachronistic). Generated new pages: [[apostolic-succession]], [[consensus-patrum]], [[vincent-of-lerins]]; substantially extended [[rule-of-faith]] (Irenaeus' "canon of truth", Tertullian's *regula fidei*).
- **Ch. III (The Holy Scriptures)** generated [[inspiration-of-scripture]] (ecstatic/possession vs. orthodox-conscious models; Augustine & Theodore as the two analytic pioneers) and [[theoria]] (Antiochene exegesis vs. Alexandrian allegory), plus new exegete pages [[diodore-of-tarsus]], [[theodore-of-mopsuestia]], [[john-chrysostom]]. Extended [[typology]] (was a stub), [[allegorical-reading]] (Origen's threefold body/soul/spirit sense), and [[nt-canon-formation]] (Montanism's role; Athanasius' 367 letter as first 27-book list; the broader [[septuagint]] OT canon).

**Contradiction noted (not adjudicated):** Kelly reads the Fathers as holding Scripture and tradition *coincident*; Walter Bauer (see [[bauer-thesis]]) reads the rule of faith as largely a victors' construct. Recorded on [[rule-of-faith]].

### Scope 2 — Pre-Nicene Theology (chs. IV–VIII, read in full 2026-06-28)
Pre-Nicene Trinity, Christology, anthropology/redemption, and ecclesiology/sacraments. New pages: [[monarchianism]], [[patripassianism]], [[subordinationism]], [[oikonomia]], [[spirit-christology]], [[recapitulation]] (concepts); [[hippolytus]], [[novatian]], [[sabellius]], [[paul-of-samosata]], [[methodius]] (figures/commentators). Extended [[trinity]], [[logos-christology]], [[modalism]], [[adoptionism]], [[incarnation]], [[eucharist]], [[baptism]], [[sacrament-of-penance]], [[origen]], [[tertullian]], [[irenaeus]], [[cyprian]]. Key themes: the [[oikonomia|economic Trinitarianism]] of the Apologists/Hippolytus/Tertullian; [[origen]]'s eternal generation and three hypostases; [[recapitulation]] as the master soteriological motif; [[ransom-to-whom|ransom]] vs. sacrifice; Cyprianic ecclesiology (*extra ecclesiam nulla salus*).

### Scope 3 — Nicaea to Chalcedon (chs. IX–XVI, read in full 2026-06-28)
The Trinitarian and Christological settlements, grace, soteriology, ecclesiology, and the later sacraments. New pages: [[homoousion]], [[theotokos]], [[logos-sarx-logos-anthropos]], [[semi-pelagianism]], [[confirmation]], [[traducianism]] (concepts); [[pelagian-controversy]] (controversy); [[leo-the-great]], [[eutyches]], [[pelagius]], [[cyril-of-jerusalem]], [[optatus]], [[hilary-of-poitiers]], [[marcellus-of-ancyra]], [[marius-victorinus]] (figures/commentators). Extended [[arian-controversy]], [[trinity]], [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction]], [[chalcedonian-definition]], [[hypostatic-union]], [[communicatio-idiomatum]], [[original-sin]], [[grace]], [[predestination]], [[eucharist]], the Cappadocians, [[athanasius]], [[cyril-of-alexandria]], [[augustine]]. Key contradiction (recorded on [[original-sin]]): the East affirms inherited mortality and a *rhopē* to sin but denies shared guilt; the West (Augustine) insists on the *massa damnata* and shared *reatus*.

### Scope 4 — Epilogue: The Christian Hope (ch. XVII, read in full 2026-06-28)
Eschatology. New page [[apokatastasis]] (concept). Extended [[bodily-resurrection]], [[millennialism]], [[last-judgment]], [[eternal-punishment]], [[purgatory]], [[beatific-vision]], [[origen]]. Key axis: the second-century chiliasm (Papias, Justin, Irenaeus) and Origen's spiritualised, [[apokatastasis|universalist]] eschatology vs. the hardening fifth-century doctrine of eternal punishment (Augustine).
