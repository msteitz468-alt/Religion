# Rigvedic Religion

> **Sect page.** The earliest phase of [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]], the religion of the
> [[rig-veda|Rig Veda]]. *(Ingested: the Rig Veda.)*

## The Religion of the Hymns

The oldest stratum: a relatively **fluid, exuberant worship of the *devas*** (gods) through the **hymns** of
the [[rig-veda|Rig Veda]] and the **fire sacrifice**. The chief gods are the heroic storm-king
**[[indra|Indra]]**, the fire-priest **[[agni|Agni]]**, the cosmic sovereign **[[varuna|Varuna]]**, and the
exhilarating **[[soma|Soma]]**. There are no temples, images, or fixed dogma — religion is **invocation,
praise, and offering**, asking the gods for cattle, sons, victory, and long life.

## Henotheism

A famous feature of Rigvedic religion (named "**henotheism**" by Max Müller from exactly this material): the
hymns **exalt whichever god is being addressed as supreme**, the greatest of all, without denying the
others — each god in turn becomes, for the moment, the whole. This fluid, non-exclusive exaltation is
distinct from both polytheism and monotheism. The late Rig Veda also begins to ask **cosmogonic and
philosophical questions** (the creation hymns) that point toward the [[upanishads|Upanishads]] and
[[hinduism|Hinduism]].
