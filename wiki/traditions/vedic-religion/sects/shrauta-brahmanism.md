# Shrauta Brahmanism

> **Sect page.** The later, ritualist phase of [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]] — religion as the **exact
> performance of the great sacrifices**. *(Stub.)*

## The Supremacy of the Sacrifice

In the Brahmana period the focus shifted from the hymns' praise of the gods to the **mechanics and meaning of
the sacrifice itself**. The **yajna (fire sacrifice)** became all-important and immensely elaborate —
multi-day **soma** rituals, the royal horse-sacrifice (*ashvamedha*), and fire-altars built to precise
geometry, performed by **specialized Brahmin priests** whose flawless execution was thought to **literally
sustain the cosmos**. The creator **[[prajapati|Prajapati]]** himself was said to have created the world *by*
sacrifice, and the sacrifice re-enacts and maintains creation.

## Ritual Over Gods

This is the purest **orthopraxy** in Indian religion: the **rite, correctly performed, compels the result** —
even the gods are subordinate to the power of the properly-executed sacrifice and the sacred word. This
exaltation of ritual (and of the Brahmin) provoked two great reactions: the **inward turn of the
[[upanishads|Upanishads]]** (seeking the reality *behind* the ritual, leading to [[hinduism|Vedanta]]), and
the **rejection of Vedic sacrifice** by the [[buddhism|Buddhist]] and Jain *shramana* movements.
