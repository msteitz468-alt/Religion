# Chinese folk religion

**Chronology:** see the [[chinese-folk-religion-timeline|Timeline of Chinese Folk Religion]] and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]].

**Chinese folk religion** (also *Chinese popular religion* or *Shenism*) is the **diffused, non-institutional
religion of the Chinese people** — the vast, syncretic field of gods, ghosts, ancestors, and spirits
(**shén** 神 and **guǐ** 鬼) worshipped at temples, household altars, roadside shrines, and seasonal festivals.
Unlike [[daoism|Daoism]], [[confucianism|Confucianism]], or Buddhism — the **"three teachings" (sanjiao)** it
freely absorbs — it has **no founder, no creed, no single canon, and no exclusive membership**. The sinologist
C. K. Yang's classic distinction calls it a **"diffused religion"**: not a separate institution one joins, but
religiosity **woven through** family, locality, occupation, and state.

## Self-understanding and the celestial bureaucracy

The organizing idea, recurring through **[[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's myths]]**, is that the unseen
world is a **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]** — a heavenly empire modeled exactly on imperial
China. At its apex sits the **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]] (Yù Huáng)**, the August Personage of Jade, who
rules Heaven as the earthly emperor rules the realm, through **ministries, departments, and ranked officials**:
gods of thunder, rain, wind, walls-and-moats, literature, wealth, war, medicine, and the **underworld courts**
of **[[diyu|Diyu]]**. Gods hold **office, are promoted and demoted, can be petitioned, bribed, and reported
on** — and, crucially, are very often **deified humans**: meritorious officials, generals, and virtuous people
**apotheosized** by imperial canonization or popular acclaim. Divinity is, in effect, a **bureaucratic
appointment**.

## Doctrine, cult, and the dead

- **Tian and Shangdi** — "Heaven" (Tiān 天) and the old high god **Shàngdì** 上帝 as the ultimate moral order
  and source of the **Mandate of Heaven**; see [[tian-shangdi|Tian / Shangdi]].
- **Ancestor veneration** — the dead remain members of the family, dependent on offerings and capable of
  blessing or harming descendants; the root of [[ancestor-veneration|the ancestral cult]] (filial piety
  extended past death).
- **Yin and yang and the cosmic order** — the cosmos as the interplay of **yīn/yáng** and the **five phases
  (wǔxíng)**; gods, ghosts, and qi all move within it; **fēngshuǐ** (geomancy) aligns the living and dead with
  it.
- **The dead and Diyu** — souls judged in the ten courts of **[[diyu|Diyu]]** under **[[yan-wang|Yan Wang]]**,
  punished and purified, then **reincarnated** (a Buddhist contribution thoroughly absorbed).
- **A vast pantheon** — from the **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]** down to the **[[kitchen-god|Kitchen God]]**
  on every hearth and the **[[tudi-gong|Earth God]]** of every neighborhood.

## Hermeneutics — syncretism and apotheosis

Chinese folk religion reads its gods through **two characteristic moves**: **syncretism** (a single temple may
honor Daoist immortals, the Buddhist **[[guanyin|Guanyin]]**, Confucian sages, and local deified heroes side by
side, with no sense of contradiction) and **apotheosis** (the continual promotion of humans into gods, recorded
in vast "investiture" narratives such as the **[[investiture-of-the-gods|Fengshen Yanyi]]**). The mythology is
carried not by scripture but by **temple cult, festival, opera, and popular novels** — *[[investiture-of-the-gods|Investiture
of the Gods]]* and *Journey to the West* function as de-facto mythological canon.

## Map of sub-traditions / cult-forms

- **[[celestial-bureaucracy-cult|The celestial-bureaucracy cult (shén worship)]]** — worship of the ranked gods
  under the Jade Emperor: city gods, ministry gods, the gods of the Werner pantheon.
- **[[ancestor-veneration|Ancestor veneration]]** — the domestic and lineage cult of the dead.
- **[[mazu-cult|The Mazu cult]]** — devotion to the deified sea-goddess Mazu (and, alongside her,
  [[guanyin|Guanyin]]) — the great popular saviour-goddess cults.
- **[[household-gods-cult|The household and local gods]]** — the [[kitchen-god|Kitchen God]],
  [[tudi-gong|Earth God]], door gods, and City God (Cheng Huang): the everyday tier of the bureaucracy.
