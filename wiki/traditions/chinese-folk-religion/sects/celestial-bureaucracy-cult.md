# The celestial-bureaucracy cult (shén worship)

The central cult-form of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]] is the worship of the **shén** 神 —
the ranked **gods of the [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]** — at temples great and small. It
treats the divine world as a **heavenly empire** that mirrors the earthly one: the **[[jade-emperor|Jade
Emperor]]** reigns at the top; beneath him are **ministries** (thunder, rain, fire, epidemics, wealth, war,
literature, walls-and-moats) staffed by gods who **hold office, wear rank, are promoted and demoted, receive
petitions, accept incense and offerings, and report to superiors**.

## Apotheosis and the investiture charter

The cult's defining doctrine is **apotheosis**: gods are very largely **deified humans** — generals,
officials, filial sons, chaste women, healers — **canonized** either by imperial decree (the state granting
divine titles, much as it granted official ranks) or by popular acclaim. Its great mythological charter is the
Ming novel **[[investiture-of-the-gods|Fengshen Yanyi]] ("The Investiture of the Gods")**, which narrates how,
after the Shang–Zhou war, the slain heroes of both sides are **enrolled by name into the offices of Heaven** —
a literal bureaucratic appointment of the dead to godhood. [[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's *Myths and
Legends of China*]] systematically catalogues this pantheon ministry by ministry.

## Practice

Worship is **transactional and reciprocal**: incense, food offerings, spirit-money, vows, and temple festivals
in exchange for protection, healing, sons, wealth, and good harvests; **divination** (poetry-slips, moon-blocks)
to read the god's will; and **opera and processions** on the god's "birthday." The historical **imperial state
cult** — the emperor sacrificing to Heaven, and granting and revoking gods' titles — was the apex of this same
system.
