# The Mazu cult (and the popular saviour-goddesses)

This cult-form gathers the **great popular goddess-devotions** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk
religion]] — above all **[[mazu|Mazu]]** (also *Matsu*; titled **Tiānhòu**, "Empress of Heaven"), the deified
sea-goddess, and alongside her the Buddhist-derived but thoroughly sinicized **[[guanyin|Guanyin]]**, the
"Goddess of Mercy." Together they are the **compassionate, accessible, life-saving face** of the pantheon, in
contrast to the remote majesty of the **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]** and the dread courts of
**[[diyu|Diyu]]**.

## Mazu

**Mazu** is the apotheosis of **Lín Mòniáng**, a real (10th-c.) fisherman's daughter of Fujian, famed for
piety and for **rescuing sailors** — said to have walked the waves and to have saved her father and brothers
from a storm by spirit-flight. After her early death she was venerated as the **protectress of seafarers,
fishermen, and travelers**, her cult spreading with Fujianese maritime trade across the southeast coast,
Taiwan, and the overseas Chinese world. The imperial state **canonized and re-titled her repeatedly**, raising
her from "Lady" to "Consort" to "Empress of Heaven" — a textbook case of [[celestial-bureaucracy|bureaucratic]]
apotheosis. Her temples (the *Tianhou* temples) and pilgrimage processions remain among the largest popular
religious events in the Chinese world.

## Guanyin alongside

**[[guanyin|Guanyin]]** — the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, transformed in China into a **female "Goddess of
Mercy"** — is venerated in the same devotional register: the one who **hears the cries of the world** and
saves all who call her name. Mazu and Guanyin are routinely honored together as the merciful mother-saviours of
the people.
