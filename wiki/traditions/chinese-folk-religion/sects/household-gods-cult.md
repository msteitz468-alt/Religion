# Household and local gods

This cult-form is the **everyday, ground-level tier** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]]: the
**tutelary gods of home and locality** who form the bottom ranks of the **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial
bureaucracy]]** — the petty officials of the divine civil service, closest to ordinary life.

- **[[kitchen-god|The Kitchen God (Zào Jūn)]]** — the most important household deity, who watches each family
  from his place above the stove all year and, just before the New Year, **ascends to report the family's
  conduct to the [[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]**. Families smear his paper image's mouth with **honey or sticky
  sweets** (to sweeten his report, or to glue it shut) before burning it to send him up.
- **[[tudi-gong|The Earth God (Tǔdì Gōng)]]** — the genial local god of a specific patch of ground,
  neighborhood, or field; the lowest-ranking but most ubiquitous god, in a small shrine on every street and
  farm, petitioned for everyday protection and prosperity.
- **The City God (Chénghuáng)** — the divine magistrate of a walled city, the spiritual counterpart of its
  human magistrate, who judges the recently dead and governs the local ghosts; his office links the household
  tier to the courts of **[[diyu|Diyu]]**.
- **Door Gods (Ménshén)** — the armored guardian-generals pasted on doors at New Year to bar demons; and the
  **God of Wealth (Cáishén)**, welcomed for prosperity.

These gods make the celestial bureaucracy **immediate and domestic**: every hearth, gate, and street corner
has its resident official, and the cosmic empire is felt in the management of daily life.
