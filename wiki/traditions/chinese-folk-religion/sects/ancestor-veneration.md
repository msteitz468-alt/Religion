# Ancestor veneration (the ancestral cult)

**Ancestor veneration** is the **oldest and most universal** stratum of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk
religion]] — older than [[daoism|Daoism]], [[confucianism|Confucianism]], or Buddhism, traceable to the
**Shang oracle-bone cult** of royal ancestors (c. 1600 BCE). Its premise is that **the dead remain members of
the family**: they pass into a spirit-existence but stay **dependent on their descendants' offerings** and
**able to bless or to afflict** them. The relationship is one of **mutual obligation** — the living feed and
honor the dead; the dead protect and prosper the living.

## Belief and practice

- **Spirit-tablets** (神主) on the domestic and lineage-hall altar house the ancestor's presence; offerings of
  food, incense, and spirit-money are made daily and at festivals (**Qingming**, the "tomb-sweeping" day, and
  the **Ghost Festival**).
- The **hún** 魂 (the higher, heaven-bound soul) and **pò** 魄 (the earth-bound, grave-bound soul) — a dual-soul
  anthropology shared with the wider tradition.
- **A neglected dead becomes a hungry ghost (guǐ)** — wandering, dangerous, and embittered; much folk practice
  aims to feed and pacify the ancestral and the unrelated dead alike.

## Relationship to the three teachings

**[[confucianism|Confucianism]]** reframed the ancestral cult as the supreme expression of **filial piety
(xiào)** and made it the moral heart of the family and state — so successfully that Confucius's own ancestral
rites became a model. **Buddhism** added the machinery of **[[diyu|Diyu]]**, karmic judgment, and merit-transfer
to aid the dead. The cult is thus the **common ground** on which the three teachings meet in ordinary life.

## Comparative note — the Chinese Rites Controversy

Ancestor veneration sits uneasily with the **exclusive monotheisms**. It was the heart of the **Chinese Rites
Controversy** (17th–18th c.), in which the Catholic Church debated whether honoring ancestors was **civil
respect for the dead** (the Jesuit accommodationist view, allowing converts to keep the rites) or **idolatry**
(the opposing Dominican/Franciscan view) — a dispute Rome eventually decided against the rites, crippling the
China mission. The practice contrasts sharply with the biblical prohibition on **consulting the dead**
(necromancy) and the Israelite marginalizing of any cult of the dead.
