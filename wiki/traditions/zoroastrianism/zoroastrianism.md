# Zoroastrianism

Top-level tradition page. **Zoroastrianism** is the ancient Iranian religion founded by the prophet
**[[zarathustra|Zarathustra]]** (Zoroaster), centered on **[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]** ("the Wise
Lord") as the supreme, wholly good God, and on a **cosmic moral dualism** between Ahura Mazda's
order and the hostile spirit **[[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]]** (Ahriman). It was the state religion
of three Persian empires (Achaemenid, Parthian, Sasanian) and survives today among the **Parsis** of
India and Iranian Zoroastrians.

Its outsized importance for this wiki is **influence-tracing**: Zoroastrianism is the prime candidate
source for ideas that later pervade the Abrahamic traditions — a structured opposition of good and evil,
ranked **angels and demons**, a coming **savior**, the **resurrection of the dead**, a **final judgment**,
and a renewed world — see [[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]].

## Canon

- The **[[avesta|Avesta]]** — the scripture, in Old/Young Avestan, much of it lost (the surviving
  corpus is a fraction of the Sasanian collection). Its liturgical heart is the **Yasna**.
- The **[[gathas|Gathas]]** — 17 hymns embedded in the Yasna, the **oldest stratum** and the only part
  attributed directly to [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]] himself; the theological core
  (`canonical_for: [zoroastrianism]`).
- The **Zand** — the Middle Persian (Pahlavi) translation-and-commentary, and later Pahlavi works
  ([[bundahishn|the Bundahishn]], Denkard) that preserve the cosmology and eschatology.

## Core Doctrine

- **[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]** — the one wholly good creator; worship is *Mazdayasna*. He evokes
  the **[[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]]** (the six Holy Immortals) and the lesser Yazatas.
- **[[asha-druj|Asha]]** vs. **Druj** — **Truth/Order** vs. **the Lie/disorder**: the cosmic and
  moral polarity.
- **Cosmic dualism** — the good spirit (Spenta Mainyu) and the hostile spirit
  **[[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]]** in conflict through a **[[the-three-times|bounded
  world-history]]** (Creation–Mixture–Separation).
- **Free moral choice**, judgment of the soul at the **[[chinvat-bridge|Chinvat Bridge]]**, the
  guardian **[[fravashi|fravashis]]**, and the **[[frashokereti|Frashokereti]]** — the final
  renovation: a savior (**[[saoshyant|Saoshyant]]**), the resurrection of the dead, a universal
  judgment, and the ultimate defeat of evil and renewal of the world.

## Hermeneutical Frameworks

Avestan **recitation** and priestly **Zand** commentary; for this wiki, **influence-tracing** is the key
mode — disciplined comparison of Zoroastrian and Abrahamic eschatology that distinguishes genuine
influence from parallel development (recorded without overclaiming).

## Practice and Observance

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], Zoroaster bound his community with a
**devotional system** that secured the faith's survival across millennia: the **five daily
prayers** (facing fire, the symbol of [[asha-druj|asha]]); the initiatory **sudreh and kusti** (the
sacred shirt and cord, the cord flicked in execration of [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] at prayer);
the **Fravarane** creed renouncing the Daevas; the **seven annual feasts** (the six *gahambars* and
**No Ruz**, the New Year that prefigures [[frashokereti|the final renovation]]); strict **purity
law**; and the **exposure of the dead** to avoid polluting earth and fire. Boyce treats living
Parsi and Iranian practice as primary evidence preserving genuinely ancient features.

## Historical Development

[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]] narrates the religion **historically and
geographically**, distinguishing the religion of the [[gathas|Gathas]] (Old Avestan), its
Young Avestan growth (the *yashts*, the *Videvdad*, the rise of the
[[mitra|yazatas]]), the religion of the [[persians|Ancient Persians]], and the codified
"orthodoxy" of the priestly books:

- **Achaemenid (c. 550–330 BCE)** — an "Avestan worldview" in the inscriptions: the
  *arta*/*drauga* ([[asha-druj|asha]]/Lie) ethic, the [[xwarenah|*xwarenah*]] of kingship,
  fire-worship without images, the [[anahita|Anahita]]/[[mitra|Mithra]] cult under
  Artaxerxes II, and the **[[paradise|*paradeisos*]]** garden. See [[persians|the
  Persians]].
- **Parthian / Arsacid (c. 247 BCE–224 CE)** — the earliest **fire temples** (Kuh-e
  Khajeh, Mele Hairam), dynastic fires, and the first ordered preservation of the
  scattered [[avesta|Avesta]] (Valakhsh).
- **Sasanian (224–651 CE)** — the establishment of an imperial **"religiocracy"** ("church
  and state born of one womb," the *Letter of Tosar*): royal **investiture** iconography
  and the *xwarrah*; the high priest **Kerdir**; the Denkard narrative of collecting the
  Avesta (Tosar under Ardashir, expansion under Shapur I, the **21-*nask*** recension
  under Shapur II and **Adurbad-i Mahraspandan**, who is the "*Zarathushtrotem*"); the
  invention of the Avestan alphabet; *poryotkesh* ("orthodoxy") and *zandik* ("heretic").
  Rose cautions that this codified orthodoxy may be a **relatively late, narrow priestly
  systematization** of a much broader lived religion.
- **Central Asia** — Zoroastrianism flourished among the **Sogdians** and in Chorasmia
  (and reached Cappadocia, Armenia, Georgia) with **regional, syncretistic** forms, until
  the Islamic conquest.
- **Islamic Iran (from 651 CE)** — under Muslim rule Zoroastrians became *dhimmi*, paying
  the *jizya*; the "**Gabr**" communities clustered in Yazd and Kerman; a great
  **apologetic literature** answered Islam, Christianity, and Judaism (the *Shkand
  Gumanig Wizar*, *Dadestan-i Denig*, the [[bundahishn|Bundahishn]], the redacted
  Denkard). Zoroastrian motifs fed Persian Sufi poetry (the *pir-e Moghan*), the *Sirat*
  bridge, and Suhrawardi's Illuminationism. The **Persian Rivayats** linked the surviving
  Iranians to the émigré [[parsis|Parsis]] of India.
- **India and diaspora** — the [[parsis|Parsi]] settlement of Gujarat and the modern
  global dispersion (see that page).

## The Sasanian Encounter with Judaism

Beyond the Persian-period influence question, Zoroastrianism is the **living neighbor** of rabbinic
Judaism in the **Sasanian Empire** (3rd–7th c. CE), where the **[[talmud-bavli|Babylonian Talmud]]** was
produced in a Zoroastrian-ruled Mesopotamia. [[shai-secunda|Shai Secunda]]'s
[[secunda-the-iranian-talmud|*The Iranian Talmud*]] reads the two communities' literatures together (the
subfield **[[talmudo-iranica|Talmudo-Iranica]]**): rabbis and **[[magi|Zoroastrian priests (magi)]]**
built mirror-image **"discourses of the Other"** (the rabbinic Jew/*goy* binary against the Zoroastrian
*ēr*/*an-ēr*), competed over purity (notably menstruation) and recitation, yet shared a dense Sasanian
cultural world — a late-antique interaction distinct from, but complementary to, the older
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|eschatological-influence]] question.

## Modern Community: "Who is a Zoroastrian?"

Per [[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]], the living religion (~110,000+
worldwide, the largest community among the Indian [[parsis|Parsis]]) is **shrinking and
aging** and is split less by ancient sect than by modern questions of **authority,
authenticity, and identity**:

- **Reform vs. tradition** — the nineteenth-century missionary attack (John Wilson) and
  Martin Haug's **Gatha-only "ethical monotheism"** (dismissing the yazatas and ritual as
  corruption) divided **reformers** (Naoroji; the "Protestant dastur" M.N. Dhalla) from
  **traditionalists** and **neo-traditionalists**; the esoteric **Ilm-i Khshnoom** added a
  third stream.
- **Birth vs. belief** — the **conversion** and **intermarriage** disputes, sharpened by
  the 1908 Bombay *obiter dicta* defining a "Parsi" **patrilineally**, and the rise of
  ethnically-Iranian and Tajik **"neo-Zoroastrians"** reclaiming an ancestral "birth
  faith," plus **"para-Zoroastrian"** movements.
- **The dualism question** — Christian and Muslim pressure made the **monotheism /
  dualism** label a live internal fault line (recorded without adjudication per the
  Contradiction Protocol).

Diaspora bodies (FEZANA, ZTFE, WZO, the anti-assimilationist WAPIZ) and a series of
**World Congresses** now frame Zoroastrians as "a community without borders," many
preferring the self-designation **Zarathushti**.

## Map of Sects

- [[zurvanism|Zurvanism]] — a (largely extinct) heterodox current making **Zurvan** (Time) the parent of
  both Ahura Mazda and Ahriman, softening the dualism.
