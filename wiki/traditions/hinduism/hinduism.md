# Hinduism

Top-level tradition page. **Hinduism** (its adherents' term *Sanatana Dharma*, "the eternal way") is
less a single creed than a vast family of related traditions sharing the authority of the **Vedas**,
the concepts of **dharma, karma, samsara, and moksha**, and a common ritual-philosophical heritage.
It is the wiki's most internally diverse tradition — at once polytheistic, henotheistic, monistic, and
(in some schools) effectively monotheistic. This page is the parent node for the Hindu sects under
`traditions/hinduism/sects/`.

## A Tiered, Shared Canon

Unlike the Buddhist case (rival canons), Hinduism's drama is a **single shared canon read in opposite
ways**. The canon is tiered:

- **Shruti** ("that which is heard") — the eternal, authorless revelation: the four **Vedas**, each
  ending in the **[[upanishads|Upanishads]]** (*Vedanta*, "the end of the Veda"). The highest authority.
- **Smriti** ("that which is remembered") — human-authored but authoritative tradition: the epics
  (incl. the **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]**, technically smriti yet functionally scripture), the
  Dharmashastras, the Puranas.

The **prasthana-trayi** ("three foundations") of Vedanta — the [[upanishads|Upanishads]], the
[[bhagavad-gita|Gita]], and the **[[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]]** — is the canon every Vedanta school
must expound. Because all three schools accept the *same* texts, the `canon_scope` of these works is
shared (`canonical_for: [hinduism]`); the division between them is **hermeneutical, not canonical** —
the wiki's clearest case of one canon, irreconcilable readings.

## Core Concepts (in place of a single theology)

**[[brahman|Brahman]]** (ultimate reality), **[[atman|atman]]** (the self), **[[moksha|moksha]]**
(liberation), **karma** and **samsara** (action and rebirth), **dharma** (cosmic/social order),
**[[maya|maya]]** (appearance/illusion). The relation of atman to Brahman is the single most contested
question — and the axis of the Vedanta schools.

## Native Hermeneutical Frameworks

The **shruti/smriti** hierarchy; **Mimamsa** (the science of Vedic interpretation); and above all the
**bhashya** (commentary) tradition, in which a teacher establishes a school by writing commentaries on
the prasthana-trayi. The three great commentators — [[shankara|Shankara]], [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]], and
[[madhva|Madhva]] — define the three schools below.

## Map of Major Sects (the Vedanta schools)

- [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedanta]] — **non-dualism** (Shankara): atman *is* Brahman; plurality is
  [[maya|maya]].
- [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita Vedanta]] — **qualified non-dualism** (Ramanuja): selves and
  world are the real body of a personal God (Vishnu/Narayana).
- [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita Vedanta]] — **dualism** (Madhva): God and souls are eternally, really distinct.

(The broad devotional *sampradayas* — Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism — and the other *darshanas*
[Yoga, Samkhya, Nyaya] will be added as sources warrant; the [[yoga-sutras|Yoga Sutras]] are ingested.)
