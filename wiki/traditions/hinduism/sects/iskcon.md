# ISKCON (International Society for Krishna Consciousness)

The **International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON)**, popularly known as the **Hare Krishna
movement**, is a global Vaishnava movement founded in Los Angeles in **1965** by **A. C. Bhaktivedanta
Swami Prabhupada** (1896–1977). It traces its theological lineage through the Gaudiya Vaishnava
tradition established by [[chaitanya|Chaitanya]] (1486–1533) in Bengal.

## Identity and Origins

ISKCON is the primary vehicle by which **Gaudiya Vaishnavism** — Bengali Krishna-bhakti centered on the
Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa tradition — became a global religion. Prabhupada arrived in New York at age 69, began
chanting in Tompkins Square Park, attracted young counterculture followers, and founded ISKCON in 1966.
By the time of his death in 1977, ISKCON had temples in over a hundred cities worldwide.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

ISKCON is a **direct continuation** of Gaudiya Vaishnavism, which flows:

- [[chaitanya|Chaitanya]] (1486–1533) → Goswamins (his disciples in Vrindavan) → Bhaktivinoda Thakura
  (19th c.) → Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati → **Prabhupada** → ISKCON

Its distinctive doctrinal claim — that **Kṛṣṇa is the supreme personality of Godhead** (not merely an
avatar of Vishnu, but Vishnu's source) — follows the Gaudiya position. It is distinguished from other
Hindu bhakti movements by:

- Aggressive global proselytization (kirtana in airports, book distribution)
- Western monasticism modeled on Chaitanya's renunciate following
- Strict vegetarianism and no intoxicants, as universal rules (not caste-specific)
- Rejection of the hereditary caste-based Brahmin priesthood in favor of initiated Vaishnavas

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

ISKCON's canon centers on:

- **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]** — read through Prabhupada's *Bhagavad-Gita As It Is* (1968;
  expanded 1972), which insists on a **personal God** (Kṛṣṇa) distinct from the devotee — rejecting
  the Advaita non-dual reading of Śaṅkara
- **[[bhagavata-purana|Bhāgavata Purāṇa]]** (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) — especially its tenth book (the
  Kṛṣṇa cycle); Prabhupada's multi-volume translation and commentary is authoritative
- The writings of Chaitanya's Goswamins (Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva Goswami) form the secondary theological canon

ISKCON's hermeneutic is **paramparā** (disciplic succession) — interpretation outside the recognized
chain of teachers is illegitimate.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

- **Achintya-bhedābheda** ("inconceivable oneness and difference"): the soul is simultaneously one with
  and different from God — a position attributed to Chaitanya's tradition, rejecting both strict
  non-duality (Advaita) and strict duality
- **Mahā-mantra**: *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama
  Hare Hare* — chanting 16 rounds daily on a japa mala is the central practice
- **Four regulative principles**: no meat, fish, or eggs; no intoxication; no illicit sex; no gambling

## Hermeneutical Method

ISKCON follows a **sampradāya** (lineage) hermeneutic: the authoritative interpretation is that passed
down through the Gaudiya chain. Prabhupada's commentaries are treated as paramparā-authorized and take
precedence over independent scholarly or alternative devotional readings. This places ISKCON within the
*parakīyā* (adulterous-love-as-model) Bengal Vaishnava tradition while domesticating it into a strict
monastic-householder structure.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

ISKCON is viewed by most Hindus as **heterodox** in its aggressive missionary activity and in its
presentation of a specific theological position (Kṛṣṇa as supreme) as the definitive interpretation.
Within the broader Gaudiya community, ISKCON is one among several successor institutions to
Bhaktisiddhanta; its unique status derives from its global reach rather than exclusive theological
authority. Some traditional Gaudiyas dispute ISKCON's innovations in monastic organization and its
treatment of Prabhupada's writings as sufficient without the full classical Goswami library.

## Historical Development and Influence

ISKCON became a major vehicle of **reverse colonization** of the West by Hinduism. The Hare Krishna
presence in airports and on streets in the 1970s–80s made it the most visible face of Hinduism in North
America and Europe. Subsequent growth shifted toward temple-based communities of Hindu diaspora families,
making ISKCON now more representative of mainstream Hindu practice than of the countercultural origins.

ISKCON's global infrastructure of temples, farms, restaurants (Govinda's), and publishing (Bhaktivedanta
Book Trust — the world's largest publisher of Vedic literature) constitutes a major institutional
presence in world religion.
