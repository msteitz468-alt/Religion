# Dvaita Vedanta

> **Sect page.** Madhva's dualist Vedanta — the pole opposite Advaita. Now grounded on
> [[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]], Vol. IV (*Indian Pluralism*), Madhva's own
> *Brahma-Sūtra-bhāṣya* having no complete free English translation.

## Identity and Origins

*Dvaita*, "dualism" (also **Tattvavāda**), was founded by [[madhva|Madhva]] (Madhvacharya, c. 1197–1276),
the third of the great Vedanta commentators, in the **Uḍupi / Tuḷuva** region of coastal Karnataka — still
the heartland of the school. It is the most uncompromising theistic-realist reading of the shared canon,
self-understood as the recovery of the Veda's true (pluralist, Vaiṣṇava) meaning against
[[shankara|Śaṅkara's]] monism.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A Vaishnava theology defined against **both** [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] (which it sees as covert
atheism/Buddhism) and, more subtly, [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] — by insisting that the
distinctions are not merely modal but **absolute and eternal**.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Expounds the same prasthana-trayi; the divergence is again **interpretive** — Madhva reads the
difference-texts literally and the apparent identity-texts figuratively (sometimes via idiosyncratic
grammatical analysis), the mirror image of Advaita's method.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **[[panchabheda|fivefold eternal difference]]** (*pañca-bheda*): between (1) God and souls, (2) God
and matter, (3) soul and soul, (4) soul and matter, (5) matter and matter — all real, eternal, and
identical with the very nature (*svarūpa*) of things. Beneath it is the twofold division of all reality
into the **independent** (*svatantra*) = **[[brahman|Brahman]] = Viṣṇu** (Nārāyaṇa/Vāsudeva), the one
self-standing Lord and "fullness of infinite qualities," and the **dependent** (*asvatantra*) = souls
(*jīvas*) and matter, fully real but never self-standing. Souls fall into **three classes** —
*mukti-yogya* (fit for emancipation: gods, sages), the transmigrating, and a third class Dasgupta calls
"the demons, ghosts and the like," the worst **cursed eternally in hell** (later Mādhva term: *tamo-yogya*);
this **doctrine of eternal damnation** is near-unique in Indian thought and follows from the eternity of
difference. The path is **bhakti**; [[moksha|liberation]] is graded
blissful service, never identity with God, the souls remaining distinct and even ranked *within*
liberation.

## Hermeneutical Method

Strict realism, argued *adhikaraṇa by adhikaraṇa* against Śaṅkara (see
[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]], Ch. XXVI). The master key is the *samanvaya* sutra
(BS I.1.4): **every word of the Upaniṣads ultimately denotes Viṣṇu** (Madhva's *vidvad-rūḍhi*), so the
contested terms Śaṅkara reads as "Brahman" all resolve to Viṣṇu specifically. On the great identity-text
*tat tvam asi*, Madhva's lever (in Dasgupta's account) is **semantic**: the text *admits a direct
(mukhyārtha) reading on the basis of difference*, so the non-difference reading — needing figurative
*lakṣaṇā* — is illegitimate. (The popularly-cited re-parsing into *atat tvam asi*, "thou art *not* that,"
is a separate later-Mādhva move, not the argument Dasgupta reports.)

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The realist pole of [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the Advaita/Vishishtadvaita/Dvaita triad]] — one
canon, three incompatible metaphysics. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Through Madhva and his successors Dvaita became a rigorous dialectical tradition: **Jaya-tīrtha**
(*Tattva-prakāśikā*, *Nyāya-sudhā*) and **Vyāsa-tīrtha** systematized its logic and epistemology, and
Vyāsa-tīrtha's anti-Advaita *Nyāyāmṛta* drew the celebrated Advaitin rejoinder, **Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's
*Advaita-siddhi*** — the long-running **dualist–monist controversy** (recorded without adjudication per
the Contradiction Protocol). The school also fed the Vaiṣṇava *bhakti* movements, notably the **Haridāsa**
singers of Karnataka.
