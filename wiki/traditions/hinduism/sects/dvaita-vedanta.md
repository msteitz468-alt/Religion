# Dvaita Vedanta

> **Sect page.** Madhva's dualist Vedanta — the pole opposite Advaita. *(Stub — Madhva's Brahma-Sutra
> commentary has no complete free English translation; see Outstanding Sources.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Dvaita*, "dualism," was founded by [[madhva|Madhva]] (Madhvacharya, 13th c.), the third of the great
Vedanta commentators. It is the most uncompromising theistic-realist reading of the shared canon.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A Vaishnava theology defined against **both** [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] (which it sees as covert
atheism/Buddhism) and, more subtly, [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] — by insisting that the
distinctions are not merely modal but **absolute and eternal**.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Expounds the same prasthana-trayi; the divergence is again **interpretive** — Madhva reads the
difference-texts literally and the apparent identity-texts figuratively (sometimes via idiosyncratic
grammatical analysis), the mirror image of Advaita's method.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **fivefold eternal difference** (*pancha-bheda*): between (1) God and souls, (2) God and matter,
(3) soul and soul, (4) soul and matter, (5) matter and matter. **[[brahman|Brahman]] is Vishnu**, the
fully independent supreme Lord; souls (*jivas*) and matter are eternally **dependent** and really
distinct from God and from each other. Souls fall into a **hierarchy**, and Madhva uniquely teaches that
some souls are eternally bound (a predestination motif rare in Indian thought). The path is **bhakti**;
[[moksha|liberation]] is graded blissful service, never identity with God.

## Hermeneutical Method

Strict realism: "that thou art" cannot mean literal identity (a dependent thing is not the independent
God); such texts are read as *similarity* or as denied identity ("thou art *not that*," on Madhva's
controversial parsing).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The realist pole of [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the Advaita/Vishishtadvaita/Dvaita triad]] — one
canon, three incompatible metaphysics. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Through Madhva and his successors (Jayatirtha, Vyasatirtha) Dvaita became a rigorous dialectical
tradition, and fed the Vaishnava *bhakti* movements (the Haridasa singers of Karnataka).
