# Arya Samaj

The **Arya Samaj** ("Society of Nobles") is a Hindu reform movement founded in Bombay (now Mumbai)
in 1875 by **Dayananda Sarasvati** (1824–1883). It claimed to restore the original, pure monotheism
of the Vedas, stripped of what Dayananda regarded as the accretions of polytheism, idolatry, and
Brahmin exploitation that had corrupted later Hinduism.

## Identity and Origins

Dayananda Sarasvati was trained as a yogi but steadily lost faith in yoga and moved toward a textual
reformism. His foundational claim: **the four Vedas are the eternal, infallible word of God**, and
anything in post-Vedic Hinduism that contradicts or supplements them — the Puranas, the Bhagavad
Gita, image worship, pilgrimage, polytheism — is false and corrupting.

His slogan: "Go back to the Vedas." His contempt extended to all other scriptures: "Those who read
or listen to the Bible, Quran, Purana, false accounts, and poetic theory — books of ideas opposed to
the Veda — they become sensuous and depraved."

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The Arya Samaj diverges from mainstream Hinduism in the most sweeping possible way: it **rejects the
entire post-Vedic tradition** as corruption. No Puranas, no Epics, no image worship, no pilgrimage,
no Gita (at least not as the Vaishnavas read it), no temple ritual. The Brahmins are attacked as
parasites who corrupted the original Vedic religion for their own benefit.

This placed the Arya Samaj in an unusual position: it attacked Brahmin authority *in the name of
the Vedas* — the texts that Brahmins claimed exclusive authority to interpret.

## Distinctive Doctrines

### Cow Protection
Dayananda's 1881 treatise *Ocean of Mercy for the Cow* (*go-karuna-nidhi*) and his 1882 founding of
cow protection committees launched what became a major political and violent movement. Arya Samaj
cow protection societies spread across British India in the 1880s–1890s, and cow slaughter was
explicitly used to justify communal violence against Muslims (who sacrificed cows at Bakr-Id) and
against Dalits (Chamars, who worked with cow carcasses). In 1893, at the Bakr-Id festival, massive
riots broke out across the entire Hindu population of villages.

### Reconversion (*Shuddhi*)
The Arya Samaj developed a **reconversion ceremony** (*shuddhi*, "purification") that allowed
Muslims who had never been Hindu to be "brought back" to the Vedic fold, and Hindu converts to Islam
to return. This was radical: traditional Hinduism had no such mechanism. The same ceremony was used
to "purify" Pariah castes.

The Arya Samaj claimed to reconvert Dalits too — while simultaneously distinguishing itself, *as
Aryans*, from "degraded" Hindus who practiced idolatry.

### Aryan Identity
The Arya Samaj appropriated the British-philological concept of the "Aryans" as the original Vedic
people and made it a cornerstone of Hindu identity politics — a move that contributed to both Hindu
nationalism and later political uses of Aryan rhetoric.

## The Vegetarian/Flesh-eating Split (1893)

Internal disputes over whether the Vedas authorized meat-eating caused the Arya Samaj to split in
1893 into the **Vegetarian party** (orthodox Arya Samaj) and the **Flesh-eating party** (those who
cited the Vedas' own sacrificial meat provisions). The split showed the fragility of any literalist
Vedic reform that had to actually reckon with what the Vedas said.

## Hermeneutical Method

The Arya Samaj reads the Vedas **literally and exclusively** — but with selective rationalization.
Passages supporting polytheism or image worship are reinterpreted as metaphorical; passages that
can be read as proto-scientific or proto-monotheistic are elevated. The Vedas are treated as
containing all knowledge, including modern science.

## Influence

- **Hindu nationalism**: the Arya Samaj's Aryan identity rhetoric, cow protection militancy, and
  reconversion ceremonies fed directly into the later Hindu nationalist movements (RSS, VHP, BJP)
- **North Indian reform**: the Arya Samaj was the dominant reform movement in Punjab and western
  India; the Brahmo Samaj was dominant in Bengal — the two movements competed
- **Education**: the Arya Samaj established its own school system (*DAV* schools — Dayananda
  Anglo-Vedic) promoting Western education in a Hindu nationalist framework

## Relationship to Orthodoxy

Regarded by orthodox Brahmins as a subversive attack on priestly authority. Regarded by the Brahmo
Samaj as insufficiently rational and too nationalist. Regarded by Muslims as the most aggressive
Hindu reconversion threat.
