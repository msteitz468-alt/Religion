# Vishishtadvaita Vedanta

> **Sect page.** Ramanuja's "qualified non-dualism," the theological backbone of Sri Vaishnavism.
> *(Ingested: Ramanuja's Brahma-Sutra commentary, the Sri-Bhashya.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Vishishtadvaita*, "non-dualism of the qualified (whole)," was given classic form by
[[ramanuja|Ramanuja]] (11th–12th c.), synthesizing the intellectual Vedanta tradition with the
passionate Tamil devotionalism of the **Alvar** poet-saints. It is the principal theology of **Sri
Vaishnavism**.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A theistic-devotional reading of the shared canon, defined explicitly **against**
[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]: it preserves a real, personal God and real, distinct souls while still
affirming a deep unity.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Expounds the same prasthana-trayi but, distinctively, also elevates the Tamil **Divya Prabandham** (the
Alvar hymns) as a devotional "Tamil Veda" — a canon addition beyond the Sanskrit shruti. The core
divergence remains **interpretive**: how to read the same Upanishadic sentences.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**The world and all souls are the real *body* (*sarira*) of God**, who is their inner self (*sariri*) —
so reality is "one" (a single organic whole) yet internally **differentiated** (God, souls, matter are
really distinct modes). Brahman is **saguna** (with qualities), personally identified with
**Vishnu/Narayana**. **[[maya|Maya]] is God's real creative power, not illusion.** The path is
**bhakti** (loving devotion) and *prapatti* (self-surrender), and [[moksha|liberation]] is eternal
loving communion with God — not absorption.

## Hermeneutical Method

Ramanuja reconciles the "identity" texts and the "difference" texts through the **body-soul relation**:
"that thou art" means the self is *inseparable from and dependent on* God as a body on its soul — unity
*and* real distinction together. This directly answers Advaita's literal-identity reading.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The great rebuttal of [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita's]] maya; itself a middle position between Advaita and
[[dvaita-vedanta|Madhva's]] outright dualism. See [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the controversy
triad]]. Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development and Influence

Through Ramanuja's *Sri-Bhashya* and the Sri Vaishnava community (with its later Vadagalai/Tengalai
split over grace), Vishishtadvaita became one of the most influential theistic philosophies of India.
