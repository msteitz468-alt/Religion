# Vishishtadvaita Vedanta

> **Sect page.** Ramanuja's "qualified non-dualism," the theological backbone of Sri Vaishnavism.
> *(Ingested: Ramanuja's Brahma-Sutra commentary, the Śrī-Bhāṣya, via Thibaut SBE 48.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Vishishtādvaita*, "non-dualism of the qualified (whole)," was given classic form by
[[ramanuja|Ramanuja]] (11th–12th c.), synthesizing the intellectual Vedanta tradition with the
passionate Tamil devotionalism of the **Alvar** poet-saints. It is the principal theology of **Sri
Vaishnavism**.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A theistic-devotional reading of the shared *prasthāna-trayī* (Upanishads, Bhagavad Gītā, Brahma
Sutras), defined explicitly **against** [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]: it preserves a real, personal God
and real, distinct souls while still affirming a deep unity. The Advaita critique runs the full length
of the *Śrī-Bhāṣya*; this is not a marginal disagreement but the system's organizing opposition.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Expounds the same *prasthāna-trayī* but, distinctively, also elevates the Tamil **Divya Prabandham**
(the Alvar hymns, 4,000 Tamil verses) as a devotional "Tamil Veda" — a canon addition beyond the
Sanskrit *śruti*. The core divergence from [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] remains **interpretive**: how to
read the same Upanishadic sentences.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

### The Three Real Categories

Reality consists of three genuinely real, irreducible categories:

| Category | Sanskrit | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **God** | *Brahman/Īśvara* | Vishnu/Nārāyaṇa; the personal, omniscient, omnipotent Lord; both free from all imperfections AND possessing all blessed qualities |
| **Souls** (*cit*) | *jīva* | Many distinct, conscious, atomic entities; knowers and agents; real modes/parts (*aṃśa*) of Brahman |
| **Matter** (*acit*) | *pradhāna/prakriti* | Non-sentient; directed by Brahman from within; genuinely real, not illusory |

Souls and matter together form the **body (*śarīra*)** of Brahman, who is their inner Self (*śarīrī*)
and inner ruler (*antaryāmin*). The whole is thus **one organic system** — but with internally real
distinctions. This is what "qualified non-dualism" means: not dualism (two independent substances), not
simple non-dualism (only one substance), but the unity of a whole whose parts are genuinely distinct.

### The *Śarīra-Śarīrī* (Body-Soul) Relation — Structural Cornerstone

The formal definition of a body:
> "A body is any substance which a conscious being is able to control and support for its own
> purposes, and which stands in an entirely subordinate relation to that being."

This is the hermeneutical key for ALL Upanishadic passages. Identity-texts ("That thou art") name the
*inseparable unity* of body and soul (a body cannot exist apart from its soul; therefore souls/matter
cannot exist apart from Brahman). Difference-texts (the individual soul worshipping Brahman as other)
name the *real distinction* of the controlled from the controller. Both types of text are true and
neither requires reinterpretation or allegorization.

See [[sarira-sariri|Śarīra-Śarīrī]] (concept page).

### *Pariṇāmavāda* — Real Transformation, Not Appearance

Against Advaita's *vivartavāda* (the world is Brahman's *appearance* via *māyā*), Vishishtadvaita
holds that the world is Brahman's real **transformation** (*pariṇāma*). The clay/pot analogy: just as
clay genuinely becomes a pot without ceasing to be clay, Brahman genuinely transforms itself into
souls and matter. The world is not a lower-level truth or a cognitive projection — it is real.

This makes [[maya|māyā]] Brahman's real creative power (*śakti*), not cosmic illusion.

### Brahman's Twofold Character

Brahman has **two irreducible sets of qualities**:
1. **Freedom from all imperfections** — no evil, no sin, no decay, no hunger or thirst
2. **Possession of all blessed qualities** — omniscience, omnipotence, creative power, bliss, perfect will

The imperfections of the bodies Brahman inhabits (including sinful souls) do NOT contaminate Brahman,
because Brahman's relation to its body is as *controller and support*, not as *experiencer*. Brahman's
connexion with bodies is **sport (*līlā*)** — it does not bind Brahman as it binds souls.

The *neti neti* formula ("not so, not so"; *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* II,3,6) does not negate Brahman's
attributes: it denies only the **restriction** of Brahman to any finite set of attributes, and is
followed immediately by positive characterizations ("True of the True"). This is directly opposed to
the Advaita reading of *neti neti* as apophatic denial.

### The Soul (*Jīva*)

The individual soul is:
- **Knower (*jñātṛ*)**: consciousness is its essential nature; Advaita's identification of the soul
  with pure consciousness mistakes the soul's *mode* for its entire being
- **Agent (*kartṛ*)**: genuinely performs actions; not an inactive witness under *avidyā*
- **Atomic (*aṇu*)**: residing in the heart; pervades the body through the spreading of consciousness,
  as a lamp fills a room while the flame is in one spot
- **Part (*aṃśa*)**: a real mode of Brahman, not a fragment broken off; "part" is used to mean the
  soul has the same relation to Brahman as an attribute to its substance — inseparable yet genuinely
  distinct
- **Many**: the plurality of souls is irreducible and persists through liberation; souls do not merge

### The *Antaryāmin* (*Inner Ruler*)

The *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* III,7 passage is the centrepiece: the Lord dwells within every entity — earth,
water, fire, sky, sun, moon, and **within individual souls themselves** — "ruling the earth from within
while the earth does not know him." Each clause ends: "that is thy Self, the inner Ruler, the Immortal."

This establishes real internal presence *and* real transcendence simultaneously. The Lord rules from
within yet remains the Immortal untouched by what he rules. See [[antaryami]].

## Hermeneutical Method

### *Upāsanā* = *Dhyāna* (Meditation as the Path)

Knowledge (*jñāna*) in Vishishtadvaita = **meditation (*upāsanā*/*dhyāna*)** = **repeated, continued
representation** of the object, maintained until death. The roots *vid* (to know), *upas* (to
meditate), *dhyai* (to contemplate) are used interchangeably in Upanishadic texts — Ramanuja argues
they denote the same practice. "Knowing Brahman" is not a cognitive flash but a lifelong discipline.
See [[upasana|Upāsanā]].

Rules governing meditation:
- The object is **highest Brahman** (not effected Brahman, not the individual soul)
- Essential attributes of Brahman (bliss, knowledge, freedom from evil, infinity) must be included
  in ALL Brahman-meditations
- The meditating subject must conceive of itself in its **released nature** (freedom from sin)
- Symbol meditations (meditating on fire, prāṇa, etc. *as* Brahman) are inferior; their practitioners
  do not attain the highest goal

### Knowledge vs. Works

Ramanuja holds (against Jaimini) that **knowledge has an independent fruit** — attaining Brahman
directly — and is not merely auxiliary to Vedic works. For householders, prescribed works remain
obligatory as prerequisites to knowledge (they please the Lord who then grants the enabling grace),
but the causal chain runs through the Lord's grace, not directly from rite to liberation.

Three stages of life (student, householder, forest-dweller/wanderer) are all equally obligatory. There
is no dispensation from one's āśrama duties while seeking knowledge.

### The *Mahāpūrvapakṣa* — Critique of Advaita

The *Śrī-Bhāṣya* opens with the "great prior objection," a seven-pronged attack on Advaita's
foundations. Key points:
- *Avidyā* cannot be defined coherently (not existent, not non-existent, not both)
- Pure consciousness cannot serve as the substrate of nescience
- A self-luminous consciousness cannot be unknowing to itself
- "Know that thou art Brahman" has no coherent agent or object if the self *already is* Brahman
- The world's texts presuppose a real cause-effect relation; allegorizing via two-truths is arbitrary

See [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the Vedanta schools controversy]] for the full alignment.

## The Soteriological Path

### Karman Categories at Liberation

Three categories of works relative to the knower:
1. **Anārabdha-kārya** — works not yet commenced: destroyed immediately at the rise of knowledge
2. **Ārabdha-kārya** — works whose fruits have already begun (current body's experience): must be
   exhausted by retributive experience; may require additional bodies
3. **Post-knowledge works** — do not cling; have no binding force

### The Path of the Gods (*Devayāna*)

The released soul exits through the 100th+1 artery (the *suṣumnā*, to the crown), illuminated by the
Supreme Person dwelling in the heart. The Path of the Gods proceeds through: light → day → bright
fortnight → northern-solstice months → Vāyu (world of the gods) → sun → lightning → Varuṇa → Indra →
Prajāpati → **Brahman**. Conducting deities assist; NOT led are those who meditated on symbols (whose
meditation is contaminated by a non-intelligent element). Death at any time (night, dark fortnight,
southern solstice) does not prevent the knower's attainment.

See [[path-of-the-gods|Path of the Gods (*Devayāna*)]] (concept page).

### The State of Release

Release = **manifestation** of the soul's eternally existing true nature, not production of a new state.
The released soul:
- Is conscious of itself as **non-divided** from Brahman — united as body to its soul, yet genuinely
  distinct as mode from substrate
- Is pure intelligence *throughout* AND possesses freedom from sin, bliss, and other positive qualities
  (Bādarāyaṇa's synthesis)
- Creates by mere will; is self-ruler; may take or lay aside a body at will
- Does NOT acquire Brahman's world-ruling power (creation/sustentation/dissolution) — this is the
  exclusive attribute of the Supreme Person
- Does NOT return to *saṃsāra* — the Lord who loves his Devotee will not eject him

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The great rebuttal of [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita's]] *māyā* and *nirguṇa* Brahman; a middle position
between Advaita (identity, illusionism) and [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita's]] outright dualism (Brahman and
souls independently real with no body-relation). See [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the controversy
triad]]. All positions recorded without adjudication.

**Sri Vaishnava internal split** (14th century):
- **Vaḍakalai ("Monkey school", North)**: The devotee actively clings to God, who saves him — as a
  baby monkey clings to its mother. Effort cooperates with grace.
- **Teṅkalai ("Cat school", South)**: The devotee is entirely passive, saved by grace alone — as
  kittens allow a mother cat to carry them. Radical receptivity; any effort impedes.

Both schools affirm *prapatti* (surrender) as the ultimate mode of bhakti.

## Historical Development and Influence

Through the *Śrī-Bhāṣya*, Sri Vaishnavism became one of the most influential theistic philosophies of
India. Ramanuja's organized the Srirangam temple community and systematized the Alvar devotional
heritage within a Vedantic framework. His influence runs through all later Vaishnava commentary, North
Indian bhakti theology, and modern movements such as [[iskcon|ISKCON]].
