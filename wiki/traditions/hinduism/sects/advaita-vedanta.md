# Advaita Vedanta

> **Sect page.** The non-dualist Vedanta school of [[shankara|Shankara]]; the most influential
> interpretation of the [[upanishads|Upanishads]]. *(Ingested: Shankara's Brahma-Sutra commentary.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Advaita*, "not-two," is the **non-dualist** school, given classic form by [[shankara|Shankara]]
(Adi Shankaracharya, 8th c.), building on Gaudapada. It holds that ultimate reality is one without a
second, and that the deepest truth of the self is its identity with that reality.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The dominant philosophical interpretation of [[hinduism|Hindu]] scripture, especially in the
intellectual and monastic tradition; the benchmark the other Vedanta schools argue against.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

**No canon divergence** — Advaita expounds the same prasthana-trayi ([[upanishads|Upanishads]],
[[bhagavad-gita|Gita]], [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]]) as its rivals. Its distinctiveness is
**hermeneutical**: it reads the *mahavakyas* ("great sayings" — *tat tvam asi*, "that thou art";
*aham brahmasmi*, "I am Brahman") as **literal statements of identity**, and treats texts that speak of
a personal God and real plurality as describing the lower, provisional level of truth.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**[[atman|Atman]] = [[brahman|Brahman]]**: the individual self is, in reality, identical with the one
Brahman. Brahman is **nirguna** (without qualities). The plural world and the personal creator-God
belong to **[[maya|maya]]** — real-as-experienced but not ultimately real (the rope mistaken for a
snake). Liberation ([[moksha|moksha]]) is **jnana** — the dispelling of ignorance (*avidya*) by
realizing one's true identity, here and now (*jivanmukti*).

## Hermeneutical Method

Two levels of truth (*paramarthika* / *vyavaharika*) let Advaita affirm dualistic scripture
conventionally while reading the identity-texts as definitive — structurally akin to the Buddhist
[[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]] move (a resemblance its opponents exploited, calling Shankara a
"crypto-Buddhist").

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The chief target of [[ramanuja|Ramanuja's]] [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] and
[[madhva|Madhva's]] [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]], who reject maya and defend a real, personal God and real
selves. See [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the controversy triad]]. Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development and Influence

From Shankara's monasteries (*mathas*) and his commentaries, Advaita became the prestige philosophy of
classical Hinduism and the form most exported to the modern West (Vivekananda, neo-Vedanta).
