# Assyrian Religion

> **Sect page.** The religion of the **Assyrian Empire**, centered on the national god **[[ashur|Ashur]]**.
> *(Stub.)*

## The National God and the Empire

Assyrian religion shared the Mesopotamian pantheon and myths but **elevated its own city-and-state god,
[[ashur|Ashur]]**, to the supreme position — even rewriting the [[enuma-elish|Enuma Elish]] to put **Ashur in
the place of [[marduk|Marduk]]** as the dragon-slaying creator-king. Religion and **empire** were fused: the
Assyrian king was Ashur's earthly **vicar and high priest**, and warfare was a sacred mission to extend the
god's order — Assyrian conquest was framed as the will and victory of Ashur.

## Cult and Divination

The fierce **[[ishtar|Ishtar]]** (of Nineveh and Arbela) was a great war-goddess and patroness of the kings.
Assyrian kings relied intensely on **divination** — armies of diviners read omens, livers, and the stars
before every decision, and the royal archives (Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh, which preserved the
Gilgamesh and Enuma Elish tablets) are our richest source for Mesopotamian religion.
