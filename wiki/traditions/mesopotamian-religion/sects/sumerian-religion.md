# Sumerian Religion

> **Sect page.** The oldest stratum of [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamian religion]] — the religion of
> **Sumer**, the world's first urban civilization. *(Stub.)*

## The Original Pantheon

Sumer gave the Near East its foundational pantheon, organized as a divine assembly: **[[anu|An]]** (sky, the
remote father of the gods), **[[enlil|Enlil]]** (the active king of the gods, lord of wind and the cosmic
order, patron of Nippur), **[[enki|Enki]]** (wisdom, fresh water, magic, and the friend of humanity), and the
mother-goddess **Ninhursag** — plus the astral gods Nanna (moon), Utu (sun), and the great
**[[ishtar|Inanna]]** (love and war). Each city had its **patron deity** dwelling in its temple
(**ziggurat**), served by a vast temple economy.

## The Me

A distinctive Sumerian idea is the **me** — the fixed **divine decrees or "powers"** that govern every
aspect of civilization (kingship, the priesthood, crafts, sex, war, music, even falsehood). Held by the
gods (especially [[enki|Enki]]) and bestowed on cities, the *me* are the ordinances that make ordered human
life possible.

## Significance

The Sumerian pantheon, myths (Inanna's Descent, Enki and Ninhursag, the flood), and temple institutions were
inherited and adapted by the later Akkadians, [[babylonian-religion|Babylonians]], and
[[assyrian-religion|Assyrians]] — the deep source of all Mesopotamian and much later Near Eastern religion.
