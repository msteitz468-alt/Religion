# Babylonian Religion

> **Sect page.** The religion of **Babylon**, centered on **[[marduk|Marduk]]** — the classic, best-attested
> form of [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamian religion]]. *(Ingested: the [[enuma-elish|Enuma Elish]] and
> the [[epic-of-gilgamesh|Epic of Gilgamesh]].)*

## The Rise of Marduk

As Babylon rose to dominance (from Hammurabi's time, c. 1750 BCE), its city-god **[[marduk|Marduk]]** was
elevated to **king of the gods**, displacing the older Sumerian [[enlil|Enlil]]. The **[[enuma-elish|Enuma
Elish]]** is essentially Marduk's charter: it tells how the young Marduk alone defeated the chaos-dragon
**[[tiamat|Tiamat]]**, created the ordered cosmos from her body and humanity from a slain god's blood, and
was awarded the **fifty names** and supreme kingship by the grateful gods.

## Cult and the New Year

Babylonian religion gave the world its most developed **astral religion and divination** — the systematic
reading of the heavens (the origin of astrology) and of omens. Its central festival was the **Akitu** (New
Year), when the Enuma Elish was recited, the king's authority was renewed before Marduk, and cosmic order was
ritually re-established for the coming year. **[[nabu|Nabu]]**, Marduk's son, god of writing and wisdom,
patronized the scribes.
