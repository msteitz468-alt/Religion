# Yoruba Religion

**Chronology:** see the [[yoruba-religion-timeline|Timeline of Yoruba Religion]] and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]].

**Yoruba religion** — the **"way of the orishas"** — is the indigenous tradition of the
**Yoruba people** of southwestern Nigeria and Benin and of its vast **Atlantic diaspora**
(Cuba, Brazil, Haiti, the wider Caribbean, and the United States). Carried across the
ocean in the transatlantic slave trade and there fused with Catholicism, it survives today
as **[[santeria|Santería]]** (Cuba and its diaspora), **[[candomble|Candomblé]]** (Brazil),
and kindred forms, claiming on some estimates **~100 million practitioners worldwide** — a
figure on the strength of which [[stephen-prothero|Stephen Prothero]] argues, against the
old "primitive religion" filing, that it is one of the world's **great religions** rather
than a "primal" footnote to them. This page is seeded from Prothero's *God Is Not One*
(2010), ch. 6.

## Self-Understanding: The Way of Connection

On Prothero's [[four-part-model-of-religion|four-part scheme]], Yoruba religion diagnoses
the human problem as **disconnection** — from one's destiny, from one another, and from
sacred power — and offers **connection / flourishing** as the goal, reached through the
**techniques of divination, sacrifice, and spirit-possession**, with the **diviners
(*babalawos* / *iyalawos*) and the orishas themselves as exemplars**. It is an eminently
**practical, this-worldly** tradition: practitioners "do not fret about the afterlife" so
much as seek the orishas' intervention in matters of love, luck, health, and work.

### Destiny and the divine head (ori)

Before birth, one of a person's souls appears before the High God **Olodumare** and
**chooses its own destiny** — lifespan, personality, occupation, fortune. Birth brings
**forgetting**: we wander "veiled from our true purposes." The religious task is to
**remember and recover** that chosen destiny. The human person comprises the body (*ara*)
and at least two souls — *emi* (breath) and **ori** (lit. "head," the spiritual centre that
chose the destiny). The *ori* is animated by the same sacred power that animates the gods
(**ashe**), so much so that a proverb runs: **"The head [*ori*] is the greatest orisha."**
Humanity, in this tradition, has enormous dignity and agency.

## Ashe — "the power-to-make-things-happen"

**Ashe** (*aché* in Cuba, *axé* in Brazil; lit. "so be it / may it happen," ≈ "Amen") is
the key concept: a sacred force or "spiritual electricity," akin to Chinese *qi*. It
resides in orishas, priests, words, names, blood, beads, herbs, rivers, mountains, storms,
drumming, and dance — making "everything, everyone, and everywhere potentially sacred."
Critically, ashe is **morally double-edged**: it connects *and* disconnects, heals *and*
harms, gives *and* takes away. This refusal to sort power into pure-good and pure-evil
distinguishes Yoruba theology sharply from the monotheisms.

## The Orishas

The cosmos is "awash in sacred power": malevolent spirits (*ajogun*), ancestor spirits
(*egungun*), and above all the **orishas** (*orixás* in Brazil, *orichas* in Cuba) — a
pantheon traditionally numbered at **401 or 601** ("with the plus-one" — there is always
room for one more), though counts run as high as 3,200, and many were lost in the Atlantic
crossing (most New-World practitioners recognize one or two dozen, or the "Seven Powers").
The orishas are **"larger than life but not other than life"**: passionate, fallible,
marrying and divorcing, capable of creation and destruction alike — yet, unlike the Greek
gods, they **suffer for their misdeeds**. They are fed by **sacrifice** and consulted by
**divination** in a system of **reciprocal exchange**: "If humanity were not, the gods would
not be."

Key orishas (candidate `figures/` pages, not yet written):

- **Olodumare** (Olorun, "Owner of the Sky") — the remote **High God**, source of all ashe,
  who delegated creation and receives little direct worship (no temples, liturgy, or
  priests) — compared by Prothero to the God of Deism. Debate persists over whether the
  orishas are *emanations* of Olodumare ("diffused monotheism," E. B. Idowu) or Olodumare an
  *abstraction* of them — i.e. whether the tradition is monotheistic or polytheistic.
- **Eshu** (Elegba/Legba/Exu) — the **messenger** and **trickster** of the crossroads, who
  carries sacrifices from earth to the gods; every rite begins by feeding him. Long
  *confused with the devil* by Christians, he is in fact an ambiguous "policeman and
  troublemaker," patron of change, chance, and (today) the Internet.
- **Orunmila** (Ifa/Orula) — orisha of **wisdom and destiny**, owner of the
  **[[ifa-corpus|Ifa corpus]]** and master of divination; present at creation, knows all
  destinies. With Olodumare and Eshu he forms, for some, a "trinity."
- **Oshun** — orisha of **rivers and sweet water**, fertility (Africa) and love (New World);
  the only female present at creation and first to divine.
- **Obatala** (Orishanla, Oxalá) — sculptor of human bodies from clay; the **"king of the
  white cloth,"** quintessence of *cool* (a central Yoruba value); the deformed are sacred
  to him.
- **Ogun** — hot orisha of **iron, war, tools, technology** (now also locomotives, cars,
  surgery); the path-clearer who first hacked the way between heaven and earth; a great
  tragic figure (Wole Soyinka's "Dionysian, Apollonian and Promethean" deity).
- **Shango** (thunder, lightning, kingship, virility), **Oya** (storm-wind, the Niger,
  cemeteries), **Shopona/Babaluaye** (smallpox and healing), **Yemoja** (ocean,
  motherhood), **Osanyin** (healing herbs).

## Ifa Divination and the Babalawo

The central technique is **Ifa divination**, the storehouse of the
**[[ifa-corpus|Ifa corpus]]**. A **babalawo** ("father of secrets") or **iyalawo** ("mother
of secrets") casts sixteen palm nuts or an eight-shell divining chain, producing one of
**256 signatures (*odu*)** (16 × 16), each with at least four memorized verse-stories
(diviners hold ≥1,000). Strikingly, the **client is active and the diviner passive**: the
client does *not* state her problem, and *she* — not the babalawo — chooses which recited
story fits her situation. "You know the mystery," the babalawo tells the client; "I know
nothing." Divination thus *recovers* wisdom the client already carries in her *ori*, akin
(Prothero notes) to **Socratic anamnesis**. Diviners are **mediators**, not oracles.

## Canon and Authority

Yoruba religion is **oral/aural by tradition**: its "scripture" is the chanted, memorized
**[[ifa-corpus|Ifa corpus]]** rather than a written book, and classical shrines use
**natural objects (stones, herbs)** rather than icons. Authority is **practical and
priestly** — vested in the diviners' prodigious memory and in lineages of initiation — not
in a creed or a single text. There is no central magisterium; the tradition varies widely
"across time and space."

## Map of Sub-Traditions (Sects)

The diaspora forms are arguably **distinct religions** — "rhizomes" connected at the root
but grown into new plants in new soils (Prothero, after J. L. Matory). The wiki carries the
two largest as sect pages:

- **[[santeria|Santería]]** (Regla de Ocha / Lucumí) — the **Cuban** form, in which orishas
  were hidden behind, and identified with, **Catholic saints** (Oshun ≈ Our Lady of Charity;
  Obatala ≈ Our Lady of Mercy; Eshu ≈ St. Anthony/the "souls"). Diffused across the
  Caribbean and the US; the *Hialeah* animal-sacrifice case reached the US Supreme Court.
- **[[candomble|Candomblé]]** (and kin: Umbanda, Macumba) — the **Brazilian** form,
  organized in *terreiros*, centred on drumming, dance, and **possession** by the *orixás*.

Related but separate Atlantic traditions (Haitian **Vodou**, etc.) draw on Yoruba and Fon
sources together. Prothero treats the African and New-World forms **together** here, judging
them more closely connected than Mormons, Protestants, and Catholics are to one another.

## Theology and the Comparative Frame

- **No absolute good/evil dualism**: the moral ambiguity of orishas and of ashe contrasts
  with the monotheisms' problem of theodicy — "In Yoruba religion no such theological
  gymnastics are necessary," since the gods are not claimed to be only good.
- **Immanence over transcendence**: "the gaps between sacred and profane … are at best
  hairline cracks." Orishas live *in the earth* (Shango and Olodumare excepted).
- **Reciprocity**: a two-way "system of communication and exchange" — humans feed the gods;
  the gods empower humans.
- **System of correspondences**: each orisha maps onto colours, numbers, foods, herbs, days,
  body-parts, drumbeats, and (in the New World) Catholic saints — a dense semiotic web that
  "makes meaning from seemingly small and unrelated things."

## Central Place

**Ile-Ife** (in Yorubaland, SW Nigeria) is recognized as the place where the orishas created
human beings and "set the world in motion" — a centre of ashe and the mythic origin point of
Yoruba sacred kingship (cf. Oduduwa, the deified founder-king).
