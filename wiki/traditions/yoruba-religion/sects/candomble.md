# Candomblé

**Candomblé** is the **Brazilian** diaspora form of **[[yoruba-religion|Yoruba religion]]**,
formed among enslaved West Africans (predominantly Yoruba, alongside Fon/Jeje and Bantu/
Angola sources) who preserved devotion to the **orixás** under Catholic cover. Organized in
**terreiros** (house-temples) and centred on **drumming, dance, and possession**, it is —
with Cuban **[[santeria|Santería]]** — one of the two great Atlantic "rhizomes" of the
Yoruba root. ([[stephen-prothero|Prothero]], *God Is Not One*, ch. 6, is the source
ingested.)

## Identity and Origins

Candomblé took shape in **Bahia and the Brazilian Northeast** under slavery, fusing Yoruba
orisha devotion with **Catholic saints** and, in some "nations," Fon and Bantu elements. It
is conventionally divided into **nations (*nações*)** by ancestral source — **Ketu/Nagô**
(the most Yoruba), **Jeje** (Fon), and **Angola** (Bantu) — each with its own ritual
language and emphases. The related, more Spiritist-inflected **Umbanda** and the (often
pejoratively named) **Macumba** are kin developments.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Candomblé inherits the **orixás**, **axé** (ashe), sacrifice, and possession from West
African Yoruba religion. As in Cuba the pantheon is **contracted** and the **saint–orixá
correspondence** is central (Oxalá ≈ Our Lord of Bonfim; Iemanjá ≈ Our Lady of the
Navigators; Xangô ≈ St. Jerome/St. Barbara; Oxóssi ≈ St. George). Distinctively, Candomblé
is famously a **"danced religion"** — "the orixás are better understood as rhythms than as
personalities" — and is often led by powerful **priestesses (*mães-de-santo*)**, a
matriarchal pattern some scholars (Matory) trace partly to New-World developments.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Candomblé shares the oral **[[ifa-corpus|Ifa corpus]]** / *odu* framework of the parent
tradition (divination chiefly via the **jogo de búzios**, the cowrie-shell oracle), with no
written scripture; transmission is oral, initiatory, and lineage-bound within the *terreiro*.
Catholic hagiography is appropriated iconographically, not as scripture.

## Doctrinal and Ritual Distinctives

Initiation and the seating of one's **orixá**; public festivals at which initiates are
**mounted/possessed** by the orixás amid drumming and dance; **ebó** (offering/sacrifice);
the *terreiro* as both temple and kin-community. Brazil's constitution now protects
Candomblé, though it continues to face hostility (notably from some Pentecostal quarters).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Historically **criminalized and persecuted** as primitive "feitiçaria," Candomblé is treated
by Prothero as a great religion in its own right. It is the **Brazilian sibling** of Cuban
**[[santeria|Santería]]** — so close that one scholar compares the two to "Siamese twins" —
and shades into **Umbanda** and **Macumba** at its edges.
