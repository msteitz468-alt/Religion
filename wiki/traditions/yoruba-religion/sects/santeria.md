# Santería (Regla de Ocha / Lucumí)

**Santería** — more properly **Regla de Ocha** or **Lucumí** religion — is the **Cuban**
diaspora form of **[[yoruba-religion|Yoruba religion]]**, formed when enslaved Yoruba
("Lucumí") in Cuba preserved orisha devotion **behind a veil of Catholic imagery**. The
name *santería* ("the way of the saints") was originally an outsiders' pejorative; many
practitioners prefer *Regla de Ocha* or *Lucumí*. It has spread across the Caribbean and to
the United States. ([[stephen-prothero|Prothero]], *God Is Not One*, ch. 6, is the source
ingested.)

## Identity and Origins

Santería crystallized under the conditions of **Atlantic slavery**: forbidden open orisha
worship, the Lucumí **identified each orisha with a Catholic saint** whose iconography or
legend resembled it, worshipping the orisha "through" the saint. The result is neither
simple disguise nor simple fusion but a durable **dual-register tradition** — what scholars
debate as syncretism, camouflage, or genuine creolization.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Santería **inherits** the orishas, **ashe**, divination, sacrifice, and possession from West
African Yoruba religion, but is reshaped by the diaspora: the pantheon is **contracted**
(one or two dozen orishas, the "Seven Powers"), oriented around the **casa/ilé** (house-
temple) and the **asiento** ("making ocha," initiation), and — Prothero notes, citing
Wande Abimbola — far **more reliant on animal sacrifice** than the African parent, where a
*babalawo* may serve twenty clients in a day without prescribing a single fowl.

## The Orisha–Saint Correspondences

The defining feature. Representative pairings (regionally variable):

- **Oshun** ≈ Our Lady of Charity (*La Caridad del Cobre*, patroness of Cuba)
- **Obatala** ≈ Our Lady of Mercy (*La Merced*)
- **Shango** ≈ St. Barbara
- **Babaluaye** (Shopona) ≈ St. Lazarus
- **Eshu/Elegba** ≈ St. Anthony / the Holy Child of Atocha / "the souls in purgatory"
- **Yemoja** ≈ Our Lady of Regla; **Ogun** ≈ St. Peter

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Santería shares the **[[ifa-corpus|Ifa corpus]]** and the 256 *odu* with the parent
tradition, transmitted in creolized **Lucumí**, and adds the simpler **dilogún** (cowrie-
shell) divination alongside full Ifa. It has **no written scripture**; authority is oral,
priestly, and initiatory. Catholic texts and saints are appropriated *liturgically and
iconographically* but not as scripture.

## Doctrinal and Ritual Distinctives

Initiation (**asiento**), the seating of an orisha "in the head" of the devotee; **bembé**
drumming festivals at which orishas **mount and possess** their initiates; **ebo**
(sacrifice, including animal sacrifice); and elaborate **botánica**-supplied material
culture (beads, herbs, images). The US Supreme Court case *Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye
v. City of Hialeah* (1993) struck down ordinances targeting Santería animal sacrifice as a
violation of free exercise.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Long branded **"brujería" (witchcraft)** by Cuban Catholic and Protestant majorities and by
the state, Santería is a **first-class religion** in its own right per Prothero's argument.
It is a sibling of Brazilian **[[candomble|Candomblé]]** (the two are "rhizomes" of one
root) and overlaps with **Palo** and, more distantly, Haitian **Vodou**.
