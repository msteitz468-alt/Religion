# Neo-Confucianism

> **Sect page.** The Song–Ming metaphysical revival of Confucianism. *(Stub — Zhu Xi's commentaries on
> the Four Books are `[COPYRIGHT]`/partial in English; see Outstanding Sources.)*

## Identity and Origins

A profound revival and **metaphysical deepening** of Confucianism in the Song (11th–13th c.) and Ming
(14th–17th c.) dynasties, developed to answer the spiritual and cosmological challenge of Buddhism and
[[daoism|Daoism]] on Confucian terms. Its towering figure is **[[zhu-xi|Zhu Xi]]** (1130–1200), who
synthesized the tradition and **canonized the Four Books**.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Claims to recover the authentic [[dao|Way]] of the ancient sages (lost, it said, after Mencius) — a
"return to the classics" that is in fact a powerful new metaphysical reading of them.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Elevates the **Four Books** ([[analects|Analects]], [[mencius|Mencius]], *Great Learning*, *Doctrine of
the Mean*) **over** the older Five Classics as the curriculum's core, framed by Zhu Xi's commentaries —
which became the orthodox basis of the imperial examinations for ~600 years. Canon-shaping by commentary.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

A metaphysics of **li** (理, principle/pattern) and **qi** (氣, vital force/material): every thing has its
*li* within the one **Supreme Ultimate** (*taiji*); self-cultivation is the "**investigation of things**"
(*gewu*) to apprehend principle and so become a sage. The two great schools:

- **Cheng–Zhu** ("School of Principle," *lixue*) — Zhu Xi: principle is sought in things and study.
- **Lu–Wang** ("School of Mind," *xinxue*) — **Wang Yangming** (1472–1529): principle is innate in the
  **mind**; "the unity of knowledge and action," realized by intuition rather than external study.

## Hermeneutical Method

A **metaphysical re-reading** of the Four Books through the li/qi framework, with Zhu Xi's commentaries
treated as nearly authoritative — Confucianism's version of canon-defining commentary.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Became the **state orthodoxy**; internally split Cheng-Zhu vs. Lu-Wang. Shaped Korea (Joseon), Japan
(Tokugawa), and Vietnam. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From Zhou Dunyi and the Cheng brothers through Zhu Xi's synthesis to Wang Yangming's idealist challenge;
the dominant intellectual framework of late imperial East Asia.
