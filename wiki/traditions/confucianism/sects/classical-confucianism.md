# Classical Confucianism

> **Sect page.** The founding stratum — Confucius, Mencius, and Xunzi. *(Ingested: the [[analects|
> Analects]] and the [[mencius|Mencius]].)*

## Identity and Origins

The original tradition of [[confucius|Confucius]] (551–479 BCE) and his two great classical successors,
**[[mencius|Mencius]]** (Mengzi, c. 372–289 BCE) and **Xunzi** (c. 310–235 BCE), grounded in the **Five
Classics** and concerned with virtue, ritual, and the moral renewal of society.

## Canon and Scripture

The Five Classics plus the early Masters' texts; the [[analects|Analects]] (sayings of Confucius) and the
[[mencius|Mencius]] become two of the later **Four Books**. `canonical_for: [confucianism]`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

An ethics of **[[ren|ren]]** (humaneness) cultivated through **[[li-confucian|li]]** (ritual propriety),
**filial piety**, and the model of the **junzi** (exemplary person); government by virtue and example
rather than by law and punishment; reverence for Heaven (*Tian*) and the ancient sage-kings.

## The Human-Nature Split

Classical Confucianism contains its own foundational disagreement: **[[mencius|Mencius]]** — human nature
is **innately good** (the "four sprouts"); **Xunzi** — human nature is **bad/unruly** and must be
reshaped by ritual and learning. Both are orthodox; the tension drives later debate.

## Hermeneutical Method

Transmission and exegesis of the inherited Classics (Confucius "transmits but does not innovate") and
teaching through **aphorism and dialogue** (the Analects' form).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The foundation that [[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]] later re-read metaphysically; in dialogue and
rivalry with [[daoism|Daoism]] (over nature vs. cultivation) and, later, Buddhism. Recorded without
adjudication.

## Historical Development and Influence

From the Warring States Masters through its establishment as state orthodoxy under the Han (2nd c. BCE),
classical Confucianism became the moral grammar of Chinese civilization.
