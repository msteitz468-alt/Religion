# Confucianism

**Chronology:** see the [[confucianism-timeline|Timeline of Confucianism]] and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]].

Top-level tradition page. **Confucianism** (Chinese *Rujia*, "the school of the scholars") is the
ethical, social, and political tradition founded on the teaching of **[[confucius|Confucius]]** (Kongzi,
551–479 BCE). Whether it is a "religion" is itself debated — it centers on **moral self-cultivation,
ritual propriety, family, and good government** rather than on a creator God or salvation — but it has
sacred texts, sage-veneration, and a profound concern with Heaven (*Tian*) and the cosmic-moral order,
and it has functioned as China's dominant moral-religious framework for two millennia. This page is the
parent node for the Confucian sects under `traditions/confucianism/sects/`.

## Canon — the Five Classics and Four Books

- The **Five Classics** (*Wujing*) — the older corpus (Odes, Documents, Changes/*Yijing*, Rites, Spring
  and Autumn Annals) that Confucius was traditionally said to have transmitted/edited.
- The **Four Books** (*Sishu*) — the [[analects|Analects]], the [[mencius|Mencius]], the *Great Learning*,
  and the *Doctrine of the Mean* — elevated by **Zhu Xi** (12th c.) into the core curriculum and the basis
  of the imperial examinations. The Four Books are the practical canon (`canonical_for: [confucianism]`).

That Zhu Xi's *selection and commentary* effectively created the operative canon is itself a key fact —
canonization by commentary (cf. the Daoist [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|commentary]] and Vedanta
[[brahma-sutras|bhashya]] traditions).

## Core Concepts (an ethics, not a theology)

- **[[ren|Ren]]** (仁) — humaneness/benevolence; the supreme virtue.
- **[[li-confucian|Li]]** (禮) — ritual propriety; the forms that shape character and society.
- **Xiao** (filial piety), **yi** (righteousness), **junzi** (the "gentleman"/exemplary person), the
  **rectification of names**, and the moral **[[dao|Dao]]** of the ancient sage-kings.

## A Defining Internal Debate (human nature)

Already in the classical period the tradition split over **human nature**: **[[mencius|Mencius]]** held it
**innately good** (the "four sprouts" of virtue), while **Xunzi** held it **bad** (in need of ritual
reshaping). This debate runs through all later Confucianism — recorded here, to be promoted to a
controversy page as sources deepen.

## Map of Major Sects

- [[classical-confucianism|Classical Confucianism]] — Confucius, Mencius, Xunzi; the Five Classics; an
  ethics of virtue, ritual, and government.
- [[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]] — the Song–Ming metaphysical revival (Zhu Xi's *li*/principle
  school vs. Wang Yangming's *xin*/mind school), responding to Buddhism and Daoism.
