# Shia Islam

> **Sect page.** The principal minority stream of [[islam|Islam]], defined by the Imamate.
> *(Stub — to be expanded; the al-Mizan tafsir of [[shia-islam|Tabatabai]] is flagged for ingest.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Shi'at Ali*, "the party of Ali." Arose from the dispute over succession to Muhammad: Shia hold that
authority passed by divine designation to **Ali ibn Abi Talib** (the Prophet's cousin and
son-in-law) and his descendants, the **Imams** — not to the elected caliphs. The martyrdom of
**Husayn** at Karbala (680 CE) is its defining sacred event. The largest branch is the **Twelvers**
(Imamiyya); others include the Isma'ilis and Zaydis.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Shares the [[quran|Qur'an]] and the Abrahamic frame with [[sunni-islam|Sunni Islam]] but locates
post-prophetic authority in the infallible **Imamate** rather than in *sunna* + community consensus —
the root of nearly every other divergence.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the same [[quran|Qur'an]] but **rejects the Sunni *Kutub al-Sittah*** (including
[[sahih-al-bukhari|Bukhari]] and Muslim), since their chains of transmission run through Companions
the Shia consider to have wronged Ali. In their place stands the Shia **Four Books** (*al-Kafi* of
al-Kulayni, *Man la yahduruhu al-Faqih*, and al-Tusi's two *Tahdhib* works). On Sunni hadith text
pages, Shia Islam appears under `rejected_by`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **Imamate** and the *isma* (infallibility) of the Imams; the **occultation** (*ghayba*) and
awaited return of the Twelfth Imam (the Mahdi); *taqiyya* (precautionary dissimulation under
persecution); a distinctive ritual calendar centered on Ashura.

## Hermeneutical Method

The Imams are the living, authoritative interpreters of the Qur'an; strong development of **ta'wil**
(esoteric/*batin* interpretation). The foundational figure is the sixth Imam
**[[jafar-al-sadiq|Ja'far al-Sadiq]]** (d. 765), credited with a threefold reading (literal /
historical / allegorical) that assigns the meaning of the **ambiguous verses** to the Imams alone —
holding that "a quarter of the Qur'an is about the Imams," who are designated by the verses of the
"Straight Path," the "Firmest Handle," and above all the **Light of God** (Q 64:8). This is the
sharpest contrast with the Sunni exegesis of [[al-tabari|al-Tabari]], for whom *ta'wil* is simply
*tafsir* and the ambiguous category is narrow. See [[tafsir|Tafsir]] and
[[allegorical-reading|Allegorical Reading]].

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The Sunni/Shia divide is Islam's central fault line; each regards itself as the faithful continuation
of the Prophet's community. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From Karbala through the Imami theological tradition to the Safavid establishment of Twelver Shiism
in Iran (16th c.) and the modern Shia world. Branches (Twelver, Isma'ili, Zaydi) diverge over the
line and number of Imams.
