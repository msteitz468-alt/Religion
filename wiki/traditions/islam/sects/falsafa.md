# Falsafa (Islamic Aristotelian–Neoplatonist Philosophy)

*Falsafa* (from Greek *philosophia*) is the school of **Islamic Aristotelian–Neoplatonist
philosophy** — its practitioners the *falasifa* — descending from al-Kindi through
**[[al-farabi|al-Farabi]]** and **[[avicenna|Avicenna]]** to **[[averroes|Averroes]]**. It is
treated here as a school within [[islam|Islam]] because it constitutes a distinct intellectual
community with its own authoritative texts (the Arabic Aristotle), method, and contested
relationship to revealed orthodoxy.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The *falasifa* held demonstrative reason (*burhan*) to reach truths that scripture conveys to
the masses in symbolic (*[[zahir-batin|batin]]*) form — subordinating, in their critics' eyes,
prophecy to philosophy. **[[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]]**'s
*[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|Tahafut al-Falasifa]]* prosecuted them on twenty theses and
declared them **infidels** (*[[takfir]]*) on three — the [[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the
world]], God's ignorance of particulars, and the denial of [[bodily-resurrection|bodily
resurrection]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

[[emanation|Emanation]] of intellects and spheres from the One; the eternity of the world;
God as the Necessary Existent and First Cause; the [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy|rational soul]]
as an incorporeal, immortal substance; causal necessity in nature (denied by Ghazali's
[[occasionalism]]).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy

Condemned by the [[asharism|Ash'arites]] and aligned, on several points (denial of attributes,
causal necessity), with the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]]. Defended by [[averroes|Averroes]] in the
*[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahafut al-Tahafut]]*.
