# Sufism

> **Sect page (mystical movement).** The esoteric dimension within [[islam|Islam]], cutting across
> [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] and [[shia-islam|Shia]]. The structural analogue of [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] in
> Judaism. Its supreme speculative metaphysician is [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]] (*al-Shaykh al-Akbar*) and
> its central esoteric text the [[fusus-al-hikam|Fusus al-Hikam]].

## Identity and Origins

*Tasawwuf* — the inward, ascetic-mystical path of Islam, aimed at purifying the self and drawing
near to (or being annihilated in) God. Emerged from early ascetic piety (8th–9th c.; al-Hasan
al-Basri, Rabi'a) and developed into organized *tariqas* (orders) around master-disciple
(*shaykh-murid*) lineages.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Not a separate sect with its own canon but a **dimension within** Sunni and Shia Islam. Most Sufis are
observant of the *shari'a*; Sufism supplies the *batin* (inner reality) that the outer law clothes —
exactly the *zahir/batin* relationship of the [[tafsir|tafsir]] tradition.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

No canon divergence: Sufism reads the [[quran|Qur'an]] and hadith esoterically, mining their **inner
(*batin*) meanings**, and adds a vast corpus of mystical literature (Rumi's *Mathnawi*, Ibn Arabi's
*Fusus al-Hikam* and *Futuhat*, al-Ghazali's *Ihya*).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

*Dhikr* (remembrance of God); the stations and states of the path; *fana* (annihilation of the ego in
God) and *baqa* (subsistence). The boundary with orthodoxy was tested by figures like **al-Hallaj**
("I am the Truth," executed 922) and defended by **al-Ghazali**, who reconciled Sufism with mainstream
theology.

The speculative summit is the **Akbarian** system of [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]] (d. 638/1240), elaborated
in the [[fusus-al-hikam|Fusus al-Hikam]] and the *Futuhat al-Makkiyya*: **[[wahdat-al-wujud|wahdat
al-wujud]]** (the unity of being — only the Real truly *is*, and all things are its self-disclosures)
and the **[[al-insan-al-kamil|Perfect Man]]** (*al-insan al-kamil*, the all-comprehensive mirror of
the divine Names, culminating in the Muhammadan Reality). Ibn Arabi's refusal to let pure
transcendence cancel divine immanence (the balance of [[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih and tashbih]]) is the
exact mystical counter-pole to [[mutazilism|Mu'tazilite]] rationalism. Transmitted through his
son-in-law **Sadr al-Din al-Qunawi** at Konya, the Akbarian school became the metaphysical idiom of
later Persianate, Ottoman, and Indian Sufism — while *wahdat al-wujud* drew sustained condemnation
from jurists (the Hanbali **Ibn Taymiyya** foremost) and the corrective **wahdat al-shuhud** ("unity
of witnessing") of Ahmad Sirhindi.

## Hermeneutical Method

Esoteric *ta'wil* — every verse has an outer and an inner sense; the cosmos and the self are read as
signs (*ayat*). A cross-tradition parallel to [[kabbalah|Kabbalist]] *sod* and Christian
[[typology|allegoresis]] (flag for a comparison page: ta'wil vs. Remez vs. Theoria).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Periodically contested — by literalist and later Salafi currents — but broadly integrated into Sunni
and Shia piety for most of Islamic history. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From early asceticism through al-Junayd's "sober" Sufism, al-Ghazali's synthesis, and Ibn Arabi's
speculative metaphysics, to the global *tariqas* (Qadiri, Naqshbandi, Mevlevi, etc.). A primary
vehicle of Islam's spread and of its poetry, music, and art.
