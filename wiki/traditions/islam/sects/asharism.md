# Ash'arism

Ash'arism is the dominant school of [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] **dialectical theology
([[kalam|kalam]])**, founded by al-Ash'ari (d. 935) as a middle path between the rationalism of
the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]] and strict traditionism. **[[al-ghazali|Al-Ghazali]]** is its most
influential exponent, and his *[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|Tahafut al-Falasifa]]* is an
Ash'arite weapon against [[falsafa|philosophy]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Central is **[[occasionalism]]**: there is no necessary connection between natural causes and
effects — God is the sole efficient cause, creating each event directly, which secures both
divine omnipotence and the possibility of [[prophetic-miracle|miracle]]. Ash'arites affirm the
divine attributes "without asking how" (*bila kayf*), the uncreatedness of the Quran, and human
acts as God-created but humanly "acquired" (*kasb*).

## Relationship to Other Schools

Against the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]] on the createdness of the Quran and the priority of reason;
against the [[falsafa|falasifa]] on the [[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the world]] and
causal necessity. See [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]].
