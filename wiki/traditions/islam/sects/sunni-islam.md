# Sunni Islam

> **Sect page.** The majority stream of [[islam|Islam]]. *(Stub — to be expanded as the Qur'an,
> hadith, and tafsir sources are ingested.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Ahl al-Sunna wa'l-Jama'a* — "the people of the *sunna* and the community." Defined over against
[[shia-islam|Shia Islam]] by accepting the legitimacy of the first four **Rightly-Guided Caliphs**
(Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali) and locating post-prophetic authority in the *sunna* of Muhammad and
the **consensus of the scholarly community**, rather than in a divinely-appointed Imamate.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The mainstream of Islam; "Sunni" is largely a retrospective label marking the side that prevailed in
the early succession disputes and that anchors authority in *sunna* + *ijma*.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Shares the [[quran|Qur'an]] with all Muslims. Its hadith canon is the **Kutub al-Sittah** (the Six
Books): [[sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]], Sahih Muslim, and the four *Sunan* (Abu Dawud,
al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah) — collections **rejected by Twelver [[shia-islam|Shia]]**, who
hold their own Four Books. On hadith text pages, Sunni Islam is the reference point for
`canonical_for`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The uncreated Qur'an; the four canonical schools of law (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali); *usul
al-fiqh* with four roots (Qur'an, *sunna*, *ijma*, *qiyas*); Ash'ari/Maturidi theology.

## Hermeneutical Method

Privileges *tafsir bi'l-ma'thur* (tradition-based exegesis — Qur'an by Qur'an, then hadith, then the
Companions); see [[tafsir|Tafsir]].

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Regards itself as orthodoxy; the Sunni/Shia divide is the central intra-Islamic fault line, recorded
without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol). Internal sub-currents (Salafi, traditional madhhab,
Ash'ari/Maturidi) coexist.

## Historical Development and Influence

Consolidated through the classical age of hadith collection (9th c.), the schools of law, and
Ash'ari/Maturidi kalam. The dominant form of Islam across most of the Muslim world.
