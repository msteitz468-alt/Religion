# Islam

Top-level tradition page. **Islam** is the Abrahamic monotheistic tradition founded on the
revelation Muslims believe was delivered to the Prophet **Muhammad** (c. 570–632 CE) through
the angel Gabriel and collected as the [[quran|Qur'an]]. This page is the parent node for the
Islamic sects filed under [[islam]] in `traditions/islam/sects/`.

Islam shares the Abrahamic patriarchal narrative with [[judaism|Judaism]] and
[[christianity|Christianity]] — the figures [[abraham|Ibrahim]], [[moses|Musa]],
[[jesus|Isa]], and others recur — but reframes it: the earlier scriptures (Tawrat, Zabur,
Injil) are held to have been genuine revelations later corrupted (*tahrif*), with the Qur'an
the final, uncorrupted seal of prophecy. This makes Islam a uniquely rich source of
**cross-tradition figure controversies** (see below).

## Shared / Mainstream Canon

The canon has two tiers, and their boundaries are themselves sect-defining:

- The **[[quran|Qur'an]]** — universally canonical across all Islamic sects; the literal,
  uncreated (Sunni) word of God. Its consonantal text is agreed; the licensed variant
  recitations are a controversy in their own right ([[qiraat-variant-readings|qira'at]]).
- The **Hadith** (reports of the Prophet's *sunna*) and the legal tradition built on them —
  here the canon **diverges by sect**: Sunnis receive the *Kutub al-Sittah* (the six books,
  incl. [[sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]] and Sahih Muslim); Twelver Shia reject those
  collections and receive their own *Four Books* (al-Kafi, etc.). This split is tracked
  structurally via each hadith text's `canon_scope`.

## Native Hermeneutical Frameworks

Qur'anic interpretation is **[[tafsir|tafsir]]**, classically divided into *tafsir bi'l-ma'thur*
(tradition-based: Qur'an by Qur'an, then hadith, then the Companions) and *tafsir bi'l-ra'y*
(reasoned/opinion-based). A parallel esoteric axis runs from the *zahir* (outer/exoteric sense)
to the *batin* (inner/esoteric sense), the latter developed especially in [[sufism|Sufi]] and
Shia *ta'wil*. These map onto the CLAUDE.md hermeneutical-tracking categories for Islam. The two
poles are concretely instantiated by the wiki's exemplars: the rationalist
[[al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] (*[[tafsir|tafsir bi'l-ra'y]]*, [[mutazilism|Mu'tazilite]] *ta'wil*
of the anthropomorphic verses) versus the mystic [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]] (the *batin* unveiled by
disclosure) — who read the same God in opposite spirits along the [[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih/tashbih]]
axis.

## Map of Major Sects / Sub-traditions

- [[sunni-islam|Sunni Islam]] — the majority (~85–90%); authority via the *sunna*, the consensus
  of the community (*ijma*), and the four Sunni schools of law (*madhhabs*).
- [[shia-islam|Shia Islam]] — the partisans of *Ali* and the *Imamate*; authority via the
  divinely-guided Imams; its own hadith canon and jurisprudence.
- [[sufism|Sufism]] — the mystical/ascetic dimension (*tasawwuf*) operating *within* both Sunni
  and Shia Islam; the *batin*, the path (*tariqa*), and union with the divine. Its summit is
  [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]]'s [[fusus-al-hikam|Fusus al-Hikam]] ([[wahdat-al-wujud|wahdat al-wujud]],
  the [[al-insan-al-kamil|Perfect Man]]).
- [[mutazilism|Mu'tazilism]] — the rationalist *kalam* school (the "People of Justice and Unity"); the
  five principles, the created Qur'an, and radical [[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih]]. Now chiefly absorbed
  into Zaydi and Twelver Shia theology; its exegetical monument is [[al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]]'s
  *al-Kashshaf*.
- [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] — a modern reformist *movement* (not a communal sect):
  gender-inclusive [[tafsir|tafsir]] reading the Qur'an holistically against an androcentric "prior
  text" ([[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur Rahman]]'s double movement; [[amina-wadud|Wadud]]'s
  [[wadud-quran-and-woman|*Qur'an and Woman*]]; the [[qiwama|qiwama]] / [[quran-4-34-male-authority|Q
  4:34]] debate). Contested as heterodox by traditionalists.
- [[asharism|Ash'arism]] — the dominant school of Sunni [[kalam|dialectical theology]];
  [[occasionalism]], the affirmation of the attributes *bila kayf*, the uncreated Qur'an. Its
  greatest exponent is [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]].
- [[falsafa|Falsafa]] — the school of Islamic Aristotelian–Neoplatonist *philosophy*
  ([[al-farabi|al-Farabi]], [[avicenna|Avicenna]], [[averroes|Averroes]]); [[emanation]], the
  [[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the world]], the immortal rational soul. Prosecuted as
  heterodox by al-Ghazali's [[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|*Tahafut al-Falasifa*]].

## Reason and Revelation: the Tahafut Debate

The classic Islamic confrontation of philosophy and theology is the exchange between
[[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]]'s [[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|*Tahafut al-Falasifa*]] (which
declares the [[falsafa|philosophers]] infidels on three doctrines — see *[[takfir]]*) and
[[averroes|Averroes]]'s reply, the [[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|*Tahafut al-Tahafut*]].

## Key Cross-Tradition Controversies (with Judaism & Christianity)

- [[crucifixion-in-the-quran|The crucifixion of Jesus]] — Q 4:157 ("they did not kill him… but
  it appeared so") vs. the Gospel passion narratives.
- The near-sacrifice: Islamic tradition predominantly identifies the son as **Ishmael**, not
  Isaac — see [[akedah-interpretation|the Akedah controversy]] and [[ishmael|Ishmael / Ismail]].
- The corruption (*tahrif*) of the earlier scriptures vs. their integrity in Judaism and
  Christianity.
