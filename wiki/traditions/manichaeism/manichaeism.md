# Manichaeism

**Chronology:** see the [[manichaeism-timeline|Timeline of Manichaeism]] and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]].

**Manichaeism** is the dualist universal religion founded by the prophet [[mani|Mani]]
(c. 216–274/277 CE) in Sasanian Mesopotamia. Conceived by its founder as the final,
complete, and self-consciously *universal* revelation — superseding and perfecting the
partial revelations of [[zarathustra|Zoroaster]], the Buddha, and Jesus — it spread from
North Africa (where Augustine was a Hearer for nine years) eastward
along the Silk Road to Tang and post-Tang China, where it survived for more than a thousand
years. Long known almost exclusively through the polemics of its enemies, Manichaeism has
been transformed as a field of study by a series of primary-source discoveries (the
[[mani|Cologne Mani Codex]], the Coptic Medinet Madi and Kellis papyri, the Turfan Iranian
fragments, the Chinese treatises).

## Self-Understanding and Origins

Mani was raised in a Judaeo-Christian **baptist (Elchasaite) community** in Babylonia. From
the age of twelve, and decisively at twenty-four, he received revelations from his heavenly
**Twin** (the *Syzygos* / *al-Tawm*), a celestial counterpart who disclosed his true nature
and mission. Breaking with the baptists over ritual washing and the cultivation of the
earth, Mani launched a public mission, gained access to the **Sasanian court of Shapur I**
(for whom he wrote the Middle Persian *Šābuhragān*), and sent missionaries across the empire
and to India. He understood himself as the **Apostle of Jesus Christ**, the **Paraclete**
promised in John's Gospel, and the *Seal of the Prophets* — the term later famously applied
to Muhammad. Crucially, and unlike his prophetic predecessors, Mani **wrote his own
scriptures**, so that his teaching could not be corrupted in transmission.

## Doctrine: The Two Principles and the Three Times

The core of Manichaean teaching is a thoroughgoing **cosmic dualism** of two co-eternal,
unoriginated principles — **Light** (Spirit, God, the Father of Greatness) and **Darkness**
(Matter, the realm of the Prince of Darkness) — narrated through **Three Times** (or
"Moments"):

1. **The Past** — the two realms exist separate and unmixed.
2. **The Present** — Darkness invades Light; in the ensuing cosmic war, particles of Light
   become trapped in matter (in plants, in the cosmos, and above all in the human soul). The
   visible universe is a vast machinery built to **filter and liberate** the imprisoned Light.
3. **The Future** — the Light is fully redeemed, the two realms are forever separated again,
   and Darkness is sealed away.

Salvation is therefore **gnosis** — the awakening of the soul to its true identity as
trapped Light — and a disciplined life that releases Light rather than further binding it.
This sharply dualist account makes Manichaeism the paradigm "Manichaean" system against
which later traditions defined themselves: Augustine's mature
doctrine of evil as *privatio boni* (privation of good) and the goodness of all being was
forged precisely against the Manichaean ontologizing of evil (see [[satan|Satan]],
[[theodicy|Theodicy]]).

## Structure: Elect and Hearers

The community was sharply two-tiered:

- **The Elect (*electi*)** — celibate, itinerant, vegetarian ascetics who renounced
  property and labor; by their eating they performed the ritual liberation of Light from
  food, and were bound by the "three seals" (of the mouth, the hands, and the breast).
- **The Hearers (*auditores*)** — the lay supporters who fed and sustained the Elect,
  married, worked, and hoped for rebirth as Elect. Augustine belonged to this class.

## Canon and Scripture

Manichaeism is unusual in antiquity for resting on a fixed canon of the **founder's own
writings**, composed mostly in Syriac/Aramaic:

- the **Living Gospel** (*Evangelion*)
- the **Treasure of Life**
- the **Pragmateia**
- the **Book of Mysteries**
- the **Book of Giants** (drawing on the Enochic giants tradition)
- the **Letters** (Epistles)
- the **Psalms and Prayers**
- the **Šābuhragān** (Middle Persian, for Shapur I)

To these the tradition added compendia of Mani's teaching such as the **Kephalaia**
("Chapters") and richly illustrated picture-books (the *Ārdahang*), Mani being remembered
also as a painter.

## Hermeneutical Method

Manichaean interpretation is governed less by a multi-sense scheme than by two moves: (1)
**the cosmogonic myth as the master key** — every scripture, ritual, and natural phenomenon
is read as an episode in the drama of Light and Darkness; and (2) **prophetological
supersession** — the revelations of Zoroaster, the Buddha, and Jesus are accepted as
genuine but partial and locally corrupted, with Mani's revelation completing and "sealing"
them. Manichaeans accordingly read the Christian scriptures selectively, rejecting the Old
Testament and its creator-God and embracing a docetic [[logos-christology|Christology]] (Jesus
only *appears* to suffer; the "Jesus patibilis" is rather the suffering Light diffused
through the cosmos).

## History, Spread, and Persecution

Mani was imprisoned and died in chains under **Bahram I** (c. 274/277), an event his
followers assimilated to the Passion (the "Crucifixion of Mani"). The religion was
persecuted almost everywhere: by the Sasanian Zoroastrian establishment (Kirdir), by Rome
(Diocletian's edict of 302 against it as a Persian fifth column), and relentlessly by the
Christian church as the arch-heresy. It nonetheless flourished for centuries in Central Asia
— becoming briefly the state religion of the **Uyghur Khaganate** (8th c.) — and persisted
in **South China** (where it merged with local religion) into the late medieval period.
Its memory shaped the heresiological imagination of the West, where later dualist movements
(the Paulicians, Bogomils, and Cathars) were branded "Manichaean" by their
opponents, though direct lineage is debated.

## Relationship to Gnosticism

Manichaeism is often classed as a **Gnostic religion** (see [[gnosticism|Gnosticism]],
[[gnosis|gnosis]]), sharing the trapped-spark soteriology, cosmic dualism, and a
[[demiurge|demiurgic]] devaluation of the material world. But it differs in being a
**deliberately founded, organized, missionary world religion** with a fixed scriptural
canon and a thousand-year institutional history, rather than a cluster of schools — which is
why it is filed here as an independent tradition rather than a sect. Kurt Rudolph's *Gnosis*
treats it within the broader Gnostic phenomenon (see
[[rudolph-gnosis-the-nature-and-history-of-gnosticism|Rudolph]]).
