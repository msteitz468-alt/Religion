# Nirmala Sikhs

A **scholarly-ascetic order** of celibate Sikhs. By tradition the Nirmala order was founded by
[[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind Singh]], who is said to have dispatched five Sikhs to Banaras to
learn Sanskrit — a story McLeod judges "highly improbable," since the order is scarcely mentioned in
Sikh literature until the late 18th century, when references rapidly multiply in the land grants and
endowments of the Sikh rulers (McLeod ~L10721–10727).

*(Scaffolded 2026-06-27 from [[mcleod-sikhism|McLeod]] Ch. 9.)*

## Identity and Organization

Like the [[udasi|Udasis]], the Nirmalas flourished under the Sikh rulers of the Punjab and continued
to prosper under **Maharaja [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]**. Their centres were *akhāṛās*, each
headed by a **mahant** who initiated celibate members; members wear saffron robes (McLeod
~L10728–10745).

## Canon, Scripture, and Hermeneutical Method

Members studied the [[guru-granth-sahib|Adi Granth]] but spent much time on **Hindu works — the
Vedas, the Shastras, the Puranas, and the Epics**; "the teachings they receive and impart are
strongly Vedantic" (McLeod ~L10734–10746). As itinerant preachers they did much to commend Sikh
teaching beyond the Punjab, especially at Hindu pilgrimage centres such as Hardwar and Allahabad.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Accepted as part of the Panth, but their ascetic discipline and "strongly Hindu" study deviate
sharply from the teaching of the [[khalsa|Tat Khalsa]]; they were regarded cordially by the **Sanatan
Sikhs**, and their fortunes declined with the Sanatan party during the Singh Sabha controversies —
"but never to the point of being totally eclipsed." They retain some influence, particularly around
Patiala (McLeod ~L10742–10754).
