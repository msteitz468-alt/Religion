# Celtic Reconstructionism and modern Druidry

Two distinct modern revivals draw on [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]]:

**Romantic Neo-Druidry** began in **18th-century Britain** with antiquarian societies and the inventions of
Iolo Morganwg (the *Barddas*, the Gorsedd ceremonies grafted onto the Welsh **Eisteddfod**). It is
**creative and ceremonial** rather than historical, and feeds groups such as the **Order of Bards, Ovates and
Druids (OBOD)** and the **Ancient Order of Druids**. Some strands are nature-spiritual and non-dogmatic;
others overlap with Wicca.

**Celtic Reconstructionism (CR)**, emerging in the **1980s–90s**, deliberately rejects romantic invention and
attempts to **reconstruct** ancient Gaelic and Brythonic practice from the **medieval texts**
([[gods-and-fighting-men|Mythological and Fenian cycles]], the [[mabinogion|Mabinogion]]), early law,
folklore, and **archaeology**. It is **polytheistic**, honors the **[[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]]**
and local land-spirits, keeps the **four fire-festivals** (Samhain, Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh), and
emphasizes historical accuracy, *fírinne* (truth), and hospitality.

Both movements face the **continuity problem**: no unbroken line connects them to the ancient
[[druidism|druids]], whose lore was deliberately never written. They are **revivals and reconstructions**,
honest about the gap, working from the same Christian-era sources this wiki uses.
