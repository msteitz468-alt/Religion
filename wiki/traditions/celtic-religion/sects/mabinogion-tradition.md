# The Mabinogion tradition (Welsh / Brythonic Celtic religion)

The **Welsh / Brythonic** stream of [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]] survives in the
**[[mabinogion|Mabinogion]]** — chiefly the **Four Branches of the Mabinogi** — where the old British gods
appear, even more thoroughly than in Ireland, as **lords and ladies of legend** rather than deities. Two
great families dominate: the **children of Dôn** (a goddess cognate to Irish Danu) — **Gwydion**, **Arianrhod**,
**Math**, **Gofannon** — and the **children of Llŷr** (cognate to Irish Lir) — **[[bran-the-blessed|Brân the
Blessed]]**, **[[branwen|Branwen]]**, **Manawydan** (the Welsh **[[manannan|Manannán]]**). Across them moves
**[[rhiannon|Rhiannon]]**, the sovereignty-goddess on her otherworldly white horse, and the lords of
**[[annwn|Annwn]]**, the Welsh **[[the-otherworld|Otherworld]]**.

## Canon and scripture

The **[[mabinogion|Mabinogion]]** (Lady Guest's translation from the *Red Book of Hergest* and *White Book of
Rhydderch*). The mythologically richest portions:

- **First Branch — Pwyll, Prince of Dyfed**: Pwyll trades places for a year with **Arawn**, king of
  **[[annwn|Annwn]]**, and wins **[[rhiannon|Rhiannon]]**, who later is falsely accused of devouring her own
  infant son (Pryderi).
- **Second Branch — Branwen daughter of Llŷr**: the giant-king **[[bran-the-blessed|Brân]]** gives his sister
  **[[branwen|Branwen]]** to the king of Ireland; her mistreatment brings catastrophic war, the magic
  cauldron of rebirth, and Brân's severed but still-speaking **prophetic head**.
- **Third and Fourth Branches**: Manawydan and the enchantment of Dyfed; **Math and Gwydion**, the creation of
  the flower-woman **Blodeuwedd**, and the fate-bound hero **Lleu Llaw Gyffes** (a Welsh reflex of
  [[lugh|Lugh]]).

## Doctrinal distinctives

- **[[annwn|Annwn]]** — the Welsh Otherworld, a kingdom one can rule, hunt in, and war with;
- the **cauldron of rebirth** that restores the slain (cf. the Dagda's cauldron, the Grail's antecedents);
- the **sovereignty-goddess** in [[rhiannon|Rhiannon]]: the land personified, wronged and vindicated;
- the **talismanic severed head** — Brân's head presiding over an Otherworldly feast.

## Hermeneutics

Welsh **bards** preserved the matter as **heroic romance**; the divinity is recoverable only by comparison
(Rhiannon ≈ the horse-goddess Epona/Rigantona "Great Queen"; Lleu ≈ Lugus; Manawydan ≈ Manannán). The
material later feeds the **Arthurian** legend and the Grail tradition.
