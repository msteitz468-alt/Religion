# Cārvāka (Lokāyata)

**Cārvāka**, also **Lokāyata** ("that which is found among people in general"), is the **materialist and
nāstika** school of Indian philosophy — one of the three heterodox systems (with [[buddhism|Buddhism]] and
Jainism) that **reject the authority of the [[vedas|Vedas]]**. It is the standard *pūrvapakṣa* (opponent's
position) against which every other Indian system argues. Its own literature is almost entirely lost;
[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] (Vol. I, Ch. V) reconstructs it from references in
[[upanishads|Upaniṣadic]] allusions to materialists and from later doxographies (Jayanta's
*Nyāyamañjarī*, Mādhava's *Sarva-darśana-saṃgraha*, Guṇaratna).

## Doctrine (per Dasgupta)

- **No soul, no afterlife.** Life and consciousness are by-products of the combination of the four material
  elements — "just as red colour results from mixing white with yellow, or the power of intoxication
  (*madaśakti*) is generated in molasses." At death the body burns to ashes; there is **no rebirth, no
  reward of actions, no virtue or vice**. Life is for enjoyment.
- **Perception alone is valid.** They deny the validity of **inference** (*anumāna*): one can never be
  certain the middle term (*hetu*) is free of some vitiating condition, so any true inference is merely
  accidental. Only what is directly perceived is trustworthy — a radical empiricism that undercuts every
  appeal to scripture or unseen causation (*adṛṣṭa*, [[karma|karma]]).
- **Two schools.** Dasgupta distinguishes the **Dhūrtta** ("rogue") Cārvākas — only the four elements
  (earth, water, air, fire) exist, the body a mere atomic combination, no self at all — from the
  **Suśikṣita** ("well-taught") Cārvākas, who allowed a soul but held it **perishes with the body**.
- **Name and origin.** The original work was in sūtras ascribed to *Bṛhaspati*; Jayanta and Guṇaratna quote
  fragments. Dasgupta derives "Cārvāka" from *carv*, "to eat" — those who "would only eat but accept no
  other religious or moral responsibility." The *Mahābhārata* tells of a Cārvāka who confronts Yudhiṣṭhira.

## Canon Stance

Cārvāka has **no scripture**; it actively **rejects** the [[vedas|Vedas]] and all śruti (it appears in the
`rejected_by` bucket on [[vedas|the Vedas page]]). This rejection, together with its denial of inference
and the afterlife, is what places it outside the *āstika* fold in the [[saddarshana|ṣaḍ-darśana]] map.

## Significance

In Dasgupta's reconstruction of the philosophical scene **before the Buddha**, Cārvāka materialism is one
of the live currents — alongside Upaniṣadic absolutism and Ājīvaka determinism —
against which the Buddha "erected a new superstructure of thought." (Another such current was the strict
determinism of the **Ājīvakas** under Makkhali Gosāla — *niyati*, fate, denying free will and moral
effort — recorded here without its own page.) It survives in the wiki chiefly as the
foil every other school must refute.
