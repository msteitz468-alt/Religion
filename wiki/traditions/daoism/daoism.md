# Daoism

**Chronology:** see the [[daoism-timeline|Timeline of Daoism]] and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]].

Top-level tradition page. **Daoism** (*Tao-chiao*, "the teaching of Tao") is the Chinese tradition
oriented to the **Dao** ("the Way") — the nameless, generative source and pattern of all things —
and to living in accord with it through sustained [[biospiritual-cultivation|biospiritual cultivation]]
and/or ritual activity. It is hermeneutically the most foreign tradition to the wiki's Abrahamic core:
there is no creator, no commandment, no covenant, and the founding text opens by declaring that
**"the Dao that can be spoken is not the eternal Dao."**

This page is the parent node for the Daoist sects under `traditions/daoism/sects/`.

## ⚠ The Philosophical/Religious Dichotomy: A Contested Framework

The traditional scholarly distinction between ***tao-chia*** ("philosophical Taoism" — the *Tao te
ching* and *Chuang-tzu*) and ***tao-chiao*** ("religious Taoism" — the organized communities from
the 2nd century CE) is challenged by [[russell-kirkland|Russell Kirkland]] (*Taoism: The Enduring
Tradition*, 2004) as a **modern scholarly fabrication** with no ancient basis:

- *Tao-chia* was a Han **bibliographic category**, not a self-designating community
- No premodern person ever identified as a practitioner of *tao-chia*
- The continuous practical core of the tradition is **biospiritual cultivation**, not wu-wei philosophy
- "Taoism" as an organized social community (*tao-chiao*) did not exist until the **5th century CE**
  when [[lu-hsiu-ching|Lu Hsiu-ching]] organized the first Taoist canon (~471 CE)

See [[taochia-taochiao-dichotomy|the controversy page]] for both positions.

The older "two streams" model (philosophical / religious) is preserved in `sects/philosophical-daoism.md`
and `sects/religious-daoism.md` as a record of the earlier wiki framework. The historically attested
sect pages are: [[heavenly-masters]], [[shang-ching]], [[ling-pao]], [[quanzhen]], [[cheng-i]].

## Canon

The proper dataset for defining Taoism is the **Tao-tsang** (Taoist Canon) — a Ming dynasty
(1444/45) compilation of all self-identified Taoist writings, including texts as diverse as
Mo-tzu and Han Fei-tzu. The shared/mainstream canon includes:

- **[[nei-yeh|Nei-yeh]]** ("Inner Cultivation," *Kuan-tzu* ch. 49, c. 4th c. BCE): Kirkland's
  candidate for the TRUE foundational Taoist text — focused entirely on biospiritual cultivation,
  no politics, no wu-wei as doctrine, no gender imagery; probably predates the *Tao te ching*
- **[[daodejing|Daodejing]]** (*Tao te ching*): a composite text grown through several stages of
  redaction; in its received form, includes biospiritual cultivation AND political instruction
- **[[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]]**: literary-philosophical masterpiece; our 33-chapter version was
  assembled by Kuo Hsiang (4th c. CE) from an original 52-chapter text

## Core Concepts (in place of theology)

- **[[dao|Dao]]** — the ineffable Way, source and course of all things.
- **[[wu-wei|Wu-wei]]** — "non-action"/effortless action; acting in accord with the grain of things.
- **[[ziran|Ziran]]** — "self-so," spontaneity/naturalness.
- **De** — virtue/power/virtuosity; the Dao as embodied in a thing.
- **[[biospiritual-cultivation|Biospiritual Cultivation]]** — the practical core: cultivating *ch'i*
  (life-energy), *ching* (vital essence), *shen* (spirit), *hsin* (heart/mind). The continuous thread
  from Nei-yeh through Quanzhen.
- ***Hsien*** — "transcendents" (often mistranslated "immortals"): those who have achieved spiritual
  transformation; goal of the cultivated life; NOT merely physical immortality.
- ***T'ai-p'ing*** — "Grand Tranquillity": the holistic harmony of cosmic, social, and individual
  orders; the political-theological vision underlying Taoist engagement with the state.

## Native Hermeneutical Frameworks

**Commentary-as-transformation** (CLAUDE.md): the great commentaries are not neutral glosses but
*practices* — Wang Bi's reading cultivates metaphysical insight; Heshang Gong's cultivates the adept's
body and longevity. Reading the text is meant to *change* the reader.

- **Biospiritual cultivation**: regulating ch'i, ching, shen — primary for T'ien-shih, Shang-ch'ing,
  Ch'üan-chen, Lung-men
- **Inner Alchemy (*nei-tan*)**: transforming "lead" into "gold" within the body; Sung and later
- **Xuanxue (Dark Learning)**: Wang Bi's philosophical-metaphysical approach (Tao as *wu*/non-being)
- **Longevity cultivation (Heshang Gong)**: images of the *Tao te ching* decode the adept's body

## Map of Historical Sects

*Historically attested communities (post-Kirkland):*

- [[heavenly-masters|T'ien-shih (Heavenly Masters)]] — foundational populist movement; women in
  sacerdotal roles; Chang Tao-ling (144 CE); evolved into [[cheng-i|Cheng-i]]
- [[shang-ching|Shang-ch'ing (Upper Clarity)]] — aristocratic; visualizational meditation; Lady Wei
  Hua-ts'un; 4th c. CE; T'ang literati Taoism via Ssu-ma Ch'eng-chen
- [[ling-pao|Ling-pao (Numinous Treasure)]] — communal liturgy; Mahayana integration; universalistic
  soteriology; organized by Lu Hsiu-ching into first canon
- [[quanzhen|Quanzhen (Integrating Perfection)]] — monastic; self-cultivation; Inner Alchemy; Wang Che
  (1113–70); women welcomed; endures at White Cloud Abbey, Beijing
- [[cheng-i|Cheng-i (Orthodox Unity)]] — hereditary southern priesthood; talismanic ritual; non-monastic;
  Tao-tsang compiled under its auspices (1444/45); dominant in Taiwan and coastal China today

*Older wiki framework (contested — see [[taochia-taochiao-dichotomy]]):*
- [[philosophical-daoism|Philosophical Daoism]] (*daojia*) — Kirkland argues this was never a real community
- [[religious-daoism|Religious Daoism]] (*daojiao*) — conflates all organized Taoism problematically

## Taoism and the State

Contrary to the Confucian falsehood that Taoists were anti-political recluses, Taoism was deeply
embedded in Chinese **imperial legitimation** across centuries. Key examples:
- Wang Yüan-chih legitimized the T'ang dynasty's founding
- [[ssu-ma-cheng-chen|Ssu-ma Ch'eng-chen]] redirected imperial state cult ceremonies per Shang-ch'ing doctrine
- The Tao-tsang was an imperial compilation project
- The concept of *t'ai-p'ing* (Grand Tranquillity) was a Taoist political theology

## Women in Taoism

Early T'ien-shih had parallel male/female sacerdotal structures. T'ang period: hundreds of abbeys for
women; notable practitioners (Huang Ling-wei, Pien Tung-hsüan, Tsu Shu). Ch'üan-chen: Sun Pu-erh as
co-founder; 20–40% female early clergy. Decline of formal roles in late imperial era reflects broader
social changes, NOT Taoist ideology. See [[tu-kuang-ting|Tu Kuang-t'ing's]] *Yung-ch'eng chi-hsien lu*.
