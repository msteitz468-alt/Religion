# Philosophical Daoism

> **Sect page.** The Lao-Zhuang wisdom tradition; the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] and [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]]
> read as philosophy. *(Both classics ingested.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Daojia*, the "school of the Dao" — the classical philosophical tradition of the [[daodejing|Daodejing]]
(attributed to [[laozi|Laozi]]) and the [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]]. Revived and systematized in the 3rd c. CE
as **Xuanxue** ("Dark Learning"), whose master interpreter was **[[wang-bi|Wang Bi]]**.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The contemplative-metaphysical pole of [[daoism|Daoism]], distinguished (by modern scholarship and the
Chinese terms) from the organized [[religious-daoism|religious]] stream — though the two share the
classics and the line between them is porous.

## Canon and Scripture

Centers on the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] and [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]], read as **wisdom and metaphysics**
rather than as manuals of immortality. The same `canon_scope` as religious Daoism (`canonical_for:
[daoism]`); the difference is how the texts are read.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **Dao** as the ineffable ground of all things — Wang Bi reads it as **wu** (non-being/nothingness)
as the root of being; **[[wu-wei|wu-wei]]** (effortless non-action) and **[[ziran|ziran]]** (spontaneous
naturalness) as the way of the sage; and (in the [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]]) a playful **skeptical relativism**
about language, fixed perspectives, and the conventional distinctions of right/wrong, useful/useless,
life/death.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Xuanxue** metaphysical commentary: [[wang-bi|Wang Bi's]] reading mines the Daodejing for an ontology
of being and non-being — a *philosophical* commentary-as-transformation aimed at insight, opposite
[[religious-daoism|Heshang Gong's]] longevity reading. See [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|the controversy]].

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Shares the classics with religious Daoism but reads them very differently; influenced Chan/Zen Buddhism
and Neo-Confucianism. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From the Warring States classics through 3rd-c. Xuanxue (Wang Bi, Guo Xiang's Zhuangzi commentary) into
the wider stream of Chinese thought, art, and poetry.
