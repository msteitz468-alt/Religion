# Ancient Roman Religion

Top-level tradition page. **Ancient Roman religion** (*religio Romana*) is the polytheistic system of the
Roman state and people — closely related to [[greek-religion|Greek religion]] (its gods largely
identified with the Greek pantheon via [[interpretatio-romana|interpretatio romana]]) but with a
distinctive character: it was above all a religion of **correct ritual practice (orthopraxy), legal
precision, divination, and the relationship between the gods and the *res publica*** — a contractual,
civic piety (*pietas*) in which the gods guaranteed Rome's success in exchange for exact worship.

## Character: Orthopraxy, Not Belief

Where Greek religion is mythic and Roman myth is largely borrowed from it, Roman religion's genius is
**procedural**: rites had to be performed **exactly** (a flaw voided the rite and required repetition),
overseen by priestly colleges (the **pontifices**, **augures**, the **Vestals**, the **flamines**). Belief
mattered far less than **doing the right thing in the right way** — a striking contrast with the
creed-centered traditions elsewhere in the wiki.

## A Religion Without Scripture

Like Greek religion, Rome had **no canon**. Its authoritative texts are literary and legal-ritual:

- **[[ovid-fasti|Ovid's *Fasti*]]** — the poem of the Roman religious **calendar** and its festivals (the
  single best source on Roman rites).
- **[[ovid-metamorphoses|Ovid's *Metamorphoses*]]** — the great repository of (Hellenized) myth.
- **[[virgil-aeneid|Virgil's *Aeneid*]]** — the national-religious epic of *pietas* and Rome's divine
  destiny.
- **[[cicero-nature-of-the-gods|Cicero's *On the Nature of the Gods*]]** — the ancient philosophical
  theology (Stoic, Epicurean, Academic views debated).
- The **Sibylline Books** and pontifical/augural records (lost).

## The Pantheon

The great gods — the **Di Consentes** (the Roman "twelve") — are largely the Greek Olympians under Roman
names: **[[zeus|Jupiter]]**, **[[hera|Juno]]**, **[[athena|Minerva]]** (the Capitoline Triad),
**[[poseidon|Neptune]]**, **[[ares|Mars]]**, **[[aphrodite|Venus]]**, and the rest — plus genuinely Roman
gods with no Greek twin: **[[janus|Janus]]** (beginnings/doorways), **[[quirinus|Quirinus]]**, the
**[[lares-and-penates|Lares and Penates]]** (household), **[[vesta|Vesta]]** and her flame, and abstract
state-deities (**[[fortuna|Fortuna]]**, Victoria, Bellona, Terminus). See [[interpretatio-romana|the
equivalence comparison]].

## Major Sects / Cult Forms

- [[roman-state-cult|The State Cult]] — the public *religio* of the priestly colleges and the calendar.
- [[imperial-cult|The Imperial Cult]] — worship of the deified emperors and the genius of the emperor.
- [[roman-household-religion|Household Religion]] — the daily cult of the Lares, Penates, and Vesta.
- [[mithraism|Mithraism]] — the mystery cult of Mithras, popular in the army.
- [[cult-of-isis-roman|The Cult of Isis]] — the Egyptian mystery religion adopted at Rome.
- [[magna-mater-cybele|The Cult of Magna Mater (Cybele)]] — the imported Phrygian mother-goddess cult.

## Reception and Influence

Foundational to the Western calendar (month and day names), law, and statecraft; the imperial cult was the
flashpoint of Christian **martyrdom** (refusal to sacrifice to the emperor); Christianity finally displaced
it (Theodosius, 380–392 CE). Revived in modern *Religio Romana* movements.
