# Mithraism

> **Sect page.** The Roman mystery cult of Mithras — and a famous rival/parallel to early Christianity.
> *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

A **secret mystery cult** of the god **Mithras** (loosely derived from the Iranian Mithra), flourishing in
the Roman Empire from the 1st to 4th centuries CE, **especially among soldiers and officials**. It met in
small underground temples (*mithraea*) and admitted only **men**, through **seven grades of initiation**
(Raven, Bridegroom, Soldier, Lion, Persian, Sun-Runner, Father).

## Doctrine and the Tauroctony

The cult left **no scripture**; its theology is reconstructed from iconography. Its central image is the
**tauroctony** — Mithras slaying a cosmic bull — laden with **astral/astrological symbolism** (often read
as a star-map) and apparently signifying salvation, life from death, and the soul's celestial ascent
through the grades.

## Significance — the Christian Comparison

Because Mithraism shared features with Christianity (a savior figure, sacred meals, initiation, the date of
**Sol Invictus / Dec 25**, moral brotherhood) and competed with it in the same centuries, it became a
classic comparandum in debates about Christian origins — the parallels real but the **dependence
contested** (a caution the wiki records without adjudication, as with
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the Zoroastrian-influence question]]).
