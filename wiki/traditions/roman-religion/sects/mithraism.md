# Mithraism

> **Sect page.** The Roman mystery cult of [[mithras|Mithras]] — and a famous rival/parallel to
> early Christianity. The wiki's richest documentation comes from the Ostia team study
> ([[laeuchli-groh-mithraism-in-ostia|Laeuchli & Groh 1967]]) and a popularizing Masonic essay
> ([[haywood-mithraism-freemasonry-ancient-mysteries|Haywood]]).

## Identity and Origins

A **secret mystery cult** of the god [[mithras|Mithras]] (loosely derived from the Iranian Mithra
of the [[avesta|Avesta]]), flourishing in the Roman Empire from the 1st to 4th centuries CE,
**especially among soldiers, slaves, freedmen, and minor officials**. It met in small
**underground temples (*mithraea*, also *spelaea*, "caves")** and admitted only **men**, through
**seven grades of initiation**. The cult left **no scripture**; its theology is reconstructed
almost entirely from **iconography and architecture**.

Haywood's popular sketch traces Mithra from an Iranian sky-god of the pre-dawn light, elevated
under Zoroastrianism "to the right hand of the awful Ormuzd," through a Babylonian astral
overlay and a Phrygian/Hellenistic phase, into Rome — entering (on his telling) via the
scattered armies of Mithridates Eupator after 66 BCE and spreading through soldiers, Syrian
merchants, slaves, and ultimately emperors. (This narrative leans on **Franz Cumont**, whose
works the volume notes were placed on the Catholic *Index*; the wiki records it as Haywood's
account, not established fact.)

## The Seven Grades

Each grade carried a planetary guardian, attested in the **Felicissimus mosaic at
[[ostia|Ostia]]** (Laeuchli & Groh):

| # | Grade | Planet | Attributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Corax** (Raven) | Mercury | raven, ritual vase, staff of Mercury |
| 2 | **Nymphus** (Bridegroom) | Venus | diadem of the goddess of love |
| 3 | **Miles** (Soldier) | Mars | helmet, lance, soldier's bag |
| 4 | **Leo** (Lion) | Jupiter | thunderbolt, sistrum, fire-shovel |
| 5 | **Perses** (Persian) | Luna | half-moon, *falx*, evening star |
| 6 | **Heliodromus** (Sun-Runner) | Sol | radiating crown, whip, morning star |
| 7 | **Pater** (Father) | Saturn | *falx*, Phrygian cap, staff of initiation, sacrificial bowl |

The grades structured both initiation and seating: lower grades sat toward the rear of the
mithraeum, higher grades toward the tauroctony at the front. Porphyry (*De abstinentia* IV.16)
reports the four higher grades were "participants" in the meal and the three lower "attendants,"
though Vermaseren and the Ostia team think all probably partook, if unequally.

## Doctrine, the Tauroctony, and Dualism

The cult's central image is the **tauroctonos** — Mithras slaying a cosmic bull (glossed by the
Ostia team as "the stylized relief of Mithra slaying the primeval bull by pulling back its head
and plunging a knife into its throat"), laden with **astral/astrological symbolism** and
signifying salvation, life from death ("out of the bull's dead body rose new life"), and the
soul's celestial ascent through the planetary grades toward **Sol**.

Crucially, Laeuchli argues that **by the time the cult reached Ostia the bull-slaying had become
symbolic**: the small, numerous Ostian mithraea were physically incapable of real bull sacrifice,
and the bloody **[[taurobolium]]** (a baptism in bull's blood) properly belonged to the
neighboring cult of [[magna-mater-cybele|Magna Mater]], not to Mithras — "the *tauroboliati* of
Ostia were Magna Mater initiates and not Mithraic ones." (Haywood, writing earlier and
popularly, **conflates** the taurobolium with Mithraism; the wiki flags this as an error.) Only
in a 4th-century "Mithraic fundamentalism" do inscriptions for actual bull-slaying reappear.

The cult's dualism is treated at length on its own page — see [[mithraic-dualism]]. The Ostia
team's key claim: Mithraic dualism was **not** a radical metaphysical cosmic dualism (the bull
"was not evil"; there is an altar to Ahriman at Ostia, yet Ahriman is no supreme principle) but
primarily a **social and cultic dualism** — the initiate circle's withdrawal from "the world" —
"a social dualism related to the masonic ritual, which fits without conflict into the daily
routine of a businessman."

## Liturgy and the Sacred Meal

Two liturgy-types are reconstructed: **the cult meal** and **services of initiation** (including
baptismal lustrations). The **sacred banquet** — reclining on the side *podia*, with bread, cake,
meat, grapes, and a *krater* of wine/water/blood-substitute — was the central rite, modeled on
the mythological feast of **Sol and Mithra** before their heavenly ascent. "The acts of the
mythology are liturgically imitated so that their saving efficacy may be realized by the
worshiper": the **Pater** enacted Mithra, the **Heliodromus** enacted Sol. Justin Martyr
(*Apology* 1.66) reports that "bread and water were used in the mysteries of Mithra," a parallel
the Church Fathers used polemically (Firmicus Maternus, Tertullian). Reconstruction is
explicitly tentative throughout — iconography is nearly the only evidence.

## The Ostian Evidence

The fullest body of evidence the wiki holds is from **[[ostia|Ostia]]**, the port of Rome,
where fourteen well-preserved mithraea were studied — see [[ostia|the Ostia page]] for the
archaeology, topography, and social makeup (a cult of dock-workers, slaves, and freedmen,
distributed evenly across the city in a way no other Ostian cult matched).

## Significance — the Christian Comparison

Because Mithraism shared features with Christianity (a savior figure, sacred meals, initiation,
the date of **Sol Invictus / Dec 25**, moral brotherhood, the house-church/mithraeum form) and
competed with it in the same centuries, it became the classic comparandum in debates about
Christian origins. Laeuchli's "Christ and Mithra" essay frames the question with a **fourfold
methodology** (Mithraic influence on Christianity; Christian influence on Mithraism; common
root; independent parallel response to the same "Roman condition") and argues the two religions
were, at bottom, **profoundly different**: Mithras "had history, but it had no theological and
communal drama which tore this history apart." The full analysis is filed at
[[christianity-mithraism-parallels|Christianity–Mithraism: Parallels, Dependence, or Common Soil?]]
— a caution the wiki records without adjudication, as with
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the Zoroastrian-influence question]]. The
separate claim of Masonic descent from the Mysteries is filed at
[[freemasonry-ancient-mysteries-descent|Does Freemasonry Descend from the Ancient Mysteries?]].

## Afterlife

Haywood traces a speculative survival of Mithraic motifs through
[[manichaeism|Manichaeism]] (Augustine's Manichee years), Paulicianism, and medieval dualist
cults — recorded as his conjecture, "necessarily vague and hard to prove."
