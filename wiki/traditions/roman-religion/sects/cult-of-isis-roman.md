# The Cult of Isis (in Rome)

> **Sect page.** The Egyptian mystery religion of Isis, hugely popular across the Roman world. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

The Roman adaptation of the Egyptian cult of the goddess **Isis** (with **Serapis** and **Horus/Harpocrates**),
which spread across the Mediterranean and became one of the most beloved religions of the Roman Empire. Rome
periodically **restricted** it (as a foreign cult) before it gained full acceptance.

## Devotion and the Mysteries

Isis was a **savior-goddess** of boundless mercy — "queen of heaven," mistress of fate and the sea, who
restored the slain Osiris and so promised her devotees **rebirth and a blessed afterlife**. The cult
offered an emotionally rich religion of **daily liturgy, personal devotion, and secret initiation**. The
fullest ancient account is the climactic Book 11 of **Apuleius' *The Golden Ass***, where the goddess
herself appears to the initiate.

## Significance

A leading example of the **personal salvation religions** that swept the Roman Empire alongside
[[mithraism|Mithraism]] and [[magna-mater-cybele|the Magna Mater]] — and a frequent comparandum (the
merciful mother-savior, "queen of heaven") in discussions of late-antique piety and Marian devotion.
