# Roman Household Religion

> **Sect page.** The everyday domestic cult of the Roman home. *(Stub.)*

## Identity

The **daily religion of the household**, performed at the home shrine (*lararium*) under the **paterfamilias**
as household priest. Its powers were intimate and protective:

- The **[[lares-and-penates|Lares]]** — household/ancestral guardian spirits, honored at the hearth and at
  crossroads (Lares Compitales).
- The **Penates** — gods of the storehouse and the family's prosperity, brought (myth said) from Troy by
  Aeneas.
- The **genius** of the paterfamilias (and the **iuno** of the materfamilias) — the generative life-spirit
  of the family line.
- **[[vesta|Vesta]]** of the hearth-fire, and **[[janus|Janus]]** of the door — the thresholds of the home.

## Significance

Household religion was the **most widespread and continuous** form of Roman piety — practiced daily by
ordinary people long after, and below the level of, the [[roman-state-cult|state cult]] and its great
temples. It illustrates ancient religion as **family and ancestral practice** rather than congregation or
creed, and is a key comparandum for ancestor veneration cross-culturally. Recorded without adjudication.
