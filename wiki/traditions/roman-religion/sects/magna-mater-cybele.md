# The Cult of Magna Mater (Cybele)

> **Sect page.** The Phrygian mother-goddess cult, the first great foreign religion officially adopted by
> Rome. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

The cult of **Cybele**, the Phrygian "**Great Mother**" (*Magna Mater*), **officially brought to Rome in
204 BCE** during the war with Hannibal (the Romans imported her sacred black stone on the advice of the
Sibylline Books, believing she would secure victory). She was given a temple on the Palatine and an annual
festival (the **Megalensia**) — yet her ecstatic Eastern rites were carefully **walled off** from Roman
citizens.

## Rites and Myth

Her myth centers on her consort **Attis**, who dies (self-castration/death) and is restored — a
**death-and-rebirth** vegetation cycle. Her **galli** were **eunuch priests** who castrated themselves in
ecstatic devotion; later the cult featured the **taurobolium**, a "baptism" in the blood of a sacrificed
bull, understood as **rebirth**. The contrast between her honored civic status and her wild rites
fascinated and disturbed the Romans.

## Significance

With [[cult-of-isis-roman|Isis]] and [[mithraism|Mithras]], a major **mystery and salvation religion** of
the empire; the **dying-and-rising Attis** and the blood-rebirth *taurobolium* are recurrent comparanda in
the study of late-antique religion and Christian origins (parallels noted, dependence not asserted).
