# Seiðr (Norse Magic)

> **Practice-tradition page.** **Seiðr** is the Norse tradition of **magic, prophecy, and trance** — a form
> of sorcery for seeing and shaping **fate**. *(Stub.)*

## The Magic of Fate

Seiðr was practiced above all by the **völva** (seeress), a wandering woman of high status who, seated on a
raised platform and aided by chants, entered **trance** to **foretell the future**, find hidden things, and
**influence fortune, weather, health, and the outcome of battles** — in effect to manipulate the very
**fate** spun by the [[norns|Norns]]. The great cosmic prophecy of the [[poetic-edda|Poetic Edda]], the
*Völuspá* ("Prophecy of the Seeress"), is framed as a völva's vision of the world's beginning and its end at
[[ragnarok|Ragnarök]].

## Its Patrons and Its Taboo

Seiðr belonged especially to **[[freyja|Freyja]]**, who is said to have **taught it to the Æsir** (and to
[[odin|Odin]] himself, the god most associated with magical knowledge). Yet for a **man** to practice seiðr
was shameful — it carried the charge of **ergi** (unmanliness), and Loki taunts Odin for having practiced it.
So Norse magic was powerful, sought-after, and socially **ambivalent** — a women's art that even the
All-Father stooped to learn.
