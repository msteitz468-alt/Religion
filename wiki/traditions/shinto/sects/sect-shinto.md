# Sect Shinto (Kyōha Shintō)

**Sect Shinto** (*Kyōha Shintō*) names the **organized, congregational denominations** of [[shinto|Shinto]] —
classically the **"Thirteen Sects"** that the Meiji state recognized as *religions* (with founders, doctrines,
and membership), distinct from the "non-religious" national [[shrine-shinto|shrine]] and
[[state-shinto|State Shinto]]. Unlike diffuse shrine practice, these are **bounded faith-communities**, often
with a **charismatic founder and revealed teaching**.

## The main types

- **Mountain-worship sects** — *Fusōkyō*, *Ontakekyō*, *Jikkōkyō*: cults of sacred mountains (especially
  **Mount Fuji** and **Mount Ontake**), pilgrimage, and ascetic practice.
- **Purification sects** — *Shinshūkyō*, *Misogikyō*: centered on **harae/misogi** (ritual purification).
- **Confucian-Shinto ethical sects** — *Shintō Taiseikyō*, *Shinrikyō*: moral cultivation framed by Shinto.
- **Faith-healing / revelation sects** — above all **Kurozumikyō** (founded by Kurozumi Munetada, devotion to
  [[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]), **Konkōkyō** (a mediating single high kami), and **Tenrikyō** (founded by the
  prophetess **Nakayama Miki**, 1838, on a revelation of *Tenri-Ō-no-Mikoto*, "God the Parent") — the last now
  often classed among Japan's **New Religions** rather than Shinto proper.

These sects gave Shinto something it otherwise lacks — **founders, scriptures of revelation, doctrine, and
conversion** — and seeded many of Japan's modern **new religious movements**.
