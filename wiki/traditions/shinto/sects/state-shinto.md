# State Shinto (Kokka Shintō)

**State Shinto** (*Kokka Shintō*) was the **modern, nationalized form of [[shinto|Shinto]]** created by the
**Meiji** government after 1868 and dismantled by the Allied occupation in **1945**. It harnessed the ancient
**imperial-descent myth** of the [[kojiki|Kojiki]] into a **state ideology** binding religion, emperor, and
nation.

## Construction and doctrine

At the **Meiji Restoration** the new government **forcibly separated Shinto from Buddhism** (*shinbutsu
bunri*, ending centuries of [[folk-shinto|shinbutsu-shūgō]]), nationalized the shrines, and elevated the cult
of the **emperor** as the living descendant of the sun-goddess **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** through
**[[ninigi|Ninigi]]** and the first emperor **[[jimmu|Jimmu]]**. Its core tenets:

- the **divinity of the emperor** (*arahitogami*, "a kami in human form") and the **unbroken imperial line**;
- **kokutai** — the unique "national essence/body" of Japan as a divine polity;
- the legal fiction that shrine rites were **"non-religious" patriotic duty** (so as not to violate the Meiji
  constitution's nominal religious freedom), making shrine attendance effectively **compulsory**.

It fostered emperor-worship, **Yasukuni Shrine** (enshrining the war dead as kami), and the ideology that drove
Japanese militarism through the Pacific War.

## Disestablishment and controversy

In **1945** the occupation's **Shinto Directive** abolished State Shinto, ended state support for shrines, and
in 1946 the **Emperor publicly renounced his divinity**. State Shinto remains deeply **controversial**: many
regard it as a modern political distortion of Shinto rather than authentic religion, and the status of
**Yasukuni** (and official visits to it) is a live diplomatic and religious dispute. Recorded here *without
adjudication* per the wiki's Contradiction Protocol.
