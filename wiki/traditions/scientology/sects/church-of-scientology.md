# The Church of Scientology

The corporate-religious organization founded by [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]] (first churches December 1953, incorporated in California February 18, 1954). The official steward of "the tech" and the normative body over against the [[free-zone-scientology|Free Zone]] of independent practitioners.

## Identity and Origins

Three "churches" were incorporated in Camden, NJ, in **December 1953**: Church of American Science, Church of Scientology, Church of Spiritual Engineering. The California church (eventually "Church of Scientology of California" / CSC) became the flagship. Hubbard's explicit motivation — documented in his April 10, 1953 letter to Helen O'Brien — was business and legal protection: religion exempts from taxation and FDA medical scrutiny.

Its current structure:
- **Church of Scientology International (CSI)**: the management church
- **Religious Technology Center (RTC)**: holds all Dianetics and Scientology trademarks; polices "standard tech"; chaired by **David Miscavige** since 1987
- **Church of Spiritual Technology (CST)**: archival preservation
- **[[sea-org|Sea Organization (Sea Org)]]**: dedicated cadre; billion-year contracts; staffs senior positions

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The **normative, trademark-holding** body that defines official Scientology. Regards [[free-zone-scientology|Free Zone]] practitioners as heretical "squirrels" (who alter the tech) and pursues trademark/copyright actions against them.

## Canon and Hermeneutics (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **entire Hubbard corpus ("Source")** as scripture — every policy letter, bulletin, recorded lecture, and published book, in verbatim form. The confidential **[[operating-thetan|OT materials]]** are an inner canon accessible only to advanced, paying members.

**Hermeneutical method**: "**Keeping Scientology Working**" (HCO Policy Letter, February 7, 1965) — exact, unaltered, verbatim application of Source. No interpretation is permitted; deviation is a punishable "squirreling." This is an exceptionally *anti-interpretive* hermeneutic — the opposite pole from the commentary traditions that dominate this wiki's other entries.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

- Exclusive authority over the application of [[auditing|auditing]] and the **Bridge to Total Freedom**
- Confidential [[operating-thetan|OT levels]]: graded, paid access to esoteric materials
- Strict management structure: ethics, conditions, Suppressive Person (SP) designations
- **Disconnection**: required severance of contact with declared SPs (critical family, former members)
- **[[fair-game|Fair Game]]** doctrine: SPs subject to harassment, litigation, deception
- **Rehabilitation Project Force (RPF)**: punitive labor unit for Sea Org members

## Historical Development and Key Events

### 1950s–60s: Becoming a Religion

Dianetics (1950) was explicitly marketed as a *science*. The transition to religion was driven by:
- Financial collapse (Purcell bankruptcy, 1952)
- FDA pressure on medical claims (1963 raid on Washington DC church)
- Tax pressure: DC church recognized as tax-exempt 1956; CSC 1957; IRS revoked CSC exemption 1967

**June 1960** HCO Bulletin declared "Scientology's closest spiritual ties with any other religion are with Orthodox (Hinayana) Buddhism." **February 1969** HCO Policy Letter: "visual evidences that Scientology is a religion are mandatory." By 1973 the E-meter was officially redefined as "a religious artifact used as a spiritual guide in the church confessional."

### Guardian's Office (1966–79)

The Guardian's Office (GO), formed March 1966, was Hubbard's intelligence arm — what Urban calls a "private CIA." Under Mary Sue Hubbard (director) and Jane Kember (Guardian Worldwide from 1969), the GO reached up to 5,000 covert agents.

Major operations:
- **Operation Snow White** (planned 1973): Michael Meisner and Gerald Wolfe ("Silver") placed inside the IRS; 30,000 pages of documents stolen; IRS Chief Counsel Office bugged
- **Operation Freakout** (vs. Paulette Cooper): attempt to frame journalist for bomb threats
- **Operation Italian Fog** (vs. Clearwater mayor Gabe Cazares)

**July 7, 1977 FBI raids**: 134 agents simultaneously raided Church headquarters in Washington, DC, and Los Angeles; crowbars and battering rams; 200,000 documents seized. Mary Sue Hubbard and Jane Kember convicted and imprisoned. Hubbard named unindicted co-conspirator; spent remaining years in hiding.

### The IRS War and 1993 Settlement

After the 1967 revocation of CSC's tax exemption, the Church fought the IRS for **26 years**:
- 2,700 simultaneous lawsuits against the IRS at the war's peak
- Private investigators surveilling IRS officials' personal lives
- Unscheduled meeting (October 1991): Miscavige and Rathbun with IRS Commissioner Fred Goldberg
- **October 1, 1993**: tax-exempt status granted to all Scientology entities; $12.5 million settlement (kept secret until WSJ leak, 1997); IRS dropped all audits of 13 entities
- Miscavige declared: "**The war is over!**"

Urban argues this battle was not just about taxes but shaped how American law *defines religion* — the IRS's 13-point "church" criteria were published in 1977, the same year Snow White arrests occurred.

### Corporate Restructure (1981–82)

Following Hubbard's retirement and the GO's disgrace, Miscavige led a corporate restructure:
- **CSI** (1981): management church
- **CST** (1982): archival
- **RTC** (1982): trademark holder; Miscavige's power base

Vaughn Young described the structure as "like a Rubik's Cube" — deliberately opaque to outside scrutiny.

### Celebrity Strategy

**Project Celebrity** (1955): Hubbard identified specific celebrities as "quarry" to be recruited. Celebrity Centre opened in Los Angeles (1969). Notable members: **Tom Cruise** (joined 1986) and **John Travolta** (1975). The Messianic Project (late 1970s): surveys conducted to market Hubbard as the next messiah comparable to Christ, Mohammed, and Buddha (apparently dropped by the early 1980s).

### Post-Hubbard (1986–present)

Hubbard died January 24, 1986. **David Miscavige** (b. 1960, joined 1971) consolidated power over the RTC. Critics allege a coup ousting rivals including David Mayo and Pat Broeker; the Church calls it a natural succession. Multiple ex-senior executives (Marty Rathbun, Mike Rinder, Paul Haggis) have since left and described Miscavige as abusive and controlling.

**Membership decline**: American Religious Identification Survey found US membership fell from 55,000 (2001) to 25,000 (2008). Former RTC Inspector General Marty Rathbun (2009): Church is "the Titanic hitting the iceberg."

**Lisa McPherson** (died Clearwater, December 1995): thromboembolism after 17-day "Introspection Rundown" at Flag Service Organization; felony charges against the Church dropped June 2000 after medical examiner amended report; remains a major touchstone for critics.

**OSA** (Office of Special Affairs): replaced the Guardian's Office post-1979; now uses private investigators as cut-outs for what critics say is the same intelligence function.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Regards [[free-zone-scientology|Free Zone]] practitioners as unauthorized "squirrels." Aggressively pursues trademark infringement and copyright cases against independent Scientologists and critics posting OT materials. The OT materials controversy in cyberspace (FACTNet, Fishman affidavit, Anonymous, Wikipedia ban 2009) has largely failed to prevent global dissemination.
