# Scientology

Top-level tradition page and the wiki's first **20th-century new religious movement** with a single named
founder, **[[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]** (1911–1986). It grew out of Hubbard's 1950 self-help system
**[[dianetics|Dianetics]]** into a full religion, the **[[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]]**
(1953–54). Whether it is a religion at all is one of its central controversies (see below).

## Worldview

Scientology teaches that a person is fundamentally a **[[thetan|thetan]]** — an immortal spiritual being
that has lived countless lifetimes and is distinct from the body and the mind. The mind has an *analytical*
part and a *reactive* part; the reactive mind stores **[[engram|engrams]]** (complete recordings of
moments of pain and unconsciousness) that cause irrational fears, illnesses, and suffering. The practice of
**[[auditing|auditing]]** — counseling with the **E-meter** — locates and "erases" engrams, first to the
state of **Clear** (free of the reactive mind), then up the **Operating Thetan (OT)** levels along the
graded path called the **Bridge to Total Freedom**, toward full spiritual ability ("cause over matter,
energy, space, time, and life").

The advanced (OT) materials are **confidential**, revealed only to members who have progressed and paid for
them. The most famous is **OT III** — Hubbard's account of **Xenu**, a galactic ruler who 75 million years
ago brought billions of beings to Earth and destroyed them, whose disembodied spirits ("body thetans")
cling to people and must be audited away. Scientology neither confirms nor publicizes these materials.

## Canon and Hermeneutics — "Source"

Scientology's scripture is the **entire corpus of Hubbard's writings and recorded lectures**, collectively
called **"Source."** Its defining hermeneutic is **verbatim fidelity**: Hubbard's "**standard tech**" must
be applied **exactly**, with **no interpretation or deviation** — the policy "**Keeping Scientology
Working**" makes correct, unaltered application the supreme value. This is an unusually *anti-interpretive*
hermeneutic (the opposite pole from the commentary traditions elsewhere in the wiki), combined with a
**graded, confidential** structure of revelation (you may not read the upper levels until you reach them).

## Organization and Sects

- [[church-of-scientology|The Church of Scientology]] — the official organization (Church of Scientology
  International; the **Religious Technology Center** holds the trademarks; the **Sea Org** is its dedicated
  cadre); led since Hubbard's 1986 death by **David Miscavige**.
- [[free-zone-scientology|The Free Zone]] — independent Scientologists who practice "the tech" *outside*
  the official Church (which regards them as heretical "squirrels").

## Status, Reception, and Controversy

Recognized as a **religion and tax-exempt** by the US IRS (1993) and accepted as a religion in some
countries, but classified as a **commercial enterprise or harmful cult** in others (Germany, France, etc.).
Heavily documented controversies include aggressive litigation, the **Guardian's Office / Operation Snow
White** (infiltration of US agencies; 1979 convictions), **"Fair Game"** and **disconnection** policies,
the 1995 death of **Lisa McPherson**, and forced-labor/abuse allegations. See
[[scientology-religion-or-commercial-enterprise|the status controversy]]. Recorded without adjudication
(Contradiction Protocol).

## Foundational Texts ("Source")

The movement's foundational books, all by Hubbard and all "Source": [[dianetics|Dianetics]] (1950),
[[science-of-survival|Science of Survival]] (1951, the [[tone-scale|Tone Scale]]), and
[[scientology-fundamentals-of-thought|Scientology: The Fundamentals of Thought]] (1956, the "basic book"
introducing the [[eight-dynamics|Eight Dynamics]] and the [[arc-triangle|ARC triangle]]).
