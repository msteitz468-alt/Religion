# Zen (Chan / Thiền / Seon)

**Zen** — *Chan* in China, *Thiền* in Vietnam, *Seon* in Korea, *Zen* in Japan, all from
Sanskrit *dhyāna* ("meditation") — is the meditation-centered school of East Asian
**[[mahayana|Mahayana]]** [[buddhism|Buddhism]]. It is famous for relativizing scripture in
favor of **direct, present-moment realization** of awakening.

## Identity and Origins

Traditionally traced to the Indian monk **Bodhidharma** (c. 5th–6th c.), who is said to
have brought "the Buddha-mind" to China, Chan crystallized in Tang-dynasty China and spread
to Vietnam (Thiền), Korea (Seon), and Japan (Zen). It defines itself by a famous four-line
slogan attributed to Bodhidharma:

> "A special transmission outside the scriptures; not relying on words and letters; directly
> pointing to the human mind; seeing one's nature and becoming a Buddha."

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Zen is a [[mahayana|Mahayana]] school: it accepts the bodhisattva ideal, the
[[sunyata|emptiness]] of the [[heart-sutra|Prajñāpāramitā]], and universal
**[[buddha-nature|Buddha-nature]]** (*tathāgatagarbha*) — every being already possesses the
awakened mind and need only realize it. Its distinctiveness is not a new canon but a new **emphasis**: awakening is *sudden* and
*direct*, available in this body and mind, not the term of long doctrinal study.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Zen shares the Mahayana sutra canon — it especially prizes the
[[heart-sutra|Heart Sutra]] (chanted daily), the *Diamond*, *Laṅkāvatāra*, and
*Vimalakīrti* sutras — but **subordinates all scripture to direct experience**. Its own
characteristic literature is the **recorded sayings** of masters and the **kōan/gōng'àn**
collections (the *Blue Cliff Record*, the *Gateless Gate*), used not as doctrine but as
meditation devices. (Shares the Mahayana corpus's `rejected_by: [theravada]` standing.)

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Universal **Buddha-nature**; **sudden awakening** (the Southern Chan emphasis, after
Huineng); the primacy of seated meditation (*zazen* / *thiền*); and the
"mind-to-mind transmission" of awakening through a teacher–student **lineage**.

## Hermeneutical Method (mandatory)

Zen's hermeneutic is the **deliberate subordination of the text**: "not relying on words and
letters." Truth is not conveyed propositionally but **pointed at** — by a kōan's logical
impasse, by a gesture, by silence — to break conceptual grasping (*prapañca*) and provoke
direct insight. Scripture, when used, is read as a "finger pointing at the moon," never the
moon itself. This is an experiential extreme of the [[neyartha-nitartha|neyārtha/nītārtha]]
logic shared with the rest of Buddhism, and a cross-tradition contrast to the text-anchored
[[theravada|Theravada]] commentarial method.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Internally divided into lineages (in China the "Five Houses," incl. what became **Linji** /
Rinzai and **Caodong** / Sōtō in Japan). It shares Mahayana's mutual non-recognition of
canon with [[theravada|Theravada]]. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From Tang China through the great Song kōan collections; transmitted to Vietnam (Thiền,
where **[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]** stands in a centuries-old lineage), Korea
(Seon), and Japan (Rinzai and Sōtō, shaping the arts — tea, calligraphy, gardens). In the
20th–21st centuries Zen, especially via **[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]**'s Plum
Village and "Engaged Buddhism," became a principal channel of [[mindfulness|mindfulness]]
into Western secular and clinical life.
