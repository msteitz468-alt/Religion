# Theravada

> **Sect page.** The oldest surviving Buddhist school. *(Ingested: [[dhammapada|Dhammapada]] +
> [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]'s Visuddhimagga.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Theravada*, "the Doctrine/Way of the Elders," is the sole survivor of the early (pre-Mahayana)
Buddhist schools, dominant in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos. It regards itself as
the most faithful preservation of the historical [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]]'s teaching.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The conservative wing of [[buddhism|Buddhism]]; the baseline against which the later
[[mahayana|Mahayana]] defined itself (and which Mahayana polemically called *Hinayana*, "lesser
vehicle").

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **[[tipitaka|Pali Canon]] alone** as *buddhavacana* and **rejects the Mahayana sutras**
([[heart-sutra|Heart]], [[lotus-sutra|Lotus]], etc.) as later, non-authentic compositions — the
defining Buddhist canon split. On those Mahayana-sutra text pages, Theravada appears under
`rejected_by`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **arhat** ideal (the individual who attains *nirvana* by following the path) rather than the
universal bodhisattva vocation; rigorous **[[anatta|non-self]]** analysis; the **Abhidhamma**
(systematic psychology-metaphysics); a gradual, monastic-centered path.

## Hermeneutical Method

Anchored in a fixed **commentarial lineage** — supremely [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]'s 5th-c.
*Visuddhimagga* and *atthakatha* (commentaries) — read analytically; the suttas are taken largely at
face value, with the [[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]] distinction used sparingly.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Mutual non-recognition of canon with Mahayana/Vajrayana (each regards the other's added scriptures as
inauthentic or as provisional teaching). Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Transmitted to Sri Lanka under Ashoka (3rd c. BCE), where the Pali Canon was committed to writing
(1st c. BCE); the Mahavihara lineage and Buddhaghosa's synthesis fixed its classical form. The base of
the modern *vipassana* meditation movement.
