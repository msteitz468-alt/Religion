# Theravada

> **Sect page.** The oldest surviving Buddhist school. *(Ingested: [[dhammapada|Dhammapada]] +
> [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]'s Visuddhimagga.)*

## Identity and Origins

*Theravada*, "the Doctrine/Way of the Elders," is the sole survivor of the early (pre-Mahayana)
Buddhist schools, dominant in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos. It regards itself as
the most faithful preservation of the historical [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]]'s teaching.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The conservative wing of [[buddhism|Buddhism]]; the baseline against which the later
[[mahayana|Mahayana]] defined itself (and which Mahayana polemically called *Hinayana*, "lesser
vehicle").

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **[[tipitaka|Pali Canon]] alone** as *buddhavacana* and **rejects the Mahayana sutras**
([[heart-sutra|Heart]], [[lotus-sutra|Lotus]], etc.) as later, non-authentic compositions — the
defining Buddhist canon split. On those Mahayana-sutra text pages, Theravada appears under
`rejected_by`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **arhat** ideal (the individual who attains *nirvana* by following the path) rather than the
universal bodhisattva vocation; rigorous **[[anatta|non-self]]** analysis; the **Abhidhamma**
(systematic psychology-metaphysics); a gradual, monastic-centered path.

## Hermeneutical Method

Anchored in a fixed **commentarial lineage** — supremely [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]'s 5th-c.
*Visuddhimagga* and *atthakatha* (commentaries) — read analytically; the suttas are taken largely at
face value, with the [[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]] distinction used sparingly.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Mutual non-recognition of canon with Mahayana/Vajrayana (each regards the other's added scriptures as
inauthentic or as provisional teaching). Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## The Visuddhimagga Path in Detail

[[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]'s *[[visuddhimagga|Visuddhimagga]]* organizes the entire path as the
**[[seven-purifications|seven purifications]]** (*satta-visuddhi*), from virtue through
concentration through the progressive **[[vipassana-insight-knowledges|insight knowledges]]** to
the four supramundane paths. Key features:

- **[[jhana|Jhāna]] (meditative absorption)**: four material and four immaterial jhānas; "dry-insight"
  (*sukkha-vipassaka*) path also recognized
- **Ten imperfections of insight** (*vipassanupakkilesa*): illumination, knowledge, rapture,
  tranquillity, bliss, resolution, exertion, assurance, equanimity, subtle attachment — arise to a
  meditator on the right course and must be recognized as defilements
- **Seven kinds of noble persons** (faith devotee, liberated by faith, body witness, both-ways
  liberated, Dhamma devotee, attained to vision, liberated by understanding) distinguished by the
  characteristic predominant at emergence and the faculty in excess
- **Change-of-lineage knowledge** as intermediate between the sixth and seventh purifications:
  takes Nibbāna as object for the first time; passes out of the ordinary man's lineage
- **Four path knowledges** simultaneously exercise four functions: full-understanding (of suffering),
  abandoning (of origin), realizing (of cessation), developing (of the path)
- **Attainment of cessation** (*nirodha-samāpatti*): complete cessation of consciousness and
  concomitants; available only to non-returners and Arahants with the eight attainments; not death

Key doctrinal commitments traceable in the *Visuddhimagga*:
- The **Pali Canon as the sole standard**: Buddhaghosa resolves canonical tensions by definition,
  not by a *neyartha/nitartha* hierarchy
- The **Suttanta vs. Abhidhamma** distinction acknowledged but not hierarchically ranked (e.g.
  on number of liberations, on "when defilements are abandoned")
- The **Abhidhamma cognitive series** (*vīthi*): life-continuum → adverting → five-door → receiving
  → investigating → determining → seven impulsions → registration → life-continuum

## Historical Development and Influence

Transmitted to Sri Lanka under Ashoka (3rd c. BCE), where the Pali Canon was committed to writing
(1st c. BCE); the Mahavihara lineage and Buddhaghosa's synthesis fixed its classical form. The base of
the modern *vipassanā* meditation movement, and of a vigorous English-language scholarship — above all
**[[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bhikkhu Bodhi]]**'s translations of the four Nikāyas and his anthology
*[[bodhi-in-the-buddhas-words|In the Buddha's Words]]*, the wiki's primary window onto the early
discourses, and **[[walpola-rahula|Walpola Rahula]]**'s *[[rahula-what-the-buddha-taught|What the
Buddha Taught]]* (1959), the classic short doctrinal exposition.
