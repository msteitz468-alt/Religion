# Gelug

> **Sect page.** The school founded by **[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]** (1357–1419) within
> [[tibetan-buddhism|Tibetan Buddhism]]; an offshoot and reform of the **[[kadam|Kadam]]** lineage of
> [[atisha|Atiśa]]. Its root path-text is the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]**.

## Identity and Origins

The Gelug ("system of virtue") was founded by **[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa Blo-bzang-grags-pa]]**
(1357–1419), who completed his **[[lamrim-chenmo|Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path]]** in 1402
at **Rva-sgreng (Reting)**, an old center of the **Kadam** order founded by [[atisha|Atiśa]]'s chief
disciple **Drom-tön-pa**. Reforming and systematizing that Kadam graded-path inheritance, the school
came to be called the "New Kadam." Its institutional birth is conventionally dated to the founding of
**Ganden** monastery (1409); it later became the dominant school of [[tibetan-buddhism|Tibetan
Buddhism]] and the lineage of the **Dalai** and **Panchen Lamas**. (Source-derived: Cutler's preface
notes that all Gelug stages-of-the-path literature "are derived from the Great Treatise.")

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A [[mahayana|Mahayana]] and [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] school; inherits the Indian
[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] and the Kadam graded-path tradition, distinguished by Tsongkhapa's
emphasis on disciplined study, Prāsaṅgika interpretation, and the integration of monastic ethics
with tantra.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

The Gelug shares the Tibetan canon (Kangyur and Tengyur) with the other Tibetan schools; its
distinctiveness lies not in a divergent scriptural canon but in **curricular** authority — the
treatises of Tsongkhapa and his successors (above all the [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]) function
as the authoritative framework for reading scripture.

## Hermeneutical Method

- **The graded path (lam rim).** All scripture is read as a single sequence for the
  [[three-types-of-persons|three capacities]], integrating the [[three-trainings|three trainings]] and
  the three principal aspects of the path ([[renunciation|renunciation]], [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]],
  correct view).
- **Analytical over quietist meditation.** Tsong-kha-pa insists realization comes through *analytical*
  discernment (the study → reflection → meditation sequence), polemicizing against the **Hva-shang**
  position that thoughtless absorption is the whole path — the school's defining methodological
  boundary, inherited from Kamalaśīla.
- **Sūtra–tantra integration.** The perfection-vehicle path is the "shared" (*thun mong*) foundation
  on which Vajrayāna is built; Tsong-kha-pa wrote a parallel *Great Treatise on the Stages of Mantra*.
- **Prāsaṅgika [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** as the definitive view (its detailed exposition belongs to
  the Lamrim's insight section, Vol. III, not yet ingested).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Rigorous monastic discipline (ethics as "the root" even of tantra); the study-and-debate curriculum;
the union of method and wisdom via the [[six-perfections|six perfections]]; and bodhicitta as the sole
entrance to the Mahāyāna.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Normative and dominant within Tibetan Buddhism, alongside the older **[[nyingma|Nyingma]]** (and Kagyu
and Sakya). Ruegg's introduction to the edition stresses that the *lam rim* genre is **cross-school** —
parallel graded-path works exist in the Sakya, Jonang, Nyingma, and Bonpo traditions — so the Gelug's
distinctiveness is one of emphasis, curriculum, and interpretation rather than a wholly separate canon.
