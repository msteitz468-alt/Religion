# Nyingma

> **Sect page.** The oldest of the schools of Tibetan [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] Buddhism — the
> "Ancient Ones" / "Old Translation school" — within which the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of
> the Dead]] is canonical scripture.

## Identity and Origins

The **Nyingma** ("the ancient ones") trace their lineage to the *first* wave of transmission of
Buddhism to Tibet, in the royal dynastic period of the **8th century**, when Indian masters —
above all [[padmasambhava|Padmasambhava]], with Śāntarakṣita and under King Trisong Detsen —
introduced the **three inner classes of tantra**. They are defined against the later "**New
Translation**" (*Sarma*) schools (Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug), which rest on a second, later wave of
translation.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Nyingma shares the common Mahayana and Vajrayana inheritance — the Kangyur (Buddha-word) and
Tengyur (commentaries) — but adds two distinctive bodies of scripture: the **Collected Tantras
of the Nyingmapa** (*rNying-ma'i rgyud-'bum*) and the vast **[[terma|treasure literature]]**. Its
classic doctrinal map is the **nine vehicles**, culminating in the three inner tantras —
**Mahāyoga** (generation stage), **Anuyoga** (perfection stage), and **Atiyoga** = **[[dzogchen|
the Great Perfection]]** — the last held to be the summit of all.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

The defining canon distinctive is the **[[terma|treasure (gter-ma) system]]**: teachings
concealed by Padmasambhava and recovered across the centuries by prophesied treasure-finders
(*gter-ston*). The [[bardo-thodol|kar-gling zhi-khro cycle]] — the Tibetan Book of the Dead —
is exactly such a terma, revealed by [[karma-lingpa|Karma Lingpa]]; it is **canonical for the
Nyingma** and not for any other school. The school's single most influential tantra is the
**Guhyagarbha Tantra**, the source of the symbolism of the
[[peaceful-and-wrathful-deities|forty-two peaceful and fifty-eight wrathful deities]]. Nyingma
distinguishes the **long lineage of oral precepts** (*bka'-ma*, an unbroken human transmission)
from the **close lineage of treasures** (*gter-ma*).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The nine-vehicle scheme; the primacy of [[dzogchen|Dzogchen]]; the terma revelation system; and
the [[rigpa|*sems* / *rig-pa* distinction]] — ordinary dualising mind versus pure awareness —
which underlies the school's view that all consciousness is pervaded by [[inner-radiance|inner
radiance]] "as a sesame seed is permeated by oil."

## Hermeneutical Method

Nyingma reads its highest scriptures through the two Dzogchen techniques: **khregs-chod**
("Cutting through Resistance," recognition of primordial purity → the dharmakāya) and
**thod-rgal** ("All-surpassing Realisation," eliciting the radiances of pristine cognition → the
buddha-body of form, the "rainbow body"). In the Tibetan Book of the Dead, Chapter 4 expresses
khregs-chod and Chapter 11's deity-visions express thod-rgal. The school enumerates **six forms
of lineage** transmission, a schema distinctive to it.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Within Tibetan Buddhism the Nyingma are a normative, fully recognised school; debate with the
Sarma schools historically centred on the authenticity of the *terma* revelations and of certain
Old-Translation tantras. The Great Perfection was integrated philosophically with the Guhyagarbha
by **Longchen Rabjampa** (1308–63), whose **[[nyingthig|Nyingthig]]** ("Heart-Essence") synthesis
is the school's contemplative core.
