# Yogācāra

**Yogācāra** ("the practice of yoga/meditation"), also called **Cittamātra** ("Mind-Only")
and **Vijñānavāda**, is — with **[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** — one of the two great
philosophical schools of Indian **[[mahayana|Mahayana]]** [[buddhism|Buddhism]], founded in
the 4th century by the half-brothers **Asaṅga** and **Vasubandhu**. [[paul-williams|Williams]]
calls it "probably the most popular and influential of philosophical schools in ancient
India associated with Mahāyāna."

## Identity and Origins

Yogācāra grows from meditative experience (hence the name) and from the
**Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra**, systematized in Asaṅga's and Vasubandhu's treatises (the
*Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, the *Triṃśikā*, the *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra*). Its root claim is that
what we take to be an external world of objects confronting subjects is a construction of
**consciousness** (*vijñāna*); the one thing that cannot be denied is the flow of cognition
itself.

## Relationship to Madhyamaka (the Core Disagreement)

Yogācāra arose in part as a **corrective to [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]**.
[[paul-williams|Williams]] frames it sharply: many Buddhists felt that taking
[[sunyata|emptiness]] to mean *literally everything* is a conceptual construct entails
**nihilism** — "it cannot be that literally everything is a conceptual construct, since
there would then be nothing left for things to be constructed out of." Yogācāra's answer is
the **three natures**, with a real (if non-dual) substratum so that emptiness does not
collapse into "there is nothing." Madhyamaka, in turn, regards Yogācāra's "Mind" as a
covert reification — a residual [[svabhava|svabhāva]]. The two schools' debate over whether
consciousness is *ultimately* real is the central inner-Mahayana philosophical dispute
(recorded, not adjudicated — Contradiction Protocol).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

- **The three natures (*trisvabhāva*)**:
  1. the **constructed/imagined nature** (*parikalpita*) — the false world of separate
     subjects and objects, the subject–object duality;
  2. the **dependent nature** (*paratantra*) — the real flow of cognitive experiences, the
     substratum that gets wrongly polarized;
  3. the **perfected nature** (*pariniṣpanna*) — things as they truly are, *the very absence
     of the constructed nature in the dependent nature*. This is **emptiness** — but
     redefined: the substratum is empty **of subject–object duality**, not empty of
     existence.
- **The store-consciousness (*ālaya-vijñāna*)** — a substratum consciousness carrying the
  "seeds" (*bīja*) of past [[karma|karma]] that ripen as experience, Yogācāra's account of
  continuity and rebirth without a [[anatta|self]].

## Hermeneutical Method (mandatory)

The **three natures** are themselves the interpretive key, and the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra*
supplies a famous **[[neyartha-nitartha|neyārtha/nītārtha]]** scheme of *three turnings of
the wheel of Dharma* — ranking the early teaching, the Prajñāpāramitā emptiness, and the
Yogācāra "elucidation of the ultimate" — used to position Yogācāra as the definitive
teaching. Verification is ultimately **yogic**: the perfected nature "has to be seen in
meditation."

## Historical Development and Influence

From Asaṅga and Vasubandhu it became, with Madhyamaka, the backbone of Indian Mahayana
scholasticism; later Indian and Tibetan thinkers produced **Yogācāra–Madhyamaka syntheses**.
In East Asia it shaped the Chinese **Faxiang** school (via Xuanzang) and fed the
[[buddha-nature|Buddha-nature]] and [[zen|Chan/Zen]] streams' emphasis on mind.
