# Kadam

> **Sect page.** The reform lineage of [[tibetan-buddhism|Tibetan Buddhism]] founded by the disciples
> of **[[atisha|Atiśa]]** (982–1054) — above all his chief Tibetan disciple **Drom-tön-pa
> (Gyel-way-jung-nay)** — out of which the [[gelug|Gelug]] ("New Kadam") later grew.

## Identity and Origins

The Kadam ("bound by the [Buddha's] word") arose in the eleventh century from Atiśa's mission to
Tibet, centered on his *Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment* and transmitted through Drom-tön-pa and
his successors (dGon-pa-ba, Po-to-ba, and the masters Tsong-kha-pa repeatedly quotes — **Bo-do-wa,
Sha-ra-wa, Pu-chung-wa, Kam-lung-ba, Neu-sur-ba, Lang-ri-tang-ba**). It is characterized by the
**[[three-types-of-persons|graded path]]**, by rigorous monastic discipline, and by the **mind-
training** (*blo sbyong*) literature rooted in the [[exchange-of-self-and-other|exchange of self and
other]].

## Relationship to the Gelug

[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** — composed at the old Kadam center
of Rva-sgreng (Reting) — is the great systematization of the Kadam graded path; his school absorbed
and continued the Kadam as the "New Kadam" (Gelug). The Kadam oral teachings function in the Lamrim as
practitioner-testimonies corroborating the Indian textual sources.
