# Buddhism

Top-level tradition page and the wiki's **first non-theistic tradition** — there is no creator
God; ultimate concern is liberation (*nirvana*) from *dukkha* (suffering/unsatisfactoriness)
through insight into the nature of reality. Founded on the teaching (*Dharma*) of **Siddhartha
Gautama, the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]]** ("the awakened one," c. 5th c. BCE, North India). This is
the parent node for the Buddhist sects under `traditions/buddhism/sects/`.

## A Tradition with No Single Canon

Buddhism is the wiki's first test of the **multi-canon, non-ancestral** model (see CLAUDE.md). There
is no one scripture shared by all Buddhists:

- **[[tipitaka|The Pali Canon (Tipitaka)]]** — the canon of [[theravada|Theravada]]; the closest to
  the early teaching. Its *Sutta* basket includes the [[dhammapada|Dhammapada]].
- **The Mahayana sutras** — a vast later corpus ([[heart-sutra|Heart Sutra]], [[lotus-sutra|Lotus
  Sutra]], the Prajnaparamita, etc.) accepted as the Buddha's word by [[mahayana|Mahayana]] and
  [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] but **not** by Theravada.
- **The tantras** — added by [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]].

The boundary is itself doctrine: whether the Mahayana sutras are *buddhavacana* (the Buddha's word)
is the defining canon dispute, captured structurally in each text's `canon_scope`.

## Core Doctrine (in place of theology)

The **[[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]]** (suffering, its origin in craving, its cessation, and
the **[[noble-eightfold-path|Eightfold Path]]**) and the **three marks of existence** — impermanence
(*anicca*), suffering (*dukkha*), and **[[anatta|non-self]]** (*anatta*) — are the shared doctrinal
core. **[[dependent-origination|Dependent origination]]** (*pratityasamutpada*) explains the arising
of suffering; **[[karma|kamma]]** powers the round of **[[samsara|saṃsāra]]**, from which
**[[nirvana|nirvana]]** is release; **[[anatta|non-self]]** denies a permanent soul (the sharpest
contrast with the Abrahamic and Hindu *atman* traditions). Mahayana adds **[[sunyata|emptiness]]**
(*sunyata*) and the **[[bodhisattva|bodhisattva]]** ideal.

## Native Hermeneutical Frameworks

The key interpretive distinction is **[[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha vs. nitartha]]** — teachings whose
meaning is *provisional/interpretable* vs. *definitive* — used by each school to rank the others'
scriptures. With it run **upaya** (skillful means: the Buddha taught different things to different
hearers); the **[[two-truths|two truths]]** (conventional vs. ultimate), the indispensable key to
[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] [[sunyata|emptiness]]; and dense **commentarial lineages** (Buddhaghosa for
[[theravada|Theravada]]; [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]/Asanga for [[mahayana|Mahayana]]).

## Map of Major Sects

- [[theravada|Theravada]] — "the Way of the Elders"; the Pali Canon; the *arhat* ideal; South/SE Asia.
- [[mahayana|Mahayana]] — "the Great Vehicle"; the bodhisattva ideal, emptiness, the Mahayana sutras;
  East Asia (incl. **[[zen|Zen/Chan]]**, Pure Land). Its great philosophical schools are
  **[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** ([[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]; emptiness, the [[two-truths|two truths]])
  and **[[yogacara|Yogācāra]]** ("mind-only"; the three natures), with the
  [[buddha-nature|Buddha-nature]] literature alongside; its modern cross-sectarian offshoot is
  **Engaged Buddhism**
  ([[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]], [[mindfulness|mindfulness]], [[interbeing|interbeing]]).
- [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] — "the Diamond Vehicle"; tantra, mantra, and guru-yoga atop Mahayana; Tibet.
