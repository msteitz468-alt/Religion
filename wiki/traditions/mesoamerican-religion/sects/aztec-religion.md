# Aztec Religion

> **Sect page.** The religion of the **Aztec (Mexica) Empire** — famous (and infamous) for its
> sacrificial cult of the sun. *(Stub; primary sources are the codices and Sahagún.)*

## The Cosmic Debt of Blood

Aztec religion took the shared Mesoamerican idea of **feeding the gods** to its most intense form. The
cosmos exists only because the gods **sacrificed themselves** to set the sun in motion (see the
[[five-suns|Five Suns]]); to keep the sun moving and forestall the world's destruction, humans owe the gods a
**continual repayment in blood** — autosacrifice and, on a large scale, **human sacrifice** (war captives
offered atop the great pyramids). This was not gratuitous cruelty but a **sacred cosmic obligation**: the
universe is fragile, and only blood keeps it alive. War itself (the "Flower Wars") was partly a means of
**capturing victims** for the sun.

## The Gods

The patron of the Mexica was the **war-and-sun god [[huitzilopochtli|Huitzilopochtli]]**, who demanded the
nourishment of sacrifice; the supreme, capricious **fate-god [[tezcatlipoca|Tezcatlipoca]]** ("Smoking
Mirror"), lord of destiny, night, and sorcery; the rain-god **[[tlaloc|Tlaloc]]** (with his own grim child-
sacrifices for rain); the **[[quetzalcoatl|Feathered Serpent Quetzalcoatl]]**, god of wind, wisdom, and the
priesthood; and the flayed god of renewal **[[xipe-totec|Xipe Totec]]**.
