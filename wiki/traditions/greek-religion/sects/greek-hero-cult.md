# Greek Hero Cult

> **Sect page.** The worship of deified mortals (heroes) at their tombs — a category between gods and men.
> *(Stub.)*

## Identity

A pervasive layer of Greek religion: the cult of **heroes** — extraordinary mortals (often the figures of
[[homer|epic]]) who, after death, retained power and were worshipped at their **tombs** with **chthonic
(underworld)** rites distinct from the Olympian sacrifices to the sky-gods. Heroes were **local and
protective**, guarding their city or grave.

## Range

- **Epic heroes** (Achilles, Theseus, Oedipus) honored at their reputed graves.
- **Deified heroes who became gods** — **[[heracles|Heracles]]** (the mortal who won full divinity) and
  **[[asclepius|Asclepius]]** (the healing hero-god, whose sanctuaries practiced **incubation** — sleeping
  in the temple for a healing dream).
- The recently dead and founders (city-founders, athletes) could receive hero honors.

## Significance

Hero cult shows the **permeable boundary between mortal and divine** in Greek religion — a structure that
fed both the [[imperial-cult|Roman deification of emperors]] and, comparatively, the veneration of saints
and the category of the "holy dead." Recorded without adjudication.
