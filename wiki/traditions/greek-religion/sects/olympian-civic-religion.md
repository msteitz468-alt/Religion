# Olympian / Civic Religion

> **Sect page.** The mainstream public worship of the [[greek-religion|Greek]] city-states. *(Stub.)*

## Identity

The ordinary, public face of Greek religion: the worship of the **[[zeus|Twelve Olympians]]** through
**civic festivals, temples, processions, and animal [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]**, organized by and for the
**polis** (city-state). Each city had its patron god ([[athena|Athena]] for Athens), its calendar of
festivals, and its sacred precincts.

## Practice and Theology

Religion here is **communal and reciprocal** (*do ut des*, "I give that you may give"): correct honors
secure the gods' favor for the city's prosperity and safety. There is little concept of personal salvation
or a happy afterlife — that hope belonged to the [[eleusinian-mysteries|mysteries]]. Authority is
inherited custom, supplemented by **oracles** (Delphi) and seers; there is no scripture, clergy-caste, or
creed (priesthoods were civic offices).

## Relationship to the Other Cults

The baseline against which the **mystery cults** ([[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusis]],
[[dionysian-mysteries|Dionysus]], [[orphism|Orphism]]) and [[greek-hero-cult|hero cult]] offered
*additional* and more personal religious goods. Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

The civic-religious model — festival, sacrifice, patron deity — shaped the [[roman-religion|Roman]] state
cult and the entire ancient Mediterranean pattern of public religion.
