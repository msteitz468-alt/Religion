# Ancient Greek Religion

Top-level tradition page. **Ancient Greek religion** is the polytheistic worship of the Greek pantheon —
the **Olympian gods** and a vast company of Titans, primordial powers, nature deities, daimones, and
heroes — practiced across the Greek world from the Mycenaean age until its suppression under Christian
Rome (and revived today as **Hellenism**). It is the wiki's first **polytheistic tradition without a
scriptural canon**: there was no creed, no church, and no holy book — religion was a matter of **ritual
practice, civic cult, myth, and reciprocity with the gods** ("I give that you may give").

## A Religion Without Scripture

Greek religion has **no canon** — which makes its `canon_scope` distinctive (mostly empty). Authority lay
in **inherited practice** and in poets and mythographers whose works were *authoritative but not
scriptural*:

- **[[hesiod-theogony|Hesiod's *Theogony*]]** — the foundational genealogy of the gods (the closest thing
  to a systematic theology).
- **[[homer|Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*]]** — the gods as living characters; the shared cultural
  "Bible" of the Greeks without canonical authority.
- The **[[homeric-hymns|Homeric Hymns]]** and **[[orphic-hymns|Orphic Hymns]]** — hymns to individual gods.
- **[[apollodorus-library|Apollodorus' *Library*]]** — the standard handbook of myth.

## The Pantheon

Greek theology is **genealogical** (Hesiod): from primordial **[[chaos|Chaos]]** come **[[gaia|Gaia]]**
(Earth), [[uranus|Uranus]] (Sky), [[nyx|Night]], and others; their children the **[[cronus|Titans]]**;
and the Titans' children the **[[zeus|Olympians]]** — twelve great gods reigning from Mount Olympus after
Zeus overthrows Cronus. Below and beside them: chthonic (underworld) gods, nature deities, personified
abstractions (Fate, Victory, Strife), and deified heroes. See the [[interpretatio-romana|Greek–Roman
equivalence comparison]] for the mapping onto the [[roman-religion|Roman]] gods.

## Cult and Practice

- **Sacrifice** ([[sacrifice|thysia]]) — the central act; animal offerings shared between gods and worshippers.
- **Temples, festivals, and games** (the Panathenaia, the Olympic Games as religious festival).
- **Oracles** — Delphi (Apollo), Dodona (Zeus).
- **Mystery cults** offering personal salvation/afterlife hope (Eleusis, Orphism, Dionysus) — see sects.
- **Hero cult** — the worship of deified mortals at their tombs.

## Major Sects / Cult Forms

- [[olympian-civic-religion|Olympian / Civic Religion]] — the public worship of the polis.
- [[eleusinian-mysteries|The Eleusinian Mysteries]] — the secret cult of Demeter and Persephone; afterlife hope.
- [[dionysian-mysteries|The Dionysian (Bacchic) Mysteries]] — ecstatic cult of Dionysus.
- [[orphism|Orphism]] — the reforming theological movement of Orpheus; the soul, reincarnation, original sin.
- [[greek-hero-cult|Hero Cult]] — the worship of heroes (Heracles, Asclepius, the dead) at their tombs.

## Reception and Influence

Allegorized by the Stoics and Neoplatonists; preserved through literature and art; the polemical foil of
early Christianity (the "pagan gods" as demons or dead men, per [[justin-martyr|Justin]] and the
apologists); the foundation of Western mythology and the [[roman-religion|Roman]] pantheon via
[[interpretatio-romana|interpretatio romana]].
