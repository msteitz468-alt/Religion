## Identity and Origins

The Yapanīya is a south Indian Jain sub-tradition whose precise origins, doctrines, and history are not detailed in this slice of Dundas's account. Two references occur:

1. Devasena's early-tenth-century critique (DS) — which defends the Mūla Saṅgha as the only legitimate Jain sect — "describes a rogues' gallery of sectarian miscreants … including … the Yapanīyas, whose behaviour marked them out, for him, as being beyond the pale." The Yapanīyas appear here alongside the Śvetāmbaras and the Buddha as targets of Mūla Saṅgha polemic.

2. The early writer Śivārya is identified as "early Digambara (or possibly Yapanīya)" — suggesting that the Yapanīya occupied an intermediate or ambiguous position relative to the [[digambara|Digambara]] mainstream.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The Yapanīya is treated in Dundas's account as a sect distinct from both [[digambara|Digambara]] and [[svetambara|Śvetāmbara]] Jainism, though associated closely enough with the Digambara milieu to be discussed alongside Digambara sectarian rivals. The Mūla Saṅgha regarded it as deviant, grouping it with the Śvetāmbaras and the Buddhist apostate as paradigm cases of heterodoxy.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Per Devasena (DS, c. early tenth century, Mūla Saṅgha perspective): classified as heterodox, grouped with the Śvetāmbaras and the Buddha-as-apostate as examples of false religion. This is a polemical Mūla Saṅgha judgment, not a neutral characterisation.

## Historical Development

The Yapanīya appears to have been active in south India during the early medieval period. No further detail on its historical development, subdivisions, or decline is provided in this source slice.
