# Atenism

> **Sect page.** The short-lived **near-monotheistic revolution** of Pharaoh Akhenaten — the most radical
> episode in Egyptian religious history. *(Stub.)*

## Akhenaten's Revolution

Around 1353 BCE, **Pharaoh Amenhotep IV** renamed himself **Akhenaten** ("effective for the Aten"),
proclaimed the **[[aten|Aten]]** — the visible **sun-disk** — the **sole god**, and set out to **abolish the
worship of all other gods**, above all the powerful [[theban-amun-cult|Amun]]. He closed temples, erased the
names of other gods (especially Amun) from monuments, moved the capital to a new city, **Akhetaten**
(Amarna), and made the king and his queen Nefertiti the **only mediators** between humanity and the one god.
Whether this was true monotheism or a "monolatry" centered on the royal family is debated.

## A God of Light, Without Myth

Atenism stripped away the rich mythology, the [[osiris|Osiris]] afterlife, and the animal-headed gods,
focusing instead on the **Aten's life-giving light** as known through nature — celebrated in the magnificent
**Great Hymn to the Aten**, whose imagery strikingly parallels [[psalms|Psalm 104]] (a famous, debated
comparandum). It had little popular roots.

## Suppression

Within a generation of Akhenaten's death the old gods were restored (under Tutankhamun), Amarna was
abandoned, and Akhenaten was branded the "heretic king" and erased from the records. Atenism is the wiki's
clearest case of a **monotheistic revolution within a polytheistic tradition** — and its swift, total
reversal. Recorded without adjudication.
