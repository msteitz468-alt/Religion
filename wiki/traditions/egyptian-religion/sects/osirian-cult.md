# The Osirian / Afterlife Cult

> **Sect page.** The afterlife religion of **[[osiris|Osiris]]** — the most personally meaningful strand of
> Egyptian religion, offering every Egyptian the hope of resurrection. *(Stub.)*

## Osiris and the Hope of Resurrection

The [[osiris|Osiris myth]] — the god murdered by [[set|Set]], restored by [[isis|Isis]], and made **king of
the dead** — became the foundation of Egyptian **mortuary religion**. Because Osiris rose from death, the
dead could too: through mummification (modeled on Osiris's own embalming by [[anubis|Anubis]]) and the proper
rites, **each deceased person became "an Osiris,"** sharing in his resurrection. Originally a royal privilege
(the [[pyramid-texts|Pyramid Texts]]), the hope was **democratized** over time until, by the New Kingdom, the
[[book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]] could secure eternal life for anyone who could afford a copy.

## The Judgment

The climactic moment is the **[[maat|weighing of the heart]]**: in the Hall of Two Truths, the dead person's
heart is weighed on the scales against the feather of **[[maat|Ma'at]]** (truth), [[anubis|Anubis]] adjusting
the balance and [[thoth|Thoth]] recording the verdict. A heart light with righteousness passes to the blessed
fields; a heavy, sin-laden heart is devoured by the monster **Ammit**, and the soul is annihilated. This
**ethical judgment of the dead** is one of the earliest in religious history.
