# Memphite Theology (Ptah)

> **Sect page.** The creation theology of **Memphis**, centered on the craftsman-god **[[ptah|Ptah]]**.
> *(Stub.)*

## Creation by Word and Thought

The Memphite Theology — most fully preserved on the **Shabaka Stone** (c. 700 BCE, claiming a far older
source) — presents one of the most **abstract and philosophical** cosmogonies of the ancient world.
**[[ptah|Ptah]]**, the patron god of Memphis, creates not by physical act but by **the heart (*ib*, mind/
thought) and the tongue (*hu*, the spoken word)**: Ptah **conceives** the world in his heart and **speaks** it
into being, and the other gods (including [[ra|Atum]] and the Ennead) are forms of his creative thought.

## Significance

This "**logos**" cosmogony — **creation by conceiving and speaking** — is a striking ancient parallel to the
"God said, let there be" of [[genesis|Genesis 1]] and the [[logos-christology|Johannine Logos]] (recorded as
a comparandum without asserting dependence). It makes Ptah the supreme **craftsman-creator**, patron of
artisans and architects (the Greeks identified him with Hephaestus).
