# Hermopolitan Theology (the Ogdoad)

> **Sect page.** The creation theology of **Hermopolis** (Egyptian *Khmun*, "Eight-Town"). *(Stub.)*

## The Ogdoad

Here creation begins not with a single god but with the **Ogdoad** — **eight primeval deities in four
male-female pairs**, personifying the qualities of the pre-creation chaos: **Nun and Naunet** (the primeval
waters), **Heh and Hauhet** (infinity), **Kek and Kauket** (darkness), and **Amun and Amaunet**
(hiddenness/air). From their interaction the primeval mound (or a **cosmic egg**, or a lotus) emerges, and
from it the sun-god rises to begin the world. **[[thoth|Thoth]]**, the ibis-headed god of wisdom, writing,
and the moon, is the patron of Hermopolis and the divine intellect ordering creation.

## Significance

The Hermopolitan system frames creation as the **emergence of order from a structured chaos**, and elevated
**[[thoth|Thoth]]** — scribe of the gods, inventor of writing, reckoner of time, and arbiter of disputes —
who in Greco-Roman times was identified with **Hermes Trismegistus** and the Hermetic tradition.
