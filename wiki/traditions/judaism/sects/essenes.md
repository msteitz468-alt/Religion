# Essenes

> **Sect page.** The separatist Second Temple sect commonly identified with the Qumran
> community and the Dead Sea Scrolls. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

A pious separatist movement of Second Temple Judaism (2nd c. BCE–1st c. CE), described by Josephus,
Philo, and Pliny, and very widely (if not unanimously) identified with the community behind the
**Dead Sea Scrolls** at Qumran. Apparently formed when priestly purists withdrew from a Jerusalem
Temple they regarded as defiled, following a founder figure called the **Teacher of Righteousness**.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A withdrawal sect: it judged the rest of Israel — Pharisees and the Sadducean Temple establishment
alike — as following a false calendar and corrupt halacha, and constituted itself as the true
remnant of the covenant (the *yahad*) awaiting imminent eschatological vindication.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

The Qumran library shows the broadest scriptural horizon of any Second Temple group: the Hebrew
scriptures (in plural text-forms) **plus** books the rabbinic canon later excluded — notably
**Jubilees** and **1 Enoch** — treated as authoritative, alongside the community's own sectarian
compositions (Community Rule, War Scroll, Hodayot, pesharim). This expansive, not-yet-closed canon
is the counter-case to the Sadducean minimal canon and a key data point for
[[nt-canon-formation|canon formation]] generally. (Cf. [[jude-1-enoch-canon|Jude's]] use of 1 Enoch.)

## Doctrinal Distinctives

A **solar 364-day calendar** (the root of their breach with the Temple); strict ritual purity and
communal ownership; a sharp **dualism** of the Sons of Light vs. the Sons of Darkness;
predestination; expectation of **two messiahs** (priestly and royal) and a final holy war.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Pesher** exegesis — reading prophetic texts as encoded prophecies of the sect's own recent history
and imminent end — together with a distinctive, stringent sectarian halacha (cf. 4QMMT).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Defined itself against both Pharisees ("seekers of smooth things") and the Sadducean priesthood;
its calendar and purity rulings made coexistence with the Jerusalem cult impossible. Often compared
with early Christianity (John the Baptist, community of goods, eschatology), though direct dependence
is unproven. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

The Qumran settlement was destroyed in the First Jewish Revolt (68 CE) and the sect did not survive
the war. Its library, rediscovered 1947, transformed the study of Second Temple Judaism, the
biblical text, and the Jewish matrix of Christianity.
