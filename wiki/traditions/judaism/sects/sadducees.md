# Sadducees

> **Sect page.** One of the major Second Temple Jewish parties, defined against the
> [[pharisees|Pharisees]] (a group page) by its rejection of the Oral Torah. *(Stub — to be
> expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The priestly-aristocratic party of Second Temple Judaism (2nd c. BCE–70 CE), associated with the
Jerusalem Temple establishment and the high-priestly families. The name is usually linked to
**Zadok**, Solomon's high priest. Known mainly through hostile or external sources (Josephus, the
New Testament, later rabbinic literature) — none of their own writings survive.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A defining rival within Second Temple Judaism. Where the [[pharisees|Pharisees]] championed the
[[oral-torah|Oral Torah]], the Sadducees recognized only the Written Torah as binding — making
them the structural forerunner of the later [[karaism|Karaite]] position (both reject the rabbinic
oral tradition).

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Accepted the **Written Torah** as authoritative and **rejected the Oral Torah** and the developing
body of Pharisaic-rabbinic tradition (some ancient reports suggest they privileged the Pentateuch
specifically). On the Oral-Torah text pages ([[mishna]], [[talmud-bavli]], the seders), this sect
appears under `rejected_by` alongside [[karaism]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Per Josephus and the NT: denial of the resurrection of the dead (Mark 12; Acts 23), of angels and
spirits, and of fate — emphasizing human free will; a literal, this-worldly reading of reward; and
the centrality of Temple cult and priestly prerogative.

## Hermeneutical Method

Strict literal reading of the Written Torah without the Pharisaic apparatus of oral interpretation,
*midrash*, or the [[thirteen-middot|Thirteen Middot]].

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Bitter rivals of the Pharisees (disputes over purity, calendar, and law); polemicized against in
the Gospels and Acts and in later rabbinic memory. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction
Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Tied institutionally to the Temple, the Sadducees did not survive its destruction in 70 CE; with no
sanctuary and no oral tradition to carry forward, the party vanished, leaving the Pharisaic stream
to become [[rabbinic-judaism|Rabbinic Judaism]]. Their anti-oral-tradition stance reappears
independently in [[karaism|Karaism]] centuries later.
