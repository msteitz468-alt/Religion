# Essenism (the Essene sect)

The **Essenes** (*Essenoi* / *Essaioi* in Greek) are a separatist Jewish sect of the Second Temple period (c. 150 BCE – 68 CE), described in ancient sources by Josephus (*War* II.119-61; *Antiquities* XVIII.18-22), Philo (*Every Good Man is Free* 75-91; *Hypothetica* 11.1-18), and Pliny the Elder (*Natural History* V.73). Since 1947 they have been very widely — though not unanimously — identified with the community that produced and preserved the **Dead Sea Scrolls** at Khirbet Qumran. Vermes makes this identification confidently; the primary source evidence from the scrolls themselves aligns closely with the classical descriptions.

## Identity and Origins

### The Name
The etymology of "Essene" (*Essenoi/Essaioi*) is uncertain. Proposed derivations include:
- From the Aramaic *ḥasayya* ("the pious ones," *hasidim*) — connecting them to the Maccabaean-era *hasidim* movement
- From the Hebrew *'osim* ("doers [of the Law]")
- From *'asayya* ("healers") — Philo associates them with healing

No consensus exists. Vermes does not adjudicate.

### Origins and the Teacher of Righteousness
The Damascus Document (CD I) describes a community that "groped like blind men" for approximately **twenty years** before God raised up the **Teacher of Righteousness** to guide them. Vermes dates this:
- **c. 170–150 BCE**: Proto-community formation (possibly in the wake of Hellenization crisis and the Maccabaean revolt)
- **c. 150 BCE**: Teacher of Righteousness arrives; community solidifies
- **c. 150–125 BCE**: Jonathan Maccabaeus (Wicked Priest) is the primary antagonist
- **c. 100 BCE**: Community settled at Qumran (archaeological evidence; Magness dates first buildings to c. 100 BCE)

The fundamental origin dispute: the Hasmonaeans usurped the **Zadokite high priesthood** (legitimate priestly line going back to Zadok of Solomon's era) by assuming the high priesthood as well as the kingship. The community's Zadokite priestly identity explains its fury at the Wicked Priest and its insistence on a legitimate priestly Messiah.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The Essenes were a **withdrawal sect** — they did not simply dispute with other groups but separated from them:
- From the **Jerusalem Temple establishment** (Sadducean/Hasmonaean priests): illegitimate high priests, wrong calendar, corrupt sacrifices
- From the **Pharisees** ("seekers of smooth things" / *dorshey ha-ḥalaqot*): too lenient in halacha, following a false calendar
- From all of "Israel" conceived as the "lot of Belial" — the broader population is assigned to the Angel of Darkness

The community constituted itself as the **"true Israel"**: the "men of the New Covenant in the land of Damascus" who alone keep the true Torah as interpreted by the Teacher.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

The Qumran library (preserved in 11 caves; c. 900 scrolls total) represents the most extensive Second Temple Jewish library known. Its canon picture:

**Shared with other Jewish groups**: All books of the later Hebrew Bible are attested at Qumran (except Esther). Isaiah, Psalms, Deuteronomy are the most widely attested.

**Authoritative but non-canonical (from Vermes)**:
- **Jubilees** (4Q216-28): The Damascus Document cites Jubilees as "Scripture" (*sefer*). Jubilees' solar calendar and angelic-historical framework are foundational for Qumran theology.
- **1 Enoch** (4Q201-12): Aramaic fragments in multiple copies; the Watchers narrative and angelic cosmology underlie Qumran Two Spirits doctrine
- **Book of Giants** (4Q530-3): Extended the Enochic tradition; found in multiple copies

**Sectarian compositions** (unique to Qumran, treated as authoritative within the community):
- [[community-rule|Community Rule (1QS)]]: constitutional document
- [[damascus-document|Damascus Document (CD)]]: for camp communities
- [[war-scroll|War Scroll (1QM)]]: eschatological battle manual
- [[thanksgiving-hymns|Thanksgiving Hymns (1QH)]]: liturgical psalms
- [[temple-scroll|Temple Scroll (11QT)]]: rewritten Torah for the ideal Temple
- [[mmt|MMT (4Q394-9)]]: halakhic letter to Jerusalem
- [[angelic-liturgy|Angelic Liturgy (4Q400-407)]]: heavenly liturgy

**Non-canonical status** for all the above in rabbinic Judaism, Sadducaean Judaism, and Christianity.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

### Solar and Lunar Calendars (Coordinated System)
The foundational cause of the Essenes' separation from the Temple: a **364-day solar year** (52 weeks exactly), in which every festival falls on the same day of the week every year. Passover always falls on Wednesday; Shavuot always on Sunday. The Jerusalem Temple used a lunisolar calendar, which the Essenes regarded as a corruption of the divinely ordained calendar (supported by Jubilees and the Astronomical Enoch). Offering sacrifices on the wrong day (by the wrong calendar) makes them invalid; the Temple priests are therefore performing illegitimate worship.

VanderKam (2010) corrects simplified accounts: the community used **both** the 364-day solar calendar and a **schematic (non-observational) lunar calendar** running in parallel. The **mishmarot** (priestly rotation) texts coordinate solar years, lunar months, and priestly courses into a grand 6-year cycle. The lunar calendar was real but was not the observation-based lunisolar calendar of Jerusalem — it was a theoretical system synchronized with the solar year. See [[dead-sea-scrolls-vanderkam]].

### Two Spirits Doctrine and Predestination
God created two spirits — Truth and Falsehood — and assigned every human being to one or the other before birth. Sons of Light are ruled by the Prince of Light; Sons of Darkness by the Angel of Darkness. This predestinarian dualism (fully developed in 1QS III–IV) is the Essenes' most distinctive theological contribution to Second Temple Judaism. See [[two-spirits-doctrine]].

### Communal Property and Organization
Members of the settled community surrendered property to the common fund upon full admission. Entry involved a three-year probationary period. The community was led by the **Many** (full assembly), the **Overseer** (*Mebaqqer*), and a Council of twelve men and three priests. Annual examination ranked every member "according to his understanding and his deeds."

The Damascus Document's **camp communities** allowed marriage and family; the Qumran settlement itself apparently practiced celibacy (at least in its main phase). VanderKam (2010) is careful to note that Josephus explicitly distinguishes **two orders of Essenes** — one celibate, one marrying — and that this is not a contradiction in the sources but represents genuine institutional diversity within the movement. The Qumran settlement exemplifies the celibate order; the Damascus Document's camp communities represent the marrying order. Both are properly "Essene."

### Two Messiahs
The community expected two distinct end-time figures: the **Messiah of Aaron** (priestly, takes precedence) and the **Messiah of Israel** (Davidic/royal), together with a **Prophet** in the mode of Moses (Deut. 18:15-18). The priestly Messiah's precedence over the royal at the messianic banquet (1QSa) reflects the community's Zadokite priestly identity. See [[two-messiahs]].

### Sanctuary of Men
The community itself functions as the eschatological Temple. With the Jerusalem Temple defiled, the community's obedience to Torah ("works of the Law") substitutes for Temple sacrifice. "A Sanctuary of Men" (*miqdash 'adam*, 4Q174) is the community where "works of the Law shall be offered as a sweet fragrance." See [[sanctuary-of-men]].

### Imminent Eschatology
The community understood itself as living in the final era of history — the "age of Belial's dominion" — which would shortly be terminated by the divine visitation and the eschatological war. The forty-year war of Sons of Light vs. Sons of Darkness (War Scroll) was expected to begin in the community's own near future.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Pesher** (*pēsher*): All prophetic texts are read as coded predictions of the community's own recent history and imminent eschatological situation. God revealed the full meaning of prophecy — hidden from the prophets themselves — to the Teacher of Righteousness. The community's pesharim (Habakkuk, Nahum, Isaiah, Psalms, Hosea commentaries) apply biblical prophecy verse by verse to current events. See [[pesher]].

**Sectarian halacha**: MMT demonstrates that the community's legal rulings were systematic and distinctive — stricter than Pharisaic law, often aligning with Sadducaean positions, but grounded in the community's own solar-calendar-based interpretation of Torah.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

**vs. Pharisees**: The Pesharim call the Pharisees "seekers of smooth things" (*dorshey ha-ḥalaqot*) — a pun on *halakha* — accusing them of leniency and flattery. The community regarded Pharisaic halacha as accommodation to the masses rather than rigorous Torah observance.

**vs. Sadducees**: The Sadducaean high priesthood is the "Wicked Priest" — the primary opponent. However, MMT's legal positions align with several Sadducaean rulings, suggesting shared priestly-legal traditions despite the violent antagonism.

**vs. Early Christianity**: Multiple structural parallels — communal property (Acts 2-5), baptism (Essene ritual bathing), eschatological urgency, dual-messiah expectation, sanctuary-of-men ecclesiology, Two Spirits dualism, pesher exegesis. Whether these parallels reflect direct influence, shared sources, or parallel development is debated. Vermes makes no claims of direct dependence in the ingested text.

## Historical Development and Decline

- **c. 150 BCE**: Origins under or before Teacher of Righteousness
- **c. 100 BCE**: Qumran settlement established (de Vaux's Phase I; Magness concurs)
- **31 BCE**: Earthquake; brief interruption (Jodi Magness's revision: gap between Phase I and Phase II was likely months, not de Vaux's proposed 27 years; no Phase Ia is archaeologically identifiable)
- **c. 4 BCE–68 CE**: Herodian/Early Roman period (Phase II); community most active
- **68 CE**: Roman legions under Vespasian advance; scrolls hidden in caves; settlement destroyed

**Magness revision**: De Vaux's phasing posited an extended abandonment (31–4 BCE) after the earthquake; Magness found no archaeological reason for a 27-year gap. The earthquake debris was cleared and the site rebuilt promptly. See [[dead-sea-scrolls-vanderkam]].

The Essenes did not survive the First Jewish Revolt. Their legacy persists through the Dead Sea Scrolls — the most important manuscript discovery of the 20th century — which transformed understanding of Second Temple Judaism, the Hebrew Bible text (multiple text families), and the Jewish matrix of Christianity.
