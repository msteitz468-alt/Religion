# Karaism

> **Sect page.** The scripturalist sect within [[judaism|Judaism]], defined by its rejection
> of the rabbinic Oral Torah. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

A Jewish movement arising in 8th-century Mesopotamia/Persia that recognizes the **Tanakh alone**
as binding scripture and rejects the authority of the rabbinic [[oral-torah|Oral Torah]].
Traditionally traced to **Anan ben David** ("search the Scripture well, and do not rely on my
opinion"); the name *Qaraʾim* derives from *qara*, "to read/recite Scripture." Flourished
intellectually in the 9th–11th centuries (the "Golden Age" of Karaism).

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The principal internal challenger to [[rabbinic-judaism|Rabbinic Judaism]]. Shares the written
Tanakh and the covenant but denies that the Mishna and Talmud carry divine authority — making it
the structural counter-case to the Rabbanite settlement.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Accepts the **Tanakh** as the sole canon and **rejects the [[mishna|Mishna]] and
[[talmud-bavli|Talmud]]** as human, non-binding tradition. This is the sharpest canon split
inside Judaism: the Oral Torah corpus is `canonical_for: [rabbinic-judaism]` but `rejected_by:
[karaism]`. See the [[oral-torah-authority|Oral Torah authority controversy]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Scripture alone as the source of law; rejection of rabbinic *halacha* where it is not derivable
from the text; greater interpretive responsibility on the individual; distinctive practice
(different calendar/festival reckoning, laws of purity, and prohibited-marriage rules).

## Hermeneutical Method

Direct, reason-based exegesis centered on the plain sense (peshat), accepting inherited
tradition (*sevel ha-yerushah*) only where the text cannot stand without it — a deliberate
contrast to rabbinic *derash* and the [[thirteen-middot|Thirteen Middot]].

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Vigorously opposed by the Geonim and by [[maimonides|Maimonides]]; Saadia Gaon's anti-Karaite
polemic was a defining Rabbanite response. Karaites regard themselves, not the Rabbanites, as
faithful to Scripture. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Communities persisted in the Byzantine world, Crimea, Egypt, and Eastern Europe; a small Karaite
population continues today, chiefly in Israel. Karaite pressure is often credited with spurring
Rabbanite achievements in Hebrew grammar, biblical exegesis, and systematic theology.
