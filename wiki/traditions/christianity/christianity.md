# Christianity

Top-level tradition page. **Christianity** emerged in the 1st century CE as a movement
within Second Temple Judaism centered on Jesus of Nazareth and spread rapidly across the
Roman world. This page is the parent node for the Christian sub-traditions and sects filed
under [[christianity]] in `traditions/christianity/sects/`.

The wiki's current Christian coverage is concentrated on the **first three centuries** —
the period in which the canon, the rule of faith, and the boundary between "orthodoxy" and
"heresy" were still being negotiated. That makes the canon and sect structure especially
load-bearing here: in the 2nd century there was no single settled canon, and rival
communities (proto-orthodox, Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite) held overlapping but divergent
scriptures.

**Chronology:** see the [[christianity-timeline|Timeline of Christianity]] (Apostolic Age →
modern denominations), and the [[comparative-timeline|Comparative Timeline of World Religions]]
for what was happening across other traditions in each era.

## Shared / Mainstream Canon

The mainstream canon is the **27-book New Testament** alongside the Old Testament inherited
from Judaism. That canon was a 2nd–4th century achievement, not a given: several books were
long contested (the *antilegomena* — [[hebrews|Hebrews]], [[james|James]],
[[2-peter|2 Peter]], [[jude|Jude]], 2–3 John, [[revelation-to-john|Revelation]]), and the boundary
was drawn against both the over-narrow canon of [[marcionism|Marcion]] and the expansive
scriptures of the [[gnosticism|Gnostics]]. The [[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]] texts and
the [[1-clement|Apostolic Fathers]] sit on opposite edges of the boundary — the former
canonical for Gnostic sects and rejected by the proto-orthodox; the latter esteemed and
near-canonical for the proto-orthodox without entering the final canon. These splits are now
tracked structurally via each text's `canon_scope`.

## Native Hermeneutical Frameworks

Christianity reads Scripture through the patristic-medieval **Quadriga** — the literal,
allegorical, [[typology|tropological/moral]], and anagogical senses — disciplined in the
proto-orthodox stream by the [[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]]. Sects diverge sharply: Marcion
rejected allegory and the OT entirely; the Gnostics read pneumatically for hidden *gnosis*;
the Ebionites read within a Jewish-Christian, law-observant frame.

## Map of Major Sects / Sub-traditions

- [[proto-orthodox-christianity|Proto-orthodox Christianity]] — the 2nd–3rd c. stream that
  became catholic orthodoxy; fourfold Gospel, apostolic succession, rule of faith.
- [[gnosticism|Gnosticism]] — the family of dualist, demiurgic, *gnosis*-soteriology
  movements; canon includes the [[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]] scriptures.
  - [[valentinianism|Valentinianism]] — the most influential Gnostic school (Valentinus).
  - [[sethianism|Sethianism]] — the "classic"/Sethian Gnostic mythological system.
- [[marcionism|Marcionism]] — Marcion's rival church; two gods; the first closed Christian
  canon (an edited Luke + ten Pauline letters).
- [[montanism|Montanism]] — the "New Prophecy"; ongoing ecstatic Spirit-revelation.
- [[ebionism|Ebionism]] — Jewish-Christian, law-observant, adoptionist; rejected Paul.
- [[arianism|Arianism]] — the 4th-c. Christological movement holding the Son to be a
  creature; condemned at [[nicaea|Nicaea]] (325); no canon divergence, an exegetical dispute.
- [[donatism|Donatism]] — the rigorist North African schism (4th–6th c.); the church as a
  communion of the pure and rebaptism of the lapsed; no canon divergence, an ecclesiological
  dispute settled against it by [[augustine|Augustine]].

The three major historic streams that descend from proto-orthodoxy (the wiki's `canon_scope`
reference points for the NT and OT canons):

- [[catholicism|Catholicism]] — the Western/Latin church under Rome; OT includes the deuterocanon.
- [[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]] — the Eastern conciliar churches; the broadest OT canon;
  split from Rome at the Great Schism (1054).
- [[protestantism|Protestantism]] — the 16th-c. Reformation traditions; 39-book OT (no deuterocanon);
  the home stream of most of the wiki's ingested commentary.

Where these communities also have **group** pages (the people as social/historical actors),
the two are kept distinct and cross-linked — e.g. [[gnostics]] (group) ↔ [[gnosticism]]
(sect). Later denominations (Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, Protestantism, the Latter-day
Saints) will receive sect pages as sources covering them are ingested.
