# Gnosticism

> **Sect page.** For the communities as social/historical actors, see the group page
> [[gnostics|Gnostics]]. This page treats Gnosticism as a *system* — its canon, doctrine,
> hermeneutics, and relationship to orthodoxy. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

A loose family of late-antique movements (late 1st–4th c. CE) sharing spirit–matter
dualism, a demiurgic cosmology, and a soteriology of saving knowledge (*gnosis*). Drew on
Jewish scripture, Middle Platonism, and Hermetic and Christian materials; the major systems
crystallized in the 2nd century. Primary sources: the [[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi
Library]] (1945) and the polemics of the heresiologists.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Developed largely within and alongside 2nd-century Christianity but was defined *out* of it
by the proto-orthodox. Shares Christ as revealer but radically reinterprets creation, the
God of the Hebrew Bible, and the meaning of salvation.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Receives a large body of revelation-dialogues, apocryphal gospels, and cosmological
treatises (much of it preserved at [[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]]) as authoritative —
texts the proto-orthodox rejected. Tends to demote or invert the Hebrew Bible, reading its
Creator as the ignorant [[demiurge|Demiurge]]. On its own text pages these works list
Gnostic sects under `canonical_for` and [[proto-orthodox-christianity]] under `rejected_by`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

[[demiurge|Demiurge]] as ignorant/malevolent world-fashioner; the transcendent true God
above him; the [[pleroma|Pleroma]] of aeons and the fall of [[sophia-gnostic|Sophia]];
the divine spark imprisoned in matter; salvation as awakening *gnosis*; three classes of
humanity (pneumatic / psychic / hylic).

## Hermeneutical Method

Pneumatic, esoteric exegesis: Scripture (especially Genesis) is read against its surface —
often inverting the roles of God, the serpent, and Eve — to recover hidden *gnosis*.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Condemned as the arch-heresy by [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]] (*Against Heresies*),
[[tertullian|Tertullian]], and Hippolytus; the chief foil against which the
[[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]] and the [[nt-canon-formation|NT canon]] were defined.
Recorded here without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Major sub-schools include [[valentinianism|Valentinianism]] and [[sethianism|Sethianism]],
plus Basilideans, Ophites, and Cerinthus. Later heirs: Manichaeism and the surviving
Mandaeans. See the phenomenological/historical synthesis in
[[rudolph-gnosis-the-nature-and-history-of-gnosticism|Rudolph, *Gnosis*]].
