# Gnosticism

> **Sect page.** For the communities as social/historical actors, see the group page
> [[gnostics|Gnostics]]. This page treats Gnosticism as a *system* — its canon, doctrine,
> hermeneutics, and relationship to orthodoxy. Its two most developed sub-schools have their
> own pages: [[valentinianism|Valentinianism]] and [[sethianism|Sethianism]].

## Identity and Origins

"Gnosticism" is a modern scholarly umbrella for a loose family of late-antique movements
(late 1st–4th c. CE) that share a spirit–matter dualism, a demiurgic cosmology, and a
soteriology of saving knowledge (*gnosis*). The ancient groups did not describe themselves as
a single movement: the heresiologists knew them by specific names — Valentinians, Sethians,
Ophites, Basilideans — while *gnostikos* ("one who knows") was a self-designation of some.
Because the category is so loose, Michael Williams (*Rethinking "Gnosticism"*, 1996) and Karen
King (*What is Gnosticism?*, 2003) have argued it should be abandoned or heavily qualified; it
nonetheless remains in general use.

On [[kurt-rudolph|Kurt Rudolph]]'s influential reading (*[[rudolph-gnosis-the-nature-and-history-of-gnosticism|Gnosis]]*),
the phenomenon is best understood not merely as a Christian heresy but as a distinct
*late-antique religion* (*spätantike Religion*) — a "product of hellenistic syncretism" that
"frequently draws its material from the most varied existing traditions… and at the same time
sets it in a new frame." Its threads of continuity are supplied by the gnostic *myth* itself
(the alien God, the Pleroma, Sophia's fall, the Demiurge, the imprisoned spark). The major
systems crystallized in the 2nd century, drawing on Jewish apocalyptic and Genesis exegesis,
Middle Platonism, Iranian dualism, and reinterpreted Christian materials. Primary sources: the
[[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi Library]] (1945) and the polemics of the heresiologists.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Gnosticism developed largely within and alongside 2nd-century Christianity but was defined *out*
of it by the proto-orthodox. It shares Christ as revealer and uses Christian scripture and
vocabulary, but radically reinterprets creation, the God of the Hebrew Bible, and the meaning
of salvation. Whether it is an internal "mutation" of Christianity (the Harnack line, defended
statistically by [[rodney-stark|Stark]]) or a broader religious type with pre-/para-Christian
Jewish and Hellenistic roots (Rudolph's multi-root account; the "Jewish heresy" thesis of
Pearson for Sethianism) is disputed and recorded here without adjudication (Contradiction
Protocol). See [[gnostics|Gnostics]] for the fuller treatment of that origins debate.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Gnostic groups receive as authoritative a large body of revelation-dialogues, apocryphal
gospels, and cosmological treatises — much of it preserved at
[[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]] (e.g. the Sethian *Apocryphon of John*, the Valentinian
*Gospel of Truth*, the *Gospel of the Egyptians*, the *Exegesis on the Soul*) — texts the
proto-orthodox rejected. Simultaneously they tend to **demote or invert the Hebrew Bible**,
reading its Creator as the ignorant [[demiurge|Demiurge]] and evaluating biblical events "with
an evaluation which is entirely contrary to the Bible" (Rudolph). Valentinians also
appropriated the emerging New Testament: Heracleon wrote the first known NT commentary (on
John), and Ptolemy's *Letter to Flora* offers a graded reading of the Mosaic law.

On the affected text pages these works list gnostic sects under `canonical_for` and
[[proto-orthodox-christianity|proto-orthodox Christianity]] under `rejected_by`. The gnostic
production of rival gospels is itself one of the pressures that accelerated the proto-orthodox
drive toward a *closed* [[nt-canon-formation|New Testament canon]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The shared mythic architecture, holding across otherwise divergent schools:

- A transcendent, **unknowable true God** ("the alien God") wholly above the cosmos and its
  maker.
- The [[pleroma|Pleroma]] ("fullness") of aeons, emanated in pairs (*syzygies*) from the
  Father; the fall or passion of [[sophia-gnostic|Sophia]] (Wisdom) produces deficiency and,
  indirectly, the material order.
- The [[demiurge|Demiurge]] (Yaldabaoth in Sethian texts) — an inferior, ignorant, or arrogant
  craftsman, "a murderer from the beginning," frequently identified with the God of the Old
  Testament — who fashions the cosmos and the human body as a prison.
- **Archons/rulers** (often seven planetary powers) who govern the material world and obstruct
  the soul.
- A composite **anthropology**: the body from the Demiurge/matter, the soul (*psyche*), and a
  divine spark (*pneuma*) trapped within. Humanity divides into three classes — **pneumatic,
  psychic, and hylic** — with the "pneumatic race" (often linked to Seth or "the perfect")
  carrying the potential for awakening.
- Salvation as [[gnosis|gnosis]]: revealed knowledge of one's divine origin, the true God, and
  the structure of the cosmos — not faith or works primarily — awakening the sleeper and
  enabling the **soul's ascent** back through the archons (sometimes with passwords or seals)
  to the Pleroma.
- A typically **Docetic or Separationist Christology**: the divine redeemer does not truly
  suffer or permanently unite with matter (see [[docetism|docetism]]).

## Hermeneutical Method

Pneumatic, esoteric exegesis. Scripture — above all Genesis — is read against its surface to
recover hidden *gnosis*, frequently inverting the roles of God, the serpent, and Eve. Rudolph
names this a **"protest exegesis"** — allegory and symbolism deployed "in so far as it runs
counter to the external text and the traditional interpretation," a "chief means of producing
one's own ideas under the cloak of the older… sacred and canonical" literature. The Ophites'
veneration of the serpent as the true bringer of knowledge, and the recasting of the Creator's
prohibition as the jealous act of an inferior god, are the paradigm cases of this "inversion
of values." Valentinian exegesis is subtler, harmonizing scripture with the Pleroma myth
through sophisticated allegory rather than open reversal.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Gnosticism was condemned as the arch-heresy and became the chief foil against which the
[[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]] and the [[nt-canon-formation|NT canon]] were defined. The major
refutations: [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]] (*Against Heresies*, c. 180 — the most extensive ancient
source, and reliable on Valentinianism), [[hippolytus|Hippolytus]] (*Refutation of All
Heresies*), [[tertullian|Tertullian]] (*Against Valentinians*), and Epiphanius (*Panarion*).
[[clement-of-alexandria|Clement of Alexandria]] both preserved Valentinian fragments and
offered a rival, orthodox *gnosis*. All positions are recorded without adjudication
(Contradiction Protocol).

Adjacent movements are distinguished on their own pages. [[marcionism|Marcion]] shares the
gnostic rejection of the Old Testament Creator but is a **disputed, borderline case**: Rudolph
places him "partly in the gnostic tradition and partly" in a "Christian-Pauline position,"
noting that Harnack "vigorously denied any such contact" and that Marcion — unlike the
gnostics before Mani — built an actual church with creed and constitution.
[[manichaeism|Manichaeism]] is Gnosis "in a new, world-historical form," systematized by Mani
in the 3rd century into a dualistic missionary world-religion. The Mandaeans of southern
Iraq/Iran are the only surviving gnostic community, a "living relic" whose baptismal rites and
mythology preserve ancient structures.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Rudolph traces an arc from early schools through the great 2nd-century systems to their
world-historical and surviving heirs:

- **Early schools**: Simon Magus and the Simonians (see [[simon-magus|Simon Magus]]); Cerinthus
  (a Separationist active in Asia Minor at the turn of the 2nd c.); the Ophites/Naassenes and
  Peratae; [[basilides|Basilides]] and the Basilideans in Alexandria (365 heavens, Docetic
  Christology).
- **The great systems**: [[valentinianism|Valentinianism]], the most elaborate and influential,
  founded by [[valentinus|Valentinus]] (Pleroma of 30 aeons, the Sophia myth, tripartite
  anthropology, the five sacraments including the "bridal chamber"), with Ptolemy, Heracleon,
  Marcus, and Theodotus; and [[sethianism|Sethianism]], a Jewish-apocalyptic strand centered on
  Seth as bearer of the divine seed, Barbelo as the divine Mother, and Yaldabaoth as Demiurge.
- **Later heirs**: [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]] and the surviving Mandaeans; and, more
  contestedly, structural parallels in medieval dualist movements such as the Cathars and in
  later esoteric traditions.
