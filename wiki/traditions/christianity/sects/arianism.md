# Arianism

> **Sect page.** The 4th-century Christological movement central to the Trinitarian
> controversy already documented in the wiki's [[nicaea|Nicaea]], [[cappadocian-fathers]],
> and [[wolfson-the-philosophy-of-the-church-fathers|Wolfson]] material. *(Stub — to be
> expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The movement named for **Arius** (c. 256–336), an Alexandrian presbyter who taught that the Son
was a creature — exalted and pre-temporal, but not co-eternal or of the same substance as the
Father ("there was when he was not"). The resulting controversy convulsed the 4th-century church
and prompted the first ecumenical council.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Not a separate church at first but a party *within* catholic Christianity contesting the right
account of the Son's divinity. After Nicaea it persisted through imperial favor and later survived
longest among the Germanic peoples (Goths, Vandals, Lombards) evangelized by the Arian Ulfilas.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

**No canon divergence**: Arians used the same scriptures as the [[proto-orthodox-christianity|
emerging catholic church]]. The conflict was *exegetical and metaphysical*, turning on how to read
texts like Proverbs 8:22 ("the LORD created me"), John 14:28 ("the Father is greater than I"), and
Colossians 1:15 ("firstborn of all creation"). Waltke's [[woman-wisdom|Woman Wisdom]] page already
notes the Arian use of Prov 8:22.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The Son is the first and greatest creature, made out of nothing before the ages, of a different
(*heteros*) or unlike (*anomoios*) substance from the Father; the Father alone is unoriginate God.
"Semi-Arian" homoian and homoiousian positions occupied the middle ground.

## Hermeneutical Method

Literal-grammatical pressure on subordinationist proof-texts, set within a Hellenistic axiom that
the divine is uniquely unbegotten — so a *begotten* Son must be a creature.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Condemned at [[nicaea|Nicaea]] (325), which confessed the Son *homoousios* (of one substance) with
the Father; reaffirmed at Constantinople (381) after the [[cappadocian-fathers|Cappadocians]]
clarified *ousia* (one substance) vs. *hypostasis* (three subsistences). Athanasius was its great
opponent. The dispute is documented from both sides without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Repeatedly ascendant and suppressed through the 4th century; an imperial and political as much as a
theological struggle. Survived among Arian Germanic kingdoms into the 6th–7th centuries. Its memory
shapes later anti-Trinitarian movements that revive similar exegesis.
