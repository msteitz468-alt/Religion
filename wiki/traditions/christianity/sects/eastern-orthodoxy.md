## Identity and Origins

The communion of autocephalous Eastern churches (Greek, Russian, Antiochian, etc.) holding the faith
of the seven ecumenical councils. Separated from [[catholicism|Rome]] at the **Great Schism (1054)**
over papal claims and the *Filioque* clause.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The Greek-speaking continuation of the proto-orthodox stream; emphasizes unbroken patristic and
conciliar continuity (the "mind of the Fathers") and especially the
[[cappadocian-fathers|Cappadocian]] theology of *ousia* and *hypostasis*.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **27-book New Testament** and the **broadest Old Testament** of the three streams —
the Septuagint-based canon including the deuterocanon plus 1 Esdras, 3 Maccabees, Psalm 151, and the
Prayer of Manasseh (with 4 Maccabees as an appendix). A notable nuance: **Revelation is canonical but
not appointed for liturgical reading** — a relic of the East's long historic hesitation over the
book (reflected in [[revelation-to-john|Revelation's]] `disputed_by` note). On ingested text pages, Eastern
Orthodoxy appears in `canonical_for`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Conciliarity rather than papal supremacy; rejection of the *Filioque*; **theosis** (deification) as
the goal of salvation (cf. [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]]); veneration of icons (vindicated at
Nicaea II, 787); a strongly apophatic, mystical theology.

## Hermeneutical Method

Scripture read within Holy Tradition and the patristic consensus, shaped by the liturgy; the literal
and spiritual senses held together.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Mutual schism with Rome (1054); regards the Protestant Reformation as a Western development outside
its history. Divisions recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

Spread through the Byzantine and Slavic worlds; organized as a family of autocephalous churches.
Detailed treatment (the councils, Palamism, the icon controversies) awaits dedicated sources.
