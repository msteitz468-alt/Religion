# Proto-orthodox Christianity

> **Sect page.** The 2nd–3rd century stream that became catholic orthodoxy. The term
> (Bauer/Ehrman) is deliberately retrospective: in the 2nd century this was *one* form of
> Christianity among several, not yet the established norm. See [[bauer-thesis|the Bauer
> thesis]]. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The network of 2nd–3rd century churches and theologians whose theology, canon, and order
prevailed and became "orthodoxy." Continuous with the apostolic communities by its own account;
named "proto-orthodox" by modern scholarship to mark that its victory was not yet secured in the
period the wiki covers most closely.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

This *is* the mainstream of [[christianity|Christianity]] in formation — the stream against which
the wiki's other Christian sects (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, Montanist) are measured and by
which they were labeled heretical.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Defined the emerging **27-book New Testament** while retaining the **Old Testament** — rejecting
both the *subtractive* canon of [[marcionism|Marcion]] and the *expansive* Gnostic scriptures of
[[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]]. Held several books in dispute longer than others (the
*antilegomena*: [[hebrews|Hebrews]], [[james|James]], [[2-peter|2 Peter]], [[jude|Jude]], 2–3
John, [[revelation-to-john|Revelation]]). On contested text pages this stream is the reference point for
`disputed_by` / `rejected_by`. See [[nt-canon-formation|NT canon formation]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

One God who is both Creator and Father of Jesus Christ (against Marcion and the Gnostics); the
real incarnation, suffering, and resurrection of Christ (against Docetism); the
[[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]] as the interpretive baseline; the fourfold Gospel; apostolic
succession and the [[monepiscopate|monepiscopate]].

## Hermeneutical Method

The full patristic toolkit — literal, allegorical, [[typology|typological]], moral, anagogical —
constrained by the rule of faith, so that exegesis stays within the apostolic deposit.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Defines itself *as* orthodoxy and the others as heresy; the polemics of [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]],
[[tertullian|Tertullian]], and Hippolytus are its boundary-drawing literature. The opposing
communities' counter-claims are recorded on their own pages without adjudication (Contradiction
Protocol).

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Consolidated through the Apostolic Fathers, the apologists, and the late-2nd-century
heresiologists; institutionalized at [[nicaea|Nicaea]] (325) and after, from which point it is
better described simply as catholic/orthodox Christianity, later subdividing into Catholicism,
Eastern Orthodoxy, and (after the Reformation) Protestantism.
