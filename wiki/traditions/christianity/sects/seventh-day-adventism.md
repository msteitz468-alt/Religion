# Seventh-day Adventism

> **Sect page.** A 19th-century Adventist-Restorationist denomination out of the Millerite movement.
> *(Ingested: [[ellen-g-white|Ellen G. White]]'s *The Great Controversy*.)*

## Identity and Origins

Grew out of the **Millerite** movement — followers of William Miller, who predicted Christ's return in
1844. After the "**Great Disappointment**" (when it did not occur), a remnant, shaped by the visions of
**Ellen G. White** (1827–1915), reinterpreted 1844 and organized the Seventh-day Adventist Church (1863).

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Broadly **evangelical Protestant** in its core soteriology (salvation by grace through faith), but marked
off by distinctive doctrines and the prophetic authority of Ellen White; mainstream Protestants treat some
of its distinctives as aberrant.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **66-book Protestant Bible as the sole canon** — no *added* scripture. The decisive nuance is
the status of **Ellen G. White**: her extensive writings are regarded as the product of the genuine "gift
of prophecy," **authoritative and inspired yet subordinate to the Bible** (a "lesser light pointing to the
greater light"). In `canon_scope` terms her works would be `authoritative_for: [seventh-day-adventism]`,
not `canonical_for` — a useful sub-canonical case.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **seventh-day (Saturday) Sabbath**; the **investigative judgment** beginning in 1844 (Christ's
heavenly-sanctuary ministry, from a historicist reading of Daniel 8:14); **conditional immortality** and
**annihilationism** (the wicked are destroyed, not eternally tormented; the soul is not innately immortal);
a strong **health and wholeness** ethic (vegetarianism, temperance).

## Hermeneutical Method

**Historicist** interpretation of the prophecies of [[daniel|Daniel]] and [[revelation|Revelation]]
(prophecy as a continuous unfolding of church history) — one of the four schools on the
[[revelation-interpretive-schools|Revelation interpretive-schools]] page — with the Bible as sole canon
read alongside White's confirming "lesser light."

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Shares much with evangelicalism (and is sometimes counted within it), but the Sabbath, 1844/investigative
judgment, and Ellen White's role keep it distinct and contested. Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development and Influence

A global church of ~22 million with a major medical/educational network; a leading modern advocate of the
Saturday Sabbath and of conditional immortality.
