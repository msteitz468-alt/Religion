# Presbyterianism (Reformed)

> **Sect page (Calvinism / Reformed).** The Reformed/Calvinist branch of Protestantism — this page
> covers the broad Reformed/Calvinist tradition as well as the Presbyterian polity branch. The wiki's
> most-represented theological stream among its ingested commentators (Woudstra, Tsumura, Waltke,
> Schreiner, Moo, et al.). See [[john-calvin|Calvin]] (commentator) and the
> [[institutes-of-the-christian-religion|*Institutes*]] (its confessional anchor).

A governing caution from [[gordon-calvins-institutes-a-biography|Gordon's biography of the
*Institutes*]]: **"Calvinism" is not Calvin.** The movement is a diverse, protean body that has
claimed Calvin's name across four centuries while often departing from the historical reformer; the
popular composite (the Weberian work-ethic moralist, the theocrat, the patron of inerrancy and rigid
"TULIP") "owes more to the Synod of Dort than to the pulpits of Geneva. Calvin never saw a tulip in
his life."

## Identity and Origins

The branch of the Reformation stemming from **Ulrich Zwingli** and especially **[[john-calvin|John
Calvin]]** in Geneva, spread to Scotland by **John Knox** (Presbyterianism) and across Europe (the
Reformed and Continental Reformed churches). Gordon stresses Calvin worked *within* a **network** —
alongside Zwingli, Heinrich Bullinger (Zurich), Martin Bucer (Strasbourg), and Philip Melanchthon —
not as a solitary founder. The [[institutes-of-the-christian-religion|*Institutes*]] became the
tradition's foundational confessional standard (authoritative, but subordinate to scripture and *not*
itself canonical).

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon**; among the strongest expositors of Scripture's **sufficiency and
authority** (*sola scriptura*) and of **covenant theology** as the framework that unifies the Testaments.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **sovereignty of God** and **predestination** — classically the "five points" (TULIP: total
depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, perseverance of the saints) —
against [[methodism|Arminianism]] (see the [[romans-7-identity|Romans 7]] and
[[all-israel-saved|election]] debates); **covenant theology** (covenants of works and grace);
**presbyterian polity** (government by elders in graded assemblies); the **regulative principle** of
worship; and the **Westminster Confession and Catechisms** as doctrinal standards.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The Reformed pole against the [[lutheranism|Lutheran]] (on the Lord's Supper and predestination) and the
[[methodism|Wesleyan-Arminian]] (on grace and free will) traditions. Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

The Presbyterian and Reformed churches worldwide; immense influence on theology (the
[[new-perspective-on-paul|NPP debates]] are largely intra-Reformed), on covenantal hermeneutics, and on
political thought (Puritanism, constitutionalism). The contested modern afterlife of Calvin and the
*Institutes* — Kuyper's neo-Calvinism, Barth and Brunner, Weber's capitalism thesis, apartheid
theology and its Reformed opponents, Chinese house churches, and the contemporary "New Calvinism" — is
traced in [[gordon-calvins-institutes-a-biography|Gordon's biography]] (wiki Scope C pending).
