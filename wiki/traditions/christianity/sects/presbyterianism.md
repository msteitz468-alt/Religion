# Presbyterianism (Reformed)

> **Sect page.** The Reformed/Calvinist branch of Protestantism; the wiki's most-represented theological
> stream among its ingested commentators (Woudstra, Tsumura, Waltke, Schreiner, Moo, et al.). *(Stub;
> [[john-calvin|Calvin]] already has a commentator page.)*

## Identity and Origins

The branch of the Reformation stemming from **Ulrich Zwingli** and especially **[[john-calvin|John
Calvin]]** in Geneva, spread to Scotland by **John Knox** (Presbyterianism) and across Europe (the
Reformed and Continental Reformed churches).

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon**; among the strongest expositors of Scripture's **sufficiency and
authority** (*sola scriptura*) and of **covenant theology** as the framework that unifies the Testaments.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **sovereignty of God** and **predestination** — classically the "five points" (TULIP: total
depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, perseverance of the saints) —
against [[methodism|Arminianism]] (see the [[romans-7-identity|Romans 7]] and
[[all-israel-saved|election]] debates); **covenant theology** (covenants of works and grace);
**presbyterian polity** (government by elders in graded assemblies); the **regulative principle** of
worship; and the **Westminster Confession and Catechisms** as doctrinal standards.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The Reformed pole against the [[lutheranism|Lutheran]] (on the Lord's Supper and predestination) and the
[[methodism|Wesleyan-Arminian]] (on grace and free will) traditions. Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

The Presbyterian and Reformed churches worldwide; immense influence on theology (the
[[new-perspective-on-paul|NPP debates]] are largely intra-Reformed), on covenantal hermeneutics, and on
political thought (Puritanism, constitutionalism).
