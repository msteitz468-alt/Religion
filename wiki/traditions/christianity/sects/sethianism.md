# Sethianism

> **Sect page.** A mythological system within [[gnosticism|Gnosticism]], distinct from the
> [[valentinianism|Valentinian]] school. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The "classic" Gnostic mythological system reconstructed by modern scholarship (Schenke,
Layton) from a cluster of related Nag Hammadi texts. May have pre-Christian, Jewish-apocalyptic
roots later overlaid with Christian elements. Named for the elevated role of **Seth**, third
son of Adam, as heavenly progenitor of the spiritual race.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Stands at a greater distance from mainstream Christianity than Valentinianism; some Sethian
texts are only lightly Christianized and draw heavily on Jewish scripture and Platonism.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Core texts include the *Apocryphon of John*, the *Hypostasis of the Archons*, the *Gospel of
the Egyptians*, *Zostrianos*, and *Allogenes* — all preserved at
[[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]] and rejected by [[proto-orthodox-christianity]]. The
*Apocryphon of John* is the fullest surviving Gnostic rewriting of Genesis 1–6.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The unknowable first principle and its first emanation **Barbelo**; the fall producing the
arrogant, ignorant archon **Yaldabaoth** (the [[demiurge|Demiurge]], identified with the God
of Genesis); the spiritual seed of Seth; salvation through the descent of a heavenly revealer.

## Hermeneutical Method

Systematic **counter-reading of Genesis**: the serpent enlightens, the Creator is ignorant,
and Eve/Sophia mediate gnosis. Cast largely in the revelation-dialogue genre.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Among the systems Irenaeus reports (the "Barbelognostics" and "Ophites"). The 3rd-century
Platonist Plotinus also wrote *Against the Gnostics*, targeting Sethian texts (*Zostrianos*,
*Allogenes*). Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

A key witness to the pre-Christian or parallel origins debate in Gnostic studies, and to the
interface between Gnosticism and Platonism (the "Platonizing Sethian" treatises).
