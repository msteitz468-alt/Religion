# Ethiopian Orthodoxy

> **Sect page.** The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church — the one Christian body with the **widest canon**,
> and the community that **canonizes [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]] and [[jubilees|Jubilees]]**. This page resolves the
> `ethiopian-orthodoxy` slug those text pages' `canon_scope` has pointed at. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

One of the oldest national churches in the world, dating to the 4th-century conversion of the Kingdom of
**Aksum** (the missionary **Frumentius** / Abba Selama, and later the **Nine Saints**). Part of the
[[oriental-orthodoxy|Oriental Orthodox]] (Miaphysite, non-Chalcedonian) communion; *Tewahedo* ("made one")
names its one-nature Christology. Long under the Coptic patriarchate, it became autocephalous in 1959.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence) — the broadest in Christendom

The Ethiopian canon is the **largest of any Christian church (~81 books)**, including books no other
tradition canonizes:

- **[[1-enoch|1 Enoch]]** — canonical *only* here among living churches (the single reason 1 Enoch's
  `canon_scope` lists `canonical_for: [ethiopian-orthodoxy]`).
- **[[jubilees|Jubilees]]** (Ge'ez *Kufale*), the books of **Meqabyan** (distinct from the Maccabees),
  4 Ezra, and an expanded "broader" canon — alongside the deuterocanon.

So the Ethiopian church is the standing proof that **canonicity is community-relative**: the
[[pseudepigrapha|pseudepigrapha]] that every other church rejects are *here* full scripture.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

[[oriental-orthodoxy|Miaphysite]] Christology; a striking layer of **Judaic practice** — Saturday Sabbath
alongside Sunday, biblical food laws, and male circumcision — reflecting deep Hebraic roots; the **Kebra
Nagast** (Glory of Kings) tradition of the Ark of the Covenant brought to Aksum; service in the ancient
liturgical language **Ge'ez**.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Within Oriental Orthodoxy (non-Chalcedonian); its wide canon and Judaic practices distinguish it even from
its Coptic and Armenian sister churches. Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

~36 million+ faithful (with the Eritrean Tewahedo Church); custodian of a unique scriptural, liturgical,
and manuscript tradition, and the reason large parts of the [[pseudepigrapha|pseudepigrapha]] survive at
all (1 Enoch and Jubilees are extant in full *only* in Ge'ez).
