# Donatism

A rigorist schism in the Latin Church of Roman North Africa, originating c. 311 in the dispute
over whether bishops who had handed over scriptures during the Diocletianic persecution (the
*traditores*) could validly ordain and administer sacraments. The Donatists held that the
**church must be a communion of the pure** and that sacraments conferred by the unworthy are
void, so converts from the catholic side were **rebaptized**. The catholic party, decisively
[[augustine|Augustine]] a century later, held that the validity of a sacrament depends on
Christ rather than the holiness of the minister, and that the church on earth is irreducibly a
**mixed body** of good and bad until the final separation.

This page is anchored, so far, in Augustine's [[on-christian-doctrine|*De Doctrina Christiana*]]
Book III and its engagement with the Donatist exegete [[tichonius|Tichonius]].

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Donatism shared the catholic canon of Scripture, the creed, and the sacramental system; the
quarrel was **ecclesiological, not doctrinal or canonical**. It understood itself not as a new
revelation but as the *true, undefiled* catholic church of Africa, with the rest of the
communion having been contaminated by communion with *traditores*. The catholic side regarded
it as a **schism** (a breach of unity) rather than a heresy in the strict sense, though
Augustine's anti-Donatist campaign eventually invoked imperial coercion against it.

## Hermeneutics and Tichonius

The Donatist [[tichonius|Tichonius]] (fl. c. 370–390) wrote the *Liber Regularum* ("Book of
Rules"), the first Latin treatise on biblical interpretation, setting out
[[tichonius-seven-rules|seven rules]] for reading Scripture. Strikingly, Tichonius's second
rule — on the Lord's **bipartite body**, the church as a mixed field of true and false members
— cut against the Donatist doctrine of a pure church, and Tichonius was at odds with his own
party while remaining within it. Augustine **adopted Tichonius's rules** into the catholic
exegetical mainstream (*De Doctrina* III), praising the method while correcting Tichonius's
construal of faith and rejecting the Donatist ecclesiology — a notable case of hermeneutical
method crossing a sectarian boundary.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy

Condemned as schismatic by the catholic councils and by imperial rescript (culminating in the
Conference of Carthage, 411); suppressed over the following two centuries and effectively
extinguished by the Arab conquest of North Africa. The Donatist controversy is the historical
matrix of Augustine's **theology of the church and the sacraments** (*ex opere operato*) that
became standard in the West.
