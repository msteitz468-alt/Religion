# Protestantism

> **Sect page (denomination).** The Reformation stream that broke from
> [[catholicism|Rome]] in the 16th century. The wiki's ingested NT commentary is overwhelmingly
> **evangelical Protestant**, so this page is the home tradition of most of its
> [[evangelical-commentary|commentators]]. Created to resolve the `canon_scope` references
> introduced by the canon backfill. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The family of churches arising from the 16th-century Reformation (Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican,
Anabaptist, and their many heirs), defined by protest against late-medieval Catholic doctrine and
authority. [[john-calvin|Calvin]] is the wiki's one ingested Reformation-era commentator; the modern
evangelical commentators (Schreiner, Moo, Waltke, et al.) stand in this stream.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A reform of Western [[catholicism|catholicism]] that retained the [[nicaea|Nicene]] creed and the
27-book NT while rejecting papal authority, Tradition as a co-equal source, and several medieval
doctrines.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **27-book New Testament** and a **39-book Old Testament that excludes the deuterocanon** —
the books Catholicism and Orthodoxy retain are relegated to the "Apocrypha" (Luther printed them
between the Testaments as useful but non-canonical). This narrower OT is the defining canon divergence
from the other two Western/Eastern streams; on ingested text pages Protestantism appears in
`canonical_for` for the protocanonical books. (Luther's hostility to [[esther|Esther]] and
[[james|James]] is documented on those pages.)

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The Reformation *solas* — **sola scriptura** (Scripture alone as final authority), **sola fide**
(justification by faith alone; cf. [[justification|justification]], [[james-paul-justification|James
and Paul]]), **sola gratia**, *solus Christus*, *soli Deo gloria*; the priesthood of all believers;
typically two sacraments (baptism and the Lord's Supper).

## Hermeneutical Method

**Grammatical-historical** exegesis with the literal sense primary; "Scripture interprets Scripture";
the rejection of a magisterial interpreter in favor of the perspicuity of Scripture. This is the
dominant method across the wiki's ingested commentaries.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Separated from Rome in the 16th century; internally fissiparous (Lutheran/Reformed/Anabaptist/Anglican
and thousands of later bodies). Its intra-Protestant disputes (Calvinist vs. Arminian, the
[[new-perspective-on-paul|New Perspective]], etc.) are already among the wiki's controversies.
Divisions recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

From Wittenberg (1517) to a global plurality of denominations and the modern evangelical movement.
The wiki's commentary base makes this the best-documented Christian stream in practice; dedicated
historical treatment awaits further sources.
