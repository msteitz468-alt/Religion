# Nestorianism (Church of the East)

The dyophysite stream of Christianity, named (by its opponents) after [[nestorius|Nestorius]] (Patriarch of Constantinople, condemned at Ephesus 431), which carried the [[theoria|Antiochene]] [[logos-sarx-logos-anthropos|Word-man Christology]] beyond the Roman frontier into Persia, where it became the normative theology of the **Church of the East**. The community as a social actor is also treated on the [[nestorius]] page; this page records it as a doctrinal system. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. XI–XII.

## Identity and the Caricature

Kelly stresses that "Nestorian" historically labels the heresy of dividing the God-man into two distinct Persons (two Sons) — but that "[[nestorius|Nestorius]] himself was not a Nestorian in the classic sense": the doctrine of "two Sons" was abhorrent to him. His failure, on Kelly's reading, was the inability to specify the metaphysical *subject* of the union, offering only a "common *prosopon*" that looked like a merely psychological unity. The school's true fathers were [[diodore-of-tarsus|Diodore of Tarsus]] and [[theodore-of-mopsuestia|Theodore of Mopsuestia]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

- Strict **two-nature** Christology; the natures retain their own *prosopa*, united in a "*prosopon* of union."
- Rejection of [[theotokos|*Theotokos*]] in favour of *Christotokos* ("Christ-bearer").
- [[communicatio-idiomatum|Communicatio idiomatum]] admitted only *homonymously* (verbally).
- Veneration of Theodore "the Interpreter" and the Antiochene exegetical tradition ([[theoria]]).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy

Condemned at the Council of Ephesus (431) and again, with Theodore's Christology, at Constantinople II (553, the "Three Chapters"). The Church of the East regards itself as the guardian of authentic apostolic and Antiochene faith. See the [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedonian]] controversy for the rival positions.
