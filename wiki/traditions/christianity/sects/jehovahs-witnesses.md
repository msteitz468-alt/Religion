# Jehovah's Witnesses

> **Sect page.** The Watch Tower Restorationist movement; strongly non-Trinitarian.

## Identity and Origins

Grew from the **Bible Student** movement begun by **Charles Taze Russell** in the 1870s (Pittsburgh) and
the **Watch Tower** Bible and Tract Society; reorganized under **J. F. Rutherford**, who adopted the name
"Jehovah's Witnesses" in 1931. Like the [[seventh-day-adventism|Adventists]] and [[latter-day-saints|
Latter-day Saints]], it is a 19th-century American **Restorationist** movement claiming to recover true
worship after a long apostasy.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Identifies as Christian but is classed as **non-Christian/heterodox** by the creedal churches, chiefly
because of its **rejection of the Trinity** and of Christ's full deity — the sharpest doctrinal break of
the modern Restorationist groups, structurally echoing ancient [[arianism|Arianism]].

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Accepts the **66-book Protestant canon** (no added scripture), but reads it exclusively in the Watch
Tower's own **New World Translation** (which renders John 1:1 "the Word was *a god*") and under the
**exclusive interpretive authority** of the **Governing Body** ("the faithful and discreet slave"). The
divergence is thus less about the canon's *contents* than about **translation and interpretive control** —
a different axis from the Mormon open canon.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Non-Trinitarianism** — Jehovah (the Father) alone is God; Jesus is his first creation (identified with
the archangel **Michael**), and the holy spirit is God's active force (cf.
[[word-was-god-1-1|John 1:1]], [[arianism|Arianism]]). **Annihilationism** (no immortal soul; the wicked
cease to exist; no hell of torment). **1914** as the year Christ began his invisible heavenly reign and
the "last days" began. Only **144,000** are anointed to heavenly life; the faithful majority hope for
**eternal life on a paradise earth**. **Refusal of blood transfusions**, abstention from politics/military
service and from celebrating birthdays and most holidays.

## Hermeneutical Method

A strongly **centralized** hermeneutic: the **Governing Body** is the sole authoritative interpreter, and
its understanding ("present truth") can be updated. Prophecy ([[daniel|Daniel]], [[revelation-to-john|Revelation]])
read to yield the 1914 chronology. The WTS's *Watchtower* of 15 September 1910 stated that studying the
Bible alone leads to spiritual darkness; studying Society publications alone leads to "the light" within
two years. This principle was confirmed under oath in the **Douglas Walsh trial (1954, Scotland)**: WTS
Secretary-Treasurer Grant Suiter testified that a Jehovah's Witness cannot understand Scripture apart from
the Society's publications. Source: [[trinity-vs-watchtower|O'Cleary]], citing WTS publications directly.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The Trinity rejection puts it furthest from creedal Christianity among the major modern sects; its history
of revised end-date expectations is a frequent point of external criticism. Recorded without adjudication.

**Pyramidology (Russell era)**: Russell's *Studies in the Scriptures*, Vol. 3, and the *Watchtower* magazine
of 1911 used the **Great Pyramid at Giza** as a prophetic calendar ("the Bible in Stone"); specific inch
measurements of the "grand gallery" were said to foretell dates including 1910. Russell also claimed
Melchizedek supervised the pyramid's construction using Egyptian labour. After Russell's death,
**Rutherford repudiated the pyramidology** as Satanic deception — an early example of the WTS's pattern
of erasing prior doctrinal positions. Source: [[trinity-vs-watchtower|O'Cleary]].

**Two-Class Christianity and the 1935 Closure**: The 144,000 "anointed class" was officially declared
complete in **1935** — all places having been filled by loyal Witnesses. This produced a two-class
economy: the anointed (heavenly destiny; born again; partake of the Lord's Supper; in the New Covenant)
and the "great crowd" of Revelation 7:9 (earthly paradise; not born again; excluded from communion;
not technically "in the New Covenant"). The anomaly of Governing Body members added after 1935 (born after
the closure year) has never been formally resolved. Source: [[trinity-vs-watchtower|O'Cleary]].

## Historical Development and Influence

**Three organizational tiers** (Schnell's framework, from insider testimony):

**Tier 1 (1879–1916) — Russell's leadership**: Charles Taze Russell founded the Bible Student movement and the Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society. Bible Students functioned as autonomous, elder-led congregations with individual Christian focus. Russell's death left a power vacuum and a contested succession.

**Tier 2 (1919–1942) — Rutherford's dictatorship**: Joseph Franklin Rutherford ("Judge Rutherford") maneuvered shareholder proxies to gain the WTS presidency over Russell's own succession instructions. He manufactured the image of martyrdom from his 1917–1918 arrest (anti-war editorial policy), used it to silence internal critics, and launched the organizational pivot:
- *Fall of Babylon* pamphlet (1919): attacked all organized Christendom
- "Advertise, Advertise, Advertise the King and the Kingdom" — Cedar Point Convention (1922) reframed witnessing as a mass bookselling campaign
- *Watchtower* articles (1925): "Birth of a Nation" class structure; "Covenant or Sacrifice: Which?" — replaced individual character development with the Kingdom Publisher role (time quotas, book placement reports submitted to Brooklyn)
- Systematic purge (1919–1931) of independent-minded Bible Students who refused organizational conformity; roughly three-fourths of the original Bible Student base departed or were expelled
- Service Directors appointed over elected Elders; Elders fully displaced by ~1927
- 1931 Columbus Convention: name "Jehovah's Witnesses" adopted; shed the name of Christ as organizationally inconvenient
- 1938 Theocracy: all congregations voted in resolution accepting WTS instructions unconditionally; Zone servants ("Theocratic Exactors") appointed by Brooklyn as the Society's eyes and ears

Key legal campaigns (1930s): deliberate engineering of arrests in New Jersey and elsewhere to generate martyrdom narrative and establish legal precedents; *Lovell v. City of Griffin* (1938, SCOTUS: literature distribution protected); *Martin v. City of Struthers* (doorbell-ringing ordinance struck down).

**Tier 3 (1942–present) — Consortium leadership**: New York corporation (Watchtower Bible and Tract Society, Inc.) incorporated to supplement Pennsylvania corporation; Board of Directors became actual governing body; charter members (6–8 per U.S. state) appointed by and dismissible by the Board, ensuring American control and self-perpetuation. American passport and postwar U.S. prestige leveraged for global missionary expansion.

**Gilead Bible College** (South Lansing, NY): established early 1940s as missionary training institution; six-month courses; graduates deployed to Latin America first, then worldwide; uses the same seven-step indoctrination program as domestic work.

**Seven-Step Indoctrination Program** (developed and described by former insider William Schnell):
1. Book placement (any method)
2. Back call (monthly minimum; prospect recorded as "person of goodwill")
3. Home Book Study (weekly; WTS books as text, not the Bible)
4. Area Book Study (group indoctrination; seasonal "debunkers" for Christmas, Easter, etc.)
5. Kingdom Hall / Watchtower Study (salvation-through-Organization message replaces salvation-through-Christ)
6. Service Meeting (quota training; report-slip completion)
7. Baptism (public initiation; official Kingdom Publisher status)

**Scale**: A global, highly organized body (~8.5 million active "publishers") known for door-to-door evangelism, the *Watchtower* and *Awake!* magazines, and landmark U.S. Supreme Court cases on religious liberty (flag salutes, blood transfusions, literature distribution).

**Critical historiography**: Schnell's *30 Years a Watchtower Slave* (1956) provides the earliest detailed insider account of the organizational construction (1924–1953) and is the source for much of the historical detail above. His account is hostile and shaped by an evangelical apologetic frame; his attributions of organizational *motive* are contested. See [[30-years-watchtower-slave]] for full documentation and source limitations.
