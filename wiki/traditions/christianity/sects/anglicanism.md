# Anglicanism

> **Sect page.** The English Reformation church and its global communion; a "middle way." *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

Originating in the **English Reformation** under Henry VIII (the 1534 break with Rome) and shaped
theologically under Edward VI and Elizabeth I, Anglicanism understands itself as a ***via media*** —
**reformed yet catholic**, retaining the episcopate and much of the historic liturgy while embracing
Reformation doctrine.

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon**; the Apocrypha is read "for example of life and instruction of manners"
but not "to establish any doctrine" (Article VI) — a position between Rome and stricter Protestantism.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **Book of Common Prayer** (Cranmer) as the unifying standard of worship and belief (*lex orandi, lex
credendi*); the **Thirty-Nine Articles**; the **threefold ministry** of bishops, priests, and deacons in
apostolic succession; authority as **Scripture, tradition, and reason** (Hooker's "three-legged stool").
Internally broad — Anglo-Catholic, evangelical, and liberal/Broad-Church wings coexist.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Protestant in doctrine but catholic in order — claimed by neither Rome nor strict Protestantism as simply
its own; the Episcopal Church (USA) and the global Anglican Communion descend from it. Recorded without
adjudication.

## Influence

A global communion of ~85 million; via the Prayer Book, a profound influence on English-language worship,
and the matrix from which [[methodism|Methodism]] emerged.
