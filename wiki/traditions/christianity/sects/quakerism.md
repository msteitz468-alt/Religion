# Quakerism (Religious Society of Friends)

> **Sect page.** The radical-Protestant movement of the "Inner Light." *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

Founded in mid-17th-century England by **George Fox** amid the radical Puritanism of the Civil War era.
Friends teach that there is **"that of God in everyone"** — an **Inner Light** by which Christ directly
teaches and leads each person, without need of clergy, creeds, or ritual.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Uses the **Protestant Bible**, but, distinctively, **subordinates the text to the Spirit**: the same Holy
Spirit that inspired Scripture speaks directly and is the *primary* authority, so Scripture is a secondary
("a declaration of the fountain, not the fountain itself," Barclay). No canon *addition*, but a notable
re-ordering of authority — closer to a continuing-revelation principle than most Protestants accept.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The **Inner Light**; **no outward sacraments** (no water baptism or physical Eucharist — these are
inward realities); **no separated clergy** (traditionally, unprogrammed **silent worship** in which any
may speak as led); strong **testimonies** — **peace** (pacifism), **equality**, **integrity**, and
**simplicity** (plain dress and speech, historically). (Today ranges from Christ-centered evangelical
Friends to liberal, universalist meetings.)

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

A radical wing of Protestantism whose rejection of sacraments and clergy and elevation of the Inner Light
put it at the edge of (or beyond) mainstream Protestant norms. Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

Small in number (~400,000) but historically outsized: pioneers of **abolition**, prison and asylum
reform, women's equality, and pacifism; William Penn's "holy experiment" in Pennsylvania.
