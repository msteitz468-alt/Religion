# Unitarian Universalism

> **Sect page.** The merged liberal tradition of historic Unitarianism and Universalism — filed under
> Christianity for its **roots**, with the caveat that contemporary UU is **post-Christian and
> pluralist**. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

Two distinct movements that merged in 1961:

- **Unitarianism** — the rejection of the **Trinity** (God is one person, not three); roots in the
  Radical Reformation (Michael Servetus, burned 1553; the Socinians and the Polish Brethren; later English
  and American Unitarianism under William Ellery Channing).
- **Universalism** — the doctrine that **all souls are ultimately saved** (universal reconciliation; Hosea
  Ballou), against eternal hell.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Historically a **liberal Christian** movement; over the 20th century it became explicitly
**non-creedal and pluralist**, drawing on many religious sources, so many UUs today do not identify as
Christian at all. Filed here for genealogical reasons, with the **boundary status flagged** — a parallel to
the contested Christian identity of [[latter-day-saints|Mormonism]] but moving in the opposite direction
(from Christianity *outward*).

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

**No binding canon.** Historic Unitarians and Universalists read the **Bible** by reason and conscience
(rejecting Trinitarian and eternal-hell readings); contemporary UU treats Scripture as **one source among
many** (alongside other faiths, science, and personal experience). Authority rests in **individual
conscience**, not a canon.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Non-Trinitarianism**, **universal salvation**, and above all **non-creedalism** — no required statement
of belief; covenantal rather than confessional community; the primacy of reason, conscience, and (in the
modern movement) shared values over doctrine.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Among the furthest from creedal Christianity (rejecting both the Trinity *and* the requirement of any
creed); shares the non-Trinitarian intuition with [[arianism|Arianism]] and the
[[jehovahs-witnesses|Jehovah's Witnesses]] but, unlike them, without a fixed doctrinal system. Recorded
without adjudication.

## Influence

Small but historically influential in American liberal religion, abolition, and social reform; a model of
non-creedal, congregationally-governed religious community.
