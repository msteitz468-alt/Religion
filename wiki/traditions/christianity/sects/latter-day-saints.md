# The Latter-day Saints (Mormonism)

> **Sect page.** The Restorationist movement founded by [[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]]; the headline case
> for the wiki's `canon_scope` design. *(Ingested: the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]], the
> [[doctrine-and-covenants|Doctrine and Covenants]], and the [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great
> Price]] — all public domain.)*

## Identity and Origins

Founded in 1830 in upstate New York by **[[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]]** (1805–1844), who reported
visions and the recovery and translation of golden plates as the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]]. The
movement holds that, after a **Great Apostasy** corrupted the early church, the true church and priesthood
authority were **restored** through Smith. The largest body is the **Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day
Saints** (Salt Lake City; the "Brighamite" line under Brigham Young); other branches (notably the
**Community of Christ**, formerly RLDS) descend from the same founder.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The defining tension: Latter-day Saints **identify as Christian** (centered on Jesus Christ as Savior),
but [[catholicism|Catholic]], [[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodox]], and most [[protestantism|Protestant]] bodies
classify them as **outside historic Christianity** because of the added scripture, the non-Nicene Godhead,
and the doctrine of exaltation. Filed here as a Christianity sect (Restorationist), per its
self-understanding; the classification dispute is itself recorded (Contradiction Protocol).

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence) — the `canon_scope` exemplar

Mormonism has an **open canon**: the Bible (KJV, "as far as it is translated correctly," with the **Joseph
Smith Translation**) **plus** three additional "standard works" — the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]],
the [[doctrine-and-covenants|Doctrine and Covenants]], and the [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great
Price]] — and the principle of **continuing revelation** through living prophets. This is precisely the
taxonomy stress-test the wiki's `canon_scope` was designed against: the **Book of Mormon** is
`canonical_for: [latter-day-saints]`, graded differently by other Restoration branches
(`deuterocanonical_for: [community-of-christ]`), and `rejected_by` the Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant
canons — *while it presents itself as a continuation of the biblical narrative* (a reception fact, recorded
on its text page, not a canon bucket).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

A **non-Trinitarian Godhead** (Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as three distinct beings, "one in purpose");
the **premortal existence** of spirits; **exaltation** (the faithful may become like God — a strong form of
[[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]]); baptism for the dead and temple ordinances; an ordained lay priesthood;
modern **prophets and apostles**. (Plural marriage was practiced in the 19th c., officially ended 1890.)

## Hermeneutical Method

Authority rests in the **living prophet** and continuing revelation as much as in the text; scripture is
read through the Restoration framework, with the Bible subordinated where it is held mistranslated and
supplemented by the modern standard works.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The "are Mormons Christian?" debate is the central boundary question — answered yes by the LDS, no by the
historic creedal churches (the dispute turns on the creeds, the canon, and the doctrine of God). Internal
divisions: LDS (Brighamite) vs. Community of Christ vs. fundamentalist (still-polygamous) offshoots.
Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development and Influence

From Smith's 1844 martyrdom and the migration to Utah under Brigham Young, the LDS Church grew into a
global body of ~17 million, with a distinctive missionary, temple, and welfare culture.
