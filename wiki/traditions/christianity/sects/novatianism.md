# Novatianism

A rigorist schism founded by the Roman presbyter [[novatian|Novatian]], who became antipope in 251 in opposition to Pope Cornelius over the readmission of the *lapsi* — those who had apostatised in the Decian persecution and now sought to return. Doctrinally orthodox (especially in Trinitarian theology), it diverged from the Catholic Church purely on **discipline**. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. VIII.

## Identity and Distinctives

The Novatianists, calling themselves *Cathari* ("the pure"), held that the Church has no power to forgive grave post-baptismal sins (idolatry, and for stricter members adultery and murder) and that it ceases to be holy if it readmits such sinners. Kelly notes the schism forced [[cyprian|Cyprian]] to ground Church unity on something other than doctrinal orthodoxy, since the Novatianists were doctrinally correct: "Whatever and whatsoever kind of man he is, he is not a Christian who is not in Christ's Church." They required rebaptism of those entering from the Catholic Church. The movement persisted into the Byzantine period.

## Relationship to Other Movements

Novatianism resembles the earlier rigorism of [[montanism|Montanism]] and the later [[donatism|Donatist]] schism in North Africa, all turning on the holiness of the Church and the [[sacrament-of-penance|penitential discipline]]; but unlike them it was provoked specifically by the question of the lapsed.
