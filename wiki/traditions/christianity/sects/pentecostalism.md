# Pentecostalism

> **Sect page.** The modern Spirit-gifts movement; with its charismatic offshoots, among the largest and
> fastest-growing forms of Christianity. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

Born in early-20th-century revivalism — Charles Parham's Topeka school (1901) and especially the **Azusa
Street Revival** in Los Angeles under **William J. Seymour** (1906) — out of the Wesleyan
[[methodism|Holiness]] movement. It restores to the center the experience of **Pentecost** (Acts 2).

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon**; no canon divergence. Its distinctive hermeneutic is **continuationism** —
the conviction that the **miraculous gifts** of [[acts|Acts]] (tongues, prophecy, healing) continue today —
and a restorationist appeal to Acts 2 as the **norm** of Spirit-filled church life.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Baptism in the Holy Spirit** as an experience subsequent to conversion, typically evidenced by
**speaking in tongues** (*glossolalia*); the active exercise of the **spiritual gifts** (1 Cor 12);
**divine healing**; lively, experiential worship; commonly **premillennial** eschatology. (Internally
divided — e.g. Trinitarian vs. "Oneness" Pentecostals.)

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Evangelical in doctrine but distinguished by continuationism against **cessationist** Protestants (many
[[presbyterianism|Reformed]] and others who hold the miraculous gifts ceased with the apostolic age). Its
influence spread into mainline and Catholic churches as the **charismatic movement**. Recorded without
adjudication.

## Influence

A global movement of ~600 million (with the broader charismatic renewal) — by some counts the
second-largest Christian grouping after Catholicism; a dominant force in Global South Christianity.
