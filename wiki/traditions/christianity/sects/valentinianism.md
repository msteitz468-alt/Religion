# Valentinianism

> **Sect page.** A school *within* [[gnosticism|Gnosticism]]. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The most influential and intellectually sophisticated Gnostic school, founded by
[[valentinus|Valentinus]] (c. 100–175), who taught at Rome c. 140 and reportedly nearly
became bishop. Continued through a distinguished line of teachers — Ptolemy, Heracleon
(author of the first known commentary on John), Marcus, and Theodotus.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The most "churchly" Gnosticism: Valentinians often worshipped alongside ordinary Christians
while holding an inner, pneumatic interpretation. This made the school especially threatening
to the proto-orthodox, who could not easily distinguish its members externally.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Used the proto-orthodox scriptures (notably Paul and John) but read them pneumatically; also
produced its own works, including the *Gospel of Truth* (preserved at
[[nag-hammadi-library|Nag Hammadi]]). Listed under `canonical_for` on the relevant text
pages where applicable; rejected by [[proto-orthodox-christianity]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

A [[pleroma|Pleroma]] of thirty aeons in syzygies; the fall of the youngest aeon,
[[sophia-gnostic|Sophia]], producing the [[demiurge|Demiurge]] and the material world;
Christ descending to impart [[gnosis|gnosis]]; the three classes (pneumatic / psychic /
hylic) with differentiated destinies.

## Hermeneutical Method

Pneumatic allegory: the literal text encodes the Pleromatic drama and the soul's ascent.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Refuted at length by [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]] (Book I of *Against Heresies* is largely
anti-Valentinian) and Tertullian (*Against the Valentinians*). [[clement-of-alexandria|Clement
of Alexandria]] preserved Valentinian sources (the *Excerpta ex Theodoto*). Recorded without
adjudication.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Split into Eastern (Anatolian) and Western (Italic) branches over the nature of Christ's body.
Its exegesis (Heracleon on John) influenced the development of Christian commentary even among
opponents.
