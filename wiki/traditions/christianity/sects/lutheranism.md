# Lutheranism

> **Sect page.** The founding magisterial-Protestant church; a sub-stream of [[protestantism|
> Protestantism]] filed flat under Christianity. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

The tradition stemming from **Martin Luther's** Reformation (the 95 Theses, 1517) — the first and
prototypical Protestant church, confessing **justification by faith alone** (*sola fide*).

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon** (Luther grouped the deuterocanon as "Apocrypha" — useful but
non-canonical; he also questioned [[james|James]], [[jude|Jude]], [[hebrews|Hebrews]], and
[[revelation-to-john|Revelation]], the *antilegomena*, without removing them). No canon divergence from
[[protestantism|Protestantism]] generally.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

The Reformation *solas*; the **law/gospel** distinction as the key to reading Scripture; **sacramental
union** — the real bodily presence of Christ "in, with, and under" the bread and wine (against both Rome's
transubstantiation and the Reformed symbolic view); the **Book of Concord** (1580, incl. the Augsburg
Confession) as the doctrinal standard.

## Hermeneutical Method

Grammatical-historical reading governed by the **law/gospel** hermeneutic and the perspicuity of
Scripture; the [[justification|justification]] debate is its theological center (cf.
[[new-perspective-on-paul|the NPP]]).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Defined against Rome (Eucharist, papacy, justification) and against the Reformed over the Lord's Supper
(the Marburg Colloquy, 1529). Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

State churches across Germany and Scandinavia; a global communion of ~75 million; foundational for all
later Protestantism.
