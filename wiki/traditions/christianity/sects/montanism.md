# Montanism

> **Sect page.** For the community as social/historical actor, see the group page
> [[montanists|Montanists]]. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The "New Prophecy," begun c. 165 CE in Phrygia (Asia Minor) by **Montanus** and the
prophetesses **Priscilla** and **Maximilla**, who delivered ecstatic oracles in the first
person as the voice of the [[paraclete-advocate|Paraclete]]. Expected the heavenly Jerusalem
to descend at Pepuza.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Not a doctrinal heresy in the usual sense — Montanism was broadly orthodox in its theology of
God and Christ. The conflict was over **authority and discipline**: whether the age of prophecy
and fresh revelation continued, and whether the church should tighten its rigor.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Accepted the emerging proto-orthodox scriptures (with notable attachment to John and
[[revelation|Revelation]]) but treated the **new prophetic oracles as authoritative
revelation** alongside them. Its claim of ongoing inspiration is one reason the proto-orthodox
pressed toward a *closed* canon; the Muratorian Fragment explicitly rejects Montanist writings.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Continuing Spirit-revelation; ecstatic prophecy; stricter discipline (longer fasts, no second
marriage, no flight from martyrdom); heightened imminent eschatology centered on Pepuza.

## Hermeneutical Method

Charismatic: the Paraclete promised in John speaks afresh through the prophets, completing
(not contradicting) the apostolic deposit.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Excommunicated by Asian synods in the late 2nd century; opposed by Hippolytus and others.
Famously, [[tertullian|Tertullian]] embraced the New Prophecy in his later career — the rare
case of a major proto-orthodox theologian crossing into a movement deemed heretical. Recorded
without adjudication.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Persisted in Phrygia for centuries despite imperial suppression. Its memory shaped later debates
over the cessation of prophecy and the role of charismatic authority in the church.
