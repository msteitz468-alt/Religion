# Ebionism

> **Sect page.** For the community as social/historical actor, see the group page
> [[ebionites|Ebionites]]. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

A Jewish-Christian movement (c. 100–400 CE) concentrated in Transjordan, whose name derives
from the Hebrew *ebyonim*, "the poor." Continued to observe the Mosaic law while confessing
Jesus as Messiah — a living survival of the law-observant wing of earliest Christianity.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Represents the path *not* taken by Gentile proto-orthodoxy: continuity with Judaism maintained
to the point of full Torah observance. Where Marcion severed Christianity from Judaism, the
Ebionites refused to sever it at all.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Retained the **Hebrew Bible** as scripture and used a Hebrew/Aramaic gospel related to
[[matthew|Matthew]] (the "Gospel of the Ebionites"). **Rejected the Pauline epistles
entirely**, regarding Paul as an apostate from the law — the mirror image of Marcion's
Paul-only canon. On the relevant text pages, Pauline letters list this sect under
`rejected_by`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Adoptionist Christology** (Jesus a righteous man on whom the Spirit/Christ descended at
baptism); rejection of the virgin birth and of pre-existence; mandatory circumcision and law
observance; hostility to Paul.

## Hermeneutical Method

Law-observant Jewish-Christian exegesis, reading Jesus within, not against, the Torah.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Opposed by [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]], [[origen|Origen]], [[tertullian|Tertullian]], and Epiphanius,
who treated Ebionite Christology and Paul-rejection as decisive heresies. Related to but
distinguishable from the Nazarenes. Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Faded by the 5th century as Gentile Christianity became dominant and the Jewish-Christian
communities were squeezed between church and synagogue. A key data point in modern debates over
the "parting of the ways."
