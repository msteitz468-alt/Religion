# Marcionism

> **Sect page.** For the community as social/historical actor, see the group page
> [[marcionites|Marcionites]]. *(Stub — to be expanded.)*

## Identity and Origins

The church founded by [[marcion|Marcion of Sinope]] (c. 85–160) after his break with the
Roman church c. 144 CE. A highly organized rival with its own clergy, liturgy, and ascetic
discipline; it spread from the Euphrates to Britain and survived for centuries.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

A radical Paulinist reform that rejected Christianity's continuity with Judaism altogether.
Where the proto-orthodox claimed the Hebrew Bible as Christian scripture, Marcion severed it
entirely.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Produced the **first closed Christian canon** — the *Evangelikon* (an edited Gospel of
[[luke|Luke]]) and the *Apostolikon* (ten Pauline letters, excluding the Pastorals), each
purged of perceived Judaizing interpolations. **Rejected the entire Old Testament** and all
other gospels and writings. This canon is the mirror-image stress case to Gnostic expansion:
Marcion's is defined by *subtraction*. Marcion's challenge is widely credited with spurring
the proto-orthodox toward [[nt-canon-formation|defining their own canon]].

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Two gods**: the just, harsh Creator/[[demiurge|Demiurge]] of the Old Testament versus the
previously unknown, purely good Father revealed by Jesus. Docetic Christology (Christ only
*appeared* to have a body); salvation as rescue *from* the Creator's world; rigorous asceticism
(celibacy, fasting).

## Hermeneutical Method

Strict **literalism**: Marcion rejected allegory and denied that the OT prophecies referred to
Jesus — the OT means what it says and describes the Creator's own (different) messiah.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

The primary heretical foil for [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]] and especially
[[tertullian|Tertullian]], whose five-volume *Against Marcion* is the fullest refutation.
Distinct from Gnosticism proper (no Pleroma/aeon myth, no salvation-by-*gnosis*), though often
grouped with it. Recorded without adjudication.

## Historical Development, Subdivisions, and Influence

Marcionite communities persisted into the 5th–6th centuries in the East, partly absorbed by
Manichaeism. Marcion's negative catalyst role in canon formation is his largest legacy.
