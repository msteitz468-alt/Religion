# The Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement

> **Sect page.** The American frontier movement to restore New Testament Christianity and unite Christians
> on "the Bible alone." *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

A union of two early-19th-century American revival movements — **Barton W. Stone's** "Christians" and
**Thomas and Alexander Campbell's** "Disciples" — that merged around 1832 with the goal of **restoring**
the simple Christianity of the New Testament and so **uniting** a divided church on Scripture alone, apart
from human creeds.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

Restorationist like the [[latter-day-saints|Mormons]], [[seventh-day-adventism|Adventists]], and
[[jehovahs-witnesses|Witnesses]] — but, unlike them, it adds **no new scripture and no new prophet**: its
whole program is to recover the *primitive* church directly from the existing Bible. Broadly evangelical.

## Canon and Scripture

The **Protestant Bible alone**, under the watchwords **"no creed but Christ, no book but the Bible"** and
"where the Scriptures speak, we speak; where the Scriptures are silent, we are silent." A strong
**primitivist** hermeneutic — the New Testament read as a directly reproducible blueprint for the church.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Believer's baptism by immersion** (understood as for the remission of sins); **weekly observance of the
Lord's Supper**; **congregational autonomy** (no denominational hierarchy); rejection of creeds and
denominational names in favor of biblical ones ("Christians," "Disciples," "Church of Christ"); and
**Christian unity** as a founding ideal.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Broadly within evangelical Protestantism; shares immersion baptism with the [[baptists|Baptists]] but
differs on baptism's role in salvation. The movement itself later **divided** into three main streams: the
*a cappella* **Churches of Christ**, the **Christian Churches/Churches of Christ**, and the more liberal
**Disciples of Christ** (a mainline denomination). Recorded without adjudication.

## Influence

Several million adherents across its branches; a distinctively American contribution — restorationist
primitivism and the unity-through-Scripture ideal — and a major presence in US Bible-Belt Christianity.
