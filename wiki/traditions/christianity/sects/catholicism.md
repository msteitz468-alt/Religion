# Catholicism

> **Sect page (denomination).** One of the three major historic streams descending from

> sources are ingested.)*

## Identity and Origins

The Western/Latin church in communion with the Bishop of Rome, understanding itself as the
continuous catholic church of the apostles. Diverged institutionally from
[[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]] at the **Great Schism (1054)** over papal authority and the
*Filioque*, and from the [[protestantism|Protestant]] reformers in the 16th century.

## Relationship to the Parent Tradition

The direct Latin continuation of the proto-orthodox stream; holds the [[nicaea|Nicene]] faith and
the ecumenical councils, adding later Western dogmatic developments.

## Canon and Scripture (Canon Divergence)

Holds the **27-book New Testament** and an **Old Testament that includes the deuterocanon** (Tobit,
Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees, and the additions to Esther and Daniel) — fixed at
Trent (1546). This is the key canon divergence from [[protestantism|Protestantism]], which excludes
those books, and a near-overlap with [[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]] (which receives a
somewhat broader OT). Those deuterocanonical books will populate the `deuterocanonical_for` bucket
once they are ingested; on the currently-ingested text pages, Catholicism appears in `canonical_for`.

## Doctrinal Distinctives

Papal primacy and infallibility (Vatican I, 1870); seven sacraments; transubstantiation; the
co-authority of Scripture and Tradition under the Magisterium; the Marian dogmas (Immaculate
Conception, Assumption); purgatory.

## Hermeneutical Method

Scripture interpreted within Tradition and the teaching office (Magisterium); classic use of the
fourfold senses (literal, allegorical, tropological, anagogical).

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Mutual schism with the East (1054) and contested separation from the Protestant reformers (16th c.);
each stream regards itself as faithful and the divisions are recorded without adjudication
(Contradiction Protocol).

## Historical Development and Influence

The largest Christian body; later subdivided liturgically (Latin and Eastern Catholic churches) and
shaped by reforming councils (Trent, Vatican I and II). Detailed treatment awaits dedicated sources.
