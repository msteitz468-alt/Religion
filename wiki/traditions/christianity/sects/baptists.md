# Baptists

> **Sect page.** The believer's-baptism, congregational stream of Protestantism — the largest single
> Protestant family in the US. Several ingested commentators (e.g. Block, Schreiner) are Baptist. *(Stub.)*

## Identity and Origins

Emerged in early-17th-century English Separatism (John Smyth, Thomas Helwys) and, in America, with **Roger
Williams** (Providence, 1638) — defined above all by **believer's baptism** (the baptism of professing
believers, not infants) by **immersion**, and a **regenerate** (converted) church membership.

## Canon and Scripture

The **66-book Protestant canon**, with a strong stress on **biblical authority and sufficiency**; reading
is governed by **"soul competency"** — every believer's responsibility and freedom to interpret Scripture
under its authority (a strong form of the perspicuity principle).

## Doctrinal Distinctives

**Believer's baptism by immersion**; **local-church autonomy** (congregational government, no binding
hierarchy); the **priesthood of all believers**; and a historic championing of **religious liberty and
the separation of church and state**. Diverse on soteriology — both Calvinist ("Particular"/Reformed
Baptist) and Arminian ("General"/Free Will Baptist) streams exist.

## Relationship to Orthodoxy and Other Sects

Within evangelical Protestantism but distinguished from the **paedobaptist** (infant-baptizing) churches
([[lutheranism|Lutheran]], [[anglicanism|Anglican]], [[presbyterianism|Presbyterian]]). Recorded without
adjudication.

## Influence

A global movement of ~100+ million (the Southern Baptist Convention is the largest US Protestant body);
major influence on revivalism, the free-church model, and religious-liberty jurisprudence.
