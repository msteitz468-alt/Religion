# Security Checking

**Security Checking** (abbreviated **"Sec Check"**) is a [[auditing|meter-assisted interrogation]] procedure used by the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] to discover information about members' loyalties, past acts, and potential threats to the organization. It is distinct from standard auditing in intent: where auditing is nominally therapeutic, Sec Checking is explicitly investigative and organizational in purpose.

## History and Renaming

Introduced by [[l-ron-hubbard|Hubbard]] in the late 1950s and formalized through the early 1960s. When Security Checks became a public relations liability (given the nature of the questions and the retention of answers), Hubbard renamed the practice:

1. **Security Check** → renamed **"Integrity Processing"** (circa 1968, during the Corfu period)
2. **Integrity Processing** → renamed **"Confessional Auditing"** (later)

The renaming was cosmetic: the question lists and procedures remained in use, and crucially, **the organization retained the answers** in the member's file. Jon Atack notes that records were kept by the organization and could be made available to the **Guardian's Office (GO)** / **Office of Special Affairs (OSA)** for use against members who later became critics or left.

## Nature of the Questions

The question lists used in Security Checks were deliberately extreme — designed to produce confessions of any conceivable transgression (real or imagined) and to instill the sense that nothing could be hidden from the organization. Questions documented by ex-members and in court proceedings include:

- "Have you ever enslaved a population?"
- "Did you come to Earth for evil purposes?"
- Standard questions about sexual conduct, criminal acts, and disloyalty
- Questions about contact with critics, journalists, and government agencies

The inclusion of fantastical questions (enslavement of populations, etc.) serves the dual function of establishing the cosmological frame (Scientology's space-opera history) and ensuring no question feels too absurd to answer.

## Organizational Function

The retention of Sec Check files by the Church — not merely by the auditor — is the practice's defining feature from a sociological standpoint. This creates:
- **Leverage**: knowledge of any transgression confessed can be used against a departing or hostile member
- **Institutional memory**: the organization accumulates detailed personal information about all members
- **Self-censorship**: members aware that their disclosures are retained become cautious about any contact with the organization's perceived enemies

Former GO and OSA members confirm (per Atack and Wright) that Sec Check files were used in exactly this way — information passed to law enforcement, given to the media, or used in litigation against ex-members.

## Relation to Auditing

Scientology publicly presents Sec Checking as a form of auditing — spiritual counseling — and has used this framing to claim First Amendment protection for the practice (privileged communication between congregant and minister). Critics argue the organizational retention and use of files contradicts this characterization. Multiple courts have examined the question; rulings have varied.
