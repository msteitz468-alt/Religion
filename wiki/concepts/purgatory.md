# Purgatory / Purgatorial Punishment

Temporary, cleansing punishment after death for the regenerate, distinct from the eternal punishment of
the damned. [[city-of-god|*City of God*]] Book XXI contains an influential early form of the idea —
"the germ of the doctrine of purgatory," as the NPNF editors note.

## Augustine's qualified affirmation

Within his refutation of those who deny [[eternal-punishment|eternal punishment]], [[augustine|Augustine]]
affirms that **some** of the regenerate "suffer temporary punishments after death" and so are *not* doomed
to the everlasting pains that follow the [[last-judgment|last judgment]] — citing Matt. 12:32 ("not
forgiven… neither in this world, neither in that which is to come," implying some sins *are* forgiven in
the world to come) and the refiner's fire of Malachi 3. The contested Pauline locus is **1 Cor. 3:11–15**
("saved… yet so as by fire"), which Augustine reads primarily as the fire of *tribulation* in this life.

He sets two firm limits: such cleansing avails only for those "regenerated in Christ" who lived neither so
badly as to be unworthy nor so well as to need nothing; and **purgatorial pains exist only *before* the
final judgment** — "let him not fancy that there are any purgatorial pains except before that final and
dreadful judgment." It is never a route *out of* hell once one is condemned. Baptized infants who die need
no such cleansing, "spiritual regeneration of itself" sufficing.

## Reception

The idea was developed more fully by Gregory the Great and became defined doctrine in the Roman Catholic
Church (with prayers for the dead and indulgences), while the **Reformers rejected** it as lacking
scriptural warrant — a major point of canon-divergence between Catholicism and Protestantism. Augustine's
text is cited by both sides.
