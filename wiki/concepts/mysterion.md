# The Mystery (*Mystērion*) in Paul

The Greek word *mystērion* (pl. *mystēria*) appears 21 times in the Pauline letters and 28 times in the NT overall. It is one of Paul's primary categories for describing the content and character of the Christian gospel. In popular usage "mystery" denotes something puzzling or unknown; in Paul it denotes something previously hidden but now disclosed — a divine secret that has been revealed. The concept is not esoteric (knowledge restricted to initiates) but apocalyptic (a divine plan hidden in God from before creation, now made public through the gospel and its heralds).

## Background

**Not the mystery cults**: Despite frequent early-20th-century attempts (the Religionsgeschichtliche Schule: Reitzenstein, Bousset) to derive Paul's *mystērion* language from Hellenistic mystery religions, this derivation is now widely rejected. The mystery cults used *mystērion* for secret initiation rites and their accompanying experiential knowledge — not for publicly proclaimed divine plans. Paul's *mystērion* is proclaimed to everyone (Col 1:28: "him we proclaim *to everyone*").

**Jewish apocalyptic background**: The closer parallel is Jewish apocalyptic literature, particularly Daniel. In Daniel 2:18–19, 27–30, the Aramaic word *rāz* (translated *mystērion* in the LXX) refers to the hidden plan of God revealed to Daniel in a dream — specifically God's plan for the succession of world empires and the establishment of his eternal kingdom. In 1 Enoch, 4 Ezra, 2 Baruch, and the Qumran literature (especially 1QH, 1QS, 1QpHab), *rāzîm* / *mystēria* are the hidden counsels of God concerning the end of the age, revealed to the righteous community. Key features: (1) The mystery is hidden until God chooses to disclose it; (2) Disclosure comes through a special intermediary (seer, apostle); (3) The mystery concerns a divine plan or event with eschatological significance; (4) Once disclosed, it is not secret but proclaimed.

**Paul's distinctive move**: Paul adopts this apocalyptic framework but identifies the formerly hidden mystery with the gospel of Jesus Christ — specifically with: (a) the inclusion of Gentiles in the people of God, and (b) Christ himself as the content of the revealed mystery.

## The Primary Pauline Texts

**Romans 11:25**: "Lest you be wise in your own sight, I do not want you to be unaware of this *mystērion*, brothers: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in, and in this way all Israel will be saved." The mystery here is the unexpected pattern of Israel's hardening and Gentile inclusion — a reversal of the expected sequence.

**Romans 16:25–26**: "The revelation of the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but has now been disclosed and through the prophetic writings has been made known to all nations, according to the command of the eternal God, to bring about the obedience of faith." The mystery = the gospel itself, disclosed at the appointed time.

**1 Corinthians 2:7**: "We impart a secret and hidden wisdom of God, which God decreed before the ages for our glory." The "rulers of this age" did not understand it — had they known, "they would not have crucified the Lord of glory" (2:8). The cross is itself the revealed mystery: the wisdom of God hidden from the world's powers.

**1 Corinthians 15:51**: "Behold! I tell you a *mystērion*. We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet." The apocalyptic transformation of the living at Christ's return.

**Ephesians 1:9–10**: "Making known to us the *mystērion* of his will, according to his good pleasure, which he set forth in Christ as a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth." The mystery here is the cosmic *anakephalaiōsis* — the summing up of all things in Christ.

**Ephesians 3:3–6** (the fullest statement): "The *mystērion* was made known to me by revelation, as I have written briefly. When you read this, you can perceive my insight into the *mystērion* of Christ, which was not made known to the sons of men in other generations as it has now been revealed to his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit. This *mystērion* is that the Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel." This is the most specific definition of the Ephesian *mystērion*: the full, equal inclusion of Gentiles alongside Jews in the one people of God. This was always present as promise in the OT (cf. Gen 12:3; Isa 49:6; Isa 56:6–8) but was not fully disclosed as a revelatory program until the gospel.

**Ephesians 5:32**: "This *mystērion* is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church" — applied to the husband-wife relationship, which Paul reads as a type (*typos*) of Christ's union with the church.

**Ephesians 6:19**: "Pray also for me, that words may be given to me in opening my mouth boldly to proclaim the *mystērion* of the gospel." The mystery's content is the gospel; its proclamation is Paul's specific vocation.

**Colossians 1:26–27**: "The *mystērion* hidden for ages and generations but now revealed to his saints. To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this *mystērion*, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory." Here the mystery = Christ himself, the indwelling Christ among the Gentiles.

**Colossians 2:2–3**: "God's *mystērion*, which is Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." The mystery is fully identified with Christ.

## The Ephesian *Mystērion* and Gentile Inclusion

In Ephesians *mystērion* occurs 8 times — more than in any other Pauline letter — and its dominant referent is the inclusion of Gentiles as full co-heirs alongside Jews. This is "not made known to the sons of men in other generations" (3:5): it was not that the OT was silent about Gentile blessing (it was not; cf. Isa 49:6; 56:6–8; Gen 12:3), but that the *mode* of their inclusion — as full co-members *within* the people of God rather than as proselytes to Israel or as subordinate beneficiaries — was the hidden aspect.

The significance of 3:6's three *syn-* compounds is sometimes underappreciated:
- *Synklēronoma* — "fellow heirs" (not second-class heirs)
- *Syssōma* — "members of the same body" (not attached externally)
- *Symmetocha* — "partakers together" (not recipients of overflow)

All three use the prefix *syn-* (together/with), insisting on full equivalence. This is the content of the previously hidden mystery.

[[frank-thielman|Thielman]] reads the *mystērion* in Ephesians against Mitton's claim that the varying usage of *mystērion* across Colossians and Ephesians demonstrates different authors. Thielman argues the variability is unremarkable given the term's range in the undisputed Paulines (1 Cor 2:7; 15:51; Rom 11:25 each use it differently).

## The Mystery and the Powers

In Ephesians 3:10 the church's role is to make known "the manifold wisdom of God" to "the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places." The disclosure of the mystery is not only to human beings through gospel proclamation — it is also an announcement addressed to the cosmic powers. The unity of Jews and Gentiles in the church demonstrates to those powers that their sustaining of division is doomed to failure. This is a unique claim in the Pauline corpus: the church's existence is itself a cosmic proclamation. See [[principalities-powers|Rulers and Authorities in the Heavenly Places]].

## The Mystery and Apocalyptic

The *mystērion* concept reflects Paul's apocalyptic framework: God has acted decisively in Christ to inaugurate the age that was promised but hidden. The mystery was "set forth in Christ as a plan for the fullness of time" (Eph 1:10) — the disclosure belongs to the eschatological "now," the time of the gospel. Compare the role of Daniel's seer, who receives the revelation of the hidden plan for history's end: Paul too receives by revelation (3:3: "the mystery was made known to me by revelation") a disclosure about God's end-time purposes, and his apostolate is to make this disclosure public.
