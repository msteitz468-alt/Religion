# Holy Orders

**Holy Orders** is the sacrament that confers spiritual power for the Church's ministry — above all
the power to consecrate the [[transubstantiation|Eucharist]] — by the imposition of hands and the
handing on of the instruments of office. [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats it in the
[[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Supplement (XP Q34–Q40); with [[matrimony]] it is one of the two "social"
[[sacraments|sacraments]] ordered to the community rather than the individual.

## The sacrament and its character (XP Q34–Q35)

Order is a true sacrament conferring active spiritual power by a visible sign (a "seal"); its
**matter** is the instruments handed to the ordinand (e.g. the chalice and paten to a priest). It
confers **sanctifying grace** and imprints an indelible **[[sacraments|character]]** in *every* grade
(Aquinas's position against narrower views) — a participation in Christ's eternal
[[priesthood-of-christ|priesthood]], indelible even in one who returns to the lay state. The baptismal
character is a prerequisite for validity.

## Requirements, grades, and minister (XP Q36–Q38)

- **Requirements** (Q36): goodness of life is required by precept but not for validity; proportionate
  (not exhaustive) knowledge of Scripture is required. One ordained while in mortal sin receives Orders
  **validly** but sins, and sins again each time he exercises them in sin.
- **The seven orders** (Q37), all organized around the Eucharist: **priest, deacon, subdeacon**
  (the three *sacred* orders), then acolyte, exorcist, reader, doorkeeper. The character is imprinted
  at the handing of the relevant instrument under the bishop's words.
- **Minister** (Q38): the **bishop** ordinarily confers Orders; a priest may confer minor orders and
  Confirmation by papal delegation. **Heretics and schismatics validly confer Orders** (the indelible
  character again — the anti-Donatist principle), though recipients receive no grace and sin.

## Impediments to ordination (XP Q39)

Aquinas lists six impediments, distinguishing those that bar **validity** from those that bar only
**lawfulness** — recorded here exactly as the text states them:

- **Female sex** — an impediment to **validity** (women cannot validly receive Orders, on his account);
- **Lack of reason / boyhood** — to validity for the episcopate, to lawfulness for sacred orders;
- **Slavery** — to lawfulness only;
- **Homicide** — irregularity, to lawfulness;
- **Illegitimacy** — to lawfulness, dispensable;
- **Notable bodily defect** — to lawfulness.

## Episcopate, tonsure, and vestments (XP Q40)

The **episcopate** is an "order" in the wide sense of an office (it adds powers over the mystical body
— ordaining, confirming), but **not** a distinct sacramental order imprinting a new character, since
its proper acts do not exceed the priest's power over the Eucharist. The **tonsure** is a preamble,
not an order, and entails no renunciation of property. The Pope is above all bishops.

## Reception and divergence

This account underlies the Catholic and Orthodox theology of a sacramentally ordained, character-bearing
ministry in apostolic succession. The **Protestant** Reformers rejected Orders as a sacrament and the
sacrificing priesthood it implies, in favor of the priesthood of all believers and ministry as
office/function. The exclusion of women from valid ordination remains, for [[catholicism|Catholicism]]
and [[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodoxy]], a defining and contested position.
