# Śarīra-Śarīrī (Body-Soul Relation)

The **body-soul relation** (*śarīra-śarīrī bhāva*) is the structural cornerstone of
[[ramanuja|Ramanuja's]] [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] system and its primary
hermeneutical tool for resolving the tension between Upanishadic identity-texts and difference-texts.

## The Formal Definition

Ramanuja provides an explicit formal definition in the *Śrī-Bhāṣya*:

> "A body (*śarīra*) is any substance which a conscious being is able to control and support for its
> own purposes, and which stands in an entirely subordinate relation to that being."

The three criteria for being a body:
1. **Controllability**: the conscious being can direct it
2. **Supportability**: the conscious being sustains it in being
3. **Subordination (*śeṣatva*)**: it exists entirely for the conscious being's purposes; it has the
   character of a *remainder* or *accessory* (*śeṣa*) — it is not an end in itself

## Application to the Metaphysical Structure

Under this definition:
- **Souls (*cit*)** are the body of [[brahman|Brahman]]: Brahman controls, sustains, and is the inner
  Self (*antaryāmin*) of every soul
- **Matter (*acit*)** is the body of Brahman: non-sentient matter is directed and sustained by Brahman
  from within
- Both souls and matter are thus **inseparable from Brahman** — as a body cannot exist apart from its
  animating soul — yet both are **genuinely distinct** from Brahman as the controlled is from the controller

## The Hermeneutical Payoff

The body-soul relation allows Ramanuja to accept **all Upanishadic texts** at face value without
allegorization or a two-truths device:

| Text type | Example | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity texts** | *Tat tvam asi* ("That thou art") | The self (as Brahman's body/mode) is identical with Brahman in the sense that a body is non-separable from its soul — qualified identity, not absolute sameness |
| **Difference texts** | Individual soul worshipping Brahman | The controlled differs genuinely from the controller; souls are real distinct modes |

Both readings are simultaneously true because the **body-soul relation is both unitive and differentiating**:
a body is part of its soul's identity ("John's body") yet is genuinely other than the soul.

## Contrast with Advaita

[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita's]] reading of "That thou art" requires absolute identity of the individual
self with Brahman, which forces either (a) the difference-texts to be explained as lower-level truth
(*vyavahārika*) or (b) a two-truths framework in which the conventional world is real at one level
and unreal at another. [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]] rejects this as:
- Incoherent (it makes Brahman the locus of *avidyā*, which is impossible for pure consciousness)
- Hermeneutically unnecessary (the body-soul relation accommodates both text types without remainder)

## The Soul's Mode-Character (*Prakāra*)

The soul's relation to Brahman is specifically as a **mode (*prakāra*)** — an attribute that cannot be
realized apart from the substance it qualifies. The soul is a qualifying attribute of Brahman, real and
distinct, yet inseparable. This is why Ramanuja says the soul is a **part (*aṃśa*)** of Brahman — not
a fragment broken off (which would imply Brahman lost something) but a modal reality that only exists as
a specification of the whole.
