# Circumcision (Brit Milah)

Circumcision — Hebrew ***brit milah*** ("covenant of circumcision") — is the removal of the foreskin, instituted in **Genesis 17** as the perpetual sign of the covenant between God and the offspring of [[abraham|Abraham]], performed on the eighth day. It is the most physically inscribed of biblical commandments and one of the most theologically contested across the Abrahamic traditions: the defining covenant-marker of Judaism, the sign that Paul declared no longer necessary for Gentile believers (the central dispute of early Christianity), the practice (*khitan*) widely observed in Islam, and — in the [[zohar|Zohar]] — the bodily seal of the **covenant of [[tsaddiq|Yesod]]**, the sefirah of the holy covenant.

---

## Biblical Foundation

- **Genesis 17**: God commands Abraham, "Every male among you shall be circumcised… it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and you"; performed on the eighth day; the uncircumcised male is "cut off" from his people. Abraham (99) and [[ishmael|Ishmael]] (13) are circumcised that day; [[isaac|Isaac]] is circumcised on the eighth day (Gen 21:4).
- **Exodus 4:24–26**: the obscure "bridegroom of blood" episode (Zipporah circumcises her son).
- **Leviticus 12:3**: the eighth-day requirement codified.
- **Joshua 5**: the mass circumcision at Gilgal before entering the land.

The Hebrew Bible also speaks of the **"circumcision of the heart"** (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4) — an inward analogue that later traditions develop.

## Tradition-Specific Understanding

### Judaism
*Brit milah* is the foundational covenant sign and a defining boundary-marker of Jewish identity, performed on the eighth day in a ritual (with a *mohel*, a *sandak* who holds the child, and the "chair of [[elijah|Elijah]]"). It is so central that it overrides the Sabbath. Its non-negotiability intensified under persecution (Antiochus, Hadrian), when circumcision became a capital marker of fidelity.

### Christianity
The status of circumcision was **the first major controversy of the church** (Acts 15; Galatians; Romans 2–4). Paul argued that Gentiles need not be circumcised: Abraham was justified by faith *while still uncircumcised* (Rom 4:9–12), so circumcision is a "seal" of a righteousness already received by faith, not its precondition; what counts is "circumcision of the heart, by the Spirit" (Rom 2:29) and "a new creation" (Gal 6:15). In **Galatians 4** Paul allegorizes [[sarah|Sarah]]/Isaac (freedom, promise) against Hagar/[[ishmael|Ishmael]] (Sinai, slavery). Mainstream Christianity thus treats physical circumcision as a superseded sign fulfilled in baptism and inward renewal. (This is recorded across the wiki's Pauline pages — Galatians, Romans.)

### Islam
Circumcision (*khitan*) is not commanded in the Qur'an but is a near-universal practice grounded in the *sunnah* and the model of Ibrahim ([[abraham|Abraham]]); it is a sign of *fitrah* (natural religion) and of belonging to the community, though its timing varies widely.

## Kabbalistic Reading (the Zohar): the Covenant of Yesod

The [[zohar|Zohar]] (especially Parashat Lekh Lekha) gives circumcision its most exalted metaphysical reading: the mark in the flesh is the bodily seal of **[[tsaddiq|Yesod]]**, the ninth [[sefirot|sefirah]] (the Foundation, the covenant, the channel of divine flow to the [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]).

- **Circumcision opens the person to the divine influx**: "*Before Abraham was circumcised, one rung spoke with him* (the lower grade, *Maḥazeh/Shaddai*); *once he was circumcised, all those rungs rested upon him*." Only after circumcision could the full sefirotic reality "rest upon" Abraham — circumcision is the precondition for the higher revelation ("I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob through El Shaddai").
- **The sign that guards against the Other Side**: "Whoever bears this sign **does not descend to Hell** if he preserves it properly — not inserting it into a foreign domain or betraying the name of the King." To misuse the covenant (illicit union, wasted seed) is "to betray the name of the blessed Holy One" — the gravest feeding of the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]. Thus circumcision and **sexual holiness** are one: the guarding of Yesod (the model is [[joseph|Joseph the Tsaddiq]]).
- **Two stages, *milah* and *peri'ah***: the Zohar reads the cutting (*milah*) and the uncovering (*peri'ah*) as corresponding to grades of revelation — and distinguishes the complete Israelite circumcision from [[ishmael|Ishmael]]'s, which attaches to a lower, outer grade.
- **The eighth day**: the eighth day binds the child to the sefirotic realm "above the seven" (the seven lower sefirot), aligning the infant with the supernal source.
- **[[elijah|Elijah]] at every circumcision**: because Elijah is the deathless guardian of the covenant (Yesod), he is present — in his "chair" — at every *brit*, witnessing Israel's fidelity (see [[elijah|Elijah]]).

## Cross-Tradition Comparison

The three traditions' divergent valuations of one rite map their deeper differences:
- **Judaism**: the irreducible, embodied covenant sign — identity, boundary, obedience.
- **Christianity**: a sign now **internalized and superseded** — "circumcision of the heart," baptism, new creation; the test case for the inclusion of the Gentiles apart from the Law.
- **Islam**: a sign of *fitrah* and Abrahamic continuity, customary rather than scriptural-legal.
- **Kabbalah**: not merely a sign *of* the covenant but a **theurgical act** that opens the person to the sefirot, guards Yesod against the demonic, and binds the body to the divine structure — the sharpest contrast with the Christian move toward interiorization, since the Zohar makes the *physical* mark cosmically efficacious.
