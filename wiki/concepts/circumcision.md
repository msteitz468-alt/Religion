# Circumcision (Brit Milah)

Circumcision — Hebrew ***brit milah*** ("covenant of circumcision") — is the removal of the foreskin, instituted in **Genesis 17** as the perpetual sign of the covenant between God and the offspring of [[abraham|Abraham]], performed on the eighth day. It is the most physically inscribed of biblical commandments and one of the most theologically contested across the Abrahamic traditions: the defining covenant-marker of Judaism, the sign that Paul declared no longer necessary for Gentile believers (the central dispute of early Christianity), the practice (*khitan*) widely observed in Islam, and — in the [[zohar|Zohar]] — the bodily seal of the **covenant of [[tsaddiq|Yesod]]**, the sefirah of the holy covenant.

---

## Biblical Foundation

- **Genesis 17**: God commands Abraham, "Every male among you shall be circumcised… it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and you"; performed on the eighth day; the uncircumcised male is "cut off" from his people. Abraham (99) and [[ishmael|Ishmael]] (13) are circumcised that day; [[isaac|Isaac]] is circumcised on the eighth day (Gen 21:4).
- **Exodus 4:24–26**: the obscure "bridegroom of blood" episode (Zipporah circumcises her son).
- **Leviticus 12:3**: the eighth-day requirement codified.
- **Joshua 5**: the mass circumcision at Gilgal before entering the land.

The Hebrew Bible also speaks of the **"circumcision of the heart"** (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4) — an inward analogue that later traditions develop.

## Bruckner (UBCS Exodus) — Exod 4:24–26: the sine qua non (reingest 2026-07-18)

Attributed to [[james-bruckner|Bruckner]]; source page [[bruckner-exodus-ubcs|Bruckner, Exodus (UBCS)]].

On the Lord's night assault and Zipporah's act: "The text tells us that circumcision is the sine qua non. Moses cannot lead the people without a circumcised family." Zipporah "proved her commitment to the God of Abraham who commanded circumcision as an 'everlasting covenant' (Gen. 17:1–14)"; her act "resolved the ambiguity of Moses' identity." Bruckner reads the episode's rhetoric as deliberately unsettling — "we encounter God's complete freedom to act in what seems to us a crazy way... Even Moses cannot presume upon God" — placed as a check against arrogance just before Moses announces the firstborn deaths. He surveys the ambiguities (who was attacked; "feet" as euphemism) via Houtman and Durham without adjudicating. Related wordplay: "faltering lips" (6:12, 30) is literally "uncircumcised lips," which he takes as probably "undisciplined" or "untrained."

## Hamilton (NICOT) — Gen 17: sign/amen, not condition of the covenant

Attributed to [[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]], *Genesis 1–17* (NICOT); source page [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot]]. Related covenant claims on [[covenant|Covenant]]. Densified 2026-07-18 reingest.

### Religious infant rite; not marriage-entry; no magic fertility

Covenant and circumcision law both eternal. Circumcision long predated Israel (Egypt, Edom, Ammon, Moab in Jer 9; Philistines alone “uncircumcised”; not Babylonian/Assyrian; Megiddo ivory; Tell el-Judeideh bronzes ~2800 B.C.). Gen 34 / Josh 5 show social/puberty contexts; **Gen 17’s reason is religious, not social** — “Gen. 17 nowhere suggests that circumcision was an essential prerequisite for marriage into the tribe” (R6 / Gen 17:9–14). Eight-day infant rite: social/puberty rite transformed into religious mark; Hebrews alone link a covenant *from God* with circumcision. Juxtaposition of covenant and circumcision: **no automatic connection between the rite and fertility** — “It is the covenant, and not circumcision per se, that makes fertility possible. Accordingly, circumcision does not have any kind of mythic or magical significance” (R6). Extension to houseborn and purchased slaves expands recipients to households, not elite sons — “Hierarchialism gives way to egalitarianism” (R6).

### Sign (*ʾôṯ*): confirmation / amen parallel to 15:6 — not condition establishing the covenant

Called both “my covenant” (synecdoche for the obligation to keep) and mark/sign (*ʾôṯ*). Hamilton surveys Genesis signs (1:14 seasons; 4:15 Cain identity; 9 rainbow mnemonic for God). Circumcision does **not** identify Israelites to outsiders (many nations circumcise; Ishmael receives the sign yet is not of the Isaac-perpetuated covenant line; clothing conceals it). Weighs mnemonic-for-self vs sign-for-God (rainbow parallel); *kareth* ultimatum for man favors not pure God-cognition. **Position:**

> “We take the position that circumcision is a means by which Abraham and his seed ratify God’s lordship over them. It is their identity sign as God’s covenant people. … Accordingly, Abraham’s circumcision is as much an amen to Yahweh as was his affirmation in 15:6.” (R6 / Gen 17:9–14)

Within Hamilton’s wider Gen 15+17 reading (one unilateral covenant reaffirmed, not replaced — see [[covenant|Covenant]]), the rite is **response/confirmation of faith**, not the ground that establishes or authenticates the covenant. *Eternal* language thrice in ch. 17 keeps stipulations (including circumcise) from converting the covenant into a bilateral pact (R6 / Gen 17:7–8).

### *Kareth*: cut or be cut off

Wordplay: he that is not cut (circumcised) will be cut off. Omission nullifies the covenant (*pārar* Hiphil *berît* — most frequent phrase for human covenant violation). *Kareth* usually for acts of commission; circumcision is one of only two *performative omissions* with *kareth* (other: corpse uncleanness, Num 19). Hamilton inclined to premature death by God (with possible preliminary excommunication not itself *kārēṯ*), not mere social ostracism alone. Breach specified is **omitting the ritual**, not failing the “walk… blameless” of v. 1 (R6 / Gen 17:14). Accountability of an eight-day-old vs parents left as an open question in the commentary.

### Execution; Ishmael receives the sign without the covenant line

Abraham (99), Ishmael (13), and every household male circumcised the same day — faith is done, not only cerebral; mark ineradicable. Ishmael deliberately not the covenant heir like Isaac, yet receives the sign: “He too is to walk as one of Yahweh’s children.” Against late-exilic invention of circumcision as Jewish distinctiveness badge, Hamilton cites Weinfeld: who in exile would share that symbol with the Ishmaelites? More likely reinforces Abraham as father of many nations (R6 / Gen 17:23–27).

### NT appropriation (Rom 4 and related)

Hamilton: NT persons circumcised include Jesus, John, Paul, Timothy; also spiritual circumcision (Phil 3:3; Col 2:11–13; Rom 2:28–29). 1 Cor 7:19; Gal 5:6: neither circumcision nor uncircumcision ultimately decisive. Paul’s misgivings target contemporary distortion making the rite end-in-itself, not the OT institution (God’s idea); Acts 15 removes it as church-membership requirement. **Rom 4:9–12:** “The value in circumcision is that it was, on Abraham’s part, a sign or seal of the righteousness God has already given him” — so circumcised and uncircumcised believers claim Abraham as father (R6). OT already internalizes: heart circumcision (Lev 26:41; Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4; Ezek 44:7, 9). Functional equation of infant baptism with circumcision is common among Christians, but the NT never simply equates them; Col 2:8–12 pairs baptism with circumcision *made without hands* (*acheiropoiḗtos* = God’s doing) without settling the infant-baptism debate; absence of a plain Acts 15 “baptism replaces circumcision” may suggest the early church did not cast baptism as simple supplanter (R6).

### Contrast: Gen 34 (not Gen 17)

In the Dinah–Shechem episode, circumcision as proposed by Jacob’s sons is tribal marriage-initiation / duplicity, **not** the Gen 17 covenant sign: “Obviously circumcision, as proposed herein, has nothing in common with the circumcision emphasis we encountered in Gen. 17. Here circumcision is not a sign of the covenant” ([[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|Hamilton 18–50]] / Gen 34; R4).

## Tradition-Specific Understanding

### Judaism
*Brit milah* is the foundational covenant sign and a defining boundary-marker of Jewish identity, performed on the eighth day in a ritual (with a *mohel*, a *sandak* who holds the child, and the "chair of [[elijah|Elijah]]"). It is so central that it overrides the Sabbath. Its non-negotiability intensified under persecution (Antiochus, Hadrian), when circumcision became a capital marker of fidelity.

### Christianity
The status of circumcision was **the first major controversy of the church** (Acts 15; Galatians; Romans 2–4). Paul argued that Gentiles need not be circumcised: Abraham was justified by faith *while still uncircumcised* (Rom 4:9–12), so circumcision is a "seal" of a righteousness already received by faith, not its precondition; what counts is "circumcision of the heart, by the Spirit" (Rom 2:29) and "a new creation" (Gal 6:15). In **Galatians 4** Paul allegorizes [[sarah|Sarah]]/Isaac (freedom, promise) against Hagar/[[ishmael|Ishmael]] (Sinai, slavery). Mainstream Christianity thus treats physical circumcision as a superseded sign fulfilled in baptism and inward renewal. (This is recorded across the wiki's Pauline pages — Galatians, Romans.)

### Islam
Circumcision (*khitan*) is not commanded in the Qur'an but is a near-universal practice grounded in the *sunnah* and the model of Ibrahim ([[abraham|Abraham]]); it is a sign of *fitrah* (natural religion) and of belonging to the community, though its timing varies widely.

## Kabbalistic Reading (the Zohar): the Covenant of Yesod

The [[zohar|Zohar]] (especially Parashat Lekh Lekha) gives circumcision its most exalted metaphysical reading: the mark in the flesh is the bodily seal of **[[tsaddiq|Yesod]]**, the ninth [[sefirot|sefirah]] (the Foundation, the covenant, the channel of divine flow to the [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]).

- **Circumcision opens the person to the divine influx**: "*Before Abraham was circumcised, one rung spoke with him* (the lower grade, *Maḥazeh/Shaddai*); *once he was circumcised, all those rungs rested upon him*." Only after circumcision could the full sefirotic reality "rest upon" Abraham — circumcision is the precondition for the higher revelation ("I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob through El Shaddai").
- **The sign that guards against the Other Side**: "Whoever bears this sign **does not descend to Hell** if he preserves it properly — not inserting it into a foreign domain or betraying the name of the King." To misuse the covenant (illicit union, wasted seed) is "to betray the name of the blessed Holy One" — the gravest feeding of the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]. Thus circumcision and **sexual holiness** are one: the guarding of Yesod (the model is [[joseph|Joseph the Tsaddiq]]).
- **Two stages, *milah* and *peri'ah***: the Zohar reads the cutting (*milah*) and the uncovering (*peri'ah*) as corresponding to grades of revelation — and distinguishes the complete Israelite circumcision from [[ishmael|Ishmael]]'s, which attaches to a lower, outer grade.
- **The eighth day**: the eighth day binds the child to the sefirotic realm "above the seven" (the seven lower sefirot), aligning the infant with the supernal source.
- **[[elijah|Elijah]] at every circumcision**: because Elijah is the deathless guardian of the covenant (Yesod), he is present — in his "chair" — at every *brit*, witnessing Israel's fidelity (see [[elijah|Elijah]]).

Altar of earth with vessel of earth for *brit*; convert as “altar of stones” must not remain hewn/hard (1:95a–95b).

### Inherit the land / Matronita via *brit*

Opening of Va-Yeḥi (Isa 60:21) makes circumcision the gateway to Yesod and to the land as Matronita:

> "they will inherit the land forever—they are worthy of **inheriting the Matronita**… Because they have been **circumcised**. … Whoever is circumcised and enters this heritage and guards this covenant, enters and cleaves to the body of the King and enters this **Righteous One**. Thereby they are called righteous… inherit the land forever." (Zohar 1:216b; Pritzker III)

*Et Joseph* blessing: *et* = sign of covenant Joseph guarded → called Righteous (1:227b–228a). Oath "hand under my thigh" = covenant on the flesh; "essence of the patriarchs… mystery of **Joseph**"; Abraham and Jacob swear thus; Isaac does not (Esau issued) (1:222b–223a). Hand under thigh earlier (Isa 52:13 servant midrash): "Righteous One, mystery of circumcision, sustaining pillar of worlds… to revive dwellers of the dust" (1:181b). End of days sought so Israel would be protected and **purified from foreskin** (1:235a).

### Shechem typology; *zot* vs impure; Simeon–Levi *sod*

Va-Yishlaḥ: Dinah–Shechem episode as impure-side attack after the serpent-thigh; **circumcision blood then slaughter** paralleled with Egypt (blood then firstborn); Simeon and Levi (severe Judgment) vs Hamor/Shechem so ox and donkey not join (Dt 22:10) (Zohar 1:172a–173a; Pritzker III). Mi-Qets: three days under guard = Shechem’s three days; *zot* (covenant) vs Shechem (1:200a–b). Va-Yeḥi on Simeon–Levi: *Sod YHVH* = holy covenant; they troubled it at **Shechem** (deceitfully) and Zimri rendered it unfit; *May my soul never enter their secret* — soul uniting with covenant above, "bundle of life" (1:236a–b). Sword on thigh = guard the covenant before war/commerce (Zebulun) (1:240b).

### Joseph’s covenant as life of worlds; double ark

Joseph crowned Righteous only after guarding the sealed covenant (Potiphar) (1:194b). "Still alive" = abided by holy Covenant called Life of the Worlds (1:211a–b). Double *aron*: ark of the covenant above inherited only by one who preserves the covenant; Joseph preserved covenant below and Covenant above (1:251a). Salt of covenant: world cannot bear without it (*Were it not for My covenant day and night…*, Jer 33:25) (1:241b–242a zone). Only covenant-preservers are called righteous (1:247b).

### Passover: two bloods; *ha-petaḥ*; circumcised but not uncovered (Bo)

> "Until a man is circumcised, he is obstructed and closed on every side. Once he is circumcised, he is opened totally." (Zohar 2:36a; Pritzker IV)

Land entry: circumcised **and** uncovered → bread without scarcity; *You will not lack kol, all* (2:40b).

Rainbow: gazing at rainbow above (colors of Shekhinah) or at covenant sign below shames above — fathers’ oath “under thigh” not “look under” (2:66b–67a). Carved image / unauthorized Torah // betrayal of covenant (intermarriage/alien domain) = betrayal of Name; first link circumcised / bond of Shekhinah (2:87a–87b). *Anokhi* letter ethics: *Yod* includes Torah / circumcision / tefillin among twelve encompassing commandments (2:91a–91b).

## The Metaphorical "Uncircumcised" in Ezekiel (Block)

Daniel Block ([[block-ezekiel-25-48-nicot|Block, Ezekiel 25–48 (NICOT)]]) highlights
Ezekiel's distinctive *metaphorical* use of uncircumcision in the death oracles:

- **Ezek 32:19–32** consigns Egypt to Sheol "among the uncircumcised" — which Block finds
  "remarkable, given the fact that they (like the Edomites and Sidonians from this list)
  practiced the rite of circumcision." The usage is "obviously metaphorical": in Israel
  circumcision meant covenant membership, and uncircumcision "meant exclusion from the family
  grave, hence consignment to the most undesirable compartment of the netherworld" (see
  [[sheol|Sheol]]). Similarly the prince of Tyre dies "the death of the uncircumcised"
  (28:10), though Block notes the Phoenicians too practiced the rite.
- **Ezek 44:7–9** bars from the future temple foreigners "uncircumcised of heart and
  uncircumcised of body" — Block traces *ʿarĕlê lēb* to Lev 26:41 (with the Jer 9:25
  parallel), joining the physical rite to the "circumcision of the heart" strand noted above.

## Cross-Tradition Comparison

The three traditions' divergent valuations of one rite map their deeper differences:
- **Judaism**: the irreducible, embodied covenant sign — identity, boundary, obedience.
- **Christianity**: a sign now **internalized and superseded** — "circumcision of the heart," baptism, new creation; the test case for the inclusion of the Gentiles apart from the Law.
- **Islam**: a sign of *fitrah* and Abrahamic continuity, customary rather than scriptural-legal.
- **Kabbalah**: not merely a sign *of* the covenant but a **theurgical act** that opens the person to the sefirot, guards Yesod against the demonic, and binds the body to the divine structure — the sharpest contrast with the Christian move toward interiorization, since the Zohar makes the *physical* mark cosmically efficacious.

## In Acts, on Bock's reading (BECNT)

*Added 2026-07-18 from the ingest of [[darrell-bock|Bock's]] BECNT [[acts|Acts]]. All interpretive
claims are [[darrell-bock|Darrell Bock]]'s.*

Bock treats circumcision as the fault-line of the church's first great controversy, resolved at the
[[jerusalem-council|Jerusalem consultation]] (Acts 15). There the demand that Gentiles be
circumcised and keep the law is, on Bock's reading, **"emphatically refuted"** as a requirement for
salvation: Peter calls it "testing God" and a **"yoke" (*zygos*)** that "no one, not even Jews," has
been able to bear (15:10), and James rules that the church should stop troubling the Gentiles
(15:19). Bock reads the "yoke" not as a critique of the law itself but as "an inability that the law
fosters" (cf. Rom. 7). The upshot: **Gentiles are included uncircumcised** — already dramatized when
the Spirit falls on the uncircumcised [[cornelius|Cornelius]] (10:44–48; cf. the objection at 11:3,
"you ... ate with" uncircumcised men).

Bock reads Paul's own practice as consistent with this, turning on the distinction between
**strategy and principle**:

- **[[timothy|Timothy]] circumcised (strategy).** Paul circumcises Timothy (16:3) "out of
  sensitivity to the Jews," because Timothy has a Jewish mother and a Greek father; the issue "is
  mixed ancestry, not Gentile inclusion" — a matter of removing needless offense in a mixed
  community (1 Cor. 9:19–23), not of requiring the rite. Bock: "Paul is not standing up for the law
  ... but is affirming Jewish heritage alongside Gentile heritage."
- **[[titus|Titus]] not circumcised (principle).** Titus, a Gentile of unmixed ancestry, is "a
  different case and principle. He is not to be circumcised" (Gal. 2:3–5) — where the principle of
  Gentile freedom is at stake, Paul refuses. Bock: knowing "which principles are worth standing up
  for and which ... are not worth elevating ... is a sign of discernment."

The later rumor that Paul teaches diaspora Jews "to forsake Moses ... not to circumcise their
children" (21:21) Bock judges false: "the likelihood is that Paul does not teach nonobservance, but
neither does he insist on observance where Gentiles are involved." The relation of this reading to
the Paul of the epistles is recorded on [[acts-paul-tensions|Acts and the Paul of the Epistles]].
