# The Tuatha Dé Danann

The **Tuatha Dé Danann** — "the people of the goddess **Danu**" (also *Tuatha Dé*, "the tribe of the gods") —
are the **divine race of Irish [[celtic-religion|Celtic]] mythology**, the Gaelic pantheon remembered in
euhemerized form. In the medieval *Lebor Gabála Érenn* ("Book of Invasions") they are the **fifth people to
settle Ireland**, arriving "in dark clouds" out of the northern isles, masters of **druidry, craft, and
magic**.

## The arc of their reign

- They **defeat the Fir Bolg** at the **First Battle of Mag Tuired**, where their king **[[nuada|Nuada]]**
  loses a hand — and with it, by the law of sovereignty, his kingship — until **[[dian-cecht|Dian Cécht]]**
  fashions him a **silver hand** ("Nuada Airgetlám").
- Under the half-Fomorian tyrant **Bres**, and then under the all-skilled **[[lugh|Lugh]]**, they overthrow
  the **Fomorians** — chaotic sea-powers — at the **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**, where Lugh kills the
  one-eyed **[[balor|Balor]]**, his own grandfather.
- They are finally **defeated by the Milesians (the Gaels)** and, by treaty, **withdraw into the
  [[the-otherworld|Otherworld]]** — the **síde** or fairy mounds — becoming the *aes síde*, the fairy-folk of
  later Irish tradition.

## The principal gods

**[[the-dagda|The Dagda]]** (the "Good God," father-figure with cauldron and life-death club),
**[[lugh|Lugh]]** (light, skill, kingship), **[[nuada|Nuada]]** (sovereignty), the **[[morrigan|Morrígan]]**
(war and fate, often a triad), **[[brigid|Brigid]]** (poetry, smithcraft, healing — a triple goddess),
**[[dian-cecht|Dian Cécht]]** (medicine), **Goibniu** (smithing), **[[manannan|Manannán mac Lir]]** (the sea
and the Otherworld), and **Ogma** (eloquence, the ogham script).

The Tuatha Dé are the Irish counterpart of the Welsh **children of Dôn** (Dôn ≈ Danu) and an Indo-European
cousin of the Norse and Vedic pantheons.
