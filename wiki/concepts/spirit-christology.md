# Spirit Christology

"Spirit Christology" is Kelly's label for what he identifies as the dominant early way of conceiving the incarnation: "the view that in the historical Jesus Christ the pre-existent Son of God, Who is divine spirit, united Himself with human nature." Here "spirit" (*pneuma*) names not the third Trinitarian Person but the *divine nature* of the pre-existent Christ. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. VI.

## Attestation

- **'Barnabas'**: the Son of God "manifested himself" in flesh, the body being "the receptacle of spirit."
- **Hermas**: God "caused the holy, pre-existent spirit which created the whole of creation to dwell in flesh."
- **2 Clement**: "Christ the Lord, Who saved us, being first of all spirit, became flesh."
- **Aristides**: "this Son of the most high God descended from heaven as holy spirit (*en pneumati hagiō*) and took flesh from a virgin."
- **Cyprian**: God's Son "descended into the Virgin and as holy spirit clothed Himself with flesh."
- [[hippolytus]] and [[tertullian]] are explicit exponents; the Roman bishop **Callistus** said what became incarnate of the Virgin was "holy spirit."

## The Creed and Luke 1:35

Kelly connects the pattern to the Old Roman Creed's clause "**who was born from holy spirit and the Virgin Mary**," and to a widespread early exegesis of Luke 1:35 that equated "the holy spirit" and "the power of the Most High" coming upon Mary **not** with the third Person but with the pre-existent Christ himself, about to incarnate in her womb. The framework receded as Trinitarian vocabulary matured and "spirit" came to be reserved for the third Person — leaving the [[logos-christology|Logos Christology]] of the Apologists as the dominant model.
