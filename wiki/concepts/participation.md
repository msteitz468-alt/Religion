## Definition

"Participation" (*methexis*; Latin *participatio*) names the relationship by which creatures depend on and share in the perfections of God. Ayres treats the term as "a cipher for a wide range of terminology in Greek and Latin" (*Nicaea and Its Legacy*), rather than as a single technical concept, and warns against assuming that any one ancient author's use maps cleanly onto another's.

## Pro-Nicene Grammars of Participation (Ayres, Interlude I, Ch. 12)

Ayres proposes that pro-Nicene theologians developed two distinct "grammars of participation," each governed by the same commitment to divine incomprehensibility.

### Grammar 1 — Within the Godhead (ruled out)

At the beginning of the fourth century many theologians spoke of the Son "participating" in the Father's attributes. Pro-Nicene theology eliminates this usage: "Son and Spirit can no longer be said to possess their attributes by sharing in the Father's attributes: generation and 'spiration' result in divine persons who have the fullness of the Godhead in themselves. Any terminology of participation that indicates possession of an attribute by dependence on that attribute's true source is ruled out". The Son is not less-than-fully divine by virtue of deriving from the Father; he possesses the fullness of the simple Godhead.

### Grammar 2 — Creator and Creation

Participation language is retained — and receives "a particular pro-Nicene cast" — for the relationship between God and the created order. Key features:

- Creation "shares in and imitates divine qualities and modes of existence in a variety of ways consequent on its ordered division".
- The human soul "occupies a special place in imitating the divine nature: but it is essential to note how, for pro-Nicenes, the mysteriousness of the soul is itself part of its imitation of the divine nature".
- "At all levels the creation contains in itself a relation to the Creator that constitutes the core of its mysteriousness. Insofar as the creation functions as a semiotic structure pointing to the Creator it should inculcate a sense of our failure to comprehend the Creator's nature".
- The character of the Logos's presence to the world "cannot be conceived on the basis of any created analogy, nor can the character of the Logos's 'possession' in perfect form of any of those goods we know in the world".

### The Role of Incomprehensibility

In both grammars, pro-Nicenes agree that "the mode of participation under discussion is beyond full human comprehension". Discourse about participation must be shaped by the same austere habits that govern all speech about God — attentive to scriptural language, honest about its failure to deliver comprehension, and oriented toward the anagogic journey of the soul.

## Burrell's Parallel (as used by Ayres)

Ayres cites David Burrell's account of Aquinas to illuminate what pro-Nicenes were doing: "The role of participation, then, is to remind us that there could be no such set of [analogical] terms were the universe itself not derived from a source from which all that is, and notably what is perfect about what-is, flows" (Burrell). For Burrell (and by analogy for pro-Nicenes), participation is not primarily a metaphysical description to be comprehended, but the ontological ground that makes analogical predication about God both possible and always incomplete. Ayres endorses this as a framework for further investigation of pro-Nicene cosmology.

## Key Author/Text Connections

- [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]]: creation's mysterious natures intentionally reflect God's unitary nature; the soul's mysteriousness is itself an imitation of the divine.
- [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil of Caesarea]]: Hexameron presents creation as "a semiotic structure" pointing to the incomprehensible Creator (*Nicaea and Its Legacy*, Ch. 12).
- [[augustine|Augustine]]: things possess stability only because of their ordering "in" the Word; the soul exists as a semiotic chain between material things and the Triune God.
- [[origen|Origen]]: earlier usage of participation — including the Son's participation in the Father — that pro-Nicene theology curtails.
