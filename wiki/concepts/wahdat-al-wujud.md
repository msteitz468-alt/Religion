# Wahdat al-Wujud (the Unity of Being)

**Wahdat al-wujud** ("the oneness/unity of being" or "of existence") is the central metaphysical
doctrine of the **Akbarian** school of [[sufism|Sufism]] descending from **[[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]]**
(d. 638/1240). In its strongest formulation there is, in reality, **only one Being** — the Real
(*al-Haqq*) — and everything else "exists" only as a self-disclosure (*tajalli*) of that single
Being. It is the most influential and the most fiercely contested idea in Sufi thought.

## The Doctrine

- Ibn Arabi himself does not use the phrase as a slogan; **the term was systematized by his
  followers** (notably Sadr al-Din al-Qunawi and Abd al-Razzaq al-Qashani). What Ibn Arabi teaches in
  the [[fusus-al-hikam|Fusus al-Hikam]] is that *wujud* (Being/existence) belongs properly to God
  alone; creatures are not independent beings but **loci of manifestation** for the divine Names.
- The cosmos is the Real's **perpetual self-disclosure**: God, the "Hidden Treasure," makes himself
  known through an unceasing theophany. Created things are the **fixed essences** (*a'yan thabita*) —
  the eternal archetypes of all possibles within the divine knowledge — receiving existence as a
  mirror receives an image.
- Hence the famous paradox: the world is "**He / not-He**" (*huwa la huwa*) — neither simply identical
  with God (which would be crude pantheism) nor simply other than him.

## The Interpretive Dispute

Whether *wahdat al-wujud* is **pantheism, panentheism, or an existential "unity of witnessing"** is
the central question of its reception:

- **Critics** — above all the Hanbali **Ibn Taymiyya** — read it as *ittihad*/*hulul* (identification
  or indwelling), a monism that erases the Creator–creature distinction and amounts to unbelief.
- **Defenders** read it as a description of *spiritual experience and ontological dependence*, not a
  claim that the cosmos *is* God; the creature's nothingness-in-itself, not its divinity, is the
  point.
- **Ahmad Sirhindi** (the Naqshbandi "Renewer," d. 1624) offered the influential corrective
  **wahdat al-shuhud** ("the oneness of *witnessing*"): the unity is a state of the mystic's
  perception, not a fact about being — God and world remain ontologically distinct.

The wiki records these as standing positions **without adjudication** (Contradiction Protocol).

## Relation to Other Doctrines

*Wahdat al-wujud* is the metaphysical ground of the **[[al-insan-al-kamil|Perfect Man]]** (the
all-comprehensive locus of the Names) and is inseparable from Ibn Arabi's balancing of
**[[tanzih-tashbih|tanzih and tashbih]]**: precisely because there is one Being disclosed in all
forms, God is both utterly transcendent *and* manifest in everything. It stands at the opposite pole
from the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazilite]] insistence on pure transcendence.
