# Varnas (the Four Social Classes)

The **varnas** are the four **hereditary social classes** of traditional Hindu society, their canonical origin
in the **Puruṣa-sūkta** (Ṛgveda 10.90), the hymn of the **Cosmic Man** (*Puruṣa*) whose sacrificial
dismemberment produces the cosmos, the gods — and the social order:

| Varna | Part of Puruṣa | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Brāhmaṇa** (Brahmin) | **mouth** | priests, teachers, scholars |
| **Kṣatriya** (Kshatriya) | **arms** | warriors, rulers, kings |
| **Vaiśya** (Vaishya) | **thighs** | farmers, merchants, herders |
| **Śūdra** (Shudra) | **feet** | servants, laborers |

The name *varna* means **"color"** or "quality" — a classification by nature/quality, not just occupation or
birth, though birth and occupation became the operative criteria in practice.

## Doniger's Social Reading

[[wendy-doniger|Doniger]] (*[[doniger-the-hindus|The Hindus]]*, Ch. 5) reads the Puruṣa-sūkta as a
**social document** as much as a cosmological one. The hymn was composed at a moment when the **four-class
system was already a lived social fact**, and the sacrificial origin myth was brought in to *naturalize* and
*sacralize* it — to make the hierarchy appear as inevitable as the creation of the world. This is a
**hermeneutic-of-suspicion** reading: not "here is the origin of the four classes" (the text's self-
presentation) but "here is the text that locked in a pre-existing social arrangement by giving it cosmic
warrant."

In Doniger's "follow the horse / follow the cow" framework: the Brahmin is paired with the **cow** (purity,
vegetarianism); the Kshatriya with the **horse** (royal power, the imported foreign prestige animal); the
Shudra with the **dog** (pollution, the excluded). These animal pairings run through the full history of
Hindu caste ideology.

## Varna vs. Jāti

The *varna* system (four theoretical classes) is distinct from the *jāti* system (thousands of **birth-
communities**, the practical caste system). Varna is the scriptural/ideological framework; jāti is the lived
social reality. Doniger (not in this scope but noted as background) distinguishes them: the Vedic texts
operate with *varna*; the complex of practical caste belongs to a later elaboration. The two systems are
routinely conflated in popular usage and by some scholars.
