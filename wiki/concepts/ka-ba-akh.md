# The Egyptian Soul (Ka, Ba, Akh)

[[egyptian-religion|Ancient Egyptian]] thought conceived the human being as a composite of several distinct
**spiritual parts**, all of which had to be sustained for survival in the afterlife — one reason for the
elaborate care of the body and tomb.

## The Parts of the Person

- **Ka** — the **life-force / vital essence**, the "double" of a person, created at birth and surviving
  death; it required continued nourishment (hence food offerings at the tomb). To die was "to go to one's ka."
- **Ba** — the **personality / soul** that makes a person an individual, often shown as a human-headed bird.
  The Ba could **leave the tomb by day** and move about, returning to the body at night.
- **Akh** — the **transfigured, effective spirit** of the successful dead — the immortal, glorified form a
  person becomes after the Ka and Ba are reunited and the judgment is passed, dwelling among the gods and
  stars.
- Also the **name (ren)**, the **shadow (sheut)**, and above all the preserved **body (mummy)** — all needed
  for the whole person to live on.

## Significance

This multipart soul explains the **logic of Egyptian funerary religion**: mummification preserved the body
for the Ba and Ka; tomb offerings fed the Ka; the spells of the [[book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]] guided
the parts safely to reunion as an **Akh**. The Egyptian afterlife was not the survival of a single "soul" but
the **reintegration of the whole person** beyond death.
