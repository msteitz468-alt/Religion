# Matrimony

**Matrimony** is, for [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]], at once an **office of nature** (ordered to the
begetting and rearing of children) and a **sacrament of the New Law** ([[summa-theologica|*ST*]]
Supplement XP Q42–Q68). It is the most extensively treated topic of the Supplement, fusing
sacramental theology with the canon law of marriage. With [[holy-orders]] it is one of the two
"social" [[sacraments|sacraments]].

## Marriage as a sacrament; consent (XP Q42–Q48)

- **The sacrament** (Q42): the **form** is the **words of consent** exchanged by the spouses, *not*
  the priest's blessing; grace is conferred **dispositively** through the bond. Aquinas notes the
  three-opinion debate (Lombard / Albert / the Bonaventure–Aquinas view) on how marriage confers grace.
- **Consent makes the marriage** (Q45–Q48): the efficient cause is the **present-tense, inward, freely
  expressed consent** of the parties — **not** carnal intercourse (consummation perfects but does not
  constitute the marriage; this is why the marriage of [[mary|Mary]] and Joseph was true though
  unconsummated). **Compulsory consent** — fear "such as would move a constant man" — voids the
  marriage; conditional consent is governed by the nature of the condition. **Betrothal** (Q43) is a
  future-tense promise, not yet marriage, possible from the age of seven.

## The three goods of marriage (XP Q49)

Following Augustine, Aquinas grounds the goodness of marriage in three **goods** (*bona matrimonii*),
which "excuse" the act from sin:

1. **Offspring** (*proles*) — the begetting and, above all, the *rearing* of children for the worship
   of God;
2. **Fidelity** (*fides*) — the mutual exclusive faith of the spouses;
3. **Sacrament** (*sacramentum*) — the **indissoluble bond**, signifying the union of Christ and the
   Church, which makes Christian marriage permanent.

## Impediments (XP Q50–Q62)

Aquinas catalogues the canonical impediments, distinguishing those that bar **validity** from those
that bar only **lawfulness**: error, the **condition of slavery** (a slave may marry without the
master's consent), **vows and orders** (a *solemn* vow even dissolves a not-yet-consummated marriage),
**consanguinity** (then reckoned to the fourth degree), **affinity**, public honesty, **spiritual
relationship** (from Baptism and Confirmation — godparenthood), **legal relationship** (adoption),
impotence, and defective age. **Disparity of worship** (Q59) — marriage to an unbeliever — voids the
sacrament (though marriage between two unbelievers is a true natural, non-sacramental marriage); here
Aquinas expounds the **Pauline privilege**: a convert's marriage to an unbeliever may be dissolved
(the believer free to remarry) if the unbeliever "blasphemes and tries to pervert" the believer.

## Dissolution, polygamy, divorce (XP Q60–Q68)

- **Wife-murder** and **second marriages** (digamy, lawful but with the nuptial blessing omitted and an
  irregularity for orders).
- **Plurality of wives** (Q65) — Aquinas's central analysis: **polygamy is against the *secondary* but
  not the *primary* precepts of natural law**, and so the OT patriarchs' plural marriages were licit
  **only by special divine dispensation** (interior inspiration), to multiply offspring for God's
  worship — a dispensation abrogated by Christ's spiritual propagation of the Church. **Concubinage**,
  by contrast, is against the *first* precepts of natural law (it hinders the rearing of offspring) and
  was **never** licit by any dispensation; the patriarchs' "concubines" were true wives as to the
  principal end. **Maimonides** ("Rabbi Moses") is named and his contrary view rejected.
- **The bill of divorce** (Q67): the **indissolubility** of marriage belongs to natural law (secondary
  precepts) and to the *sacramentum*. The **Mosaic permission of divorce** was a "permission" only in
  the sense of *omitting punishment* — a lesser evil tolerated "for the hardness of their hearts" to
  prevent wife-murder — not a moral sanction; **Christ abrogated it**, restoring the full natural law.
- **Illegitimate children** (Q68): their categories, civil disabilities, and modes of legitimization.

## Reception and divergence

The Catholic theology of marriage as an indissoluble sacrament conferring grace, with consent as its
efficient cause and the elaborate law of impediments, descends directly from this treatise (codified
at Trent). The **Protestant** Reformers denied marriage sacramental status (treating it as a worldly
estate under God's ordinance) and, accordingly, admitted divorce in some cases. The patriarchs'
polygamy and the Mosaic divorce permission are also live questions in **Jewish** and cross-tradition
marriage law (cf. [[matrimony]] discussions with [[judaism]] and [[islam]] on plural marriage).
