# Mool Mantar (Mul Mantar)

The **Mool Mantar** (also spelled *Mul Mantar* — "root formula" or "seed teaching") is the brief theological axiom that opens the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] and is prefixed to each new rāg section throughout the Granth. It is the most fundamental statement of [[sikhism|Sikh]] theology, compressing the nature of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] (God) into nine attributes followed by a soteriological phrase.

## Text and Translation

Ik Oaṅkār — **One Universal Creator God**
Sat Nām — **Truth is His Name**
Kartā Purakhu — **He is the Creative Being**
Nirbhau — **He is without Fear**
Nirvair — **He is without Hatred**
Akāl Mūrat — **He is the Image of the Undying** (Timeless Form)
Ajūnī — **Beyond Birth**
Saibhaṅ — **Self-Existent**
Gur Prasādi — **By Guru's Grace**

The phrase "Gur Prasādi" (By Guru's Grace) closes the doctrinal list and encodes the principle of [[nadar|Nadar]] — that knowing God depends on grace, not solely effort or caste status. It thus completes the attributes with a soteriological clause.

## Theological Significance

The nine attributes span the full range of Sikh monotheism:
- **Ik Oaṅkār**: radical oneness — not 1 among many, but the One in whom all multiplicity subsists
- **Sat Nām**: ontological identity of God with Truth (*sat*); the Name IS God
- **Kartā Purakhu**: God as active Creator-Person, not an impersonal absolute
- **Nirbhau / Nirvair**: freedom from the reactive qualities of the lower self — God has no fear and no hatred
- **Akāl Mūrat**: Timeless Form — the paradox of an "image" (*mūrat*) that transcends time
- **Ajūnī**: beyond the cycle of birth and death (*jūn* = species/form); not subject to reincarnation
- **Saibhaṅ**: God's aseity — self-existent, uncaused, not created by another
- **Gur Prasādi**: grace as the only path to knowing this God

The Mool Mantar is intoned at the start of daily prayer and appears again at the opening of each rāg in the Granth, marking every musical division as a fresh encounter with the same foundational truth.
