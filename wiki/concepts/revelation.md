# Revelation

**Revelation** — the claim that a deity discloses something (itself, its will, or a message)
that human beings could not have reached unaided — is one of the load-bearing categories of the
theistic traditions. It is the hinge on which scripture, prophecy, canon, and religious
authority all turn: what a community takes revelation to *be* determines what it takes its
texts to be, who may speak for it, and whether the disclosure is finished.

This is a **general concept page**. It gathers the tradition- and thinker-specific treatments
the wiki holds and points to them; it does not synthesize them into a single doctrine, and it
does not adjudicate between them. Each account below is attributed.

> **Disambiguation.** For the New Testament apocalypse, see
> [[revelation-to-john|The Revelation to John]]. For Heschel's specific constructive theory,

## The core problem

Every account of revelation must answer at least three questions, and traditions divide along
each:

| Question | Divergent answers held somewhere in this wiki |
|---|---|
| **What is disclosed?** | Propositions and information · a person · an event · a law · God's own self |
| **Through what medium?** | Prophetic speech · a written text · an incarnate person · nature · continuing institutional voice |
| **Is it closed?** | Sealed canon · continuing revelation · reopened by a new prophet |

The third question is not merely chronological — it is the fault line between closed-canon
traditions and those that hold disclosure to be ongoing (see
[[continuing-revelation|Continuing Revelation]]).

## Christian treatments

### Barth — revelation as God's self-giving, not information

[[karl-barth|Karl Barth]]'s account, developed across *Church Dogmatics* I.1 and recorded at
length on this wiki, is the most fully documented treatment in the collection. Its distinctive
moves, as recorded on [[barth-church-dogmatics-i1|the source page]]:

- **Revelation is identical with God, not a deposit left behind by God.** In the §8 thesis,
  "God's Word is God Himself in His revelation" — so revelation cannot be reduced to a corpus
  of fixed propositions. See [[revealer-revelation-revealedness|Revealer / Revelation /
  Revealedness]], which Barth presents as the *root* of the doctrine of the Trinity.
- **Revelation is the original form of the Word**, presupposed by Scripture and by church
  proclamation, which witness to it rather than constitute it — the schema recorded at
  [[threefold-word-of-god|The Threefold Word of God]] and [[word-of-god|Word of God]].
- **Veiling and unveiling are simultaneous.** The *Deus revelatus* is also the *Deus
  absconditus*; "God's presence is always God's decision to be present."
- **It cannot be reached from below.** This drives Barth's rejection of
  [[natural-theology|natural theology]] and the *analogia entis*, and his counter-proposal of
  the [[analogia-fidei|analogia fidei]] — see also
  [[knowability-of-the-word-of-god|Knowability of the Word of God]] and
  [[analogia-entis|Analogia Entis]].
- **The Spirit is the subjective side.** "Revealedness" — the impartation of revelation to
  actual people in actual history — is the Spirit's work; see [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]].

Barth's position is recorded here as *Barth's*, not as the Christian account. It was polemical
in its own moment, and the [[natural-theology|natural theology controversy]] page records the
opposing positions.

### Revelation and the written text

Whether revelation is *located in* the words of scripture or *witnessed to by* them is a live
division, tracked at [[inspiration-of-scripture|Inspiration of Scripture]] and compared
across traditions at [[scripture-canon-and-interpretation|Scripture, Canon, and
Interpretation]]. Barth's answer — Scripture is authoritative as witness, not as an inerrant
text in itself — is one position among several and is contested by traditions holding verbal
or plenary inspiration.

## Jewish treatments

[[abraham-joshua-heschel|Heschel]]'s constructive theory — revelation as **event** rather than
process, "an ecstasy of God," with the resulting paradox of Sinai and the notion of
co-revelation ("the word of God and man") — is developed on its own page at
[[revelation-heschel|Revelation (Heschel's Theory)]] and in
[[god-in-search-of-man|*God in Search of Man*]]. Heschel frames it as a two-front argument,
against both fundamentalism and a purely critical reduction. Related: [[mitzvah|mitzvah]] as
the human answer to revelation, and [[religion-of-time|religion of time]].

The wiki's coverage of *classical* Jewish accounts of revelation (Torah from Sinai as a legal
and metaphysical claim, medieval prophetology, the Maimonidean theory of prophecy) is
**thinner than its coverage of Heschel** and is not yet gathered here. That is a collection
gap, not a judgment about relative importance.

## Latter-day Saint treatments

The claim that revelation remains open — that new material can enter scripture through a
prophet-president — is the load-bearing difference recorded at
[[continuing-revelation|Continuing Revelation]] and reflected in the `canon_scope` of
[[doctrine-and-covenants|Doctrine and Covenants]] and
[[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great Price]]. This is a structural divergence from
closed-canon traditions, not a difference of degree.

## Coverage note

This page is a hub over what the collection actually holds. The Christian material —
specifically the Barth stream — is deep; the Jewish material is concentrated almost entirely in
Heschel; **Islamic accounts of *waḥy* and the created/uncreated Qur'an debate have no concept
page yet**, despite being one of the most consequential revelation controversies in any
tradition. Buddhist and Hindu analogues (*śruti* as heard/unauthored) are likewise unbuilt.
These absences reflect ingestion history, not significance.
