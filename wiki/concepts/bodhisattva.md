# Bodhisattva

A **bodhisattva** ("awakening-being") is one who vows to attain full buddhahood **for the liberation
of all sentient beings**, postponing final *nirvana* out of compassion. The ideal is the defining
contrast between the great Buddhist vehicles.

## Theravada vs. Mahayana

- In [[theravada|Theravada]], "bodhisattva" denotes mainly the previous lives of the historical
  [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]]; the normal goal for practitioners is to become an **arhat** (one
  liberated by following the path).
- In [[mahayana|Mahayana]], the bodhisattva path is the **universal vocation**: every practitioner
  takes the bodhisattva vow, cultivating the six *paramitas* (perfections — generosity, ethics,
  patience, effort, meditation, wisdom). Great celestial bodhisattvas (Avalokiteshvara, Manjushri)
  become objects of devotion. This is the heart of [[shantideva|Śāntideva]]'s
  [[bodhicaryavatara|Bodhicaryāvatāra]] ("Guide to the Bodhisattva Way of Life"), whose chapters are
  organized around the perfections and whose meditation on the
  [[exchange-of-self-and-other|exchange of self and other]] generates [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]].

The arhat-vs-bodhisattva ideal is, with the canon question, the substance of the Theravada/Mahayana
divide — a candidate comparison/controversy page.

In [[tibetan-buddhism|Tibetan Buddhism]], the bodhisattva path is set out as a graded curriculum in
[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]**: generating [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]],
adopting and maintaining the vow, and training in the **[[six-perfections|six perfections]]** plus
the four ways to gather disciples (the "great capacity" path).

## Key Virtue

The bodhisattva unites **wisdom** (*prajna* — insight into [[sunyata|emptiness]]) with **compassion**
(*karuna*); **[[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]]**, the awakening-mind aimed at others' liberation, is its
motive force.
