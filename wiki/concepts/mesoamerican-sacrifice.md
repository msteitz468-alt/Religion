# Sacrifice and the Sun

**Blood sacrifice** is the defining (and most notorious) feature of [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerican
religion]] — understood not as cruelty but as a **sacred cosmic necessity**: blood is the vital energy that
**sustains the gods and keeps the universe alive**.

## The Logic of the Debt

The gods **sacrificed themselves** to create the world and to set the sun in motion (see the [[five-suns|
Five Suns]]); humanity, made to **nourish and worship** its makers, must **repay that debt in blood**. The
forms ranged across the cultures:

- **Autosacrifice (bloodletting)** — universal: kings, priests, and ordinary people drew their own blood
  (piercing the tongue, ears, or genitals with maguey spines or stingray spines) to offer to the gods — the
  [[maya-religion|Maya]] divine king's central rite, often a means of visionary contact with the gods and
  ancestors.
- **Human sacrifice** — practiced widely but raised by the **[[aztec-religion|Aztecs]]** to a vast scale: war
  captives (and others) were offered atop the great pyramids — most often by **heart-extraction**, the still-
  beating heart held up to the **sun** — to feed [[huitzilopochtli|Huitzilopochtli]] and keep the **Fifth Sun**
  moving. Other victims were offered to [[tlaloc|Tlaloc]] (children, for rain), to [[xipe-totec|Xipe Totec]]
  (by flaying), and others.

## Reciprocity, Not Wrath

The framework is **reciprocity** (*do ut des*): the gods give the rain, the maize, and the sun; humans give
back blood and reverence. A fragile cosmos, perpetually near collapse, is held together by this exchange. (The
scale and meaning of Aztec sacrifice — and how far Spanish accounts exaggerated it — remain debated; recorded
without adjudication.)
