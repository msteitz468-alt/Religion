# Criteria of Apostolic Continuity (Canon, Creed, Episcopate)

[[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Jaroslav Pelikan's]] organizing thesis (Vol. I, ch. 2) for how the early church defined orthodoxy against the "outsiders." Pressed by [[marcionism|Marcion]], the [[gnosticism|Gnostics]], and the [[montanism|Montanists]] — who all "stressed distinctiveness even at the cost of continuity" — the church developed **three interlocking criteria** by which authentic apostolic teaching could be told from its rivals. "The church looked increasingly not to the future, illumined by the Lord's return, nor to the present, illumined by the Spirit's extraordinary gifts, but to the past, illumined by the composition of the apostolic canon, the creation of the apostolic creed, and the establishment of the apostolic episcopate."

## The Three Criteria

1. **The canon of the New Testament** — apostolicity became the test of canonicity, until "canonicity and apostolicity became almost synonymous terms." See [[nt-canon-formation]].
2. **The rule of faith / creed** — a condensed, confessable summary of apostolic teaching, needed "before, during, and after" canon formation because the canon was "too long and complex to act as a standard." Its irreducible content: the [[trinity|Trinity]] and the passion-and-resurrection. See [[rule-of-faith]].
3. **The apostolic episcopate** — the publicly verifiable [[apostolic-succession|succession of bishops]], answering the Gnostic claim to a *secret* apostolic tradition with a *public* one ([[irenaeus|Irenaeus]]: the apostles "transmitted openly to all"). See [[apostolic-succession]], [[monepiscopate]].

## Pelikan's Qualifications

- **Causation, carefully stated:** "It is an oversimplification to maintain that the heresies… *produced* these criteria… One can guess, however, that [the system of authority] might not have developed *when it did* and *as it did* without them."
- **No *sola scriptura*, no *traditio sola*:** in the ante-Nicene church "there was no notion of *sola Scriptura*, but neither was there a doctrine of *traditio sola*" — Scripture and [[consensus-patrum|tradition]] were coincident (cf. Kelly on [[rule-of-faith]]).
- **The cessation of prophecy:** the criteria entailed pushing the Spirit's decisive activity into the apostolic past. [[hippolytus|Hippolytus]] made the Apocalypse of John "the last valid prophecy," so that the apostles became "a sort of spiritual aristocracy, and the first century a golden age of the Spirit." This sealed the obsolescence of the [[montanism|Montanist]] New Prophecy.
- **Result:** "apostolic," "catholic," "traditional," and "orthodox" became effectively synonymous.
