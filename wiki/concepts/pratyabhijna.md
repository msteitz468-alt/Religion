# Pratyabhijñā (Recognition)

**Pratyabhijñā** — "re-cognition," recognition — is the central soteriological idea of non-dual
**[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaivism]]**, and the name of its philosophical school (founded by
**Utpaladeva**, systematized by **[[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta]]**). It answers the question *what is
liberation?* with a single move: liberation is **recognizing what is already the case** — that one's own
self is, and has always been, **Śiva**, the one infinite consciousness.

## The Core Idea

The bound soul is not *separated* from God and seeking to *become* divine; it **already is** Śiva but has
**forgotten** this, through a self-contraction (the *malas*, "impurities," beginning with *āṇava-mala*,
the sense of being a limited, isolated self). Salvation is therefore not acquisition but **anamnesis**:
like a woman who has been told her beloved is near yet does not enjoy him until she *recognizes* him
standing before her, the self gains nothing new — it simply recognizes its own already-divine nature.
This recognition can dawn suddenly, by grace and the "descent of power" (*śaktipāta*), and grants
[[moksha|liberation]] **in this life** (*jīvanmukti*).

## Contrast with Advaita

Both Pratyabhijñā and [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedanta]] are "non-dual," but they differ sharply:

- For Advaita, the world is **[[maya|māyā]]** — an illusion superimposed by ignorance
  ([[adhyasa|adhyāsa]]); knowledge dissolves it, leaving the static, attribute-less witness
  ([[brahman|nirguṇa Brahman]]).
- For Pratyabhijñā, the world is Śiva's **real and free self-manifestation** (*svātantrya*); recognition
  does not negate the world but reveals it as the **dynamic** play of consciousness. Knowing (*dṛk*) and
  acting (*kriyā*) are Śiva's own powers — so action is not a problem to be transcended (cf.
  [[jnana-karma-samuccaya|jñāna-karma-samuccaya]]).

The slogan-test: where Advaita reads *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art") as the **erasure** of the apparent
self into Brahman, Pratyabhijñā reads it as the **recognition** of the self as Śiva (see
[[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the comparison]]).

## In the Gita

[[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta's]] [[marjanovic-abhinavagupta-gita|Gītārtha-saṃgraha]] reads Krishna's
teaching to Arjuna as, at its esoteric core, a summons to this recognition — the all-inclusive
consciousness in which "there is no state, within or without, where He does not shine" (8.11).
