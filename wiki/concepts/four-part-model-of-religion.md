# The Four-Part Model of Religion

A **comparative-analytic scheme** devised by [[stephen-prothero|Stephen Prothero]] (used,
he says, "for years in the classroom") and made the organizing device of *God Is Not One*
(2010). Each great religion, on this model, articulates four things:

1. a **problem** — its diagnosis of what is wrong with the human condition;
2. a **solution** to that problem, which doubles as the **religious goal**;
3. a **technique** (or techniques) for moving from problem to solution; and
4. an **exemplar** (or exemplars) who chart the path from problem to solution.

## The Point: An Anti-Reductive Device

The model's purpose is **polemical as well as pedagogical**. Because the *problem* differs
from tradition to tradition, so does everything downstream of it — and that is precisely
what the "all religions are one" view ([[perennialism|perennialism]], "Godthink") obscures.
"If practitioners of the world's religions are all mountain climbers, then they are on very
different mountains, climbing very different peaks, and using very different tools." Asking
which religion best secures *salvation* is, Prothero argues, like asking which sport best
**scores runs**: it privileges baseball (Christianity) from the start, because *runs* —
like *sin* and *salvation* — is a term native to one game only. See
[[religious-pluralism-debate|the pluralism debate]] and [[perennialism|perennialism]].

## The Eight Traditions Mapped

| Tradition | Problem | Solution / Goal | Technique | Exemplar(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[islam|Islam]] | self-sufficiency / pride | submission; Paradise | the Five Pillars | Muhammad; the *ummah* |
| [[christianity|Christianity]] | sin | salvation | faith and/or good works | saints; people of faith |
| [[confucianism|Confucianism]] | social chaos | social harmony | ritual propriety (*li*), education | the *junzi* / sage |
| [[hinduism|Hinduism]] | bondage to rebirth (*samsara*) | release (*moksha*) | the yogas | gurus; the liberated |
| [[buddhism|Buddhism]] | suffering (*dukkha*) | *nirvana* | the Noble Eightfold Path | arhat / bodhisattva / lama |
| [[yoruba-religion|Yoruba religion]] | disconnection | connection / flourishing | divination, sacrifice, possession | *babalawos*; the orishas |
| [[judaism|Judaism]] | exile / estrangement | return (*teshuvah*) | story and law (*halakha*) | the patriarchs; the people |
| [[daoism|Daoism]] | lifelessness / unnaturalness | flourishing, naturalness | *wu wei*; longevity arts | the sage; the immortal |

## Caveats Prothero Attaches

- It is **"admittedly simplistic … a starting point" that "must be nuanced."** One cannot
  compress millennia of a tradition into four sentences.
- It hides **internal diversity**: Catholics and Protestants disagree on *how* salvation is
  achieved; Theravada and Mahayana on what *nirvana* is, or whether it is an "achievement"
  at all. The "many Buddhisms" strain the one-problem-per-religion grid (a standard
  criticism — see [[stephen-prothero|Prothero §Criticisms]]).
- It is paired in the book with a second, more contestable device — **ranking the eight by
  contemporary "impact"** (not goodness): Islam, Christianity, Confucianism, Hinduism,
  Buddhism, Yoruba religion, Judaism, Daoism.

## Relation to Other Frameworks

- It descends from **Ninian Smart**'s "seven dimensions" of religion (ritual, narrative,
  experiential, institutional, ethical, doctrinal, material) and from **family-resemblance**
  accounts of "religion" (no single essence, only overlapping tendencies).
- It is the methodological expression of **[[religious-pluralism-debate|particularism]]**:
  difference is built into the comparison from the first question ("what is the *problem*?")
  rather than smoothed away at the end.
