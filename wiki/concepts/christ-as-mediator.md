# Christ as Mediator

That [[jesus|Christ]] is the **one Mediator between God and humanity** (1 Tim. 2:5) is treated by
[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] at the [[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Tertia Pars Q26, the capstone of his
treatise on the Incarnation. It draws together the [[priesthood-of-christ|priesthood]] and the
[[atonement|reconciling work]] of Christ.

## Aquinas's account (ST III Q26)

- **Christ alone is the perfect Mediator** (A1): "to unite men to God perfectively belongs to Christ,
  through whom men are reconciled to God" (2 Cor. 5:19). A mediator must be **distinct from both
  extremes** and **unite them**; subordinate mediators (prophets, priests, angels) cooperate only
  "dispositively and ministerially" — they do not perfect the union. Aquinas (with Augustine) rules
  out the **good angels** as mediators, since they share immortality and beatitude with God but have
  nothing in common with mortal, unhappy man; the demons are "evil mediators," sharing immortality
  with God and unhappiness with men, but perversely.
- **Christ is Mediator *as man*** (A2): "not because He is the Word… but He is Mediator, as man"
  (Augustine). As God He is equal to the Father and shares the one nature and dominion; mediation
  requires being a *mean*, which befits Him in his **human nature** — having "beatitude in common with
  God, mortality in common with men." Hence the **Holy Spirit**, being equal to God, is **not** a
  mediator. Christ's mediatorial work as man is "to unite men to God, by communicating to men both
  precepts and gifts, and by offering satisfaction and prayers to God for men."

## Significance and reception

The sole and perfect mediation of Christ is common Christian ground, but its scope is contested: the
Reformers pressed *solus Christus* against any creaturely mediation (the invocation of saints, the
Marian title *Mediatrix*), while [[catholicism|Catholic]] theology distinguishes Christ's unique
*perfective* mediation from the subordinate, participatory "mediation" of [[mary|Mary]] and the saints —
exactly Aquinas's "dispositive and ministerial" category. The doctrine is the soteriological
counterpart of the [[hypostatic-union]]: only because the one Person is *both* God and man can He be
the bridge between them.
