# Joyous Perseverance (Vīrya)

The fourth of the **[[six-perfections|six perfections]]**, defined by [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]
(Lamrim Chenmo, Vol. II) via [[shantideva|Śāntideva]]: "What is joyous perseverance? It is delight in
virtue." Asaṅga's *Bodhisattvabhūmi* adds it is "a flawless state of mind that is enthusiastic about
accumulating virtue and working for the welfare of living beings." It is "the principal and highest
cause for the correct attainment of a bodhisattva's virtuous qualities" — without it "the lazy lack
all perfections from generosity to wisdom."

## Three Divisions

1. **Armor-like** joyous perseverance — the resolve to persevere over immeasurable eons for even a
   single being ("the inexhaustible armor").
2. Joyous perseverance of **gathering virtue** — applying oneself to the six perfections.
3. Joyous perseverance of **acting for the welfare of beings** — the eleven activities for others.

## The Three Lazinesses and Four Powers

The incompatible factors (Śāntideva): "laziness, adhering to what is ignoble, / And self-contempt out
of discouragement." Against them, four **powers** develop perseverance:

1. **Aspiration** (yearning), rooted in meditation on karma — "The Sage declared aspiration / The root
   of all aspects of virtue."
2. **Steadfastness / pride** — about the task, about ability, and over the afflictions ("I shall be
   victorious over these").
3. **Joy** — insatiable delight in virtuous work, "like those who want the pleasure that results from
   play."
4. **Relinquishment** — resting when fatigued, then resuming; effort even "like a river," neither too
   intense nor too slack.

Sustained by **mindfulness and vigilance** — the warrior who instantly retrieves a dropped sword;
the man carrying a brimming pot of oil under threat of death.
