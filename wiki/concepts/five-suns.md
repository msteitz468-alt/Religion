# The Five Suns

The **Five Suns** is the [[aztec-religion|Aztec]] cosmology of **cyclical world-ages** — the belief that the
world has been created and destroyed **several times over**, each age (a "Sun") presided over by a different
god and ending in catastrophe. The present world is the **Fifth Sun**, and it too is destined to be destroyed.

## The Ages

Each previous Sun ended in a different cataclysm:

1. **First Sun** (Earth / [[tezcatlipoca|Tezcatlipoca]]) — its people devoured by jaguars.
2. **Second Sun** (Wind / [[quetzalcoatl|Quetzalcoatl]]) — destroyed by hurricanes; people turned to monkeys.
3. **Third Sun** (Rain/Fire / [[tlaloc|Tlaloc]]) — destroyed by a rain of fire.
4. **Fourth Sun** (Water) — destroyed by a great flood; people turned to fish.
5. **Fifth Sun** (our age, **[[tonatiuh|Tonatiuh]]**) — destined to end in **earthquakes**.

## The Sacrifice that Made the Sun

The present Fifth Sun was born at **Teotihuacan**, where the assembled gods needed one of their number to
**throw himself into a great fire** to become the sun. The humble, sickly **Nanahuatzin** leapt in and rose as
the sun — but the sun **would not move** across the sky until the other gods, too, **sacrificed themselves** to
give it motion. This is the mythic root of the Aztec demand for **[[mesoamerican-sacrifice|human sacrifice]]**:
since the gods gave their own blood to set the sun moving, humans must keep repaying that debt in blood to keep
the Fifth Sun alive and stave off the world's end.

## Cross-Tradition Note

A radically **cyclical, fragile cosmos** — created, destroyed, and re-created in ages, sustained only by
continual sacrifice — the sharpest possible contrast with the **linear, once-created, providentially-secured
world** of the Abrahamic traditions (cf. the cyclical *yugas* of [[hinduism|Hinduism]]). Recorded without
adjudication.
