# Qiwama

**Qiwama** (قِوامة, from *qawwamuna 'ala*, Q 4:34) is the Qur'anic concept of men's
responsibility for / authority over women — the single most debated locus in Islamic gender
discourse. This page collects the cluster of Qur'anic terms that travel with it; the
interpretive dispute itself is carried on [[quran-4-34-male-authority|the Q 4:34 controversy
page]].

## The Term Cluster

- **Qiwama / qawwamuna 'ala** ("maintainers of / in charge of," Q 4:34). Classically a male
  guardianship and authority; in reformist readings ([[amina-wadud|Wadud]],
  [[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur Rahman]]) a **conditional responsibility** tied by the particle *bi-*
  to two contingencies — *faddala* and material support — such that "if either condition fails,
  the man is not *qawwam*."
- **Faddala** (فضّل, "to prefer," Q 4:34; verbal noun *tafdil*; God's *fadl* = benevolence). A
  preference God grants; the Qur'an's only *specified* material male preference is the double
  inheritance share. Reformists stress it reads *ba'd* over *ba'd* ("some over some"), not
  men-as-class over women-as-class, and that even prophets "preferred" over others are also
  said to have "no distinction" made between them (Q 2:285) — so *faddala* is **relative**.
- **Darajah** (درجة, "step, degree, rank," Q 2:228, "men have a degree above them"). A *darajah*
  separates levels of heaven and hell and ranks of the righteous; between the sexes it appears
  once, in a **divorce** context (the male's unilateral right of repudiation), which reformists
  read as restricting it to that subject rather than a universal hierarchy.
- **Nushuz** (نشوز, "recalcitrance, marital disorder," Q 4:34 of wives; **Q 4:128 of
  husbands**). Because the same word describes husbands, reformists (with Sayyid Qutb) argue it
  cannot mean "disobedience to the husband" but a **mutual** breakdown of marital harmony.
- **Daraba** (ضرب, "to strike," Q 4:34, *wadribuhunna*). The third step in the Q 4:34
  de-escalation sequence (admonish → separate beds → *daraba*). Standardly "beat/scourge"; per
  Lane and *Lisan al-'Arab* its range includes "to set forth / strike out / separate from," and
  it is the **non-intensive** form against *darraba* (to beat repeatedly) — grounds on which it
  is read as a curb on, not a license for, violence.
- **Qanitat** (قانتات, "devout," Q 4:34). Used of **both** sexes toward God (e.g. Q 33:35);
  reformists reject its frequent narrowing to "obedient [to the husband]," reserving mere
  creaturely obedience for *ta'a*.

## Significance

The cluster is where the structural question of Islamic gender relations is decided: whether
the Qur'an installs a fixed male hierarchy or a conditional, reciprocal partnership. It is the
engine of [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] and the heart of
[[wadud-quran-and-woman|Wadud's *Qur'an and Woman*]].
