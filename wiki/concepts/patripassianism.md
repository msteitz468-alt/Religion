# Patripassianism

Patripassianism (Latin *patripassianus*, "the Father suffers") is the corollary of [[modalism|modalistic Monarchianism]]: if Father and Son are merely names for one identical divine Person, then it was the Father himself who was born, suffered, and died in Christ. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. V.

## Exponents

- **Noetus of Smyrna** embraced it openly: "What wrong have I done, glorifying one only God, Christ, Who was born, suffered and died?"
- **Praxeas** (refuted by [[tertullian]]'s *Adversus Praxeam*) taught that the Father entered the Virgin's womb and suffered; later Praxeans retreated to saying the Father only "co-suffers" (*compatitur*) with the Son.
- Pope **Callistus** adopted the guarded formula that the Father "co-suffered with the Son."

The charge of patripassianism dogged all modalist theology and was a principal reason the Church developed the language of [[oikonomia|economy]] and, eventually, the [[trinity|Trinitarian]] distinction of Persons.
