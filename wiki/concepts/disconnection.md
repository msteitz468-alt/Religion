# Disconnection

**Disconnection** is the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] practice of **cutting off all contact** with a person who has been designated a **Suppressive Person (SP)** — someone the Church has declared to be an enemy of Scientology. A Scientologist ordered or pressured to disconnect must cease all communication with the SP, including close family members.

## The Policy

The policy is rooted in Hubbard's distinction between **Potential Trouble Sources (PTS)** and **Suppressive Persons**. A person is PTS if they are "connected to" (in regular contact with) an SP. To remain PTS is to remain in spiritual danger; the solution is either to "handle" the SP (convince them to stop their suppressive behavior and reconnect with Scientology) or to **disconnect** from them entirely.

Disconnection is applied to:
- Family members who have left Scientology or spoken critically of the Church
- Friends who have been declared SPs
- Journalists, lawyers, and critics who have been designated enemies

## The Haggis Case (Wright's Primary Documentation)

[[paul-haggis|Paul Haggis]]'s resignation from the Church in 2009 was triggered in part by disconnection. His wife **Deborah Rennard**'s parents were declared Suppressive Persons in August 2006 (via a "goldenrod" — the Church's pink or yellow ethics announcement). Deborah was pressured to disconnect from her parents. The goldenrod read: "We tried to do everything we could.… You don't have any money, you're broke."

When Haggis saw [[tommy-davis|Tommy Davis]] on CNN explicitly deny that disconnection policy existed — telling the interviewer: "No, no. There's no such thing" — Haggis knew this was a direct lie based on his own family's experience. This was a decisive factor in his decision to resign.

## Church's Official Position

The Church has officially denied that disconnection is a current mandatory policy — presenting it as a voluntary personal choice. Tommy Davis's CNN denial is the most public example. However, multiple former members have documented specific instances of being required to disconnect or face consequences themselves.

**Contradiction Protocol**: All positions recorded without adjudication.

## Cross-Tradition Comparisons

Wright draws the comparison explicitly in the Epilogue: disconnection "is not unique to Scientology, nor is the longing for religious sanctuary." Comparable practices:
- **Jehovah's Witnesses**: "disfellowshipping" (complete shunning of former members)
- **Amish**: Meidung (shunning of those who leave the community)
- **Ultra-Orthodox Judaism**: various forms of communal exclusion
- **Early Christianity**: excommunication

The structural parallel is real; the controversy focuses on whether Scientology's version is *coerced* (with institutional enforcement through ethics procedures) or genuinely voluntary.
