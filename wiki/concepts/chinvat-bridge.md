# The Chinvat Bridge (the "Crossing of the Separator")

The **Chinvat Bridge** (Avestan *Chinvato Peretu*, "Crossing/Bridge of the Separator") is the
[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] site of the **individual judgment of the soul after death** — the
passage every soul must attempt, which widens for the righteous and narrows to a blade-edge for the
wicked.

## From a Pagan Ford to a Moral Tribunal

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], the older Indo-Iranian belief knew a
dangerous crossing — a ford or bridge over a dark river — that the soul must pass to reach the
underworld of **Yima** (Vedic [[yama|Yama]]), where success depended on **status and the offerings
made by one's heirs**. [[zarathustra|Zoroaster's]] reform **moralized** it: the bridge becomes a
place of **ethical judgment**, where each soul depends "not on power or wealth of offerings… but on
its own ethical achievements." This democratization — judgment by **deeds, open to women as well as
men, servants as well as masters** — broke with the aristocratic afterlife of the old faith.

## The Judgment

- The tribunal is presided over by **Mithra**, flanked by **Sraosha** (Obedience) and **Rashnu**,
  who holds the **scales of justice**.
- The soul's **thoughts, words, and deeds** are weighed, good against bad.
- If the good outweigh, the soul is met by its **Daena** — a beautiful maiden who is the
  **personification of its own conscience** — and led across the broadened bridge up to
  **Paradise** (the "House of Song").
- If the bad outweigh, the bridge **contracts to a razor's edge**, a hideous hag (the soul's own
  evil conscience) seizes it, and it falls into **Hell**, "the dwelling-place of Worst Purpose," a
  long age of misery and darkness.
- Between the clearly good and clearly wicked lies an intermediate place (later *Hamistagan*) for
  souls whose deeds are evenly balanced.

This individual judgment is, however, **provisional**: it lasts only through the present age of
Mixture (see [[the-three-times|the Three Times]]). At [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]] comes the
**general resurrection and Last Judgment**, after which soul and body are reunited forever.

## The Daena

The **Daena** — the maiden who meets the righteous soul — is one of Zoroastrianism's most striking
images: a person's accumulated conscience and faith **objectified as a being who comes to greet
them**, beautiful or hideous according to how they lived. (The same word *daena* also denotes
"religion/conscience" more broadly.)

## Significance and Influence

The Chinvat Bridge fuses an **individual post-mortem judgment**, a **weighing of deeds**, a
**bridge over hell**, and **heaven/hell as ethical destinations** — a complex with notable
resonances in later Jewish, Christian, and especially Islamic eschatology (the *Sirat* bridge over
Hell in Islamic tradition is frequently compared). Recorded, without adjudication, on
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]].
