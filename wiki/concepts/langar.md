The *langar* is the communal dining hall with attached kitchen that is always part of every [[gurdwara|gurdwara]]. Per [[mcleod-sikhism|McLeod]], it was "introduced into Sikh usage to demonstrate that all castes have equal access to liberation" (McLeod ~L114–116). After public worship, the *sangat* (congregation) joins for a common meal in the langar.

## Egalitarian Principle

When seated for a meal in the langar, all participants must sit in status-free lines (*pangat*). High status must not be claimed by sitting forward, nor inferior status implied by a position further back. The organizing principle is the radical erasure of caste hierarchy in a shared meal (~L116–118).

One qualification applies: an Amrit-dhari Sikh who shares a *thali* (plate) may eat only from one used by another Amrit-dhari (~L119–121).

## Food

Meat, chicken, and fish are never served in a langar — not because all Sikhs are vegetarian (McLeod notes that "many certainly are not"), but because those who are vegetarian "may regard the provision of non-vegetarian food as an offensive custom" (~L121–124).

## Access

The same freedom of access that applies to gurdwara worship applies to the langar: no distinction is made between Sikhs and non-Sikhs, and no discrimination is permitted on grounds of caste or status. During the distribution of *karah prasad* (sacred food), "no consideration should be given to differences of caste, nor to those between Sikh and non-Sikh" (~L216–218).
