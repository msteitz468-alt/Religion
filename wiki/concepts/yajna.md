# Yajna

**Yajna** is the **fire sacrifice** at the heart of [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]] — the central act by
which humans relate to the gods and sustain the cosmos. Offerings (clarified butter, grain, the [[soma|soma]]
plant, sometimes animals) are cast into the **sacred fire**, where the fire-god **[[agni|Agni]]** consumes
them and **carries them up as smoke to the gods**, who in turn send back rain, cattle, sons, and victory — a
**reciprocal exchange** between the human and divine worlds.

## Word, Fire, and Order

There were **no temples and no images**: the yajna was performed on a temporary altar by trained **Brahmin
priests** reciting the [[rig-veda|Vedic hymns]] with **flawless precision** — for the rightly-spoken sacred
word (*mantra*) is itself efficacious. The whole apparatus rests on **[[rita|rita]]** (cosmic order): a
correctly performed sacrifice maintains the order of the universe. In the later [[shrauta-brahmanism|Brahmana]]
period the sacrifice became supremely powerful and elaborate — the creator [[prajapati|Prajapati]] was said
to have made the world *by* sacrifice (the [[rig-veda|Purusha Sukta]]), so each yajna **re-enacts creation**.

## Cross-Tradition Note

Yajna is a pure ancient **orthopraxy** — religion as exact ritual that *compels* its result — strikingly
parallel to [[roman-state-cult|Roman state ritual]] and [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamian]] cult, and the
target of the inward, anti-ritual reactions of the [[upanishads|Upanishads]] and [[buddhism|Buddhism]].
