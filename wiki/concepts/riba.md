# Riba (Usury/Interest)

*Riba* (Arabic: ربا, lit. "increase" or "excess") is the Islamic legal and ethical prohibition of receiving or paying an unjustified increment in a financial transaction. In [[sahih-al-bukhari]] (Vol. 3), riba appears both as a broad prohibition of commercial dishonesty and as a precise technical legal category governing exchange of specific commodities and currencies.

---

## Definition and Scope

The Bukhari hadiths treat riba in two overlapping senses:

1. **Riba al-Fadl** (riba of excess): Exchanging the same commodity for an unequal amount of the same commodity. Explicitly prohibited for six categories.
2. **Riba al-Nasaʾa** (riba of delay): Any credit transaction in the same commodity category where the exchange is not hand-to-hand (simultaneous), even if the amounts are equal.

The six commodity categories named in the hadiths are: **gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt** (Umar's narration names gold, silver, wheat, barley, and dates; Abu Said's narration, No. 385, adds the prohibition on non-present gold or silver for present gold or silver).

---

## Key Hadiths

### The "Hand to Hand" Rule (Riba al-Fadl)

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 344** (narr. ʿUmar bin al-Khattab):
> "The bartering of gold for silver is Riba (usury), except if it is from hand to hand and equal in amount, and wheat grain for wheat grain is usury except if it is from hand to hand and equal in amount, and dates for dates is usury except if it is from hand to hand and equal in amount, and barley for barley is usury except if it is from hand to hand and equal in amount."

**Vol. 3, Book 34, Nos. 382–383** (narr. Malik bin Aws / Abu Bakra):
> "The selling of gold for gold is Riba (usury) except if the exchange is from hand to hand and equal in amount... Don't sell gold for gold unless equal in weight, nor silver for silver unless equal in weight, but you could sell gold for silver or silver for gold as you like."

Cross-commodity exchange (gold for silver, wheat for barley) is permitted in unequal amounts, as long as it is hand-to-hand. The prohibition of unequal exchange applies only within the same commodity.

**Vol. 3, Book 34, Nos. 384–385** (narr. Abu Said al-Khudri):
> "Do not sell gold for gold unless equivalent in weight... Do not sell gold or silver that is not present at the moment of exchange for gold or silver that is present."

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 294** (narr. Abu Said):
The Prophet prohibited exchanging 2 *sas* of mixed dates for 1 *sa* of fine dates: "No (bartering of) two Sas for one Sa nor two Dirhams for one Dirham is permissible."

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 405** (narr. Abu Said and Abu Huraira): The governor of Khaybar bartered 2 *sas* of ordinary dates for 1 *sa* of fine dates. The Prophet said:
> "Do not do so (as that is a kind of usury) but sell the mixed dates (of inferior quality) for money, and then buy good dates with that money."

This establishes the proper method for quality exchange: a two-step transaction through a different medium (money), not direct barter at unequal ratio.

### The Riba-Eater: Eschatological Warning

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 298** (narr. Samura bin Jundab — the Prophet's dream vision):
> "This night I dreamt that two men came and took me to a Holy land... I reached a river of blood, where a man was standing, and on its bank was standing another man with stones in his hands. The man in the middle of the river tried to come out, but the other threw a stone in his mouth and forced him to go back to his original place. So, whenever he tried to come out, the other man would throw a stone in his mouth and force him to go back to his former place. I asked, 'Who is this?' I was told, 'The person in the river was a Riba-eater.'"

### The Curse on Riba Participants

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 299** (narr. ʿAun bin Abu Juhaifa, from his father):
> "The Prophet forbade the acceptance of the price of a dog or blood, and also forbade the profession of tattooing, getting tattooed and receiving or giving Riba, (usury), and cursed the picture-makers."

The Prophet explicitly cursed *both* receiver and giver of riba — it is bilateral. **Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 440** repeats the same prohibition alongside tattooing, earnings from prostitution, and picture-making.

### Money Exchange: The Question of Delay

**Vol. 3, Book 34, No. 276**: "If it is from hand to hand, there is no harm in it; otherwise it is not permissible."

A minority view attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas (citing Usama, No. 386) suggests riba applies only to delay (not to unequal cross-commodity exchange), contrasted with the stricter view of Abu Said requiring equality in same-commodity exchanges. The compiler includes both without adjudication.

---

## Theological Rationale

No explicit theological rationale for the riba prohibition is given in these hadiths beyond the eschatological warning (No. 298). The texts approach riba as a clear divine prohibition (*haram*), in the same category as alcohol, pork, and idols (No. 438). The counter-model is the parable of the wise merchant who forgives debtors ("Forgive him so that Allah may forgive us" — Book 34, No. 292): *generosity and leniency* in financial dealings, whereas riba represents the extraction of increment from financial necessity.

---

## Affected Transactions

The following are explicitly prohibited as riba or riba-adjacent:
- Unequal barter of gold/silver/wheat/barley/dates for the same commodity, unless hand-to-hand
- Any delayed exchange of the same commodity, even if equal in amount
- Selling silver for gold on credit (No. 387)
- Muzabana: fresh dates for dried dates by measure (uncertainty/speculation overlap)
- Habal al-habala: sale of unborn offspring's offspring
- Selling goods for delivery before taking full possession
