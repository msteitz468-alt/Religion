# Pseudepigrapha

**Pseudepigrapha** ("falsely attributed writings") are Jewish (and some Christian) works of roughly the
Second Temple period attributed to ancient figures — [[enoch|Enoch]], [[moses|Moses]], the patriarchs,
Ezra, Baruch — but composed much later. The term marks a **canon status**, and a contested one: these are
the books that fell *outside* the rabbinic and most Christian canons yet were treasured by some
communities.

## A Spectrum of Canon Status (why it needs `canon_scope`)

"Pseudepigrapha" is not a fixed canonical verdict but a *relation*, and the same book can sit very
differently across communities — the precise problem the wiki's `canon_scope` field models:

- **Canonical** for the **Ethiopian** churches (and Beta Israel): [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]], [[jubilees|
  Jubilees]].
- **Authoritative** for the **[[essenes|Essene]]/Qumran** community (the broadest Second Temple canon —
  the Dead Sea Scrolls preserve many pseudepigrapha).
- **Disputed / used** by some early Christians — [[jude|Jude]] quotes [[1-enoch|1 Enoch]] as prophecy
  (see [[jude-1-enoch-canon|the controversy]]).
- **Rejected** from the [[rabbinic-judaism|rabbinic]], [[catholicism|Catholic]],
  [[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodox]], and [[protestantism|Protestant]] canons (distinct from the
  **deuterocanon/Apocrypha**, which Catholics and Orthodox *do* receive).

## Relation to "Apocrypha"

Terminology varies by tradition: Protestants often call the deuterocanonical books "Apocrypha" and the
rest "pseudepigrapha"; Catholics reserve "apocrypha" for what Protestants call pseudepigrapha. The wiki
keeps these straight structurally through `canon_scope` buckets rather than a single label.

## Major Works

[[1-enoch|1 Enoch]], [[jubilees|Jubilees]], 4 Ezra and 2 Baruch (apocalypses responding to 70 CE), the
Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs, the Assumption/Testament of Moses (also cited in [[jude|Jude]]), and
many more — the literary world *between* the Hebrew Bible and the Mishnah.
