# Peace (Bahá'í Teachings)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 24167–24267.*

## The Two Peaces

In his letters to rulers and elsewhere, [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] appealed for world peace, referring both to a 'Great Peace' (*ṣulḥ-i-akbar*, translated by [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] as the 'Lesser Peace') and a 'Most Great Peace' (*ṣulḥ-i-a'ẓam*). Peace was the chief means for the protection of humanity (ESW 30) (lines 24167–24174).

**Most Great Peace**: The union of all the world's peoples in one common faith — the 'sovereign remedy' for the healing of the world. This was the remedy provided by religion. However, since the world's rulers had refused the Most Great Peace by not accepting Bahá'u'lláh, they should instead strive to establish the Lesser Peace (lines 24175–24152).

**Lesser Peace**: A political peace between nations. An 'all-embracing' assembly attended by the world's rulers must be held to establish its foundations, and the great powers must become reconciled. Thereafter, peace would be upheld by collective security — all other nations acting together against any aggressor. This would obviate the need for armaments, apart from quantities required to maintain internal order. The ministers of the House of Justice must also promote this peace (ESW 30–1; TB 165, 89) (lines 24183–24204).

## 'Abdu'l-Bahá's Teachings

[[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] stated that true civilization would only be established when world rulers came together 'with firm resolve and clear vision' to establish universal peace through a binding treaty. This treaty would delimit international frontiers, define international relations, and restrict each nation's military forces. Breach of the agreement would lead to international intervention against any aggressor. Under such circumstances, governments would need only a small force for internal order, removing the need for arms races. Human energies could instead be devoted to the well-being of the world's peoples (SDC 64–7) (lines 24205–24226).

'Abdu'l-Bahá emphasized that peace work needed to be combined with wider societal reconstruction: expansion of democratic government, education, enfranchisement and growing political power of women were of major importance in bringing about international peace (PUP 108, 134–5, 167, 375). He also predicted that excessive military expenditure would ultimately force nations to seek peace; stressed that disarmament should be multilateral; and called on the United States and Canada to take the lead (lines 24227–24244).

## Shoghi Effendi's Schema

Shoghi Effendi reiterated these teachings, distinguishing between:
- The **Lesser Peace**: to be established by the governments of the world
- The **Most Great Peace**: to be established by the expansion of the Faith, characterized by development of the 'Bahá'í World Commonwealth' (see [[world-order-bahai|World Order]])

In 1985 the Universal House of Justice issued its 'peace statement', *The Promise of World Peace*, a forceful expression of the Bahá'í 'peace programme', addressed to the peoples of the world and widely distributed to world leaders (lines 24245–24261).
