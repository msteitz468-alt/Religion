# Neo-Platonism

Neo-Platonism is the late-antique development of [[plato|Platonism]], founded by [[plotinus|Plotinus]] (205–270) and incorporating Aristotelian, Stoic, and Oriental elements, which became the dominant philosophy of the third- to sixth-century Mediterranean world and the principal philosophical idiom of patristic theology. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. I.

## Core Structure

Reality is a hierarchy descending by [[emanation]] from a first principle:
1. **The One** — beyond being and mind, ineffably simple, the source to which all strives to return.
2. **Mind / Nous** — the second hypostasis, containing the world of [[plato|Forms]], identified with Plato's Demiurge.
3. **Soul** — the third hypostasis, with a higher aspect akin to Mind and a lower aspect (Nature) animating the phenomenal world; individual souls are emanations of the World Soul.

Matter "in itself is darkness or non-being, and as such is evil," yet the visible cosmos, ordered by the higher soul, is "the best of all possible worlds." The soul's goal is a three-stage ascent — purification, philosophical ascent to Mind, and finally **mystical union with the One** in ecstasy.

## Influence on Christianity

Neo-Platonism shaped [[origen]], the [[cappadocian-fathers|Cappadocians]], [[augustine]] (via [[marius-victorinus|Marius Victorinus]]), and [[pseudo-dionysius|Pseudo-Dionysius]]. Kelly notes that the loss of "the Neo-Platonic vision of the same reality existing at different levels" is precisely what drove the [[arianism|Arians]] to deny the Son's divinity. Contrast the earlier [[middle-platonists|Middle Platonism]].
