# Logos-sarx vs Logos-anthropos Christology

Kelly's organising typology for fourth- and fifth-century Christology: two rival models of how the Word united himself with humanity, broadly corresponding to the [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian]] and [[theoria|Antiochene]] schools. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. XI–XII.

## The Two Models

**Word-flesh (Logos-sarx)** — "the incarnation as the union of the Word with human flesh," making no allowance (or no theological room) for a human soul in Christ; the Word is the sole governing principle (*hēgemōn*) and subject of all Christ's experiences. Premise: the Platonic view of man as a body animated by an alien soul. Associated with **Alexandria** — the [[arianism|Arians]], [[athanasius|Athanasius]], [[apollinarius-of-laodicea|Apollinarius]], and the earlier [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]].

**Word-man (Logos-anthropos)** — "the Word united Himself with a complete humanity, including a soul as well as a body," the human soul being the principle of Christ's saving acts and the subject of his sufferings. Premise: the Aristotelian view of man as a psycho-physical unity. Associated with **Antioch** — Eustathius, [[diodore-of-tarsus|Diodore]], [[theodore-of-mopsuestia|Theodore]], [[nestorius|Nestorius]], [[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret]].

## Why It Mattered

The Word-flesh scheme made it easy for the Arians to attribute Christ's ignorance and weakness directly to a creaturely Word; Athanasius used the same scheme but referred those experiences to "the flesh." Its dangers surfaced in **[[apollinarius-of-laodicea|Apollinarianism]]** (the Word replacing the human mind) — answered by [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory of Nazianzus]]'s axiom "**what has not been assumed cannot be restored**." The Word-man scheme protected Christ's full humanity but risked dividing him into "two Sons" ([[nestorianism]]). [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedon]] (451) sought to hold both: one *hypostasis* in two natures.
