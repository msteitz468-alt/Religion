# Brahm Gyani (God-Conscious Being)

**Brahm Gyani** (from Sanskrit *Brahma-Jñānī*, "knower of Brahman/God") in [[sikhism|Sikhism]] refers to the spiritually perfected person — one who has attained full awareness of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] and in whom that awareness is constant and unshaken. The concept receives its most sustained and definitive treatment in the 8th Ashtapadī of [[sukhmani-sahib|Sukhmani Sahib]] (Angs 273–274), which develops the portrait through a sustained series of natural similes.

## Portrait in Sukhmani Sahib (Ashtapadī 8)

> "The God-conscious being is always unattached, as the lotus in the water remains detached." (L60942–60948, Ang 273)

> "The God-conscious being is the highest of the high. Within his own mind, he is the most humble of all." (L61001–61006, Ang 273)

> "O Nanak, the God-conscious being is Himself the Supreme Lord God." (L61145–61148, Ang 274)

The Ashtapadī develops these qualities through further similes: the Brahm Gyani is unstained like the sun, impartial and life-giving like the wind, patient and supporting like the earth, free of all sorrow, not bound by karma, and knows only God as the source of all things. The final couplet makes the highest identification: the Brahm Gyani does not merely know God — the Brahm Gyani *is* God. This non-dual culmination echoes the [[naam|Naam]]-theology in which meditation on the Name ultimately merges the devotee into the divine.

## Relation to Jivan Mukta

The Brahm Gyani is closely related to the *Jivan Mukta* (liberated while alive; Ashtapadī 14 of Sukhmani Sahib): as is joy, so is sorrow; as is gold, so is dust; as is nectar, so is poison; as is honor, so is dishonor — the Jivan Mukta sees all equally, abiding in God's Will. The Brahm Gyani is the theological-devotional term for this same state, emphasizing the *knowledge* dimension; Jivan Mukta emphasizes the *liberation* dimension.

## Relation to Gurmukh

The Brahm Gyani is the fullest realization of the [[gurmukh-manmukh|Gurmukh]] orientation — the person turned fully toward the Guru/God rather than toward the self. The Brahm Gyani has so fully transcended [[haumai|Haumai]] (ego) that no trace of self-orientation remains.
