# The New Law (Law of the Gospel) — Aquinas

The climax of [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]'s Treatise on Law ([[summa-theologica|*Summa
Theologiae*]] I-II, Q106–108) is the **New Law** or **Law of the Gospel**, which completes the
analysis of [[natural-law|the kinds of law]] and joins it to [[grace]].

## The New Law is chiefly the grace of the Holy Spirit (Q106, A1)

Aquinas's central thesis: "that which is preponderant in the law of the New Testament, and whereon
all its efficacy is based, is **the grace of the Holy Ghost, which is given through faith in
Christ**. Consequently **the New Law is chiefly the grace itself of the Holy Ghost**" (Q106, A1). It
is therefore an **interior law inscribed on the heart** (Jer. 31:33; Heb. 8:10), not primarily a
written code — Augustine (*De Spiritu et Littera*): "What else are the Divine laws written by God
Himself on our hearts, but the very presence of His Holy Spirit?" Only **secondarily** is it a
written law (the Gospel documents and sacraments), which *dispose* us to receive that grace and
direct its use. It is distinct from the [[natural-law|natural law]] (also "in the heart") because it
is added by a *gift of grace* that both indicates what to do and *helps* accomplish it.

## The New Law justifies (Q106, A2)

Because its chief element is the inwardly bestowed grace of the Holy Spirit, **the New Law
justifies** — "it is the power of God unto salvation to everyone that believeth" (Rom. 1:16) —
whereas the Old Law's written precepts, of themselves, did not confer the grace they required (cf.
[[grace]], [[justification]]). Its secondary, written element does not justify of itself.

## The New Law compared with the Old (Q107)

The two are **not specifically distinct** — they share the same end, subjection to God — but differ
as **perfect to imperfect**: the Old Law was a "pedagogue of children" (Gal. 3:24), restraining "the
hand, not the will," with fear and temporal promises; the New is the "law of charity," pouring out
the Holy Spirit and love (Rom. 5:5). The New Law **fulfils** (does not abolish) the Old (Matt.
5:17), supplying by Christ's Passion what the Old foreshadowed — contained in the Old "virtually, as
the corn in the ear." It is **lighter** in external observances yet more demanding interiorly, the
grace it confers making the burden light (Q107, A4).

## What the New Law contains; the evangelical counsels (Q108)

The New Law prescribes or forbids only what is essential to receiving or rightly using grace — the
**sacraments** and the **moral precepts** bound up with virtue — leaving the rest to discretion;
hence it is a "law of perfect liberty" (Jas. 1:25), its interior life ordered by the Sermon on the
Mount. Beyond its **precepts** (binding all, necessary for salvation) it adds **counsels** (Q108,
A4): the **evangelical counsels** of **poverty**, **perpetual chastity**, and **obedience** —
optional means to reach the end more surely, renouncing respectively the lust of the eyes, of the
flesh, and the pride of life (1 Jn. 2:16), and forming the basis of the religious life ("If thou wilt
be perfect…", Matt. 19:21).
