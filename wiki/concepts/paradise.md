# Paradise (*pairidaeza* → *paradeisos*)

**Paradise** is, etymologically, an **Iranian** word. The Old Persian / Avestan
**\*pairidaeza** ("a walled enclosure," *pairi-* "around" + *daeza* "wall") named the
[[persians|Achaemenid]] **enclosed garden or park** — preserved in Elamite *partetash* —
which **Xenophon** and other Greeks rendered as **παράδεισος (*paradeisos*)**, "a
paradise." From Greek it passed into the **Septuagint** (for the Garden of Eden, Gen.
2–3), into Latin and the New Testament (Luke 23.43; 2 Cor. 12.4; Rev. 2.7), and into
Arabic (*firdaws*), shaping the imagery of the heavenly garden across the Abrahamic
traditions.

## The Persian Garden as Theology

Per [[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]], the Achaemenid emphasis on cultivating
crown and private lands as **orchards and produce-gardens** was not merely aesthetic: the
royal garden materialized the king's task of generating **increase, abundance, and
order** ([[asha-druj|asha]]) — the *shiyati* ("happiness, well-being") that prevails when
the rule of [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] predominates. The well-watered, walled,
fruit-bearing enclosure was a **microcosm of the good creation** and an image of the
flourishing the righteous bring about (and will enjoy at the end).

A grimmer cognate is the Avestan **pairidaeza** of the *Videvdad* — an enclosure built on
barren land to **contain pollution** — showing the same "walled space" logic applied to
the boundary between the pure and the impure.

## Eschatological Paradise

The garden imagery feeds the Zoroastrian "best existence" (Avestan *vahishta ahu*; MP
*vahisht*, NP *behesht*) — the luminous reward of the [[chinvat-bridge|righteous soul]]
beyond the Chinvat Bridge — and its counterpart, the "House of Song." This optimistic,
**embodied** picture of a restored and fruitful world (the goal of
[[frashokereti|Frashokereti]]) is among the features comparatists weigh when tracing
Iranian influence on later **heaven/garden** eschatology, recorded without overclaiming
on [[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]].
