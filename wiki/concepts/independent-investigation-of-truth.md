# Independent Investigation of Truth

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17950–17983.*

## Definition

Independent investigation of truth is a basic Bahá'í principle, repeatedly stressed by [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]. Everyone should investigate truth for themselves rather than following tradition and the beliefs of others; they should depend on their own perceptions and be guided by their own consciences; they should use the power of [[reason|reason]], itself "a divine creation and gift" (l. 17950–17962).

## Arguments

In the investigation of religion, seekers should seek divine assistance for themselves (l. 17962–17963). "As reality is one, such investigation will lead to religious unity and human solidarity" (l. 17963–17965). The blind following of the past led historically to the rejection of the successive [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestations of God]] (l. 17966–17969). Unthinking imitation of the past stunts the mind; independent enquiry frees society from endlessly repeating the mistakes of the past (l. 17970–17973).

## Contrast with Shí'í *taqlíd*

There is an implicit contrast here with the Shí'í principle of *taqlíd* ("imitation"), whereby the ordinary believer is considered to need to follow a learned specialist (*mujtahid*) in matters of religious law (l. 17974–17978). 'Abdu'l-Bahá was rejecting *taqlíd* and the clerical status of certain *'ulamá* (the mujtahids) which rested on it (l. 17979–17983).

## Relationship to Authority

This principle coexists in some tension with the Bahá'í insistence on religious authority (the [[covenant-bahai|Covenant]], [[infallibility-bahai|infallibility]], and obedience to Bahá'í institutions). Smith notes that for "the great majority of Bahá'ís the coexistence of these 'liberal' and 'authoritarian' elements within their Faith has probably been unproblematic: the two elements are seen as component parts of a single religious system" (l. 17360–17367). Difficulties with this tension have arisen particularly in Western Bahá'í communities (l. 17370–17391).
