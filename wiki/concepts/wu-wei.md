# Wu-wei

**Wu-wei** ("non-action," "non-doing") is the central practical ideal of [[daoism|Daoism]] — *not*
inactivity or passivity, but **effortless, spontaneous action in accord with the [[dao|Dao]]**: acting
with the grain of things rather than against it, so that nothing is forced and nothing is left undone.

## Meaning

The [[daodejing|Daodejing]] pairs it with the paradox *wei wu wei* ("act without acting") and the maxim
that the sage ruler governs best by **not interfering**, letting things follow their [[ziran|ziran]]
(self-so nature). The [[zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]] illustrates it through **skill stories** — Cook Ding's blade
glides through the ox along the natural openings, never dulling — where mastery looks like effortlessness
because the agent has aligned with the pattern of the situation.

## Cross-Tradition Note

Wu-wei is a distinctive contribution with no exact parallel: it contrasts with the **striving** of
Confucian self-cultivation and ritual effort (*[[analects|li]]*), with the **disciplined effort**
(*samadhi*) of the Buddhist [[four-noble-truths|path]], and with the Abrahamic ethic of obedient *doing*
of commandments. The nearest resonance is grace/letting-go motifs in mystical traditions. A candidate
comparison page on effort vs. effortlessness in the spiritual life.
