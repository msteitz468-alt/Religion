# Tian and Shangdi (Heaven and the High God)

**Tiān** 天 ("Heaven") and **Shàngdì** 上帝 ("the High / Supreme Lord") are the names of the **ultimate
divine principle** in Chinese religion — the moral order and source of authority **above even the
[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]** and the [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]].

- **Shangdi** is the older term: the **high god of the Shang dynasty** (2nd millennium BCE), a personal
  supreme deity and divine ancestor who governs the cosmos, sends weather and harvest, and grants victory.
- **Tian** rose to prominence under the **Zhou**, who justified their conquest by the **Mandate of Heaven
  (Tiānmìng)**: Heaven is a **moral power** that confers the right to rule on the virtuous and **withdraws it
  from the corrupt**, legitimating dynastic change. Tian is often **less personal** than Shangdi — more an
  impersonal, all-seeing moral order — though the two terms were frequently equated.

## In the traditions

- **[[confucianism|Confucianism]]**: Confucius speaks of **Tian** as the source of his mission and of the moral
  order; Heaven is the guarantor of righteousness, often impersonal but responsive to virtue.
- **Imperial state religion**: the emperor, the **"Son of Heaven" (Tiānzǐ)**, alone sacrificed to Heaven (at
  the **Temple of Heaven**), holding the realm by Heaven's mandate — the apex of the
  [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]].
- **[[chinese-folk-religion|Folk religion]]**: ordinary people invoke **"Lǎo Tiān Yé"** ("Old Heavenly
  Grandfather") and treat the Jade Emperor as Heaven's personal administrator; Tian remains the impartial
  background justice behind the whole pantheon.

Because **Tian / Shangdi** stand for a near-monotheistic supreme principle, Christian missionaries (the
**"Term Question"**) debated whether **Shangdi** or **Tianzhu** ("Lord of Heaven") best renders the biblical
God — a controversy noted in [[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner]].
