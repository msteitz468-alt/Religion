# Naam (the Divine Name)

**Naam** (Punjabi/Sanskrit: *nāma*, "name") is the central soteriological concept of [[sikhism|Sikhism]]: the Divine Name or divine reality that pervades all creation and whose internalization (*[[nam-simaran|nām simaraṇ]]*) constitutes the path to liberation (*mukti*). In the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]], Naam is simultaneously the Name of God and God's self-revelation present within all reality. It is received through the [[the-guru|Guru]]'s grace ([[nadar|nadar]]) and meditated upon as the means of overcoming [[haumai|haumai]] (ego) and escaping *māyā*.

## Naam in Japji Sahib

Pauri 19 of [[japji|Japji Sahib]] makes the cosmic scope of the Naam explicit (Thind/Khalsa translation, Ang 4):

> From the Word, comes the Naam; from the Word, comes Your Praise. From the Word, comes spiritual wisdom, singing the Songs of Your Glory. From the Word, come the written and spoken words and hymns. From the Word, comes destiny, written on one's forehead. (L740–757)

> The created universe is the manifestation of Your Name. Without Your Name, there is no place at all. (L774–779)

The Naam is therefore not merely a devotional name but the ontological ground of all creation. The universe exists *as* the manifestation of the Naam.

Pauri 20 establishes Naam as the sole cleanser of the sin-stained mind: "when the intellect is stained and polluted by sin, it can only be cleansed by the Love of the Name" (L809–812). Pauri 4 connects Naam to the daily Sikh discipline: "In the Amrit Vaylaa, the ambrosial hours before dawn, chant the True Name, and contemplate His Glorious Greatness" (L214–222).

In the Sodar section, Mehl 1 states: "Chanting it [the Name], I live; forgetting it, I die" — the Naam as the breath of spiritual life. Mehl 4 (Raag Goojaree): "Through the Guru's Teachings, the Naam is my breath of life" (L2157–2162, Ang 9).

## Naam and Nadar

The Naam is not obtained by individual effort but by [[nadar|Nadar]] (divine grace). "By His Grace, the Gate of Liberation is found" (Japji Pauri 4). "By His Glance of Grace, He bestows His Blessings" (Japji Pauri 24). This grace-priority distinguishes Sikh soteriology from karma-based soteriologies: karma provides the body and social station; only Nadar opens the door to the Naam.

## Naam and Haumai

[[Haumai]] (ego) is the opposite of Naam-consciousness: Japji Pauri 2 establishes that understanding [[hukam|Hukam]] dissolves haumai, and throughout the Granth, Naam-simaran is the practice by which haumai is progressively overcome. The Manmukh (self-willed person) forgets the Naam; the Gurmukh (Guru-oriented person) lives by it.
