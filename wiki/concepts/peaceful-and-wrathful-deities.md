# The Peaceful and Wrathful Deities (zhi-khro)

The **peaceful and wrathful deities** (Tibetan *zhi-khro*) are the **hundred deities** —
**forty-two peaceful and fifty-eight wrathful** — who constitute the maṇḍala at the heart of the
[[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]] and the wider [[nyingma|Nyingma]] tradition. Their
locus classicus is the **Guhyagarbha Tantra**; in the cycle they reside within the practitioner's
own body (the peaceful in the heart, the wrathful in the skull/brain) and arise in the
[[bardo|bardo of reality]] after death.

## What They Are

The maṇḍala "represents a perfected state of being": the deities are the **natural purity and
natural transformation of the meditator's own awareness, aggregates, elements, and sensory
processes**. The **peaceful** deities embody the quiescent natural purity (*ka-dag*) of these
components; the **wrathful** deities embody their dynamic transformation. The text insists
repeatedly that they "have not arisen extraneously" — they are projections of one's own mind.

## The Forty-Two Peaceful Deities

- The primordial buddha-couple **Samantabhadra and Samantabhadrī** in union (awareness and
  emptiness; the [[trikaya|dharmakāya]]).
- The **five male buddhas** (Vairocana, Akṣobhya-Vajrasattva, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha,
  Amoghasiddhi) — the natural purity of the five aggregates, each carrying a
  [[five-pristine-cognitions|pristine cognition]] — and the **five female buddhas**
  (Ākāśadhātvīśvarī, Buddhalocanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī, Samayatārā) — the natural purity of the
  five elements.
- **Eight male and eight female bodhisattvas** (the classes and objects of consciousness), **four
  male and four female gatekeepers**, and the **six emanational sages** of the
  [[six-realms|six realms]].

## The Fifty-Eight Wrathful Deities

- The **six male herukas** (Mahottara, Buddha, Vajra, Ratna, Padma, Karma Heruka) — wrathful
  forms of the male buddhas, "blood-drinkers" (*khrag-'thung*) symbolising the dynamic
  transformation of the dissonant mental states — with their six **Krodheśvarī** consorts.
- The **eight Mātaraḥ** (Gaurī etc.), the **eight animal-headed Piśācī** (Siṃhamukhī etc.), the
  **four wrathful gatekeepers**, and the **twenty-eight Īśvarī** (the "queens of yoga," in four
  directional groups enacting the four rites).

Their terrifying forms are adopted "in order to actively confront deep-seated egohood"; wrath
here is the "natural transformative process of buddha-mind," not anger.

## Significance

To **recognise** these deities as one's own awareness in the bardo of reality is to attain the
sambhogakāya. Two root mantras unify them: *OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ BODHICITTA…* (the forty-two peaceful) and
*OṂ RULU RULU HŪṂ BHYOḤ HŪṂ* (the fifty-eight wrathful).
