# Typology

Typology is the interpretive technique for bringing out the correspondence between the two Testaments, in which "the events and personages of the Old were 'types' of, i.e. prefigured and anticipated, the events and personages of the New." Kelly calls it "the characteristically Christian" method of reading the Old Testament. This account draws on [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. III, and (for Hebrews) [[gareth-lee-cockerill|Cockerill]]’s NICNT reading.

## History Taken Seriously

The decisive feature of typology, distinguishing it from [[allegorical-reading|allegory]], is that it does **not** abolish the literal-historical sense. The typologist "took history seriously; it was the scene of the progressive unfolding of God's consistent redemptive purpose," the earlier stages being "shadows or…rough preliminary sketches of the later." Precisely because the Old Testament events "had really happened on the plane of history," they could be read "by the eye of faith as trustworthy pointers to God's future dealings with men."

Typology is not a Christian invention: Deutero-Isaiah already construed Israel's past (Exodus, wilderness) as types of a coming deliverance. But it became the standard Christian answer to the [[marcionites|Marcionite]] severing of the Testaments, grounding the unity of the two Testaments in a single divine plan.

## Standard Correspondences

Universally agreed patristic types include: Adam and Moses → Christ; the flood → baptism and judgment; the sacrifice of Isaac (the *Akedah*) → Calvary; the crossing of the Red Sea and the manna → baptism and the Eucharist; the fall of Jericho → the end of the world. [[justin-martyr|Justin]] read Leah and Rachel as the Synagogue and the Church; [[origen]] held that "the Jewish mystery in its entirety was, as it were, a rehearsal of the Christian mystery."

## Typology and Theoria

The Antiochene school refined typology into the disciplined method of [[theoria]], insisting (against [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]]) that the spiritual sense never dissolve the historical base. [[john-chrysostom|Chrysostom]] gave the classic definition of a type: **"a prophecy expressed in terms of things"** (*hē dia pragmatōn prophēteia*).

## Cockerill (NICNT) — Hebrews: continuity-and-fulfillment; Aaron type (2026-07-18)

Attributed to [[gareth-lee-cockerill|Gareth Lee Cockerill]] ([[cockerill-hebrews-nicnt|Hebrews NICNT]]). Digest R2 (Introduction: Message); corroborating material in R6–R8.

**Definition in Hebrews.** For Cockerill, the old/new relationship falls within general typology: OT institutions, persons, and events through which God redeemed were types foreshadowing Christ; correspondence yet superior fulfillment; types supply pattern/structure/vocabulary. In Hebrews the types are **revelatory and anticipatory but devoid of ultimate saving efficacy** (R2 L1525).

**Aaron, not Melchizedek, is the type.** Load-bearing distinction: **Aaron is the type Christ fulfilled; Melchizedek provides Scriptural evidence for that fulfillment** (R2 L1515). Because the Son is “like” Melchizedek (7:15–19) he is superior to Aaron and fulfills what Aaron foreshadowed. Using “typology” for both of these almost opposite relationships (Melchizedek-as-pattern of eternity vs. Aaron-as-earthly-copy of high-priestly approach) introduces confusion (R2 L1525; R6 L2774). Melchizedek stands above both the history of the faithful and Old Covenant institutions — neither example to follow nor antiquated institution; his function is determined by Ps 110:4. Without him there would be no adequate Scriptural substantiation for the Son’s high priesthood (R2 L1515). See [[melchizedek|Melchizedek]], [[aaron|Aaron]], [[priesthood-of-christ|Priesthood of Christ]].

**Correspondence + inadequacy.** Pentateuchal descriptions supply both basis for correspondence with Christ’s work and signs of the old order’s inability to provide salvation: the old foreshadowed entrance into God’s presence through cleansing by effective sacrifice and competent high priest administering a covenant; inadequacy shown by earthly Tent only, once a year, only for the high priest (R2 L1503). Interlocking images — Sinai, Promised Land, Tent — frame the sermon (R2 L1499). “Pattern and shadow” (8:5), “parable” (9:9), and “type” language: insufficient means of salvation **and** God-designed anticipations of heavenly entry, New Covenant, and conscience-cleansing blood — not discarded history nor Philonic eternal forms (R7 L3034, 3074, 3086, 3180).

**Continuity-and-fulfillment, not continuity-and-discontinuity.** Against Graham Hughes and others who treat the first pair of hermeneutical principles (Pentateuchal descriptions + new-order promises) as establishing discontinuity: Cockerill holds that the typological relationship is one of **continuity**. The OT’s own testimony shows the Old Covenant was never intended to be final — always typological and anticipatory. Relationship best described as **continuity and fulfillment**. “Abolished” (7:18) means the faithful must no longer practice old sacrifices since Christ fulfilled them; continuing them would deny the continuity Christ established through fulfillment (R2 L1521–1523). One people of God throughout history; the faithful of Heb 11 are **examples**, not types of a new people (R2 L1525).

**Moses as type-witness; people not types.** Through describing the Old Covenant and early history, Moses bears “witness to the things that would be spoken” in the Son (3:5) (R2 L1497). Moses foreshadows the Pioneer role; Aaron typifies high priesthood (R3 on Sinai frame). OT people of God are not a type of the new — one people; disobedient (wilderness, Esau) are apostates to avoid, not typological “Israel” replaced (R2 L1505).

## Woudstra (NICOT Joshua) — rest / Joshua–Jesus; restrained typology

Attributed to [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] ([[woudstra-joshua-nicot]]; Introduction VI; digests A, G). Once the land’s “essentially spiritual” character is clear, symbolico-typical significance is graspable (Christian hymnody’s heavenly rest; Liberation theology’s Canaan as righting wrongs). Traditional typology needs caution against early-church extremes, but Scripture-permitted use stands:

> "The book of Hebrews draws a parallel between Joshua and Jesus, and between that which Joshua accomplished and that which Jesus has accomplished (Heb. 4:8). The common context is that of “rest,” a term which is very significant theologically."

Typology must rest on symbolism already present for ancient Israelites; it is only one among links between Testaments — not the sole present use of Joshua’s revelation. Rest has “ever expanding horizons” (Exod 33:14; Deut 12:9–10; Josh 1:13, 15; 21:44; 22:4; 23:1 → David/Solomon → Heb 4). Rest from enemies is a foretaste of the Messianic kingdom (on Josh 23:1). Woudstra rejects Origen-style excess allegorization of Canaanite cities as vices while affirming Heb 4:8’s restrained Joshua–Jesus rest typology. See [[promised-land|Promised Land]], [[joshua|Joshua]], [[herem-canaanite-conquest|Ḥerem controversy]] (Position 5).
