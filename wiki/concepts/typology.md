# Typology

Typology is the interpretive technique for bringing out the correspondence between the two Testaments, in which "the events and personages of the Old were 'types' of, i.e. prefigured and anticipated, the events and personages of the New." Kelly calls it "the characteristically Christian" method of reading the Old Testament. This account draws on [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. III.

## History Taken Seriously

The decisive feature of typology, distinguishing it from [[allegorical-reading|allegory]], is that it does **not** abolish the literal-historical sense. The typologist "took history seriously; it was the scene of the progressive unfolding of God's consistent redemptive purpose," the earlier stages being "shadows or…rough preliminary sketches of the later." Precisely because the Old Testament events "had really happened on the plane of history," they could be read "by the eye of faith as trustworthy pointers to God's future dealings with men."

Typology is not a Christian invention: Deutero-Isaiah already construed Israel's past (Exodus, wilderness) as types of a coming deliverance. But it became the standard Christian answer to the [[marcionites|Marcionite]] severing of the Testaments, grounding the unity of the two Testaments in a single divine plan.

## Standard Correspondences

Universally agreed patristic types include: Adam and Moses → Christ; the flood → baptism and judgment; the sacrifice of Isaac (the *Akedah*) → Calvary; the crossing of the Red Sea and the manna → baptism and the Eucharist; the fall of Jericho → the end of the world. [[justin-martyr|Justin]] read Leah and Rachel as the Synagogue and the Church; [[origen]] held that "the Jewish mystery in its entirety was, as it were, a rehearsal of the Christian mystery."

## Typology and Theoria

The Antiochene school refined typology into the disciplined method of [[theoria]], insisting (against [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]]) that the spiritual sense never dissolve the historical base. [[john-chrysostom|Chrysostom]] gave the classic definition of a type: **"a prophecy expressed in terms of things"** (*hē dia pragmatōn prophēteia*).
