# The Amesha Spentas (the "Holy/Bounteous Immortals")

The **Amesha Spentas** (Avestan, "Holy/Bounteous Immortals") are the six great divine beings whom
[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] evokes through his Holy Spirit **Spenta Mainyu** — forming, with Ahura
Mazda himself, a **heptad** of seven that creates and governs the world. The doctrine of the six
Holy Immortals is, in [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce's]] account,
**fundamental to [[zarathustra|Zoroaster's]] teaching**.

## *Spenta* and the Hypostasis of Divine Attributes

The key word **spenta** — used of Ahura Mazda and all his creation — basically means "possessing
power (to aid)," hence "furthering, benefiting"; through religious use it acquired the overtones of
English **"holy."** The Amesha Spentas **hypostatize qualities or aspects of Ahura Mazda**: divine
attributes isolated and worshipped as independent beings, a move (Boyce notes) already
characteristic of the old Iranian religion (Mithra surrounded by Friendship, Obedience, Justice,
etc.). They remain "of one mind, one voice, one act" with the supreme Lord — described in one
Pahlavi image as torches lit from a single torch.

## The Six (with their Creations)

Each Holy Immortal is linked to one of the **seven creations**, so that the physical thing can
stand for the divinity that guards it:

- **Vohu Manah** — "Good Purpose / Good Mind" (guardian of cattle); the Being who first led
  Zoroaster into Ahura Mazda's presence.
- **Asha Vahishta** — "Best [[asha-druj|Truth/Righteousness]]" (fire); the hypostasis of asha.
- **Khshathra (Vairya)** — "(Desirable) Dominion / Power" (the metal sky, stone); guardian of
  warriors.
- **(Spenta) Armaiti** — "Holy Devotion / Right-Mindedness" (the earth); guardian of herdsmen,
  the virtue of humble submission.
- **Haurvatat** — "Wholeness / Health" (the waters).
- **Ameretat** — "Immortality / Long-life" (plants).

Ahura Mazda himself, often associated with **humankind** (and with the priests who represent
humanity at the *yasna*), completes the seven. A worshipper should invoke **all** of them to
"become a perfect man," and should bring them so fully into his own being that no room remains for
vice.

## From Gathic Web to Fixed Heptad (Rose)

[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]] traces a **development**: in the
[[gathas|Gathas]] *asha*, *vohu manah*, and *armaiti* appear as abstract qualities in
intimate relationship with [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] "as children to a parent" —
**emanations of the one light** rather than a fixed, numbered group (the very phrase
*amesha spenta* is absent from the Gathas, appearing first, in inverted order, in the
*Yasna Haptanghaiti*). Only in the **Young Avesta** do they become a **defined group of
six** (Ahura Mazda counted separately to make seven), each now firmly **paired with one
of the good creations** — asha vahishta with fire/light, vohu manah with cattle, khshathra
with the metal sky, armaiti with the earth, haurvatat with water, ameretat with plants.
Rose likewise distinguishes **spenta mainyu** (in the Gathas, Ahura Mazda's own inner
"beneficent inspiration"; in the yashts, more of a separate power) from its contrary
**[[angra-mainyu|angra mainyu]]**.

## The Yazatas

Beneath the great six, the heptad in turn evokes the **Yazatas** ("those worthy of worship") — the
many lesser beneficent divinities, largely the gods of the old Iranian pantheon brought into
Zoroaster's good creation (Mithra, the *fravashis*, Sun, Moon, the waters). The Amesha Spentas are
the supreme tier of this graded host of good beings under the one God.

## Significance and Influence

The heptad gives Zoroastrianism a **ranked celestial hierarchy** radiating from a single supreme
God — structurally the prototype most often cited for later Jewish, Christian, and Islamic
**angelology** (ranked archangels under God), recorded without overclaiming on
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]]. The association of each
Immortal with a concrete creation also undergirds the Zoroastrian **ethic of caring for the
material world** (water, earth, plants, animals, fire) as religious duty.
