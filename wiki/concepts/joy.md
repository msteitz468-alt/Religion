# Joy (in Philippians)

**Joy** (*chara* / *chairō*) is the emotional-theological keynote of [[philippians|Philippians]]. [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]] (PNTC) counts **fourteen** joy notes in the letter and treats joy not as temperament but as **confidence in God** who began and will complete the good work of [[partnership|*koinōnia*]] (1:4–6).

## Hansen’s profile

- First sounded in thanksgiving prayer (1:4): petitions combined with thanksgiving yield joy.
- Compatible with chains, envy among preachers (1:18), and being poured out as a drink offering (2:17–18) — reciprocal joy shared between apostle and church.
- Climactic imperatives: “Rejoice in the Lord always” (4:4); gentleness, prayer against anxiety, and the peace of God (4:5–7) surround the joy commands.
- Friendship-letter motif: friends share common joys (Hansen’s Hellenistic parallels).

Joy is thus ecclesial and theological: rooted in the gospel’s advance, partnership, and the story of Christ ([[christ-hymn|Christ hymn]]), not in the absence of suffering.
