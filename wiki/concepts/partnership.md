# Partnership (*koinōnia*) in Philippians

**Partnership** translates Greek *koinōnia* — a major structural theme of [[philippians|Philippians]] in [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]]’s PNTC. Hansen stresses that *koinōnia* covers a spectrum of close relationships “involving mutual interests and sharing” (friendship, family, business, citizenship, cult associations) and that a **major purpose of the letter** is to transform *koinōnia* in light of life in Christ.

## Pauline uses in the letter (Hansen)

| Locus | Hansen’s emphasis |
|---|---|
| **1:5** | Partnership in the gospel “from the first day until now” — friendship sharing common faith *and* participation in salvation benefits; not a false dichotomy of “spiritual participation” vs “mission support” |
| **1:7** | Co-partners in grace (including chains / defense and confirmation of the gospel) |
| **2:1** | *Koinōnia* of the Spirit among the four “if” certainties grounding unity |
| **3:10** | *Koinōnia* of Christ’s sufferings — knowing Christ includes shared path of cross |
| **4:14–15** | Philippians alone shared in “giving and receiving”; financial support as gospel partnership, not mere remittance |

## Friendship-letter frame

Hansen reads Philippians broadly as a **letter of friendship** that also functions as deliberative speech for unity. Among Hellenistic friendship motifs he finds in the letter: affection, *koinōnia*, unity of soul/spirit, like-mindedness, yokefellow, giving and receiving, common struggles and joys, absence/presence, virtue friendship. Paul, Hansen argues, **redefines** these ideals in Christ rather than merely echoing Greco-Roman ethics.

Aristotle’s maxim that “all friendship involves *koinōnia*” is cited as cultural background for the partnership language.

## Gift and thanksgiving

Hansen insists the letter must be read as a **thank-you** from the opening thanksgiving (1:3) through the formal-receipt language of 4:18 (gifts via [[epaphroditus|Epaphroditus]] as fragrant offering). Continuous financial partnership from the “first day” (Macedonia/Philippi support pattern) expresses *koinōnia* without reducing it to money alone.

## Joy

Hansen’s first of fourteen joy notes is tied to *koinōnia*: joy is confidence in the God who began and will complete the good work of partnership (1:4–6).
