# Cultural Digestion

A concept developed by [[rajiv-malhotra]] in [[being-different-malhotra]] to describe the process by which a dominant civilization absorbs elements from a weaker or subordinate civilization **by mapping those elements onto its own framework**, stripping them of their original meanings, and eliminating the source tradition as an independent entity.

## The Tiger-and-Prey Metaphor

Malhotra's central image: when a tiger eats prey, the prey's substance is absorbed into the tiger's body and transformed into the tiger's flesh. The prey ceases to exist as itself. Cultural digestion works analogously — the "digestee" tradition's practices, symbols, or concepts are taken in, renamed, and reconstituted in the dominant civilization's terms, so that the original framework is no longer recognized as a distinct source.

Examples developed in *Being Different*:
- **Yoga** absorbed into Western fitness culture: the practices are retained but the metaphysical framework (*prana*, *bandhu*, liberation from *samsara*) is stripped away and replaced with therapeutic/fitness language.
- **Meditation** → "mindfulness" in clinical psychology: Buddhist *vipassana* or Hindu *dhyana* is operationalized as a stress-reduction technique, severed from its soteriological context.
- **Brahman** recast as "God" or the Judeo-Christian deity: the non-dual, panentheistic character of Brahman is lost; it becomes a personal creator-God.
- **Karma** → "what goes around comes around": stripped of its technical metaphysical content (accumulated *samskara*, rebirth, causal mechanism) and made into a folk morality.
- **Inculturation** (Catholic missionary strategy): Roberto de Nobili (south India, 1608) adopted Brahmin dress, diet, and ritual vocabulary while substituting Christian theology — the structural form is "inculturation" but the effect is digestion: the surface of Hindu practice is used as a vehicle to replace Hindu content.

## Digestion vs. Mutual Influence

Malhotra distinguishes cultural digestion from legitimate cross-cultural exchange:
- **Exchange**: two traditions interact, each modifying itself in response to the other while retaining their distinct identities and frameworks (the "Indra's Net" model of mutual interdependence)
- **Digestion**: one tradition is dismembered and its parts are reconfigured within the other's framework; the original ceases to function as an independent system

The distinction turns on whether the source tradition's own categories are preserved and attributed, or whether they are absorbed and renamed so the source becomes invisible or is declared to have "really" been saying what the absorbing tradition says.

## Difference Anxiety as Driver

Cultural digestion is often triggered by "difference anxiety" — the Abrahamic/Western discomfort with genuine alterity. When a tradition is too different to be dismissed as primitive or too sophisticated to be ignored, the response is often to absorb it: "Hindus are really saying what we are saying, in different words." This produces the perennialist claim ("all religions say the same thing") as a mode of digestion — dharmic distinctiveness is eliminated by assimilation.

## Relationship to Post-Colonial Theory

Malhotra engages Edward Said's *Orientalism* (1979) as partial but insufficient: Said describes the *misrepresentation* of the East, but not the *absorption* mechanism. The post-colonial framework, Malhotra argues, leaves dharmic traditions in a reactive posture — critiquing Western misreadings — without providing the positive re-articulation of dharmic distinctiveness that genuine *purva paksha* requires.
