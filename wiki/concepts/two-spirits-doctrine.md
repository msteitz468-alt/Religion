# Two Spirits Doctrine
A **theological anthropology and cosmology** developed in the Qumran Community Rule (1QS III–IV) asserting that God created two spirits at the foundation of creation, and every human being is assigned — from before birth — to the dominion of one or the other. The doctrine combines strict **monotheism** (both spirits serve under God's sovereignty) with thoroughgoing **moral dualism** and **predestinarianism**.

## The Text
The foundational passage is 1QS III:13–IV:26 (Community Rule, cols. III–IV). Key statement:

> "He created man to govern the world, and appointed for him two spirits in which to walk until the time of His visitation: the spirits of **truth** and of **falsehood**. Those born of truth spring from a fountain of light and those born of falsehood spring from a source of darkness. All the children of righteousness are ruled by the Prince of Light and walk in the ways of light, but all the children of falsehood are ruled by the Angel of Darkness and walk in the ways of darkness."

## Core Elements

### The Two Principals
- **Prince of Light / Angel of Truth** (*Sar ha-Orim* / *Malakh ha-Emet*): ruler of the Sons of Light; assists and protects them
- **Angel of Darkness** (*Malakh ha-Hashekh*): ruler of the Sons of Darkness; causes error even in Sons of Light — "all his allotted spirits seek the overthrow of the sons of light"
- Both are subordinate to God and operate within his sovereign plan

### Character Profiles
**Spirit of Truth** (in Sons of Light): "healing, great peace in a long life, and fruitfulness, together with every everlasting blessing and eternal joy in life without end, a crown of glory and a garment of majesty in unending light"

**Spirit of Falsehood** (in Sons of Darkness): "greediness of hands in slothful magics, wickedness, lying, pride and haughtiness, falsity and deceit, cruelty and abundant evil, ill-temper and much folly and brazen impertinence, horrible works inspired by lust of debauchery"

### Predestination
Every person's spiritual quality is determined from birth by their allotment between the two spirits. This is not earned or chosen. The proportions of light and darkness can vary within a person (explaining degrees of virtue and vice), but the fundamental belonging — Sons of Light vs. Sons of Darkness — is fixed by divine decree before creation.

> "Until now the spirits of truth and falsehood struggle in the hearts of men and they walk in both wisdom and folly. According to his portion of truth so does a man hate falsehood, and according to his inheritance in the realm of falsehood so is he wicked and so hates truth."

### Eschatological Resolution
At the "time of God's visitation" (the final judgment), God will "purify by His truth all the deeds of man and refine for Himself the human frame by rooting out all spirit of falsehood from the bounds of his flesh, and by cleansing him with the Holy Spirit from all wicked deeds. He will destroy the spirit of falsehood forever."

## Relationship to Zoroastrianism
Scholars note strong structural parallels to Zoroastrian theology — the opposition of Ahura Mazda/Spenta Mainyu vs. Angra Mainyu/Ahriman. However, the Qumran doctrine preserves **Jewish monotheism**: both spirits are created by God and operate under his sovereignty. The Zoroastrian opposition is more cosmologically independent. Whether Qumran dualism derives directly from Iranian influence (Babylon exile transmission? Persian period?) or represents parallel development remains debated. The conceptual vocabulary differs significantly.

## Relationship to Early Christianity
The Two Spirits doctrine provides background for several New Testament and early Christian themes:
- **John's Gospel**: "Light has come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light" (John 3:19); "walking in light vs. darkness" (1 John 1–2); Johannine dualism of light and darkness
- **Paul**: "flesh vs. spirit" in Romans 7–8 and Galatians 5 shares structural features (two competing principles within the human person); Paul's predestinarian anthropology (Rom. 9) parallels Qumran's
- **1QS and 1 Thessalonians 5:5**: "You are all sons of light, sons of the day; we belong neither to darkness nor to night" — exact language
- **"Holy Spirit"**: The Qumran texts use "Holy Spirit" in the sense of the divine spirit by which God purifies the chosen at the end of time — an eschatological gift, not a third person of a Trinity

## Relationship to Zoroastrianism (see also [[angra-mainyu]], [[asha-druj]])
The structural parallel (two cosmic principles, light vs. darkness, truth vs. lie) is notable, but the Qumran version maintains monotheistic subordination of both spirits under God's creative sovereignty in a way that the Zoroastrian system does not. The relationship remains a live scholarly question.
