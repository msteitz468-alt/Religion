# Tanzih and Tashbih (Transcendence and Likeness)

**Tanzih** (تنزيه, "declaring [God] free / incomparable") and **tashbih** (تشبيه, "likening,
making comparable") name the two poles of Islamic talk about God, and the axis along which the major
schools of [[islam|Islam]] divide. *Tanzih* stresses God's absolute **transcendence and
unlikeness** — he is utterly beyond creaturely categories; *tashbih* stresses his **likeness and
nearness** — the senses in which God is genuinely described by the attributes scripture gives him
(hearing, seeing, sitting on the throne, the "hand" and "face" of God). How a thinker weights the two
determines how he reads the **anthropomorphic ("ambiguous," *mutashabih*) verses** of the
[[quran|Qur'an]].

## The Spectrum of Positions

- **Radical *tanzih* — the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]]**: God has *no* anthropomorphic attributes
  whatever; ascribing body, place, direction, face, hand, or eye to him compromises his unity
  (*[[tawhid|tawhid]]*). The anthropomorphic verses must therefore be read by
  **[[allegorical-reading|ta'wil]]** — "hand" as power, "face" as essence, "sitting" as dominion.
  This is the method of [[al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]]'s [[quran|*al-Kashshaf*]].
- **Crude *tashbih* — the anthropomorphists** (*mushabbiha*, *mujassima*): taking the attributes
  literally as bodily, the position the others define themselves against.
- **The Sunni mean — Ash'arism / Hanbali *bila kayfa***: affirm the scriptural attributes as real
  **"without asking how"** (*bila kayfa*) — neither emptying them by metaphor (against the Mu'tazila)
  nor likening God to creatures (against the anthropomorphists).
- **The synthesis — [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]] and [[sufism|Sufism]]**: in the **Noah bezel** of the
  [[fusus-al-hikam|Fusus al-Hikam]] — the locus classicus — Ibn Arabi argues that *tanzih* taken
  *alone* is itself a limitation and a "veil," every bit as one-sided as bare *tashbih*. The gnostic
  holds **both at once**: because there is but one Being ([[wahdat-al-wujud|wahdat al-wujud]]),
  disclosed in all forms, God is simultaneously the wholly Incomparable and the One manifest in
  everything. To absolutize either pole is to miss him.

## Why It Matters Here

This axis is exactly where the wiki's two Islamic ingests **meet and diverge**. The Mu'tazilite
[[al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] and the Sufi [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]] read the *same* anthropomorphic
verses and the *same* God in opposite spirits: the one resolving every likeness into pure
transcendence by rational *ta'wil*, the other refusing to let transcendence cancel the divine
presence disclosed in the cosmos. The contrast is the clearest single illustration of the rationalist
and mystical poles of [[tafsir|tafsir]].
