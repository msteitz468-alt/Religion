# Nit-nem

The **Nit-nem** ("daily rule/discipline") is the fixed sequence of prayers a devout Sikh recites each
day, drawn from both the [[guru-granth-sahib|Adi Granth]] and the [[dasam-granth|Dasam Granth]]. Three
orders structure the day (McLeod, agents 3, 4, 6):

- **Dawn (Amrit Vela):** [[japji|Japji]] (Guru Nanak), [[jap-sahib|Jap Sahib]] and the **Ten
  Savayyas** (Guru Gobind Singh, from the Dasam Granth).
- **Sunset:** **Sodar Rahirās** — named from the opening word *Sodar*; includes So Purakh, Benatī
  Chaupaī, and stanzas of Anand Sahib.
- **Before sleep:** **Kīrtan Sohilā** — the shortest order, "because the person reciting it is assumed
  to be weary and ready for sleep."

[[ardas|Ardas]] is recited at the conclusion of the first two orders. All are performed with the head
covered and shoes removed. The Nit-nem is the everyday form of the discipline of
[[nam-simaran|nām simaraṇ]], and selections (Japji, Jap, Ten Savayyas, Benatī Chaupaī, Anand Sahib)
also constitute the recitation that consecrates [[amrit-sanskar|amrit initiation]].
