# Kavod (Glory)

**Kavod** (Hebrew: "glory"; root sense "heaviness / weight / worth") is, in
[[abraham-joshua-heschel|Heschel]]'s reading, the **presence of God** pervading the world —
not God's essence and not a physical phenomenon. "The glory is the presence, not the
essence of God; an act rather than a quality; a process not a substance." Isaiah's seraphic
proclamation "the whole earth is full of His glory" (Isaiah 6:3) is taken as a present fact,
not a messianic promise.

## Glory, Vehicle, and Perception

Heschel distinguishes the glory from the fire, cloud, or storm that may accompany it: "A
sublime phenomenon … provides a setting for the glory; it is not the glory itself." Drawing
on Moses' request "Show me, I pray Thee, Thy glory" (Exodus 33:18), he equates the glory
with the divine goodness rather than any visible thing. Human beings are characteristically
*blind* to it — "The whole earth is full of His glory, but we do not perceive it; it is
within our reach but beyond our grasp" — a blindness rooted in "hardness of heart," the
biblical root of sin. What finally matters is not our perceiving the glory but "our
awareness of being known by it" (see [[divine-pathos]]).

## Relation to Other Concepts

The glory is the biblical name for [[the-ineffable]] and is apprehended through [[awe]] and
[[radical-amazement]]. It correlates in later tradition with the *Shechinah*. The desire to
behold it animates the Jewish lyric/mystical tradition Heschel cites (Halevi, the Zohar).
