# Fate — the Moirai (Parcae)

**Fate** (Greek *moira*, "allotment"; Latin *fatum*, "that which is spoken") is one of the most powerful
ideas in Greco-Roman religion: an **impersonal destiny** that binds even the gods. It is personified as the
**Moirai** (Greek) / **Parcae** (Roman) / **Fates** — three goddesses who allot each mortal's portion.

## The Three Fates

- **Clotho** ("Spinner") — spins the thread of each life.
- **Lachesis** ("Apportioner") — measures it out.
- **Atropos** ("Unturnable") — cuts it, ending the life.

## Fate Above the Gods

A striking feature of Greek theology: **fate (*moira*) is, in some sense, above even [[zeus|Zeus]]**. In
[[homer|Homer]], Zeus weighs the fates of heroes on his golden scales but does not simply override them; the
gods may delay but not finally avert what is fated. The relation of Zeus to fate is left unresolved (sometimes
Zeus *is* fate's executor, sometimes its subject) — an ancient version of the providence-vs-necessity problem.
The [[roman-religion|Roman]] *fatum* is more bound up with **providence** and Rome's destiny ([[virgil-aeneid|
the *Aeneid*]]).

## Cross-Tradition Note

A rich comparandum: Greco-Roman fate as **impersonal allotment** contrasts with the **personal, sovereign
will of God** in the Abrahamic traditions (cf. the [[davidic-covenant|providence]] and predestination debates),
and resonates with the karma/destiny ideas of the Indian traditions and Zoroastrian
[[zurvanism|Zurvan/Time]]. A candidate for a dedicated comparison page on fate, karma, and providence.
