# Purva Paksha

*Pūrva pakṣa* (Sanskrit: "prior position" or "opponent's view") is the classical Indian method of dialectical debate in which a thinker is required to **accurately represent and engage the opponent's position in the opponent's own terms** before proceeding to refutation or synthesis. It is the structural opposite of a straw-man argument.

## Classical Function

In Sanskrit philosophical texts, the format is typically:
1. **Purva paksha** — the prior view, stated fully and sympathetically (often introduced with *iti ced* — "if someone says…")
2. **Uttara paksha** or **Siddhanta** — the reply, which must engage the strongest version of the prior view

The discipline requires mastering the opponent's framework — its premises, key terms, and internal logic — before attempting refutation. This is why classical commentaries can reconstruct the positions of destroyed or lost schools: opponents stated them faithfully before refuting them (see the note in [[being-different-malhotra]], Appendix A endnote 48).

The *Sarva-Darshana-Sangraha* of Madhavacharya arranges all philosophical systems in ascending order, each serving as a purva paksha for the next — not as a list of errors but as a ladder of increasingly comprehensive positions, none absolutely falsified.

## Contemporary Extension (Malhotra)

[[rajiv-malhotra]] redeploys purva paksha as a **methodological framework for civilizational dialogue**. In his account, dharmic thinkers have generally been reactive — responding to Western scholarship's misreadings of Hinduism without first fully understanding Western civilization in its own terms. His project in [[being-different-malhotra]] is to perform a purva paksha of Western/Abrahamic civilization: to reconstruct its deep assumptions (history-centrism, synthetic unity, anxiety over chaos, the Logic of the Excluded Middle, cultural digestion) from within before proposing dharmic alternatives.

Gandhi's *Hind Swaraj* (1909) is cited as an exemplary historical purva paksha: Gandhi engaged British civilization fully — its strengths and its internal contradictions — before articulating *svaraj* and *swadeshi* as alternatives grounded in his own framework (satyagraha, ahimsa, svadharma).

## Relationship to Hermeneutical Systems

Purva paksha functions differently from Western dialectical traditions (Socratic elenchus, Hegelian Aufhebung) in that:
- It does not presuppose that synthesis is possible or desirable; genuine difference can remain
- It is a precondition for respectful engagement, not a move toward absorption
- It is linked to the concept of *adhikara* (qualification/authority) — one must have sufficient mastery of the position to engage it

In Buddhist dialectics, purva paksha is structurally related to [[catuskoti]] (four-way logic) and to *upaya-kaushalya* (Skilful Means): before responding to an interlocutor, the Buddha determines that person's state and needs.
