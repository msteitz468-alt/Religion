# Atman

**Atman** is the innermost self or soul in [[hinduism|Hindu]] thought — not the body, mind, or
personality, but the unchanging witness-consciousness at the core of a being. The [[upanishads|
Upanishads]]' central discovery is the relation of atman to **[[brahman|Brahman]]**, summed in the
*mahavakya* ***tat tvam asi*** — "that thou art."

## Vedic Development of the Word

[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] (Vol. I, Ch. II) traces *ātman* through three senses
across the pre-Upaniṣadic literature. In the **[[rig-veda|Ṛgveda]]** the words for soul are *manas*,
*ātman*, and *asu*, and *ātman* generally means **vital breath**; in at least one hymn the poet presses
past breath and blood to *ātman* as the **inmost self of the world**; and in the Brāhmaṇas (e.g.
Taittirīya) *ātman* becomes **omnipresent**, knowledge of which frees one from evil. So the term moves
from *vital breath in man* → *the self of the world* → *the self in man*, approaching the great ātman
doctrine of the [[upanishads|Upaniṣads]].

## The Upaniṣadic Ātman = Brahman (Dasgupta, Vol. I, Ch. III)

For the [[upanishads|Upaniṣads]], [[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] writes, "the sum
and substance" of the teaching "is involved in the equation **Ātman = Brahman**" — *brahman* reserved for
the ultimate essence of the universe, *ātman* for the inmost essence in man, the two declared one. Three
strands of his account:

- **The five sheaths.** The self is reached by passing inward through five [[pancha-kosha|kośas]]: the
  self made of food (*annamaya*), of vital breath (*prāṇamaya*), of will (*manomaya*), of consciousness
  (*vijñānamaya*), to the innermost **self as pure bliss** (*ānandamaya*).
- **The negative method.** Because no positive predicate fits it, the ātman is indicated only by
  **Yājñavalkya's** *neti neti* — "He is not this, not this… inconceivable, unchangeable, untouched"
  ([[yajnavalkya|Yājñavalkya]] in Bṛh. IV); when Bāhva is asked Brahman's nature he answers by silence,
  "the Ātman is silence."
- **Prajāpati, Indra and Virocana** (Chā. VIII): [[prajapati|Prajāpati]] leads Indra by stages — the self
  is *not* the bodily reflection (Virocana's error), *not* the dreaming self, *not* the self in dreamless
  sleep — to the bodiless self untouched by pleasure and pain. The ātman is "like a lump of salt," all
  savour through and through; "Bliss is not an attribute of it but it is bliss itself."

## How the Schools Read It

- **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]**: atman *is* Brahman, fully and without remainder; realizing this
  identity is [[moksha|liberation]].
- **[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]]**: atman is a real, distinct self that is **inseparable
  from and dependent on** God as a body on its soul.
- **[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]]**: atman is eternally, really **distinct** from God and from every other
  atman.

## Cross-Tradition Significance — the Atman/Anatta Axis

Atman is the precise doctrine the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]] **denied**: Buddhist [[anatta|anatta]]
("non-self") holds there is no permanent self at all. This Hindu–Buddhist disagreement over whether
there *is* an enduring self is one of the deepest fault lines in the wiki — a single Sanskrit prefix
(*an-atman*) marking the divide. It also contrasts with the Abrahamic **immortal soul** and the
Kabbalist structured soul ([[zoharic-afterlife|nefesh/ruach/neshamah]], [[gilgul|gilgul]]): all affirm
an enduring self, but only Advaita identifies it with the ultimate.
