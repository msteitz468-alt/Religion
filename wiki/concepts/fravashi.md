# Fravashi (the guardian spirits)

The **Fravashis** (Avestan *fravashi*; cf. Old Persian *fravarti*) are, in
[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] belief, the **guardian spirits** associated with the dead, the
living, and the unborn — venerated as a host of protective, often warrior-like beings who aid their
descendants.

## Origin: a Hero-Cult

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], the *fravashi* most likely originated as
the **departed soul of a hero**, especially potent to help and protect his kin — implying an
ancient Iranian **hero-cult** comparable to that of the Greeks. The fravashis were conceived as
**female, winged beings inhabiting the air** (Boyce compares the Valkyries): if satisfied by
offerings, they fly swiftly to their families' aid, bring rain, ensure the birth of children, and
**fight invisibly beside their descendants in war**. This specialized fravashi-cult ran alongside,
and blurred with, the general cult of the *urvan* ("soul") of the ordinary dead.

## Place in Zoroastrian Belief

- In developed doctrine the fravashi becomes a **pre-existent higher self / guardian** of every
  person — the eternal spiritual prototype that exists before birth, accompanies the soul in life,
  and persists after death.
- The fravashis of the righteous (those who "conquered for righteousness") are honored
  collectively; the great **Fravardin Yasht** (Yt 13) praises them.
- They are venerated especially in the **night watch** (Aiwisruthrema) and at the year's-end
  festival of **all souls**, when the fravashis are believed to revisit their homes — the
  ancestral feast that survives in the Zoroastrian calendar and feeds into the imagery of **No
  Ruz**.

## The Festival, the Fravohar, and the "Guardian Angel" (Rose)

[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]] adds that the *fra+var* compound implies the
fravashis **"always choose for the good,"** on Ahura Mazda's side, and that they aid him
in creation and in forming "sons in the womb." They are honored at the year-end festival
**Fravardigan** (Parsi **Muktad**) — already attested in the [[persians|Achaemenid]]
period (the Cappadocian month *Artana*; the Persepolis *Irtana-fruiritish*), and possibly
echoed in the Septuagint's *Phrouraia* for **Purim**. Europeans read the fravashi as a
**guardian angel**, a term Zoroastrians themselves adopted by the late nineteenth century;
the winged figure over the Achaemenid king is popularly the **fravohar**, though it is
also read as [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] or the [[xwarenah|*xwarenah*]].

## Significance

The fravashi expresses a Zoroastrian intuition that each person has an **eternal, God-given
spiritual identity** that is both individual guardian and part of a great army enlisted in
[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda's]] cause — fighting for [[asha-druj|asha]] against the Lie. The cult of
the protective dead also illustrates how Zoroaster's reform **absorbed** older Indo-Iranian
ancestor-veneration into a moralized framework rather than abolishing it.
