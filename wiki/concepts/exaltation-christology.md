# Exaltation Christology

**Exaltation Christology** is the understanding of Jesus as a **human being whom God
exalted to divine status** — a Christology "from below," contrasted with
[[incarnation|incarnation Christology]] ("from above"), in which a pre-existent
divine being becomes human. The term is used and preferred by
[[bart-ehrman|Bart Ehrman]] in [[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]]
over the older labels "low Christology" and "adoptionist Christology": "the man
Jesus is showered with divine favors beyond anyone's wildest dreams … elevated to a
divine status on a level with God himself, sitting at his right hand."

**Note on Phil 2:** [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]] (PNTC) reads the [[christ-hymn|Christ hymn]] as *preexistence + incarnation + exaltation* (not pure “from below” adoptionism): exaltation **reveals** identity already true in the form of God rather than conferring equality previously lacking. That evangelical confessional reading is a distinct position from Ehrman’s developmental “Christ Poem” reconstruction; both are recorded without adjudication.

On Ehrman's reconstruction this is the **earliest** Christology — the belief that, at
his [[resurrection|resurrection]], God exalted Jesus to heaven and "to a position of
virtually unheard-of status and authority." The resurrection, in the oldest tradition,
*was* the exaltation: not a reanimation of a body later taken up, but an immediate
elevation into the heavenly realm (the separate physical **ascension** of Acts is
Luke's later development, framed to counter spirit-only views). Jesus then comes to
bear four exalted titles — Messiah, [[kyrios|Lord]] (*kyrios*),
[[son-of-man|Son of Man]], and Son of God — "God in a variety of senses" but, in this
early period, "in no sense … God the Father." The proof-text is **Psalm 110:1** ("The
LORD says to my Lord, 'Sit at my right hand'").

## The "Backward Movement"

Following Raymond Brown, Ehrman traces the *moment* of Jesus's divine sonship being
pushed progressively earlier in the canonical sources:

| Moment | Texts |
|--------|-------|
| **Resurrection** | Pre-Pauline creed Rom 1:3–4; speeches in Acts 13:32–33, 2:36, 5:30–31 |
| **Baptism** | Mark 1:9–11; original text of Luke 3:22 ("today I have begotten you," Ps 2:7) |
| **Conception/Birth** | Luke 1:35; Matthew |
| **Pre-existence** | John, Paul, Phil 2, Col 1, Heb 1 → [[incarnation|incarnation Christology]] |

Ehrman complicates Brown's schema: the development was **not linear** — some
Christians held pre-existence ([[incarnation|incarnation]]) views "even before Paul
began to write in the 50s," earlier than some exaltation texts.

## The Pre-Pauline Creeds

- **Romans 1:3–4** — "arguably the oldest fragment of a creed in all of Paul's
  letters": Jesus "descended from the seed of David according to the flesh," "appointed
  Son of God in power … by his resurrection." Markers of pre-Pauline (and Aramaic/
  Palestinian) origin: the structured form, "seed of David" (nowhere else in Paul's
  undisputed letters), and the Semitism "Spirit of holiness." Paul adds "in power"
  because he himself already held Jesus was Son *before* the resurrection.
- **Acts speeches** — "God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you
  crucified" (2:36); "today I have begotten you" applied to the resurrection (13:33);
  "exalted to his right hand as Leader and Savior" (5:31). Luke incorporates these
  older traditions despite his own birth Christology because they stress God's
  **reversal** of Jesus's crucifixion.

## Relation to Adjacent Concepts

Exaltation Christology, taken to its extreme and frozen, becomes
[[adoptionism|adoptionism]] (Jesus "adopted" as Son, not divine by nature), the view
of the [[ebionites|Ebionites]] and Theodotians later condemned as heresy. Pushed in
the other direction — once the exalted Jesus was thought to have been an angelic being
*all along* — it "very quickly morphed into" [[incarnation|incarnation Christology]].
Ehrman reads **Philippians 2:6–11** (the "Christ Poem") as a **transitional**
Christology combining both: a pre-existent angelic being who, after obedience unto
death, is *exalted* to full equality with God ([[logos-christology|cf. Logos]]).
