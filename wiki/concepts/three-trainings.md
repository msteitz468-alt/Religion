# The Three Trainings

The path leading to liberation, set out at the close of the **medium-capacity** scope in the
**[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** (Vol. I, chs. 23–24): **ethical discipline** (*śīla*),
**meditative concentration** (*samādhi*), and **wisdom** (*prajñā*). Nāgārjuna's *Friendly Letter*:
"Through ethical discipline, concentration, and wisdom, achieve nirvāṇa, an undefiled state of peace
and restraint."

## Why Three, and in This Order

[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] gives their certainty on three grounds: the **stages of disciplining the
mind** (ethics makes the distracted mind undistracted; concentration balances it; wisdom liberates
it); their **results** (ethics → happy rebirth; concentration → higher realms; wisdom → liberation);
and the **objects eliminated** (ethics weakens the afflictions; concentration suppresses their
manifest forms; wisdom eradicates their seeds). The order is fixed because each is the root of the
next — "Ethical discipline is a very steady root … concentration depends on ethical discipline; wisdom
depends on meditative equipoise" (*Sūtra Requested by Brahmā*).

## Ethical Discipline as Foundation

Ethics is "the ladder to all virtues … the banner of all teachings" (*Great Final Nirvāṇa Sūtra*) and
the root even of the mantra vehicle ("The root of the mantra vehicle is, in the first place, ethical
discipline"). Tsong-kha-pa lists its six branches and the sixteen aspects of the wisdom training (four
aspects of each of the [[four-noble-truths|four noble truths]]). The three trainings thus frame the
liberation sought by the medium-capacity person, on which the great-capacity [[six-perfections|six
perfections]] then build.
