# Grace (Aquinas)

[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]'s Treatise on Grace ([[summa-theologica|*Summa Theologiae*]] I-II,
Q109–114) concludes the *Prima Secundae* and is the central scholastic account of grace, justification,
and merit. It is a primary reference point — and point of contention — for the later
Catholic–Protestant disputes of the Reformation and for the intra-Catholic *de auxiliis* controversy
(cf. [[predestination]]).

## The necessity of grace (Q109)

Aquinas's signature move distinguishes the **state of integral nature** (before the Fall) from the
**state of corrupt nature** (after it). In *both* states God must be the **First Mover** of any good
act; but fallen man needs grace for *two* reasons — to be **healed** (*gratia sanans*) and to be
**elevated** to a supernatural end (*gratia elevans*). Determinations (Q109):
- man **can** know natural truths by reason, but needs God as mover for every act (A1);
- he **cannot** fulfil the commandments "as to the mode of charity," love God above all things in the
  state of corrupt nature, merit eternal life, prepare himself for grace, rise from sin, or long avoid
  mortal sin — **without grace** (A2–10). **Pelagianism** is named and rejected (A4).

## The essence of grace (Q110)

Grace is a real, supernatural **quality** (an accident) infused into the **essence of the soul** — a
**participation of the divine nature** (2 Pet. 1:4) — distinct from and prior to the infused virtues
that flow from it (A2–4). Because grace is a real form in the soul, the remission of guilt cannot be
mere non-imputation; it requires a real effect in the sinner (A1).

## Operating and cooperating, prevenient and subsequent grace (Q111)

These are distinctions of *effect*, not of essence. As **operating** grace God moves the will to a
new good end (the will is moved, not moving); as **cooperating** grace He assists the will it has
healed in carrying that willing into act. Augustine's formula is the anchor: "He operates that we may
will; and when we will, He cooperates that we may perfect." The same grace is **prevenient** and
**subsequent** under five effects.

## Whether one can know he is in grace (Q112, A5)

Three modes: by special **revelation** (exceptional); by **natural certitude** — **impossible**,
since grace's principle is God, who exceeds our knowledge (1 Cor. 4:3–4; Job 9:11); and by
**conjecture** from signs (delight in God, no consciousness of mortal sin), which is possible but
"imperfect." This denial of ordinary certainty of one's own grace is a notable contrast with later
Reformed teaching on the assurance of salvation (recorded under Contrasts below).

## Justification of the ungodly (Q113)

Justification requires **four things**, simultaneous in time but ordered in nature: (1) the **infusion
of grace**; (2) a movement of free-will **toward God** by faith; (3) a movement of free-will
**against sin**; (4) the **remission of sin** (A6). It is **instantaneous** (A7). Crucially, the
remission of guilt *requires the infusion of grace* — justification is a real interior renewal, the
infusion and the remission being one act under different aspects (A2). Aquinas calls it, as to the
good caused, greater even than the creation of heaven and earth (A9). See [[justification]].

## Merit (Q114)

By **condign** merit (*meritum de condigno*) a person in grace can merit eternal life — but *only*
insofar as the act proceeds from the **grace of the Holy Spirit**, not from free-will alone; by
**congruous** merit (*de congruo*) lesser fittingness. No one can merit the **first grace** for
himself (anti-Pelagian, with Augustine's self-correction cited), and the wayfarer **cannot merit final
perseverance** — God bestows it freely. The increase of grace and charity *can* be merited condignly.

## Contrasts (Contradiction Protocol)

Aquinas's account stands in tension with the magisterial **Protestant** Reformation on three points,
recorded here without adjudication:
- **Justification** as real infused renewal (Aquinas, Q113) vs. forensic **imputation** of an alien
  righteousness (Luther, [[john-calvin|Calvin]]) — see [[justification]].
- **Assurance**: Aquinas denies ordinary certainty of being in grace (Q112, A5); the Reformers
  affirm assurance of salvation.
- **Merit**: Aquinas affirms grace-enabled condign merit of eternal life (Q114); the Reformers deny
  any merit. (Aquinas's own anti-Pelagian guardrails — God as first mover, no merit of first grace,
  unmeritable perseverance — are themselves close to the Augustinian core the Reformers claimed.)
