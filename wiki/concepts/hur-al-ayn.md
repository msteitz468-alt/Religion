# Hur al-'Ayn

**Hur al-'ayn** (حور العين, "[those with] white-and-dark, lustrous eyes") are the companions
of [[paradise|Paradise]] promised to the believers in several Makkan-period suras of the
[[quran|Qur'an]] (e.g. Q 44:54; 52:20; 56:22). The term *huri* was, [[amina-wadud|Wadud]] notes,
**pre-Islamic** — "so called by the Arabs of the desert because of her whiteness or fairness or
cleanness," a youthful, fair-skinned, large-dark-eyed female of pliant character — and the
Qur'anic descriptions are "specific and sensual."

## Interpretive Dispute (recorded, not adjudicated)

- **Popular / traditional reading**: the *hur al-'ayn* are beautiful female (sometimes
  "perpetually virgin") companions rewarding pious men, often multiplied (the "many virgins"
  trope), supported by reading every *azwaj* (mates) in the Paradise passages as equivalent to
  *huri*.
- **[[wadud-quran-and-woman|Wadud's reading]]**: the *huri* are a **culturally specific,
  Makkan-period incentive** pitched to "the dreams and desires of those [Jahili] Arabs" — "it
  is impossible to believe that the Qur'an intends white women with large eyes to represent a
  single universal description of beauty for all humankind." She distinguishes three levels of
  Paradise-companionship in the Qur'an: (1) the *hur al-'ayn* (Makkan, ethnocentrically
  specific); (2) the gender-neutral **zawj** (mate/companion) of the Madinan passages, the
  model for an actual community of believers (male *and* female); (3) a transcendent
  companionship beyond both. She rejects equating *zawj* with *huri* — the verb *zawwaja* "to
  pair" (Q 44:54) joins a believer to a companion, it does not multiply virgins — arguing the
  conflation "reduces the Qur'anic depiction of the highest reality to a single ethnocentric
  world-view," and that "multiple erotic pleasures" contradict the self-restraint the pious are
  praised for.

This is part of her larger case for the Qur'an's **eschatological equity**: each *nafs* is
recompensed by its own deeds (Q 3:195; 4:32), male and female alike.
