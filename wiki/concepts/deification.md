## Definition

Deification (*theosis*, *divinization*) names the transformation of the human soul — and, through it, the human body and the whole person — into a progressively closer union with the divine life. In pro-Nicene theology the concept is inseparable from Christology: salvation occurs through participation in Christ's person (the incarnate, crucified, resurrected, and ascended Word), and because that Word is coequal with the Father within the simple Godhead, union with Christ is a real union with "the one life of God" (*Nicaea and Its Legacy*).

## Pro-Nicene Account (Ayres, Ch. 12)

### Nyssa's Cautious Account

Ayres identifies [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]] as the most considered pro-Nicene voice on this theme, while noting that "Nyssa is cautious in his terminology and reluctant to speak of Christians 'becoming divine'". Nyssa describes the soul's progress as "the soul's imitation of the divine through imagining a shaping of the soul's life or activity such that it eventually displays nothing but the activity (ἐνέργεια) of love. This activity both transforms the soul into a perfect image (achieving its natural end) and draws the soul into union with the life of God".

Nyssa insists on the non-competitive, immediate presence of God: "If then all things exist in him and he exists in all things, why are [some people] shocked at a scheme of revelation (οἰκονομία) which teaches that God became man, when we believe that even now he is not external to man?" (Cat. or. 25). Union in the incarnation differs in *mode* but not in *degree* from God's general sustaining presence — "the presence in us now and then is equal (κατά τὸ ἴσον)".

### Four Shared Pro-Nicene Themes

Ayres identifies four themes in Nyssa's account of deification/transformation that are equally present in [[augustine|Augustine]], constituting "formal similarities driven by common pro-Nicene concerns":

1. **Sanctification as incorporation into Christ's person.** "Sanctification and redemption are understood as participation in the body of Christ, as union with the person of Christ". The Word's intimate union with the Father means that union with Christ is union with the one divine power.
2. **Shaped by the Creator/creation distinction.** "Pro-Nicene accounts of the unity of the divine persons and their existence 'within' a simple divine Godhead conditions Gregory's vision of the Word as mediator". The soul's transformation is not absorption into God — the incomprehensible distinction between Creator and creature remains — but a mysterious union.
3. **The purified soul reflecting the Word.** "Nyssa's account of what it means to be taken up into the divine life revolves around an account of the purified soul reflecting the Word in whose image it is made and exhibiting its own mysterious 'union' with the divine life present in it".
4. **Incomprehensibility shapes contemplative practice.** "Insistence on the mysterious, incomprehensible nature of God shapes a particular set of intellectual and contemplative practices".

### The Body of Christ (Nyssa on 1 Cor. 15:28)

Nyssa describes a two-stage union: first, all Christians are unified with Christ through mastery of the passions and ordering of soul and body; then "the union of all with Christ brings all to share what Christ has from the Father." Nyssa's text: "Unity then means to be one body with him. When the good pervades everything, then the entirety of Christ's body will be subjected to God's vivifying power... Christ unites all mankind to himself, and to the Father through him... he who is in the Father effects our union with this very same Father" (In 1 Cor. 15.28).

### Augustine's Parallel

Augustine articulates a structurally identical vision in different terminology. FromCE: "Just as he ascended, you see, and still didn't depart from us, so we too are now there with him... we too are going to ascend, not by our own virtue, but by our and his oneness (sed nostra et illius unitate)" (Serm. 263.2–3). Augustine understands the "love that is God" as truly present in the Christian community: the indwelling Spirit brings the love that *is* God, so that in loving action God is genuinely "seen." Ayres notes scholarly discussion of whether Augustine uses explicit deification language, but insists that the formal structure is the same as Nyssa's.

### Distinction from Mühlenberg's Reading

Ayres explicitly rebuts Ekkehard Mühlenberg's claim that Gregory of Nyssa's strong Creator/creation distinction rules out any doctrine of *theosis*: "In my account it is precisely this distinction that renders possible that doctrine". The incomprehensibility of the divine, far from closing off deification, ensures that the soul's union with God is itself mysterious and inexhaustible — an infinite progress, not a finite arrival.

## Dual-Focus Purification (Ch. 13)

Deification requires "dual-focus" anthropology: the soul must simultaneously govern the body *and* be ordered toward contemplation of God. Ayres identifies this as a shared pro-Nicene pattern across [[ambrose|Ambrose]], Augustine, [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory Nazianzen]], and Gregory of Nyssa. The purification of the soul is accomplished through the joint action of bodily practice (virtuous action, ascetic discipline) and interior reformation of desire and cognition — neither alone suffices.

## Key Author/Text Connections

- [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]]: the most fully developed pro-Nicene account; *In 1 Cor. 15.28*; *Life of Moses*; *Commentary on the Song of Songs*.
- [[augustine|Augustine]]: structurally parallel account in different terminology; *Serm.* 263; *Ep. Io. tr.* 7; *De Trinitate* 8.
- [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory Nazianzen]]: "Walk towards him shining" (*Or.* 40.37); deification as an aesthetics of faith in light.
- [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil of Caesarea]]: purification shaped by attention to God's immediate but incomprehensible presence in creation.
- [[origen|Origen]]: earlier tradition of spiritual progress that pro-Nicenes inherit and transform.

## Related Concepts

- [[participation]] — the ontological basis for deification; the creature's sharing in divine qualities.
- [[incarnation]] — the indispensable Christological vehicle of deification; the Word's union with humanity is the means of humanity's union with God.
- [[logos-christology]] — the Word's coequal divinity is precisely what makes union with Christ a union with the one life of God.
- [[divine-simplicity]] — guarantees that union with Christ is union with the whole undivided Godhead, not with a subordinate mediator.
- [[pro-nicene-theology]] — the broader culture and habitus within which deification is understood.
- [[trinity]] — the triune structure of God shapes how the soul is drawn into the divine life (through the Son, in the Spirit, toward the Father).
