# Infallibility (Bahá'í)

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 18150–18229.*

## Definition

The Bahá'í term for infallibility — *'isma* (Arabic) — means both "sinless" and "infallible" and is of Islamic origin (in Shí'í belief, Muḥammad, Fáṭima, and the Twelve Imáms all possessed *'isma*) (l. 18154–18158). Bahá'ís distinguish two kinds of infallibility.

## The 'Most Great Infallibility'

Bahá'u'lláh taught that this belongs to the [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestations of God]] alone, whereby they are both sanctified from error and constitute in themselves the standard of truth for others to follow (l. 18162–18168). They are not limited by human standards: "if they pronounced water to be wine, or heaven to be earth, it was the truth" (l. 18169–18171). It is incumbent on others to adhere to what they ordain (l. 18172–18174).

[[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] commented that the Manifestations' infallibility was "essential to themselves and inseparable from them"; whatever emanated from them was "identical with the truth"; whatever they said was "the word of God"; and whatever they did was "an upright action" (l. 18181–18185). If their words and actions were not understood, this was "because of human ignorance" (l. 18186–18189).

## 'Conferred' or 'Acquired' Infallibility

This is granted by God to those who are mediators of grace and guidance between God and human beings (l. 18192–18194). It is not inherent in their persons (l. 18198–18199). 'Abdu'l-Bahá stated it would be granted to the [[universal-house-of-justice|Universal House of Justice]] as a body, but not to its individual members (l. 18200–18205). As Guardian, [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] would also be under God's unerring guidance (l. 18206–18208); and as appointed interpreter, 'Abdu'l-Bahá himself "voiced the very truth" and whatever his pen recorded was correct (l. 18209–18212).

Shoghi Effendi defined his own infallibility as confined to the [[interpretation|interpretation]] and application of scripture and to the protection of the Bahá'í Faith — not extending to economics, science, or technical matters (l. 18213–18218). Unlike the Manifestations of God he was "not omniscient at will" (l. 18218–18219). He also distinguished his own position from that of 'Abdu'l-Bahá, "in whose person human nature and 'superhuman knowledge and perfection' had been blended" (l. 18227–18229).

## Relationship to the Covenant

The Bahá'í doctrine of infallibility is intimately connected to the [[covenant-bahai|Covenant]] and the concept of [[guardianship|Guardianship]]. The conferred infallibility of successive Bahá'í leaders provides the authoritative basis for the Bahá'í administrative order.
