# Sanctuary of Men (*Miqdash Adam*)
**Hebrew**: מקדש אדם (*miqdash 'adam*)

A Qumran theological concept in which the **community itself replaces the Jerusalem Temple** as the legitimate sacred space, with Torah study and communal obedience substituting for Temple sacrifices. This is one of the most significant theological moves in Qumran thought and provides important background for early Christian ecclesiology.

## Primary Source: Florilegium (4Q174)
The Florilegium (*midrash ha-eschaton*) is a thematic pesher collecting biblical proof-texts about the eschatological Temple and the Davidic Messiah. The central passage:

> "This is the house which [they shall not build for Him] until the end of days. As it is written in the book of Moses, 'A Sanctuary, O Lord, which your hands have established. The Lord shall reign for ever and ever' (Exod. 15:17-18). This refers to the house into which shall never enter the unclean… but [it is a **Sanctuary of Men** (*miqdash 'adam*)] where they shall offer in it, for Him, **works of the Law** as offerings of sweet fragrance, not like in this material sanctuary."

The argument:
1. The physical Temple in Jerusalem is defiled and unacceptable
2. God's "sanctuary" is now the community
3. The community's obedience to the Law ("works of the Law," *ma'asay Torah*) is the acceptable sacrifice

## Community Rule (1QS)
The Community Rule develops the same concept without using the exact term:

> "They shall atone for guilty rebellion and for sins of unfaithfulness **by the practice of justice and by suffering the sorrows of affliction**… When these are in Israel, the Council of the Community shall be established in truth. It shall be an **Everlasting Plantation**, a Holy House for Israel and the Foundation of the Holy of Holies for Aaron; **true Witnesses** regarding judgement, and the **Chosen of Goodwill** who shall atone for the Land and pay to the wicked their reward."

The language is sacrificial: "atone," "sweet fragrance," "planting," "foundation of the Holy of Holies." The community functions as the sacred precinct.

## Rationale: Rejection of the Physical Temple
The Qumran community had separated from the Jerusalem Temple because:
1. The Hasmonaean high priesthood was illegitimate (not Zadokite)
2. The Temple operated on the wrong (lunar) calendar
3. The priests were corrupt — accused of greed, fornication, and uncleanness

Rather than abandoning the category of sacred space and sacrifice, the community reinterpreted it: the community itself is the **true Temple**, and its members are the **true priests** performing the **true sacrifices** (study, obedience, suffering).

## Relationship to MMT (4QMMT)
The Halakhic Letter (MMT) addresses the Jerusalem leadership with 20+ specific legal disputes about purity in the Temple context. MMT's phrase "works of the Law" (*ma'asay ha-Torah*) is the same language used for the community's substitute "sacrifices" in 4Q174. The concept of legitimate vs. illegitimate Temple worship pervades both documents.

## Relationship to Early Christianity
The "Sanctuary of Men" concept provides rich background for early Christian ecclesiology:
- **1 Corinthians 3:16-17**: "Do you not know that you are God's temple and God's Spirit dwells in you?"
- **1 Corinthians 6:19**: "Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit"
- **Ephesians 2:19-22**: "You are… built into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit… a holy temple in the Lord"
- **1 Peter 2:5**: "You yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ"

In early Christianity, the logic is virtually identical to Qumran's: the physical Temple is replaced by the community; the community's acts of obedience/faith are the new sacrifices. The mechanism of replacement differs (for Qumran: Torah obedience; for Paul: Spirit-indwelling through Christ), but the conceptual structure is strikingly parallel.
