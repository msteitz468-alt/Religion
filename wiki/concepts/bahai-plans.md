# Plans (Bahá'í Missionary)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 24628–24756.*

## Overview
Organized campaigns to fulfil specific goals, particularly missionary expansion, are a significant element in modern Bahá'í activity. The 'charter' for much of this activity is [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]'s *Tablets of the Divine Plan* (1916–17), addressed to the American and Canadian Bahá'ís, calling for a systematic endeavour to teach the Faith throughout the world and stressing the need to learn local languages and produce relevant literature (lines 24628–24645).

## National Plans (1937–1953)

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] began to implement the objectives of 'Abdu'l-Bahá's Plan with the first North American Seven Year Plan (1937–44). The plan called for at least one local spiritual assembly in every American state and Canadian province; resident Bahá'ís in each Latin American and Caribbean republic; and completion of the exterior ornamentation of the Wilmette temple (lines 24652–24673).

Subsequent national plans included:
- **India and Burma**: Six Year (1938–44), Four-and-a-Half Year (January 1946–June 1950), Nineteen Month (September 1951–April 1953)
- **British Isles**: Six (1944–50) and Two (1951–3) Year Plans
- **United States and Canada**: Second Seven Year Plan (1946–53)
- **Iran**: Forty-five Month Plan (October 1946–July 1950)
- **Australia and New Zealand**: Six Year Plan (1947–53)
- **Iraq**: Three Year Plan (1947–50)
- **Egypt and the Sudan**: Five Year Plan (1948–53)
- **Germany and Austria**: Five Year Plan (1948–53)
- **Canada**: Five Year Plan (1948–53)
- **Central America**: One Year Plan (1952–3)

These plans introduced the concept of domestic goals to all national Bahá'í communities, and began establishing Bahá'í groups and local assemblies in Europe, South East Asia and Africa. The two-year 'Africa campaign' (1951–3) involved collaboration between several national assemblies and was the forerunner of subsequent international plans (lines 24678–24722).

## The Ten Year Crusade (1953–1963)
An elaborate and ambitious international project aiming to:
- Settle Bahá'ís in every significant territory and island group throughout the world
- Enormously increase the total number of national and local assemblies, Bahá'í centres, and range of languages in which there was Bahá'í literature
- Secure 'international' goals, including codification of the *Kitáb-i-Aqdas*, establishment of a Bahá'í court in Israel (preliminary to the emergence of the Universal House of Justice), construction of Bahá'í Houses of Worship, and development of the Bahá'í World Centre

All twelve national spiritual assemblies formed by 1953 participated. The results surpassed expectations, with the beginning of massive growth in a number of communities (lines 24724–24756).

## Plans under the Universal House of Justice (1963–)
Since its establishment the Universal House of Justice has continued the pattern, with a total of six international plans to date:
- Nine Year Plan (1964–73)
- Five Year Plan (1974–9)
- Seven Year Plan (1979–86)
- Six Year Plan (1986–92)
- Three Year Plan (1993–6)
- Four Year Plan (1996–2000)

A subsidiary two-year plan (1990–2) co-ordinated Bahá'í activity in Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union under the new conditions of religious freedom following the collapse of communist regimes. Plans have included expansion goals, proclamation activities, international conferences, construction of Houses of Worship, and development of qualitative aspects of Bahá'í community life (lines 24767–24793).
