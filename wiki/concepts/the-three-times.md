# The Three Times (Creation, Mixture, Separation)

Zoroastrian cosmology frames all of existence as a **bounded, linear drama in three ages** — a
profound break (per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]]) with the older Indo-Iranian
expectation that the world-process, once started, would simply continue forever. This **linear,
finite, goal-directed** time is one of [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism's]] most distinctive and
consequential ideas.

## Menog and Getig: the Two States

[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] creates in **two stages**:

- **Menog** (Pahlavi, "spiritual, immaterial") — the world first brought into being in a
  disembodied state.
- **Getig** (Pahlavi, "material") — the same creation given solid, sentient **form**.

The *getig* state is **better**, not worse, than the *menog*: embodiment is a *good* added to
perfect creation. This positive valuation of matter and the body is theologically load-bearing — it
is why bodily **resurrection** is coherent and desirable in Zoroastrianism (against the
matter-disparaging dualisms of Gnosticism), and it grounds the duty to cherish the physical world.

## The Three Times

1. **Creation (Bundahishn).** Ahura Mazda fashions the *menog* and then the *getig* world — the
   seven creations, sustained by the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]]. At this moment the sun
   stands still at noon and the world is perfect and motionless. The act of creation is itself
   called **Bundahishn**, the title later given to [[bundahishn|the Pahlavi cosmological book]].

2. **Mixture (Gumezishn).** The *getig* world, being vulnerable, is **assaulted by
   [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]]**, who breaks in through the sky, fouls water, earth, plant,
   animal, man, and fire, and introduces death and every evil. Time begins to move; good and evil
   are entangled. This is the **present age** — the era of moral struggle, in which every human
   choice for [[asha-druj|asha against druj]] aids Ahura Mazda's cause.

3. **Separation (Wizarishn).** At [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]], the "making wonderful," good is
   **separated** from evil for good: the dead are raised, all pass through the river of molten
   metal, Angra Mainyu and the Daevas are destroyed, and the perfected, deathless world endures
   **eternally**. Because evil is annihilated, the time of Separation has no end.

## The 3,000-Year Schema

Later priestly tradition (the [[bundahishn|Bundahishn]]) systematized this into a vast schema of
**limited time** (often 9,000 or 12,000 years in millennial blocks), postponing the consummation
that Zoroaster himself seems to have expected **soon** into a remote, calculable future. Each year
the **No Ruz** (New Year) festival ritually prefigures the renewal; noon (**Rapithwina**), the
moment time stood still at Creation, is its daily emblem.

## Significance and Influence

Linear time with a **beginning, a decisive middle conflict, and a definitive end** — rather than
cyclical or endless time — is arguably Zoroastrianism's signal contribution to the history of
religious thought, the structural template behind later **[[apocalyptic-literature|apocalyptic]]**
periodization of history (cf. the world-ages of Daniel) and the "new heavens and new earth." See
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]] and
[[cohn-cosmos-chaos-and-the-world-to-come|Cohn]].
