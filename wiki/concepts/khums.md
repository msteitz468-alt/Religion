# Khums (One-Fifth)

**Khums** (Arabic: خمس, "one-fifth") is the Islamic obligation to allocate one-fifth of war booty
(*ghanima*) and, in Shia law, of annual surplus income, to designated recipients. The Quranic basis
is Q 8:41: "Know that whatever of a thing you acquire as spoils of war, then indeed for Allah is
one-fifth thereof and for the Messenger and for the near of kin…"

---

## In Sahih al-Bukhari (Book 53 — Kitab al-Khumus)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Book 53 (Nos. 324–412). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Quranic Basis and Core Obligation

**No. 327** (Ibn Abbas): The Prophet listed Khumus alongside prayer, zakat, and fasting as a
core obligation when addressing the delegates of Abd al-Qais: "I order you to believe in Allah …
to offer prayers perfectly; to pay Zakat; to fast the month of Ramadan, and to pay the Khumus
(i.e. one-fifth) of the war booty to Allah."

The secondary Quranic basis cited is Q 59:6 (*Surat al-Hashr*) on *fai* (booty acquired without
fighting): "What Allah bestowed as (Fai) Booty on his Apostle (Muhammad) from them — for this
you made no expedition with either cavalry or camelry…"

### Distribution Principles

- The Prophet distributed shares to fighters but also sometimes to those who had not
  participated in a specific battle, especially Companions who had been in Ethiopia (No. 364,
  re: Khaibar — "They share the booty with you").
- To recently-converted Quraysh leaders, the Prophet gave disproportionate shares to cement
  their adherence to Islam: "Let them adhere to Islam, for they are near to their life of
  Ignorance" (No. 374). This caused Ansar resentment.
- **Spoils of individuals**: awarded to whoever killed a specific enemy, with proof (No. 370).
- **Conquered agricultural land** (No. 354, re: Khaibar): Umar decided not to divide Khaibar
  among the fighters but to hold it as a permanent endowment for future Muslims — an
  alternative to the one-fifth rule for real property.

### The Distinction: Ghanima vs. Fai

- **Ghanima** (war booty from fighting): distributed; one-fifth goes to designated recipients.
- **Fai** (booty acquired without fighting, e.g. diplomatic tribute, abandoned property): the
  Prophet's own portion; not divided among fighters. The extended hadith (No. 326) clarifies
  that the Prophet's property from Khaibar, Fadak, and Medina was *fai*, not *ghanima*, and
  therefore not inheritable property — it would be distributed as *sadaqa*.

### Booty Legalized for This Ummah

**No. 351** (Jabir): "Allah's Apostle said, 'Booty has been made legal for me.'" This is
explained (No. 353) as a special divine concession — previous prophets could not keep or
distribute booty; a divine fire would consume it. The legalization is specific to Muhammad's
community.

### Protection of Treaty-Holders

**No. 391**: "Whoever killed a person having a treaty with the Muslims, shall not smell the
smell of Paradise though its smell is perceived from a distance of forty years." The khums
framework coexists with the absolute prohibition on killing protected persons (*ahl al-dhimma*).

### The Fadak Controversy

The most theologically and historically significant episode in Book 53 concerns Fatima's claim
to property that had been the Prophet's. Full detail: see [[fadak-inheritance|the Fadak
controversy page]].

**No. 325** (Aisha): After the Prophet's death, Fatima (his daughter) asked Abu Bakr for her
share of the Prophet's fai properties — Khaibar, Fadak, and Medina charity. Abu Bakr refused:
"Allah's Apostle said, 'Our property will not be inherited, whatever we (i.e. prophets) leave
is Sadaqa (to be used for charity).'" Fatima stopped speaking to Abu Bakr until her death six
months later.

**No. 326** (Malik bin Aus): The full arbitration before Umar, with both Ali and Abbas present.
Umar confirmed the same ruling: prophetic property is not inherited but remains as *sadaqa*.
He then placed Khaibar and Fadak under the same management terms the Prophet had used and gave
the Medina property to Ali and Abbas to manage under the same covenant.

**No. 328** (Abu Huraira): "My heirs should not take even a single Dinar (i.e. anything from
my property)… whatever I leave, excluding the expenditure of my wives and my laborers, will be
Sadaqa."

## In Sunni and Shia Law

- **Sunni**: Khums applies primarily to war booty (*ghanima*); the five shares (per Q 8:41) go
  to Allah/Prophet, relatives of the Prophet (*dhawi al-qurba*), orphans, the poor, and wayfarers.
  The Prophet's and Allah's shares were incorporated into the community treasury after his death.
- **Shia**: Extended to all annual profit above living expenses (*arbaah mukasibin*); one of the
  primary sources of revenue for the Marjas (senior Shia jurists who receive the "imam's share").
  This extension is a major point of divergence from Sunni law and is grounded in Shia
  jurisprudence deriving from the Imams' authority.
