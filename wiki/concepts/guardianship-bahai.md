# Guardianship (Bahá'í)

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

The Guardianship is an institution created by [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] in his Will and Testament to provide a hereditary line of heads of the Bahá'í Faith. In many respects it is reminiscent of the Shí'í imamate (lines 15576–15583).

## Establishment

In his Will and Testament, 'Abdu'l-Bahá appointed [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] as his successor, naming him "Guardian of the Cause of God" (*Válí amru'lláh*) and the first in a projected line of Guardians. As such, Shoghi Effendi was:
- "Expounder of the words of God"
- Under the protection of Bahá'u'lláh and 'Abdu'l-Bahá
- Obedience to him was obligatory on all Bahá'ís (lines 15584–15594)

## Succession Rules

The line of Guardians was to follow among the **male descendants of Bahá'u'lláh** (*Aghṣán*), though generally through the firstborn of the lineal male line, subject to the Aqdas provisions (lines 15595–15603). It was incumbent upon each Guardian to appoint his successor during his own lifetime, to ensure the Bahá'ís would not be left without leadership after his death (lines 15604–15612).

Requirements for the appointed successor included: detachment from worldly things, purity, the fear of God, knowledge, wisdom, and learning (lines 15614–15618). The approval of the elected **Hands of the Cause** was also required (line 15620).

## Powers and Functions

The Guardian was to be:
- The sacred head of the [[universal-house-of-justice|Universal House of Justice]] following its establishment, and a lifelong member
- Authorized to expel any of its members who committed acts "injurious to the common weal"
- Responsible for receiving the [[huququ-llah|Ḥuqúqu'lláh]]
- Responsible for appointing the [[hands-of-the-cause|Hands of the Cause]] and directing them in their work
- Supreme authority to whom all were submissive and subordinate (lines 15621–15631)

Shoghi Effendi noted that each Guardian was empowered "to reveal the purport and disclose the implications of the utterances of Bahá'u'lláh and of 'Abdu'l-Bahá" — interpretations described as "authoritative and binding" (lines 15636–15641).

Together with the Universal House of Justice, the Guardianship was designed to:
- Ensure the continuity of divinely appointed authority within the Faith
- Safeguard the unity of its followers
- Maintain the elasticity and flexibility of its teachings
- Administer and co-ordinate its activities
- Protect its interests (lines 15643–15654)

Shoghi Effendi stressed that the two institutions were complementary, each with its own specific sphere of jurisdiction: the Guardian could only interpret what had already been revealed, and could only legislate in his capacity as a member of the House of Justice; he could propose that a particular action be taken but not dictate the activities and procedures of the House (lines 15655–15667).

## Limits of the Guardian's Station

Shoghi Effendi stressed the essential nature of the Guardianship (without it, the Bahá'í world order would lack stability), but also the distinction between the spiritual station of the Guardian and that of 'Abdu'l-Bahá: the distance between them was greater than that which separated the station of 'Abdu'l-Bahá from his father; unlike 'Abdu'l-Bahá, no Guardian would ever be a perfect exemplar of Bahá'u'lláh's teachings. He stressed that his own interpretations generally extended to matters relating to the Faith, not to subjects such as economics and science (lines 15679–15697).

## End of the Guardianship

Shoghi Effendi did not have any children, and during his lifetime expelled his brothers and cousins from the Faith as Covenant-breakers. By the 1950s there were none among the male descendants of Bahá'u'lláh who remained loyal (lines 15699–15707). Shoghi Effendi felt himself unable to appoint a successor, and in consequence left no will. Apart from the practical issue of succession, his death in 1957 created doctrinal problems (lines 15707–15714).

The newly formed Universal House of Justice declared in October 1963 that no further Guardians could be appointed (line 15715). In subsequent letters they made clear that their own legislative functioning was not impaired by the absence of a Guardian, since they were assured of divine protection and guidance by 'Abdu'l-Bahá, and were able to turn to the great mass of interpretation left by Shoghi Effendi (lines 15718–15731).

## Theological Significance

The Guardianship represents the Bahá'í answer to the problem of succession and authoritative interpretation in a new religious movement. Its premature end — with no successor named — has been one of the most theologically challenging events in Bahá'í history, requiring the community to function without the institution that was supposed to be essential to the Administrative Order.

## Controversies

The Remey claim (1960): Charles Mason Remey, a Hand of the Cause and President of the International Bahá'í Council, claimed to be the second Guardian on the basis of his presidency of the Council; this claim was rejected by virtually all Bahá'ís and Remey was subsequently removed from the Faith (lines 16186–16191).
