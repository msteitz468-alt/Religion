# Ajiva (Non-Soul)

*Ajiva* ("non-soul") is the collective term in Jain ontology for the four (or five, in Digambara reckoning) fundamental entities that are not the [[jiva]]. Together with the *jiva*, the *ajiva* entities constitute the five *astikaya* ("existing collections") that are the building blocks of reality and permeate the [[loka|universe]].

## The Five/Six Fundamental Entities (*Astikaya*)

Dundas explains that "Jainism claims that there are five ontological categories called *astikaya* (*asti* meaning 'it exists' and *kaya* 'body' or 'collection'), fundamental entities which permeate the *loka* and are the building-blocks responsible for the maintenance of existence. The most important of these is the *jiva*, usually translated as 'soul' … with the other four, motion (*dharma*), rest (*adharma*), atoms (*pudgala*) and space (*akasa*), collectively forming a category called 'non-soul' (*ajiva*). The Digambaras came to add time as a sixth category to this list" (lines 215–224).

The four *ajiva* entities "provide the mechanism by which the *jiva* functions" (lines 238–239).

## Development

"It must be assumed that an analysis of the world into five entities developed only gradually, for the first book of the *Acaranga* makes no mention of the non-soul category, merely dividing existents into those which have thought and those which do not" (lines 226–228). The canonical texts do not offer philosophical proofs for these entities; their truth is taken to be established by the omniscient teachers who alone can witness them directly. "All other people must infer their existence in the same way that the wind is known to exist despite the fact that it is never seen" (*Bh* 18.7, lines 232–234).

## The Four *Ajiva* Categories

### 1. Dharma (Motion)

In Jain metaphysics, *dharma* functions not as "salvific truth" but literally as "that which carries" — it is "the support and creating the conditions for souls and atoms whose natural state would otherwise be movement, just as water is the occasioning cause for fish to swim" (*TSRV* on *TS* 5.17, lines 318–322). Without *dharma*, motion within the *loka* would be impossible.

Dundas notes that "the idea of motion (*dharma*) and rest (*adharma*) as representing separate ontological categories is peculiar to Jainism among Indian metaphysical systems" and likely developed from cosmographical speculation about what lies outside the *loka* (lines 302–306). Souls and atoms cannot exist in the *aloka* (the region outside the *loka*) precisely because dharma is absent there (lines 308–310).

The "Exposition of Explanations" extends *dharma*'s scope: it is "responsible not just for ordinary physical motion but also for verbal and mental activity" (*Bh* 18.4, lines 324–326).

### 2. Adharma (Rest)

*Adharma* is the ontological counterpart to *dharma*: it "creates the conditions for [the] stasis [of souls and atoms]" (line 322–323). It "is involved in the stabilising of the mind through contemplation" (*Bh* 18.4, lines 325–327). *Adharma* also "restrict[s] [souls and atoms] to the confines of the *loka*" (Abhayadeva on *Sth* 8 and 337, lines 311–313).

### 3. Pudgala (Atoms / Matter)

*Pudgala* — the material, atomic dimension of reality — provides "the bodily and non-spiritual dimension for the *jiva*" (*TS* 5.19–20, lines 329–331). "Through the accumulation of atoms brought about by karma, the *jiva* forms a body which for most creatures is one of flesh and blood (called the *audarika* body)" (lines 332–334). Language and breath are composed of atoms; "we also owe to them our direct, physical experience of pleasure and pain, birth and death" (lines 334–336).

Crucially for Jain soteriology, **karma itself is understood as fine *pudgala*** — physical, atomic matter that adheres to the *jiva* through the passions. See [[karma]] for the full account.

### 4. Akasa (Space)

*Akasa* "is the locus in which the other entities perform their proper tasks. According to Akalanka, it functions in the same way as a great pot in which rice is being cooked (*TSRV* p. 477). It is the only entity which interpenetrates both the *loka* and the *aloka*" (lines 337–340).

### 5. Kala (Time) — Digambara Addition

The [[digambara|Digambaras]] add *kala* (time) as a sixth fundamental category, though this is not included in the Svetambara list of five (line 224).

## The *Tattvas* and *Ajiva*

The *ajiva* entities (motion, rest, matter, space) alone do not explain why the *jiva* becomes embodied. "It was in the context of karmic theorisation that such an explanation was given and it took the form of a series of nine 'reals' (*tattva*) which maps out in abbreviated form the various modifications which can effect change in the spiritual status of the *jiva*" (lines 344–350). The *tattvas* include *jiva* and *ajiva* plus the karma-related categories (*asrava*, *bandha*, *samvara*, *nirjara*, *moksa*, *punya*, *papa*). See [[tattvartha-sutra]] for Umasvati's seven-*tattva* version.
