# Limbo

**Limbo** (*limbus*, "border/hem") is, in Latin scholastic eschatology, a state on the "border" of
hell for souls excluded from the [[beatific-vision|beatific vision]] but not subject to the punishment
of the [[hell|damned]]. [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] distinguishes two limbos: the **limbo of the
Fathers** (*limbus patrum*) and the **limbo of children** (*limbus infantium*).

## The limbo of the Fathers (limbus patrum)

In the [[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Tertia Pars Q52 (Christ's descent into hell), the *limbus patrum* is
where the **just who died before Christ** were detained — held back from the vision of God **through
original sin** (not personal guilt), because "the price of man's redemption was not yet paid." At His
**descent into hell**, Christ's soul was locally present **only** in this region, which He delivered,
leading the holy Fathers into glory at His Resurrection. See [[atonement]] for the descent. With the
Passion accomplished, the *limbus patrum* is empty.

## The limbo of children (limbus infantium)

In the Supplement Appendix, Aquinas treats the fate of **those who die in original sin alone** — chiefly
**unbaptized infants**. Their **only** punishment is the **pain of loss** (*poena damni*): the
deprivation of the beatific vision. There is **no pain of sense** (sensible pain answers to personal
sinful pleasure, of which they had none), and — on the view Aquinas adopts (the third of three he
surveys) — **no interior sorrow**: never having been adapted to possess eternal life, these souls
rejoice in their natural goods and a natural knowledge of God. The key authority is **Gregory
Nazianzen**, who places unbaptized infants in a *middle state* — "neither glory nor eternal pain."

## Reception and status

The *limbus infantium* was a widely held theological opinion in medieval and later
[[catholicism|Catholicism]], **never defined as dogma**; modern Catholic teaching (the 2007
International Theological Commission, *The Hope of Salvation for Infants Who Die Without Being
Baptised*) treats it as one hypothesis and expresses hope for their salvation. **Eastern Orthodoxy**
and the **Protestant** traditions never adopted the Latin limbo scheme. The fate of unbaptized infants
remains a candidate `controversies/` topic.

## In Dante's Inferno

Dante gives Limbo memorable form as the **first circle** of [[inferno|Hell]] (Inf.
4): the home of the unbaptized and the virtuous pagans, who suffer no torment but
live "in desire, but hopelessly," sighing rather than weeping. [[virgil|Virgil]]
himself dwells there, among Homer, Aristotle, Saladin, and Averroes; it is the
deficiency of faith, not positive sin, that holds them. The recurring question of
why such virtue must still be excluded runs through the whole poem.
