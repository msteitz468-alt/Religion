*Gnosis* (Greek: γνῶσις, "knowledge") in the context of ancient Gnosticism refers to a specific form of salvific, esoteric, revealed knowledge that awakens the divine spark within the human being, discloses the true nature of God, the cosmos, and the self, and enables the return of the pneumatic element to its divine origin. It is not ordinary intellectual knowledge (*epistēmē*), faith (*pistis*), or ethical obedience, but a transformative, mystical recognition that liberates from the ignorance (*agnoia*) and alienation of the material world.

## Definition and Core Features (per Rudolph)

Kurt Rudolph's phenomenological analysis (*Gnosis: The Nature and History of Gnosticism*) identifies *gnosis* as the central soteriological category that unifies the diverse Gnostic schools. Key characteristics:

- **Revealed and esoteric**: Gnosis is not achieved by human effort or philosophy alone but granted by a heavenly revealer/redeemer (often a descending figure who brings light/knowledge into darkness). It is "knowledge of who we were, what we have become; where we were, wherein we have been thrown; whither we hasten, from what we are redeemed; what birth is, and what rebirth" (famous Valentinian formulation preserved in Clement of Alexandria, *Excerpta ex Theodoto* 78.2).
- **Existential and cosmological**: It reveals the radical dualism between the transcendent, alien, true God (beyond the cosmos) and the inferior Demiurge who created this flawed material world; the entrapment of the divine spark (*pneuma*) in body and soul; and the path of ascent through the hostile archons (cosmic rulers) back to the Pleroma (divine fullness).
- **Soteriological efficacy**: Gnosis itself effects salvation for the pneumatic (spiritual) class of humanity. It is the "call" or "awakening" from sleep/forgetfulness. In some systems it has a sacramental or ritual dimension (passwords, seals, names for the soul's ascent).
- **Distinguished from faith and works**: While some Gnostic groups (esp. Valentinians) allowed a place for "psychics" (ordinary Christians) saved by faith and ethics, the true pneumatics are saved by gnosis. This produces the characteristic self-understanding of Gnostics as "the few" or "the perfect" in contrast to the "many."

Rudolph emphasizes that gnosis has both a noetic (intellectual/mystical insight) and a practical (liberating, reorienting) dimension; it is "knowledge that saves" because it realigns the knower with their true divine identity and origin.

## Origins and Development

Gnosis as a soteriological concept draws on multiple streams:
- Jewish apocalyptic and wisdom traditions (esoteric knowledge of heavenly secrets, "knowledge of God" as relationship and revelation).
- Hellenistic philosophy and mystery cults (ascent of the soul, passwords in the afterlife, Platonic "recollection" reinterpreted).
- Early Christian motifs (revelation of the Son, "knowing the truth will set you free" in John, but radically revalued).

In fully developed Gnostic systems (2nd c. CE onward), gnosis becomes the organizing principle that inverts or transcends ordinary religious categories: the God of Genesis is often the ignorant or malevolent Demiurge; the serpent or other "villains" of Scripture become bearers of gnosis; salvation is escape from rather than redemption of the created order.

Rudolph notes that while Christian Gnosticism (Valentinian, Sethian) is the best attested, the structure of gnosis as saving knowledge appears in non-Christian or marginally Christian forms as well (e.g., some Hermetic texts, Manichaeism).

## Tradition-Specific Variations

**Sethian Gnosticism** (e.g., *Apocryphon of John*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*): Gnosis is closely tied to the revelation of the divine Mother Barbelo and the restoration of the seed of Seth. Strong emphasis on the "five seals" or baptismal/ascensional rites that impart or confirm gnosis. Often more "Jewish" in its heavy use of Genesis reinterpretation and apocalyptic motifs.

**Valentinianism** (Valentinus, Ptolemy, Heracleon, *Gospel of Truth*, *Gospel of Philip*): More philosophically elaborated and "Christian" in vocabulary. Gnosis is the recognition of one's origin in the Pleroma, the fall of Sophia, and the work of the Savior (Christ) who brings the "knowledge of the Father." The *Gospel of Truth* presents gnosis in meditative, almost homiletical form as the healing of error and forgetfulness. Valentinians developed a rich sacramental life in which gnosis was enacted (baptism, chrism, Eucharist, redemption, bridal chamber as symbol of restoration to the Pleroma).

**Basilideanism and others**: Gnosis often linked to specific numerological or cosmological knowledge (365 heavens in Basilides); salvation as progressive enlightenment through the aeons.

**Manichaeism**: Gnosis systematized into a complete dualistic world religion with extensive canonical scriptures. The "knowledge of the two principles" (Light vs. Darkness) and the "three moments" (past, present, future) constitutes the core gnosis preached by Mani. Ascent and liberation of light particles is central.

**Mandaeism** (surviving tradition): Gnosis (*manda* = knowledge) remains central; repeated baptismal rites effect purification and ascent; rich mythology of revealers (e.g., Hibil-Ziwa) who impart knowledge against the archons and the "seven" (planets).

## Key Sources and Influence

Rudolph's *Gnosis* provides the most systematic phenomenological account. Primary witnesses: Nag Hammadi tractates (especially *Apocryphon of John*, *Gospel of Truth*, *Gospel of Philip*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*); heresiologists (Irenaeus on Valentinian gnosis; Hippolytus); Manichaean and Mandaean texts. The recovery of primary sources since 1945 has allowed scholars to hear Gnostic self-descriptions of gnosis rather than only polemical caricatures.
