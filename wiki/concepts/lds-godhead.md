# LDS Godhead

The **LDS Godhead** is the Latter-day Saint doctrine of **three distinct divine personages** — God the Eternal Father, his Son Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost — **one in will and purpose**, not one in ontological substance as in Nicene Trinitarianism.

## Textual Anchors

- **Articles of Faith 1** — "We believe in God, the Eternal Father, and in His Son, Jesus Christ, and in the Holy Ghost."
- **Joseph Smith—History 1:16–17** — First Vision: **two Personages**; "This is My Beloved Son. Hear Him!"
- **D&C 130:22** — Father and Son have bodies of flesh and bones as tangible as man's; Holy Ghost is a personage of Spirit.
- **Book of Mormon** — often uses "one God" and Father/Son/Holy Ghost language (2 Ne 31:21; 3 Ne 11:27, 36; Mormon 7:7) that LDS read as unity of purpose; some passages (e.g. Mosiah 15) have been read as more modalist by outsiders.
- **3 Nephi 11** — Father's voice introduces the Son; nail prints; baptism in three names; "the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Ghost are one."

## Contrast with Creedal Trinity

| | LDS Godhead | Nicene Trinity |
|--|-------------|----------------|
| Persons | Three distinct beings | Three persons, one essence |
| Embodiment | Father and Son corporeal | God is spirit (classical) |
| Substance | Not consubstantial | Homoousios |

This contrast is central to [[mormon-christian-identity]].
