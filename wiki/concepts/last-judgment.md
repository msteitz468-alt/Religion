# The Last Judgment

The final, universal judgment of God at the end of the world — the subject of [[city-of-god|*City of
God*]] Book XX, where [[augustine|Augustine]] assembles the proofs from both Testaments.

## God's hidden judgments made manifest

Augustine distinguishes God's continuous, **hidden** judgments — by which "this good man is poor and
that bad man rich" — from the **final** judgment, when "true and full happiness shall be the lot of none
but the good, while deserved and supreme misery shall be the portion of the wicked." Ecclesiastes'
exposure of "vanity under the sun" is read as driving the reader toward that day (Eccl. 12:13–14, "God
shall bring every work into judgment").

## The two resurrections

Central to Augustine's eschatology is the distinction (from John 5) between **two resurrections**:

- the **first resurrection** — present and *spiritual*, the soul's regeneration now ("the hour is
  coming, and now is"), which preserves from the second death;
- the **second resurrection** — future and *bodily*, at the end, when "all that are in the graves shall
  hear His voice" and rise "unto the resurrection of life" or "of judgment."

## The millennium (Rev. 20)

Augustine reads the **thousand years** not as a future earthly reign but as the present age of the
[[two-cities|Church]] between Christ's two comings — the influential **amillennial** reading. He
explicitly rejects the **chiliasts / millenarians**, who expect a carnal sabbath of "immoderate carnal
banquets" (a view he says he once held). The "thousand" is the cube of ten, a number of perfection
signifying the whole of this age; Satan is *bound* (unable to seduce the nations from which the Church
is gathered) and at the end *loosed* for "a short season." **Gog and Magog** are not literal barbarian
nations but all the peoples in whom Satan is shut up, in the final assault of "the whole city of the
devil" on "the whole city of Christ." See [[millennialism]].

## The order of the last things

Augustine's tentative sequence (admitting "how, or in what order, human understanding cannot perfectly
teach us"): **Elijah** comes and gives the Jews a spiritual reading of the Law; **the Jews believe**;
**Antichrist** (the "man of sin," 2 Thess. 2; the fourth-beast adversary of Dan. 7) persecutes for
"time, times, and half a time" (3½ years) with "signs and lying wonders"; **Christ judges** — coming
*manifestly*, having first come *hiddenly* to be judged; **the dead rise**; **good and wicked are
separated** (some "saved as by fire," some condemned); **the world is burned** ("the figure passes
away, not the nature"); and a **new heaven and new earth** appear. On the "restrainer" of 2 Thess. 2,
Augustine "frankly confess[es] I do not know what he means," and reports the Roman-Empire and
hypocrites-in-the-Church interpretations without adjudicating.
