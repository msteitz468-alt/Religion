# Takfir (Declaration of Unbelief)

*Takfir* is the act of declaring a professed Muslim (or a doctrine) to be **outside Islam**, an
infidel (*kafir*) — a grave verdict with legal consequences. Its boundary against mere
**heretical innovation (*bid'a*)** is contested.

## Al-Ghazali's Selective Takfir of the Philosophers

In the Conclusion of the *[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|Tahafut al-Falasifa]]*,
[[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]] pronounces the [[falsafa|philosophers]] **infidels** on exactly three
doctrines that "do not agree with Islam in any respect": the
[[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the world]]; that God's knowledge does not encompass
temporal particulars; and the denial of [[bodily-resurrection|bodily resurrection]]. Their other
errors he classes as *bid'a* shared with sects such as the [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]] — not
*kufr*. This careful triage became a model case in Islamic theology of *where* takfir is
warranted. [[averroes|Averroes]] contested both the verdict and its grounds.

## Averroes's Response to the Three Charges

In the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahāfut al-Tahāfut]]*, [[averroes|Averroes]] answers each
of the three capital charges and reframes the boundary of unbelief itself:

1. **Eternity of the world** — he holds the dispute is partly verbal ("eternal becoming" vs.
   temporal creation) and that *neither* side demonstrates its case. See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
2. **God's knowledge of particulars** — Avicenna's "universal way" is a misformulation; God's
   knowledge is "neither universal nor individual," being the *cause* of existents. See
   [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]].
3. **Denial of bodily resurrection** — Averroes affirms resurrection as religiously necessary but
   reinterprets it as *simulacra* (specifically, not numerically, identical bodies). See
   [[bodily-resurrection]].

He turns the charge around: the genuine ground of unbelief is **publicly disputing the principles of
religion**, and on his account a learned man who doubts those principles "merits more than anyone
else that the term unbeliever should be applied to him" — while the inner sense reserved for the
elect is no heresy. See [[harmony-of-religion-and-philosophy]]. He also notes Ghazālī's own
inconsistency in applying the resurrection charge.
