# Bahá'í Calendar (Badí' Calendar)

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from lines 8433–8644 unless noted.*

## Definition and Origin

The Badi' ("Wondrous") calendar was established by [[the-bab|the Báb]] and subsequently amended slightly by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] as a distinctive Bahá'í calendar (lines 8434–8437). It begins on the ancient Iranian new year (Naw-Rúz) at the spring equinox — normally 21 March.

## Structure

The year is divided into nineteen months of nineteen days each, with four intercalary days (five in a leap year) added to make up a full solar year of 365 days (19 × 19 = 361 + 4/5). The months are named after attributes of God (lines 8437–8444, 8621–8625).

The first year of the calendar is 1844, the year of the Báb's declaration. Years are named with letters or attributes having numerical values. The cycle of 19 years is called a *váhid* ("unity" = 19), and the cycle of 361 years a *kull-i-shay'* ("all things" = 361) (lines 8615–8617).

At the present time only the monthly divisions are in common use, as these fix the dates of the regular nineteen-day [[feasts|Feasts]] held in local Bahá'í communities (lines 8631–8635).

## Days of the Week

The Bahá'í week runs from Saturday (*Jalál*/Glory) to Friday (*Istiqláll*/Independence). Days are named: Jalál (Saturday), Jamál (Sunday), Kamál (Monday), Fidál (Tuesday), 'Idál (Wednesday), Istijlál (Thursday), Istiqláll (Friday). The day begins at sunset. Friday is the day of rest (lines 8524–8556).

## Intercalary Days (Ayyám-i-Há)

The intercalary days are placed between the eighteenth and nineteenth months. They are a period of charity and gift-giving (lines 8636–8638).

## Fasting Month

The last month ('Alá', 2–20 March) is the Bahá'í month of [[fasting|Fasting]] (lines 8639–8640).
