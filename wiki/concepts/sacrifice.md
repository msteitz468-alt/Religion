# Sacrifice

Sacrifice — the offering rendered to the divine — is treated most fully in this wiki through
[[augustine|Augustine]]'s [[city-of-god|*City of God*]] **Book X**, where it becomes the hinge of his
argument that worship is due to the one true God alone.

## Latreia — Worship Due to God Alone

Augustine opens Book X with a terminological argument: the Greek **λατρεία (latreia)** names the service
"paid to God alone by worship," distinct from *douleia* (service to humans) and broader than the Latin
*cultus*, *religio*, or *pietas* ([[pietas]]). Sacrifice in the strict sense belongs only to God: "He that
sacrificeth unto any god, save unto the Lord only, he shall be utterly destroyed" (Ex. 22:20). The good
angels themselves **refuse worship** and direct it to God — "we and they together are the one city of
God" ([[two-cities]]).

## True Sacrifice vs. Figural Sacrifice

Augustine distinguishes the outward, animal sacrifice from the inward reality it signifies:

- A material sacrifice is "the visible sacrament or sacred sign of an invisible sacrifice."
- The **true sacrifice** is "every work which is done that we may be united to God in holy fellowship" —
  pre-eminently the **contrite heart** ("the sacrifice of God is a broken spirit," Ps. 51), the body
  offered as "a living sacrifice" (Rom. 12:1), and the whole redeemed city offered to God through Christ
  the High Priest: "we, being many, are one body in Christ. And this also is the sacrifice which the
  Church continually celebrates in the sacrament of the altar."
- **Christ is both Priest and Victim** — "in the form of a servant He chose rather to be than to receive a
  sacrifice… Thus He is both the Priest who offers and the Sacrifice offered."

## Old Testament Sacrifice as Type

The animal sacrifices of the Hebrew cult "signified the things which we do for the purpose of drawing near
to God" — figural signs ([[typology]]) pointing forward to the one true sacrifice in Christ. This reading
is not Origenist allegory but a typology in which the historical rites *as events* function as
*sacramenta* of the invisible offering.

## Against Pagan Sacrifice and Theurgy

The same argument excludes sacrifice to gods, demons, or angels — and refutes [[porphyry|Porphyry]]'s
**theurgy**, which claimed to purify the soul through ritual but in fact trafficked with deceiving
demons. Sacrifice to demons (1 Cor. 10:20) is the false worship the [[plato|Platonists]] could not escape.
