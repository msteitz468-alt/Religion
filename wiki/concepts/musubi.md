# Musubi (the generative kami-force)

**Musubi** (産霊, also written 結び) is the **generative, binding, creative power** of [[shinto|Shinto]] — the
mysterious force of **growth, fertility, production, and union** that brings things into being and ties them
together. The word is linked to *musubu*, "to bind / to bring forth," and stands behind Shinto's deep
orientation toward **life, generation, and increase** rather than salvation from sin or escape from the world.

## In the myth and pantheon

Musubi is **personified among the very first kami**. In the **[[kojiki|Kojiki]]**, before the world is formed,
among the primordial deities who "come into being alone" are **Takami-musubi** ("High-August-Producing-Wondrous
deity") and **Kami-musubi** ("Divine-Producing-Wondrous deity) — abstract **creative powers** present at the
origin, who continue to act through the age of the gods (Takami-musubi helps direct the **[[ninigi|descent of
Ninigi]]**, and Kami-musubi revives the slain **[[okuninushi|Ōkuninushi]]**). Musubi is thus the **engine of
[[kami|kami]]-activity**: the power by which gods, land, and life are produced and renewed.

## Meaning

The concept expresses Shinto's characteristic **life-affirming, productive vision**:

- the cosmos is not created from nothing by a transcendent will but **continually generated** by an immanent
  vital force;
- **fertility, harvest, and reproduction** are sacred (much Shinto ritual is agricultural and life-giving);
- human **connection and union** — even the bond of marriage, and the tying of *musubi*-knots — partake of the
  same generative power.

Musubi underlies the Shinto sense that **to live, grow, and bring forth is itself holy**, in keeping with the
[[harae|purity]]-and-vitality emphasis over moral struggle.
