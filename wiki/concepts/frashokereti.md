# Frashokereti ("the Making Wonderful")

**Frashokereti** (Avestan; Pahlavi *Frashegird*), "the making wonderful," is the [[zoroastrianism|
Zoroastrian]] doctrine of the **final renovation of the world** — the climactic, definitive triumph of good
over evil at the end of time, when the cosmos is **made perfect and incorruptible forever**. It is, on
[[cohn-cosmos-chaos-and-the-world-to-come|Norman Cohn's]] argument, the **first fully eschatological vision of
history** and the fountainhead of later apocalyptic faith.

## The Doctrine

Where the older religions imagined a **static cosmos** perpetually defended against chaos (see [[the-combat-myth|
the combat myth]]), [[zarathustra|Zoroaster]] taught that the long war of [[asha-druj|*asha* against *druj*]]
— of [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] against Angra Mainyu — **would have an end**. At the consummation:

- the savior **[[saoshyant|Saoshyant]]** (a "future benefactor," eventually triplicated in later
  tradition) appears;
- the **dead are resurrected** in their bodies and reunited with their souls (the general Last
  Judgment that completes the provisional verdict of [[chinvat-bridge|the Chinvat Bridge]]);
- all pass through a **final fiery ordeal / molten-metal judgment** that purges evil;
- **Angra Mainyu and hell are destroyed**, and the world is restored to the perfect, deathless state it was
  always meant to have — a "changeless realm... over which the supreme god will reign... for evermore."

Frashokereti is the third and final of [[the-three-times|the Three Times]] — the age of
**Separation** (*Wizarishn*), in which good is parted from evil for good and, since evil is then
annihilated, lasts **eternally**. Zoroaster seems to have expected it **soon**; later Zoroastrian
priests, serving an established empire, **postponed** it into a remote, schematized future (the
millennial scheme of [[bundahishn|the Bundahishn]]). Each year the **No Ruz / Nowruz** (New Year)
festival prefigures the renewal.

## Significance and Influence

Frashokereti bundles together **linear time, final judgment, bodily resurrection, a savior, and a renewed
world** — the very cluster that becomes central to Abrahamic eschatology (the "new heavens and new earth,"
the resurrection of Daniel 12, the messianic savior). Whether this is **influence or independent parallel**
is the standing question of [[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]] — recorded
without overclaiming.
