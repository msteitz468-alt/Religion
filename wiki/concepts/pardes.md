# PaRDeS (פַּרְדֵּס)

The fourfold scheme of Jewish biblical interpretation, named by the acronym **PaRDeS** — formed from the initial letters of its four levels: **Peshat**, **Remez**, **Derash**, **Sod**. The word *pardes* itself means "orchard" or "garden" (a loanword from Persian, cognate with "paradise"), and the term puns on the famous Talmudic legend of the "four who entered the orchard" (*pardes*) of esoteric study (*Ḥagigah* 14b), only one of whom (Rabbi Akiva) "entered in peace and left in peace." The acronym thus carries an implicit warning: the deepest levels of interpretation are spiritually perilous.

The scheme was crystallized in 13th-century Kabbalistic circles — Moses de León uses it, and it is associated with the broader Castilian Kabbalah that produced the [[zohar|Zohar]]. It both organizes the tradition's interpretive practices and asserts the supremacy of the fourth level, **Sod** (the mystical/secret sense), which is the [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]]'s own domain.

## The Four Levels

| Level | Hebrew | Meaning | Mode of Reading |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **Peshat** | פְּשָׁט | "plain / simple" | The straightforward, contextual, grammatical-historical meaning |
| **Remez** | רֶמֶז | "hint / allusion" | The allegorical or philosophical meaning hinted beneath the surface |
| **Derash** | דְּרָשׁ | "inquiry / homily" | The midrashic, homiletical, applied meaning derived by rabbinic exegetical rules |
| **Sod** | סוֹד | "secret / mystery" | The mystical meaning — the sefirotic/theosophical reality the text encodes |

### Peshat — The Plain Sense
The contextual meaning of the words as governed by grammar, syntax, semantics, and narrative flow. The medieval *peshat* revolution (Rashi, Rashbam, Ibn Ezra) recovered the plain sense against the dominance of midrash. Crucially, *peshat* does not mean "literal" in a wooden sense; it means the meaning that fits the context (*peshuto shel miqra*). Kabbalah does not reject *peshat*; it claims to read beneath it.

### Remez — The Allusive/Allegorical Sense
The hint beneath the surface — typically the philosophical-allegorical meaning. In medieval usage, *remez* often denotes the philosophical reading practiced by the Maimonidean rationalists (e.g., reading the Garden of Eden as the active intellect). The kabbalists appropriated the category but redirected it.

### Derash — The Homiletical Sense
The interpretive mode of classical rabbinic Midrash — deriving moral lessons, legal rulings, and narrative expansions through the established middot (hermeneutical rules: *gezerah shavah*, *qal va-ḥomer*, etc.). Derash fills gaps, harmonizes contradictions, and applies the text to communal life. See [[talmudic-hermeneutics|Talmudic Hermeneutics]] and [[oral-torah|Oral Torah]].

### Sod — The Mystical/Secret Sense
The deepest level — the claim that Scripture, beneath its plain, allusive, and homiletical meanings, narrates the inner life of God: the dynamic interplay of the ten [[sefirot|sefirot]], the union of Tif'eret and [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]], the struggle against the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]. At the level of Sod, every divine name, narrative detail, and ritual commandment is a coded reference to sefirotic reality. This is the level at which the Zohar operates almost exclusively.

## Sod as Practiced in the Zohar

The Zohar's reading at the Sod level employs a distinctive set of techniques:

1. **Sefirotic correspondence** — every divine name maps to a sefirah; every biblical image (water, tree, well, garment, light) maps to a sefirah or a relationship among sefirot
2. **Theogonic reading** — narratives that *peshat* reads as external history (creation, the Flood, the patriarchs' lives) are simultaneously accounts of events *within* the divine. Genesis 1 is not (only) about the world's creation but about the self-unfolding of the sefirot from Ein Sof
3. **Reverse parsing** — re-reading the grammar of a verse to expose a hidden subject. The classic example: Genesis 1:1, normally "In the beginning God (Elohim) created the heavens and the earth," is re-parsed as "With *Beginning* (= Ḥokhmah), [Ein Sof] created *Elohim* (= Binah)" — making the word "God" the *object*, not the subject, of creation
4. **Letter and vowel mysticism** — the shapes, names, and numerical values of Hebrew letters encode cosmic processes; the crownlets on letters carry meaning
5. **Gematria and notarikon** — numerical and acrostic decoding

The Zohar's signature claim about Torah and Sod is voiced in its famous parable of the "beautiful maiden in the palace" (*Saba de-Mishpatim*, in a later volume) — Torah reveals herself to her lover by degrees, from the outer garment (*peshat*) to her naked body (*sod*); the one who loves her reads through the garments to the living reality beneath. The corollary is the Zohar's polemic against reading Torah only for its narratives:

> *"Woe to the person who says that Torah comes to tell mere tales... Every word of Torah contains a sublime secret."* (Zohar 3:152a — *Be-Haalotekha*; anticipated throughout Vol. I)

## PaRDeS and the Other Tradition-Specific Schemes

PaRDeS is the Jewish member of a family of multi-level interpretive schemes that developed across the Abrahamic traditions:

**vs. the Christian Quadriga** (the fourfold sense): literal, allegorical, tropological (moral), anagogical (eschatological). The structural parallel with PaRDeS is striking and not coincidental — both schemes arose in milieus of intense scriptural commentary and likely cross-pollinated in medieval Iberia and Provence. The correspondences are rough:
- Peshat ≈ literal
- Remez ≈ allegorical
- Derash ≈ tropological (applied/moral)
- Sod ≈ anagogical (the hidden divine/eschatological reality)
But the content differs sharply: the Christian allegorical sense finds *Christ* beneath the Old Testament; the Jewish Sod finds the *sefirot*. See [[fourfold-sense|Fourfold Sense (Quadriga)]].

**vs. Islamic Zahir/Batin**: the exoteric (*zahir*) vs. esoteric (*batin*) distinction, and the practice of *ta'wil* (esoteric interpretation) in Sufism and Shi'ism, parallels the peshat/sod distinction. The Sufi who reads the inner meaning of the Qur'an is the structural counterpart of the kabbalist reading Sod. See [[allegorical-reading|Allegorical Reading]].
