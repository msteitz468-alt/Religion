# He Whom God Shall Make Manifest

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

"He Whom God Shall Make Manifest" (*Man-yuẓhiruhu'lláh*) is the Babi messianic figure repeatedly referred to by [[the-bab|the Báb]] in the [[the-bayan|Bayán]]. This personage was said by the Báb to be the origin of all divine names and attributes; his command was equivalent to God's command (lines 16552–16554).

## The Báb's Teachings About This Figure

Key attributes as stated in the Bayán (lines 16554–16566):
- All should seek refuge in him
- Not to believe in him would be to cease to be a believer
- A thousand perusal of the Bayán were not equal to reading one of his verses
- The Bayán itself was a gift to him and revolved around his word
- The day of his advent was known only to God, but would certainly occur
- It was impossible for anyone to falsely claim to be him
- He would be known by his own self, and not by the Bayán
- In the meantime, all must rise on hearing his name and in expectation

## Historical Claimants

After the Báb's execution (1850) and the disruption of Babism as an organized movement (1852), several Babis claimed high-ranking spiritual stations, including that of Man-yuẓhiruhu'lláh (lines 16573–16583). [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] himself eventually laid claim to be the Babi promised one in the 1860s (line 16583).

## The Azali Objection

The [[azalis|Azalis]] objected to Bahá'u'lláh's claim on the grounds that the Báb had stated the promised one would come before the number of "mustagh'áthu'lláh" ('the Beseeched', a divine attribute; numerically equivalent to 2,001 years) or "aghyathu'l-aghyáth" ('the Most Succoured', 1,511 years) — i.e., in the distant future (lines 16584–16592).

Bahá'ís countered by stressing that the Báb had also said Man-yuẓhiruhu'lláh would arise suddenly at a time known only to God, and had noted the significance of the "year nine" (i.e., 1269 AH/1852–3) as the year of Bahá'u'lláh's arrival in Baghdad (lines 16611–16634).

## Significance

This concept is central to the transition from Babism to the Bahá'í Faith. Bahá'u'lláh's claim to be this figure constituted the decisive moment of the Faith's founding claim, and the concept is therefore foundational to understanding Bahá'í theology of [[progressive-revelation|progressive revelation]] and the relationship between the Babi and Bahá'í dispensations.
