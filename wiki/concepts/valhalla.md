# Valhalla and the Norse Afterlife

**Valhalla** ("Hall of the Slain") is [[odin|Odin's]] great hall in Asgard, where **warriors who die bravely
in battle** are gathered to feast and fight until [[ragnarok|Ragnarök]] — the most famous image of the
[[norse-religion|Norse]] afterlife, and the engine of its **heroic warrior ethos**.

## The Hall of the Slain

The **Valkyries** ("choosers of the slain"), Odin's battle-maidens, ride over the battlefield and **choose
the worthy dead**, bringing them to Valhalla. There the chosen warriors (the **einherjar**) **fight each day
and feast each night** — healed of their wounds, eating the boar Sæhrímnir and drinking mead — training for
the final battle, when they will march out beside the gods at Ragnarök (and fall with them). To die a
**glorious death in combat** was thus the highest hope; Odin gathers an army precisely because he knows the
doom that is coming.

## A Many-Roomed Afterlife

Valhalla was **not** the destiny of all. Half the battle-slain go to **[[freyja|Freyja's]]** hall **Fólkvangr**;
those who die of sickness or old age go down to **[[hel|Hel]]**, the dim underworld realm; the drowned belong
to the sea-goddess Rán; and there are hints of rebirth and of dwelling in ancestral howes. The Norse afterlife
was thus **plural and this-worldly** in flavor — less a moral judgment than a sorting by the **manner of
death**.
