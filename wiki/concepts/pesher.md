# Pesher
**Hebrew**: *pēsher* (פשר) — "solution, interpretation, explanation" (cognate to Aramaic *peshar*; cf. Dan. 2:4, where the same word is used for interpretation of dreams)

The distinctive **biblical interpretive method** of the Qumran community, in which prophetic and poetic texts of Scripture are treated as coded references to events in the community's own immediate history and eschatological situation. The key claim: the prophets received inspired words whose full meaning was hidden from the prophets themselves and from all subsequent readers — until God revealed the meaning to the **Teacher of Righteousness**, who applies it to current events.

## The Interpretive Formula
Pesher texts follow a fixed formula:
1. Quote the biblical text verbatim
2. Follow with "its *pesher* [interpretation] concerns…" (*pishro 'al…*)
3. Apply the text to the community's immediate historical experience

Examples from the Habakkuk Commentary:
- *Hab. 1:6 — "I am raising up the Chaldaeans"* → "its *pesher* concerns the Kittim [= Romans]"
- *Hab. 2:4 — "the righteous shall live by his faith"* → "its *pesher* concerns all those who observe the Law in the House of Judah… because of their faith in the Teacher of Righteousness"

## Types of Pesher
Vermes identifies five forms of biblical interpretation at Qumran:
1. **Reworked Pentateuch** (4Q158, 364-7): expansive rewriting of Torah with interpolated material
2. **Expanded narrative** (Genesis Apocryphon type): retelling and amplification of biblical stories in new narrative form
3. **Free compositions inspired by the Bible**: new works in biblical style
4. **Pesher proper** (prophetic fulfillment): running commentary applying the prophetic text to the community's history
5. **Parabiblical texts**: thematically biblical without being commentary (testaments, apocalypses)

### Continuous Pesher (*pesher nifrad*)
Running commentary on a biblical book chapter by chapter: Habakkuk Commentary (1QpHab), Isaiah Commentary (4Q161-5), Hosea Commentary (4Q166-7), Nahum Commentary (4Q169), Psalms Commentary (4Q171, 173).

### Thematic Pesher (*pesher medubbaq*)
Collects proof texts from multiple books on a single theme: Florilegium (4Q174), Testimonia (4Q175), Melchizedek text (11Q13).

## The Epistemological Claim
Pesher rests on a specific theory of prophetic inspiration: the prophets spoke under divine compulsion without understanding their own words. The full meaning was *sealed* until the eschatological era. God then revealed the meaning to the Teacher of Righteousness as a special revelatory gift:

> "God told Habakkuk to write down that which would happen to the final generation, but He did not make known to him when time would come to an end. And as for that which He said, *That he who runs may read it* — its *pesher* concerns the Teacher of Righteousness, **to whom God made known all the mysteries of the words of His servants the Prophets**." (1QpHab VII:1-5)

This creates a **two-stage theory of revelation**: prophecy itself (incomplete) and pesher interpretation (complete). The Teacher's interpretation is authoritative and binding.

## Relationship to Other Interpretive Methods
Pesher is distinct from but related to:

- **Midrash** (rabbinic): also interprets Scripture creatively, but typically explores multiple meanings and invites dialogue; not generally claiming that one interpreter alone has the definitive meaning
- **Allegory** (Philo): treats the literal text as a code for philosophical or ethical truths; Qumran pesher treats the text as a code for historical events, not abstract ideas
- **Typology** (early Christian): finds historical anticipation of later events; closer to pesher in that it sees prophecy fulfilled in specific events, but Christian typology does not require a single specially authorized interpreter

The key distinctive of Qumran pesher: **contemporizing application** (prophetic text = current events), **authorized interpreter** (the Teacher alone decodes it), and **eschatological urgency** (the events described are happening *now*, in the final era).

## Historical Significance
The Qumran pesharim are the earliest known examples of running biblical commentary applied to contemporary events. They shed light on:
- The development of scriptural interpretation in late Second Temple Judaism
- New Testament methods of citing and applying OT prophecy to Jesus and the early church (Matthew's formula quotations; 1QpHab parallels)
- The formation of messianic and apocalyptic exegetical traditions
