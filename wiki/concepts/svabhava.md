# Svabhāva

**Svabhāva** (Sanskrit, "own-being, intrinsic nature") is the metaphysical concept at the heart of
the Buddhist emptiness debate. To have *svabhāva* is to possess a nature that is **intrinsic** —
belonging to a thing in and of itself, independent of facts about anything else. Its denial is the
content of **[[sunyata|emptiness (śūnyatā)]]**: to be empty *just is* to lack *svabhāva*.

## In Abhidharma

For the [[abhidharma|Abhidharma]] schools, *svabhāva* is the mark of the ultimately real. Only the
simple, impartite **dharmas** have it; composite wholes (a chariot, a person) do not — their
properties (size, shape) are **extrinsic (*parabhāva*)**, "borrowed" from the parts and the mind's
aggregating. A dharma, by contrast, has exactly **one** intrinsic nature, because it is what remains
when analysis has dissolved all mental construction. *Svabhāva* is thus what makes a statement
*ultimately* (not merely conventionally) true or false. (See [[two-truths|the two truths]].)

## Nāgārjuna's Critique

[[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s [[mulamadhyamakakarika|MMK]] turns this against itself. He accepts the
Abhidharma definition — *if* anything were ultimately real it would have *svabhāva* — but argues
**nothing can have it**:

- Anything that **arises through causes and conditions** ([[dependent-origination|dependent
  origination]]) gets its character *from* those conditions, and so its nature is borrowed, not
  intrinsic — "if you look upon existents as real intrinsically, you regard them as without cause
  and conditions" (MMK 24.16). Hence **dependent origination entails emptiness** (MMK 24.18–19): no
  dharma is non-empty.
- An intrinsic nature could not change, arise, or cease; but the dharmas are impermanent and
  causally produced. *Svabhāva* and causation are incompatible.

So the dharmas go the way of the chariot: conceptual fictions, empty of *svabhāva*. And **emptiness
itself is empty** — it is not a new *svabhāva*-bearing ultimate, but a "dependent concept."

## Cross-Tradition Note

*Svabhāva*-denial is the precise inverse of substance metaphysics elsewhere in the wiki — the Hindu
**[[atman|ātman]]/Brahman** as self-existent reality, the scholastic *substantia*, or any *aseity*
predicated of the divine. The Buddhist claim is that intrinsic, independent existence is found
**nowhere**.
