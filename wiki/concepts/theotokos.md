# Theotokos (*Mother of God*)

*Theotokos* (Θεοτόκος, "God-bearer," *Dei genetrix*) is the title of the Virgin [[mary|Mary]] that became the flashpoint of the fifth-century Christological controversy. To affirm it is to affirm that the one born of Mary is the divine Word himself; to deny it (as [[nestorius|Nestorius]] did) is — on the Cyrilline reading — to divide Christ into two subjects. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. XI–XII.

## History of the Title

- Used early by Eustathius of Antioch and, in the fourth century, by [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory of Nazianzus]] ("if anyone does not accept Mary as Theotokos, he is cut off from the Godhead") and (as a concession to popular usage) [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]].
- **[[nestorius]]** ruled it of "doubtful propriety" unless balanced by *anthropotokos* ("man-bearer"), preferring *Christotokos*: "God cannot have a mother… Mary bore a man, the vehicle of divinity but not God."
- **Pope Celestine** (Rome, 430) and **[[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s First Anathema** affirmed it: she "bore after the manner of flesh the God-Logos made flesh."
- The **Formula of Reunion (433)** and the **[[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedonian Definition (451)]]** both enshrined it: Christ was "begotten from the Virgin Mary, the Theotokos, as regards His manhood."

The dispute is fundamentally Christological, not (in this period) primarily Marian: the title is a test of the unity of the divine and human in the one Christ. See [[hypostatic-union]], [[communicatio-idiomatum]].
