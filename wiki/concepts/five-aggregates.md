# The Five Aggregates

The **five aggregates** (Pali *pañcakkhandha*; Sanskrit *pañca-skandha*) are the five
bundles of constantly changing physical and mental processes into which Buddhism analyzes
what is conventionally called a "person" or "self." The analysis is the engine of the
**[[anatta|non-self]]** doctrine: when a being is exhaustively resolved into the five
aggregates, **no permanent self is found** among or behind them.

## The Five

1. **Rūpa** — form / materiality: the body and the physical, including the sense faculties.
2. **Vedanā** — feeling / sensation: the hedonic tone (pleasant, unpleasant, neutral) of
   each experience.
3. **Saññā** — perception / recognition: the cognitive labeling that identifies and
   categorizes objects.
4. **Saṅkhāra** — mental formations / volitions: the constructing activities, above all
   **volition** (*cetanā*) — this is the aggregate that produces [[karma|karma]].
5. **Viññāṇa** — consciousness: awareness arising in dependence on a sense faculty and its
   object (not a persisting soul or "knower").

## Aggregates, Dukkha, and Non-Self

[[walpola-rahula|Rahula]]'s exposition makes the aggregates the hinge of the
[[four-noble-truths|First Truth]]: "the five aggregates of clinging (*upādānakkhandha*) are
[[dukkha|dukkha]]." Clinging to the aggregates as "I" and "mine" — when they are
impermanent ([[three-marks-of-existence|anicca]]), unsatisfactory, and
[[anatta|selfless]] — is precisely the structure of suffering. There is no sixth thing, no
owner standing behind the five; the "self" is a convenient designation
(*paññatti*) for their changing interplay.

## Relation to the Abhidharma and Madhyamaka

The aggregate scheme is foundational to the [[abhidharma|Abhidharma]] project of analyzing
experience into ultimate constituents (*dharmas*). [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] accepts the
person's emptiness via the aggregates but presses further: the aggregates **themselves** are
empty of [[svabhava|intrinsic nature]] ([[mulamadhyamakakarika|MMK]] ch. 4 examines
*rūpa*) — the move from "person empty, dharmas real" to "all empty."
