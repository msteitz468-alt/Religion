# Cities of Refuge

**Cities of refuge** (*ʿārê miqlāṭ*) are designated places of asylum for the unintentional manslayer under Torah legislation (Num 35:9–34; Deut 19:1–13; Josh 20). They distinguish accidental killing from intentional murder, protect the killer from the blood-avenger (*gōʾēl haddām*) until trial, and ground land theology in the principle that **shed human blood pollutes the land** and must be answered—by the murderer’s death, not by ransom, for intentional homicide.

> Note: This page is **not** [[refuge|Going for Refuge (the Three Jewels)]] (Buddhist *śaraṇagamana*).

## Biblical Framework (Numbers 35)

Num 35 pairs **Levitical cities** (vv. 1–8: forty-eight cities with pasturelands; six of them refuge cities) with **refuge procedure** (vv. 9–34). Key elements in the final form:

- Six cities: three west of the Jordan, three east (Transjordan settlement requires balanced access).
- Distinction: intentional murder (lying in wait, hatred, iron/stone/wood weapon used to kill) vs unintentional (without enmity, “God let it happen”).
- The congregation judges; the manslayer remains in the city until the **death of the high priest**, then may return to his ancestral land.
- No ransom (*kōp̄er*) accepted for the life of a murderer; blood pollutes the land (cf. Gen 9:5–6).

## Ashley (NICOT Numbers) — Attributed Positions

[[timothy-ashley|Ashley]] treats 35:9–34 as load-bearing for life in the land after the wilderness generation’s failures:

- **Bloodguilt and land pollution:** illicit blood defiles the land so that Yahweh cannot dwell among the people; the land itself is stakeholder (echoes Lev 18:25–28; prophetic *ṭāmēʾ* language).
- **Asylum is not blanket amnesty:** procedure protects due process and the avenger’s legitimate role while preventing vendetta against the unintentional killer.
- **High-priest death as horizon:** release is theological (priesthood and atonement economy), not merely political calendar; Ashley relates it to the broader Numbers theme that **only legitimate priestly structures mediate life with a holy God** (cf. chs. 16–19).
- **Witness requirement:** at least two witnesses for capital conviction (aligns with Deut 17:6; ANE parallels such as Hammurabi §§9–11 noted in apparatus).
- **Ransom forbidden for murder** but *kōp̄er* language elsewhere (e.g. Num 31 gold atonement levy) shows the term’s range—Ashley distinguishes contexts rather than collapsing them.

## Cross-Tradition Notes

- **Deuteronomy / Joshua:** procedural and conquest-settlement implementation; list of cities in Josh 20–21.
- **Rabbinic:** detailed *miqlāṭ* law in Talmud (e.g. Makkot); high-priest mother’s tradition of supplying food/clothing so refugees not pray for high-priest death (reception noted in secondary literature; not Ashley’s focus).
- **Christian reception:** sometimes typologically related to Christ as refuge; Ashley’s NICOT does not make that the controlling exegesis of Num 35.
