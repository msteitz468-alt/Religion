# The Double Homoousion

The **double homoousion** (double consubstantiality) is the Christological formula that Jesus Christ is "**consubstantial with the Father according to his divinity, and consubstantial with us according to his humanity**." First appearing as a fixed formula in the synodal letter of the Oriental bishops at Ephesus (431) and the **Formula of Reunion** (433), it was incorporated into the Definition of the Council of Chalcedon (451). Often used in a conceptually innocent sense (Christ is fully divine and fully human), it carried — within the framework of [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|the Cappadocian classical theory]] — far-reaching and destabilizing implications, as Johannes Zachhuber argues in [[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]].

## Why It Was Destabilizing

Under the classical theory, a common *ousia*/*physis* exists **only and exclusively** in its individual instantiations (hypostases). Applying *homoousios* to **both** of Christ's natures therefore raised a chain of uncomfortable questions:

- If Christ is consubstantial with us, does his human nature exist as a **hypostasis** — i.e. does it "subsist" (*hyphistēken*) after the union? ([[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s second *Letter to Succensus* preserves exactly this objection.)
- If each *homoousios* nature is individuated in a hypostasis, does the Incarnate contain **two hypostases** as well as two natures? And if so, how is his personal unity preserved?
- The widely shared fifth-century inference (taken for granted by Cyril *and* [[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret]] alike) was precisely that **two natures imply two hypostases** — a conclusion the [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedonian]] "two natures in one hypostasis" formula flatly denied, leaving it philosophically unsupported.

Zachhuber notes an alternative escape route, anticipated implicitly by Gregory of Nyssa in his anti-Apollinarian writing: **reduce the classical theory to its abstract dimension** — taking Christ's consubstantiality with us to mean only that he shares the properties typical of human individuals, *without* the further claim that those properties exist in a separate human hypostasis. This abstract reduction became a favourite move of the later Chalcedonians.

## Early History

- **[[apollinarius-of-laodicea|Apollinarius]]** is the single author who most popularized the formula in the fourth century — strikingly, while *also* denying that Christ shared human *ousia* in the technical (Adam-derived) sense. He seems to have used "consubstantial with us" loosely to mean "truly human flesh," unconcerned with terminological parallelism between Trinity and Christology.
- **[[nestorius|Nestorius]]** speaks repeatedly of Christ "consubstantial with us," but in a pre-Cappadocian *derivative* sense (a mother bears what is consubstantial with her — hence his case against *Theotokos*), not as a fixed symmetric formula.
- **[[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]** embraced the symmetric double homoousion against Nestorius and accepted it in the Formula of Reunion (433) — more than mere political acquiescence.
- The Council of **Chalcedon (451)** made it integral to its Definition; Eutyches' refusal of it was a major cause of his condemnation.
