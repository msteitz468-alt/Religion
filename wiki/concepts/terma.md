# Terma (Treasure Revelation)

**Terma** (Tibetan *gter-ma*, "treasure") is the [[nyingma|Nyingma]] system of **concealed and
later-revealed scripture**. According to tradition, [[padmasambhava|Padmasambhava]] — through his
consort Yeshe Tsogyal and other disciples — concealed teachings and sacred objects at power-places
(*gnas*) across Tibet, to be recovered in future generations by prophesied **treasure-finders**
(*gter-ston*). It is the distinctive Nyingma mechanism of continuing revelation, and the means by
which the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]] entered the world (revealed by
[[karma-lingpa|Karma Lingpa]]).

## Modes of Treasure

- **Earth-treasures** (*sa-gter*) — physical texts/objects unearthed from rock, lakes, temples
  (the Book of the Dead, from Mount Gampodar).
- **Mind-treasures** (*dgongs-gter*) — revealed telepathically, directly from the enlightened
  intention of buddha-mind.
- **Pure visionary experiences** (*dag-snang*).

The Nyingma distinguish the **close lineage of treasures** (*nye-brgyud gter-ma*) from the
**long lineage of oral precepts** (*ring-brgyud bka'-ma*, an unbroken human transmission). Whole
anthologies of revealed treasure exist (e.g. the 19th-c. *Store of Precious Treasures*,
*Rin-chen gter-mdzod*).

## Significance and Dispute

Terma allows the canon to remain **open**: scripture attributed to Padmasambhava can appear
centuries after him. This is precisely why the New Translation (Sarma) schools historically
**disputed** the authenticity of terma revelations — a canon-formation question peculiar to
Tibetan Buddhism. (Comparable "hidden text" motifs are attributed in the tradition to
Nāgārjuna recovering the Prajñāpāramitā from the nāgas.)
