# Schism

**Schism** (Greek *schisma*, "a tearing") is the sin of rupturing the unity of the Church, as
distinct from **heresy**, the corruption of its faith. The classic scholastic analysis is
[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]'s ([[summa-theologica|*ST*]] II-II Q39), which treats schism among the
vices opposed to [[charity-aquinas|charity]] — for the unity schism breaks is the unity of
*ecclesiastical charity*, not of faith.

## Aquinas's account (ST II-II Q39)

- **A special sin, distinct from heresy (A1).** Following [[augustine|Augustine]]: "a schismatic
  holds the same faith and practises the same worship as others, and takes pleasure in the mere
  disunion of the community, whereas a heretic holds another faith." Schism is **essentially opposed
  to the unity of the Church** — both the mutual communion of the members and their subordination to
  one head, **Christ, whose vicar is the Sovereign Pontiff**; schismatics "refuse to submit to the
  Sovereign Pontiff." Yet schism is **"the road to heresy,"** since it tends to devise a doctrine to
  justify the separation (Jerome).
- **Its gravity (A2).** Schism is **less grievous by genus than unbelief/heresy** (which is against
  God himself, the First Truth, while schism is against a participated good, ecclesiastical unity);
  but among sins against the neighbour it is the greatest, being opposed to "the spiritual good of
  the multitude."
- **Sacramental power vs. jurisdiction (A3).** Schismatics **retain sacramental power** (conferred by
  consecration, indelible), so their sacraments remain valid, but **lose the power of jurisdiction**
  (conferred by appointment); their use of sacramental power is unlawful.
- **Punishment (A4).** Excommunication fits the crime — schism severs from communion, so the
  schismatic is severed by the Church.

## Historical applications

The category names some of Christianity's deepest ruptures, on which separate pages elaborate: the
**Donatist** controversy (Augustine's original target), the **East–West Schism** of 1054 between
[[catholicism|Catholicism]] and [[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]] (where each side's claim
about the Roman primacy is precisely what is in dispute), and the Western Schism of rival popes. The
distinction schism/heresy remains central to how each communion classifies the others.
