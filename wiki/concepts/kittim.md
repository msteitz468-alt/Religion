# Kittim
**Hebrew**: כִּתִּיִּים (*Kittiyim*)

A biblical term (originally: inhabitants of Kition/Cyprus; then expanded to denote western seafaring peoples generally) that became in Qumran literature a **coded name for the Romans**. The identification is Vermes's firm conclusion based on multiple specific internal references.

## Biblical Background
- **Genesis 10:4 / 1 Chronicles 1:7**: Kittim = descendants of Javan (Iavan = Ionia/Greeks), specifically associated with Cyprus
- **Numbers 24:24**: "Ships from Kittim shall afflict Ashur and Eber" — used in LXX as "Romans from Italy"
- **Daniel 11:30**: "Ships of Kittim shall come against him" (Antiochus IV) — already interpreted as Romans in the Maccabaean period
- **1 Maccabees 1:1**: Alexander the Great comes from "the land of the Kittim" — showing the term's flexibility for any western empire

## Qumran Usage

### Habakkuk Commentary (1QpHab)
The fullest identification. The Kittim are:
- "Quick and valiant in war, causing many to perish"
- They come "from the islands of the sea" — the Mediterranean
- They "trample the earth with their horses and beasts"
- Their rulers ("officers") succeed one another in destruction
- **They worship their military standards** (*nismat kelev*) — a specific and distinctive Roman practice, attested by classical sources
- They sacrifice to their standards "as to gods" — Roman legions worshipping their aquilae
- **Their cavalry use greaves** (*shinin*) — a detail Vermes connects specifically to cavalry greaves introduced by Julius Caesar's reforms; this places the Habakkuk Commentary in the Roman period

### War Scroll (1QM)
The final eschatological war pits the Sons of Light against the "Kittim of Asshur" and "the Kittim in Egypt" — both identified as the Roman forces occupying the Near East. The War Scroll's military details (trumpets, cavalry, legionary formations) also reflect Roman military practices of the 1st c. BCE.

### Nahum Commentary (4Q169)
A rare Qumran text that uses actual historical names. "Demetrius, king of Greece, who sought to enter Jerusalem on the advice of those who seek smooth things" (Demetrius III Eucaerus, c. 88 BCE). "The Kittim" appear in the context of the subsequent Roman ascendancy. The "furious young lion" (= Alexander Jannaeus) crucifies those who sought Demetrius's help — showing that the community distinguishes between Seleucid (Demetrius) and Roman (Kittim) overlords chronologically.

## Why "Romans" Not "Greeks"
Although "Kittim" originally denoted Greeks, several features of the Qumran Kittim specifically point to Romans, not Greeks:
1. Worship of military standards (Roman legionary eagles)
2. Cavalry greaves (Roman military equipment)
3. "Islands of the sea" (consistent with Italy / Mediterranean West)
4. Succession of rulers — matches Roman consular/imperial succession rather than Seleucid dynastic succession

## Significance for Qumran Chronology
The Kittim = Romans identification is important for dating the composition of the sectarian texts:
- The War Scroll's Roman-style military organization and the Habakkuk Commentary's "worship of standards" both date composition to the Roman period (after 63 BCE when Pompey entered Jerusalem)
- The community therefore existed and was producing texts in the 1st c. BCE at minimum
