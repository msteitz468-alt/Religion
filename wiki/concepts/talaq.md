# Talaq (Islamic Divorce)

*Talaq* (Arabic: طلاق) is the act of divorce in Islamic law, primarily initiated by the husband's pronouncement. Bukhari's Vol. 7, Book 63 is the principal hadith source for the Sunni law of divorce.

## Sources Ingested

- Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 7, Book 63 (Divorce), Nos. 178–262 — ingest 2026-06-27

## Types of Divorce

### Talaq (Husband-Initiated)

The husband's unilateral pronouncement of divorce. Three key principles govern its operation:

1. **Divorce during menses (*talaq al-bid'a*) is forbidden**: Ibn Umar divorced his wife while she was menstruating; Umar asked the Prophet, who said to take her back and divorce only when she is pure (not pregnant). This remains the Sunni standard (No. 178 area). The divorce counts as one (*raja'i*, revocable) and must not be pronounced during menstruation.

2. **Three divorces = irrevocable separation**: After three pronouncements of divorce (whether spread across three periods or stated at once), the marriage is permanently dissolved. The wife cannot return to the first husband unless she has genuinely married and consummated a marriage with a second husband (see *tahlil* below).

3. **Ibn Umar's approach**: Exemplary for the Sunni school — one divorce during purity, revocable, wife returned, then the pattern may be repeated at most twice more.

### Khul' (Wife-Initiated Divorce)

The wife's right to dissolve the marriage by returning the *mahr* (bride-gift). The classic case in Bukhari:

Thabit bin Qais's wife came to the Prophet saying: "I do not blame Thabit for defects in his character or his religion, but I, being a Muslim, dislike to behave in un-Islamic manner." The Prophet asked: "Will you give back the garden which your husband has given you as Mahr?" She said "Yes." The Prophet then ordered Thabit: "O Thabit! Accept your garden, and divorce her once." (No. 197 area, Ibn Abbas)

This establishes: (a) the wife may dissolve the marriage without fault, merely on grounds of incompatibility; (b) she must return the mahr; (c) the husband pronounces one irrevocable talaq; (d) the qadi/Prophet supervises the process.

### Ila' (Oath of Abstinence)

The husband swears an oath not to have sexual relations with his wife. The Quranic limit is four months (Q 2:226–227). After four months, he must either return to her or divorce her. Bukhari records that a man who refuses is to be imprisoned until he chooses one of the two options.

### Lian (Mutual Oath of Imprecation)

When a husband accuses his wife of adultery without four witnesses, both undergo mutual oath-taking (Q 24:6–9):
- The husband swears four times that he is telling the truth, plus an oath calling Allah's curse on himself if lying
- The wife swears four times that he is lying, plus an oath calling Allah's wrath on herself if he is telling the truth

After *lian*: permanent separation, child belongs to the mother, mahr is not returned. The case is irrevocable regardless of later confession. This procedure is documented in Bukhari (No. 224 area).

## The Tahlil Requirement

After three irrevocable divorces, a wife cannot return to her first husband until she has:
1. Genuinely married another man
2. The second husband has consummated the marriage
3. The second husband has voluntarily divorced her

This is the *tahlil* (legalizing) requirement, rooted in Q 2:230. The Prophet condemned men who marry women merely as a legal instrument to allow them to return to their first husbands: "Do you want to return to Rifa'a? No (you cannot) unless you enjoy the sexual relation with 'Abd-ur-Rahman." (No. 229 area, Aisha — in the case of Rifa'a's ex-wife who had married Abd-ur-Rahman but only experienced an unsatisfactory consummation).

## Waiting Period ('Idda)

After divorce (or death of husband), the woman must observe a waiting period before remarrying:
- **Divorced woman**: three menstrual cycles (Q 2:228)
- **Pregnant woman divorced or widowed**: her 'idda ends at delivery (the Subai'a case: No. 256 area — she gave birth shortly after her husband's death; the Prophet declared her 'idda complete at delivery, not at four months ten days)
- **Widow (not pregnant)**: four months and ten days (Q 2:234)

### Widow's Mourning Restrictions

During the 'idda a widow must not: wear perfume (except the minimum allowed for cleansing after menses), dress ornamentally, use kohl (except at night for necessity), or leave the house except for necessity.

## Aisha vs. Fatima bint Qais: Residence During 'Idda

A recorded intra-Companion disagreement: Fatima bint Qais reported that the Prophet did not provide her housing or maintenance during her 'idda after her husband divorced her. Aisha contested this: "It is not good for you to publicize it." This dispute has legal consequences for whether a divorced woman has the right to remain in the marital home during her 'idda — an open question in Sunni law that the two positions in Bukhari preserve without resolution. (No. 241 area)
