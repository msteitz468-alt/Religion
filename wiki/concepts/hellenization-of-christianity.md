# The Hellenization of Christianity (and Dehellenization)

A historiographical category for the thesis — associated above all with Adolf von Harnack — that the development of Christian dogma was essentially the progressive penetration of the original gospel by Greek philosophy. Drawn from [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Jaroslav Pelikan]], Vol. I, ch. 1.

## The Charge and Its Ancient Roots

The accusation is itself ancient: Hippolytus's *Little Labyrinth* charged the adoptionists Theodotus and Artemon with "deserting the Holy Scriptures of God" for Euclid and Aristotle; [[nestorius|Nestorius]] accused his opponents of being "led astray by the mentality of the Greeks." In modernity "the idea of dogma as the hellenization of Christianity became a widely circulated explanation of the development of early Christian doctrine."

## Pelikan's Qualification: "Dehellenization"

Pelikan judges "hellenization is too simplistic and unqualified a term for the process that issued in orthodox Christian doctrine," and proposes a striking inversion: dogma may be better described as **"the *dehellenization* of the theology that had preceded it,"** since "by its dogma the church threw up a wall against an alien metaphysic." On this reading the truly hellenized Christianity is found in **the speculations and heresies** ([[gnosticism|Gnosticism]], the [[logos-christology|Logos]] speculation, [[arianism|Arianism]]) "against which the dogma of the creeds and councils was directed."

## The "Pyrrhic Victory"

Yet Pelikan concedes the victory was double-edged: "The victory of orthodox Christian doctrine over classical thought was to some extent a **Pyrrhic victory**, for the theology that triumphed over Greek philosophy has continued to be shaped ever since by the language and the thought of classical metaphysics" — most visibly in the axioms of [[divine-impassibility|divine impassibility]], divine substance/[[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|ousia]], and the [[bodily-resurrection|immortality of the soul]]. [[boethius|Boethius]] embodies the paradox: an orthodox theologian who, awaiting execution, found solace "more in philosophical contemplation based on natural reason than in the Christian revelation to which his theological works pointed."
