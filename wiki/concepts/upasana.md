# Upāsanā (Meditation / Devotional Contemplation)

**Upāsanā** (from *upas* = "to sit near, attend upon, meditate") is the technical term in Vedānta for
the **meditative practice** by which the practitioner approaches Brahman. In [[ramanuja|Ramanuja's]]
[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]], it is a central soteriological category — the form that
liberating knowledge actually takes.

## The Core Equation: *Upāsanā* = *Dhyāna* = *Vedana*

Ramanuja's key terminological argument: the Sanskrit roots *vid* (to know), *upas* (to meditate /
sit near), and *dhyai* (to contemplate) are used **interchangeably** across the same Upanishadic texts —
they denote the **same practice**. When the Upanishads say a person "knows (*veda*) Brahman" in the
context of liberating knowledge, they mean the person **meditatively attends upon (*upāste*) Brahman**
in the sense of *dhyāna*.

This equivalence has major theological consequences:
- Liberating knowledge is NOT a one-time cognitive event (a flash of insight)
- It is a **repeated, sustained practice** (*abhyāsa* = repetition) that must be **continued until death**
- "Acting thus as long as life lasts he reaches the world of Brahman" (*Chāndogya* VIII,15)

## Structure of Upāsanā

### The Object

The object of meditation must be the **highest Brahman** — not:
- An effected (*kārya*) Brahman such as Hiraṇyagarbha (the cosmic soul / Brahmā)
- The individual soul itself (though the soul is a mode of the object)
- A symbol such as prāṇa, the sun, or fire *by itself*

Meditations on the highest Brahman across different Upanishads (*vidyā*s — named meditations such as
the Sandilya-vidyā, the Dahara-vidyā, the Vaiśvānara-vidyā) are **separate meditations** because they
differ in terms, form, name, subject-matter, and result. However, the **essential attributes** of
Brahman (bliss, knowledge, freedom from evil, infinity) must be **included in all of them** — one cannot
meditate on Brahman while mentally attributing only a subset of Brahman's essential nature.

### The Subject

The meditating subject must conceive of itself in its **released nature** — freedom from sin, freedom
from the body's limitations. Because the individual soul is itself a mode of the object meditated upon
(Brahman as body includes the soul), the soul must be apprehended in its pure state, not in its current
bondage-qualified state.

### The Posture and Setting

Sitting posture is prescribed (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka* I,5,23) — not lying down or wandering; the meditation
is a focused, deliberate practice.

## Symbol Meditations (*Pratīka Upāsanā*)

A distinct category: meditating on a *symbol* (*pratīka*) such as *prāṇa*, the sun, *ākāśa*, or
fire **as Brahman** (i.e., Brahman's aspect superimposed on the symbol). In these:
- Brahman's superior aspect is superimposed *on* the inferior symbol (not vice versa)
- The meditation is of a lower order
- Symbol-meditators are NOT conducted to the highest Brahman on the **Path of the Gods** (*Devayāna*)
  at death — their meditation is contaminated by the non-intelligent element (*acit*) of the symbol
- They attain correspondingly more limited results

## The Three Auxiliaries

*Bṛhadāraṇyaka* III,5 names three auxiliaries that accompany upāsanā:
1. **Paṇḍitya** (scholarship) — full mastery through hearing (*śravaṇa*) and pondering (*manana*)
2. **Bālya** (child-like quality) — specifically **not manifesting pride or arrogance**; the absence of
   the sage's self-display, not naïveté
3. **Mauna** (silence) — in Ramanuja's reading, this IS the upāsanā practice itself: the root *man*
   (*mauna* from *man* = to think/reflect) connects it to *manana*; "silence" = reiterated vivid
   representation = the meditation

All three must be maintained until death.

## Upāsanā and Works

Upāsanā does not replace Vedic works for householders — prescribed rituals (Agnihotra etc.) remain
obligatory as **prerequisites** to knowledge. Their function for the knower is to please the Lord,
who then grants the grace enabling sustained upāsanā. The Agnihotra etc. do not directly produce
liberation but are the instrumental conditions enabling the path.

## Contrast with Advaita

In [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]], the liberating knowledge (*brahma-jñāna*) is in principle a one-time
recognition — the realization that Ātman is already Brahman, with no further practice required once the
recognition dawns. [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]] rejects this: the Upanishadic texts consistently use language
of **repeated practice** (*vedana*, *upāsanā*, *dhyāna*), and the soul's bondage is real enough to
require sustained effort to overcome, not a cognitive flip.
