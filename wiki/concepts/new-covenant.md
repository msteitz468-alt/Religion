The New Covenant (*bĕrît ḥădāšâ*) is announced in Jeremiah 31:31-34 — the climax of the "Book of Comfort" (chs. 30-33) and the single most important OT covenant text for NT soteriology. It is the longest OT quotation in the entire NT (Hebrews 8:8-12; 10:16-17) and provides the theological vocabulary for the Lord's Supper, Paul's contrast of letter and Spirit, and the Epistle to the Hebrews' entire argument for the supersession of the Levitical priesthood.

## The Text: Jeremiah 31:31-34

> "Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah, not like the covenant that I made with their fathers on the day when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt, my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband, declares the LORD. For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the LORD: I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts. And I will be their God, and they shall be my people. And no longer shall each one teach his neighbor and each his brother, saying, 'Know the LORD,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest, declares the LORD. For I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more."

## Four Characteristics

### 1. Internalized Torah

"I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts."

The Sinai covenant was written on stone tablets (Exod 31:18; 32:15-16). The new covenant will be written on the *heart* (*lēb*) — the seat of will, understanding, and commitment in OT anthropology. This is not the abolition of Torah but its *internalization*: the same divine instruction, now written on the human person rather than on external stone.

This connects to Jeremiah's earlier diagnosis (17:1): "The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron; with a point of diamond it is engraved on the tablet of their heart." The problem is that sin was written on the heart before Torah was. The new covenant promises to inscribe Torah more deeply than sin.

### 2. Universal Knowledge of God

"And no longer shall each one teach his neighbor... saying, 'Know the LORD,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest."

The need for mediated religious instruction will end. Every member of the covenant community — not just priests, prophets, or scribes — will have direct personal knowledge of YHWH. This is the most radical feature of the promise: it transcends the Levitical teaching office, the prophetic office, and any mediating institution. The problem of Israel's covenant history (they were told about YHWH but kept forgetting him) will be resolved at its root.

### 3. The Covenant Formula Fulfilled

"I will be their God, and they shall be my people."

This is the standard covenant formula (*Bundesformel*) that runs throughout the OT (Exod 6:7; Lev 26:12; Jer 7:23; 11:4; 24:7; 30:22; Ezek 36:28; Zech 8:8; Rev 21:3). It represents Yahweh's fundamental relational intention — the purpose of all covenant-making. In Jeremiah's context, the formula appears repeatedly as God's *original intention* and *future goal*, never as present reality (see 7:23, 11:4 for the future form). The new covenant is the eschatological fulfillment of what every prior covenant promised.

### 4. Forgiveness That Forgets

"For I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more."

The basis for the entire covenant: not Israel's improved performance but YHWH's unilateral act of forgiveness and *forgetting*. Divine forgetting (*lōʾ ʾezkār*) is an active, willed non-remembering — not ignorance but the deliberate removal of the past from the relationship. The covenant of Sinai was repeatedly broken and required repeated atonement (Yom Kippur, daily offerings); the new covenant promises a final, comprehensive deal with sin that removes the need for ongoing sacrificial remediation.

## Historical Context in Jeremiah

The new covenant promise appears in Jeremiah 31 within the "Book of Comfort" (chs. 30-33) — written while Jeremiah was imprisoned during the final siege of Jerusalem (32:2), with the city's fall imminent. Lalleman's structural reading: the new covenant promise is only possible because the 604 BCE "point of no return" has already passed. Now that judgment is certain, only divine initiative (not human repentance) can secure a future. The new covenant is the most extreme expression of the *šûb* shift from human imperative to divine promise: God himself will write Torah on hearts, ensure that his people know him, and forgive them.

The adjacent passage (31:35-37) underlines the covenant's permanence by linking it to the fixed order of creation: the new covenant will be as reliable as day and night.

## Ezekiel 36:25-27 — The Parallel Text

Ezekiel's restoration oracle uses different imagery to describe the same reality as Jeremiah 31:31-34:

> "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses, and from all your idols I will cleanse you. And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes." (Ezek 36:25-27)

Comparison:
| Feature | Jeremiah 31:31-34 | Ezekiel 36:25-27 |
|---|---|---|
| Torah internalized | Written on hearts | Spirit enabling obedience |
| Divine initiative | I will write | I will put my Spirit within |
| Imagery | Writing / inscription | Water / heart transplant / Spirit infusion |
| Covenant formula | "I will be their God; they shall be my people" (v. 33) | Implied in 36:28 ("you shall be my people and I will be your God") |
| Forgiveness | "I will forgive... remember no more" (v. 34) | Cleansing from uncleanness (v. 25) |

Both texts point to the same eschatological reality: YHWH will solve the problem of covenant infidelity from the inside, not by external enforcement but by internal transformation through his Spirit and his inscribed word. Paul draws on both in 2 Cor 3:3-6.

## Related OT Promises

- **Ezekiel 36:25-27**: "I will sprinkle clean water on you... I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh." Same concept; different imagery. Where Jeremiah uses the law-writing metaphor, Ezekiel uses the heart-transplant metaphor.
- **Jeremiah 32:40**: "I will make an everlasting covenant with them: I will never stop doing good to them, and I will inspire them to fear me, so that they will never turn away from me."
- **Isaiah 55:3**: "I will make an everlasting covenant with you, my faithful love promised to David" — connecting the new covenant to the Davidic covenant
- **Deuteronomy 30:6**: "The LORD your God will circumcise your hearts and the hearts of your descendants, so that you may love him with all your heart" — a Mosaic anticipation of the internalization theme

## Jewish Interpretation

In rabbinic Judaism, the new covenant has not historically been read as inaugurated with Jesus. Two main approaches:

1. **Historical fulfillment**: The promise was fulfilled with the return from Babylonian exile — the Mosaic covenant was renewed, and the Torah-centered life of the Second Temple period represents the fulfillment
2. **Messianic future**: The full promise awaits the Messianic age; Torah will be so deeply internalized in the age to come that the need for instruction will indeed end

The Dead Sea Scrolls community (Qumran) applied the "new covenant" language to their own community, describing themselves as "the covenanters of the new covenant" (*bĕrît ḥădāšâ*) — evidence that the phrase was already recognized as a distinct covenant category before the NT.

## NT Appropriation

### Hebrews 8:8-12 and 10:16-17

The longest OT quotation in the NT, used twice in Hebrews to argue that the Levitical priesthood and its sacrificial system have been superseded by Jesus's high priesthood. Hebrews 8 quotes the full text of Jer 31:31-34 and comments: "In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away" (8:13). Hebrews 10 quotes the final clause of the covenant ("I will remember their sins no more") as the basis for claiming that where there is forgiveness, there is no longer any offering for sin (10:18) — the argument that Christ's single sacrifice is sufficient and final.

### Luke 22:20 / 1 Corinthians 11:25

"This cup is the new covenant in my blood" — Jesus at the Last Supper explicitly invokes Jeremiah's new covenant. The Lord's Supper is constituted as the covenant meal of the new covenant.

### 2 Corinthians 3:3-6

Paul contrasts "a letter on tablets of stone" (Sinai covenant) with "a letter... written on tablets of human hearts" (new covenant), and "the old covenant" (which kills) with "the new covenant" (which gives life through the Spirit). The Spirit is Paul's way of articulating how the new covenant's internalization promise is fulfilled.

### Romans 11:27

Quoting Isaiah 59:21 and 27:9 rather than Jeremiah 31, but with the same new covenant logic: "And this will be my covenant with them when I take away their sins."

## The Phrase "New Covenant" as the NT's Name

The Greek *kainḕ diathḗkē* (new covenant/testament) is the term from which "New Testament" derives. Every NT document is understood to be the witness to the covenant inauguration that Jeremiah 31 promised. The very name of the Christian second canon is a claim that the new covenant has been inaugurated through Jesus Christ.
