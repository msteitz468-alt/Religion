# Homoousion (*Consubstantial*)

*Homoousios* (ὁμοούσιος, "of one substance/being," Latin *consubstantialis*) is the term inserted into the [[arian-controversy|Nicene Creed]] (325) to affirm that the Son shares the very being of the Father, against [[arianism|Arius]]'s teaching that the Son is a creature. It became the badge of [[trinity|Trinitarian]] orthodoxy. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. IX–X.

## Original Sense at Nicaea

The Creed affirms the Son is "out of the substance of the Father" (*ek tēs ousias tou patros*) and "of one substance with the Father." Kelly argues that, prior to Nicaea, *homoousios* primarily bore a **generic** sense ("of the same kind of substance"); it had been borrowed from the Gnostics and used by Origen, Methodius, and Eusebius in this looser way. The Nicene Fathers intended it chiefly to affirm "that the Son was fully God, in the sense of sharing the same divine nature as His Father" — not yet, explicitly, numerical identity of substance. The term's awkward history (it had been **rejected** by the synod that condemned [[paul-of-samosata|Paul of Samosata]] in 268) made it suspect to many. [[athanasius|Athanasius]] later pressed it toward numerical identity: "the divinity of the Father is identical with that of the Son."

## The Spectrum of Slogans

The decades after Nicaea generated a spectrum:
- ***homoousios*** — "of one/the same substance" (Nicene)
- ***homoiousios*** — "of like substance" (the Homoeousian compromise of Basil of Ancyra)
- ***homoios*** — "like" the Father (the deliberately vague Homoean formula)
- ***anomoios*** — "unlike" the Father (the Anomoeans, [[eunomius|Eunomius]] and Aetius)

The reconciliation of *homoousios* and *homoiousios* parties — eased by Athanasius (*De synodis*) and [[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary]], and the [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|one-*ousia*/three-*hypostases*]] formula — prepared the triumph of Nicene orthodoxy at Constantinople (381). The same term was later extended to the **[[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]]** (Athanasius, the [[cappadocian-fathers|Cappadocians]]).

## Ayres: Homoousios Was Not a Rallying Cry in 325

Lewis Ayres ([[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its Legacy*]], 2004) stresses that *homoousios*
"is rarely if ever mentioned" between 326 and 350 and was **not** a decisive settlement in 325:
it was chosen at Nicaea mainly because [[arius|Arius]] was known to reject it (formulae like
"like the Father" and "from God" failed precisely because his supporters could accept them).
Only later does it become a touchstone — [[athanasius|Athanasius]] reading it (in *De decretis*)
as a cipher for the Son being "from God," and [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil]] confessing it just
once in *Contra Eunomium* (1.20, from Heb. 1:3) and "not as a point of departure." The term's
authority is thus an achievement of [[pro-nicene-theology|pro-Nicene theology]], not a fixed
point inherited from the council. See [[arian-controversy]].
