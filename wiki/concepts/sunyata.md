# Sunyata (Emptiness)

**Sunyata** (Sanskrit; "emptiness, voidness") is the central [[mahayana|Mahayana]] doctrine: all
phenomena (*dharmas*) are **empty of inherent, independent existence** (*svabhava*). It extends
[[anatta|non-self]] from persons to *everything*.

## The Teaching

Systematized by **Nagarjuna** (2nd c.) in the Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") school: because everything
arises dependently (dependent origination), nothing possesses a fixed, self-contained essence.
Emptiness is **not nihilism** — it is reconciled with conventional reality through the **two truths**:
conventionally, things function and matter; ultimately, they lack inherent existence. The
[[heart-sutra|Heart Sutra]]'s "form is emptiness, emptiness is form" is its most compressed statement.
Crucially, emptiness is *itself* empty (no reified Absolute), which distinguishes it from a substance
metaphysics.

## Cross-Tradition Significance

A rich and contested comparandum:

- vs. Hindu **Brahman** (the [[upanishads|Upanishads]]) and [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] — sunyata is precisely
  *not* a fullness or ground of being; the Mahayana "void" and the Kabbalist/Vedantic "plenum" are
  often paired in comparative mysticism but are structurally opposite.
- vs. Christian/Sufi **apophatic theology** ([[cappadocian-fathers|the Cappadocians]], Pseudo-Dionysius,
  Ibn Arabi) — both negate predicates of the ultimate, but apophasis points to a transcendent God while
  sunyata denies inherent existence altogether. A strong comparison-page candidate.
