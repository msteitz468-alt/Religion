# God-fearers (*Sebomenoi* / *Phoboumenoi ton Theon*)

**God-fearers** were Gentile sympathizers with Judaism who adopted Jewish monotheism and piety —
attending the synagogue, praying, giving alms — **without becoming full proselytes** (that is,
without circumcision and the complete legal conversion). In the Greek of [[acts|Acts]] they are
described with participial phrases such as *phoboumenos ton theon* ("fearing God," 10:2) and
*sebomenē ton theon* ("worshiping God," 16:14). As read by [[darrell-bock|Darrell Bock]] (BECNT
*Acts*, 2007), the category is the crucial **bridge to the Gentile mission**: God-fearers stand at
the threshold between the synagogue and the full incorporation of uncircumcised Gentiles.

## Definition (Bock)

Bock defines a God-fearer as a Gentile who is responsive to Israel's God and who has adopted Jewish
piety "without embracing detailed legal practice" — crucially, one who "would not have been
circumcised." He distinguishes the class from full **proselytes**, who took "the final step" of
circumcision, baptism, and sacrificial offering (Bock defines a proselyte in the Acts 2:11 context
as a Gentile who is circumcised, baptized, and offers sacrifice). Bock cites the **Aphrodisias
inscription**, which distinguishes Jews from "God-worshipers," and Schneider's description of a
Gentile sympathizer who adopted Jewish piety, as background.

**Bock's caution: not necessarily a strict technical term.** Bock notes that "God-fearer" is
"probably not a technical term" for Luke — i.e. Luke may not deploy *sebomenos / phoboumenos*
consistently as a fixed status-designation, so the category should not be pressed into a rigid
institutional class. At 13:43 Bock likewise reads "God-fearing proselytes" (*tōn sebomenōn
prosēlytōn*) as a combination of God-fearers and proselytes, marking the two as distinct steps:
"God-fearers worship Israel's God but do not become circumcised, receive baptism, or offer a
sacrifice, whereas proselytes take the final step."

## The Exemplars

Bock treats God-fearers as a recognizable **character class** in Acts, exemplified by:

- **[[cornelius|Cornelius]]** — the centurion of Caesarea (10:1–48), "devout" (*eusebēs*), one who
  "fears God," gives alms, and prays continually; the pivotal first Gentile household to receive
  the gospel and the Spirit while uncircumcised. Bock: a Gentile responsive to Israel's God
  "without embracing detailed legal practice."
- **The [[ethiopian-eunuch|Ethiopian eunuch]]** — the God-fearing court official of Acts 8, read
  by Bock among the God-fearers in his survey of Acts' human character types.
- **[[lydia|Lydia]]** — the purple-dealer of Thyatira at Philippi, described as *sebomenē ton
  theon* ("a worshiper of God," 16:14), a former polytheist now attending the synagogue without
  keeping the whole law; the first Philippian convert.
- **[[theophilus|Theophilus]]** — Bock speculates that Luke's addressee "may well have been a
  'God-fearer,'" a high-status Gentile struggling with his choice to join the Jesus movement;
  Luke's answer to him is that "God designed all of this," including Gentile inclusion.

Bock also lists Titius Justus (18:7) as "the last mention of a God-fearer in Acts."

## Significance: the Bridge to the Gentile Mission

For Bock the God-fearers are the natural entry point of the gospel into the Gentile world. Because
they already worship Israel's God and frequent the synagogue, they are prepared hearers when Paul
turns "to the Gentiles" in city after city; yet because they are **uncircumcised**, their inclusion
raises in acute form the question settled at the [[jerusalem-council|Jerusalem consultation]]
(Acts 15): on what basis are uncircumcised Gentiles admitted to the community? The God-fearer is
thus the concrete figure over whom the [[circumcision|circumcision]] dispute is fought — the person
who is neither a full Jew nor a pagan idolater, and whose acceptance "without their being
circumcised" (cf. 11:3) marks the widening of the people of God (see [[gentiles|Gentiles]]).
