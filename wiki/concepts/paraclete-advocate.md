# The Paraclete/Advocate (Spirit of Truth)

The *paraklētos* (Paraclete) is the distinctive Johannine title for the Holy Spirit as promised by Jesus in the farewell discourses (John 14–16). The term is unique to the Johannine corpus in the NT and is one of the most significant pneumatological contributions of the fourth Gospel.

## Occurrences

*Paraklētos* appears five times in the NT, all in the Johannine corpus:

- **John 14:16** — "I will ask the Father, and he will give you *another* Advocate (*paraklēton*), that he might be with you forever"
- **John 14:26** — "the Advocate (*ho paraklētos*), the Holy Spirit, which the Father will send in my name, will teach you all things and remind you of all things that I said to you"
- **John 15:26** — "When the Advocate comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth who goes forth from the Father, he will testify about me"
- **John 16:7** — "it is to your advantage that I am going away, for unless I go away the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you"
- **1 John 2:1** — "if anyone sins, we have an Advocate (*paraklēton*) with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" — here the title is applied to Jesus himself as heavenly intercessor, not to the Spirit

## Translation

The Greek *paraklētos* is difficult to translate; its range includes:
- "Advocate" (legal context; [[j-ramsey-michaels|Michaels]]'s preferred translation; ESV, NRSV)
- "Comforter" (KJV; older tradition; emphasizes pastoral consolation)
- "Helper" (NIV, NASB)
- "Counselor" (RSV)

Michaels favors "Advocate" because the legal/forensic dimension of *martyria* (testimony/witness) pervades the farewell discourses and the Paraclete's role includes testifying about Jesus before a hostile world (15:26–27; 16:8–11). The transliterated "Paraclete" is also commonly used to avoid committing to one nuance.

Note: the Johannine Epistles use the same term (*paraklētos*) for Jesus as heavenly intercessor before the Father (1 John 2:1), suggesting a broader range of meaning in the community's vocabulary. See [[letters-of-john|Letters of John]].

## Functions

The Paraclete/Advocate performs four distinct roles in the farewell discourses:

**1. Reminder and teacher** (14:26): "He will teach you all things and *remind* you of all things that I said to you." The Spirit does not bring new revelation beyond Jesus; his role is to illuminate and make present what Jesus has already said. This is the function the Gospel writer identifies retrospectively in the disciples' post-resurrection understanding of events (2:17, 22; 12:16) — the Advocate's work of enabling the disciples to understand, perhaps for the first time, what they had witnessed.

**2. Witness** (15:26–27): "He will testify about me — and you too must testify because you are with me from the beginning." The Paraclete testifies *alongside* the human witnesses. This is the pneumatological grounding of the Gospel's testimony chain: the written Gospel is guaranteed by the Spirit, not merely by the Beloved Disciple's individual eyewitness.

**3. Convictor of the world** (16:8–11): "He will convict the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment." The Spirit continues the "trial" of the world that was the public ministry (chs. 2–12). He presses the charges: sin = unbelief in Jesus; righteousness = Jesus' vindication in his return to the Father; judgment = the ruler of this world has been condemned.

**4. Guide into all truth** (16:13): "When that one comes, the Spirit of truth, he will lead you in all the truth." Michaels: this is a promise of comprehensive illumination of what Jesus has revealed, not a promise of ongoing new revelation beyond Jesus. The Advocate's revelation is bounded by and serves Jesus' revelation.

## The Paraclete as Gospel's Guarantor

Michaels's key interpretive move: it is the *Advocate* — not "the disciple whom Jesus loved" — who is the ultimate guarantor of the Gospel's truth. The Beloved Disciple is the eyewitness and author; the Advocate is the one who ensures the disciples (and through them the written testimony) have access to the truth of what Jesus said and did. This is why the Gospel can be "true" even when the Beloved Disciple's identity is deliberately withheld — truth does not depend on name-dropping but on the Spirit's guarantee.

See [[testimony-martyria|Testimony/Witness (Martyria)]] for the full testimony chain.

## Against Montanism

In the second century, Montanists in Asia Minor appealed to the Paraclete promises (especially 16:13, "he will lead you into all truth") to claim ongoing new revelation through charismatic prophets, superseding or supplementing the apostolic testimony. Michaels (and the broader evangelical tradition) argues the text rules this out: the Advocate "reminds you of all things that I said to you" (14:26) — his role is to *remind* and *illumine*, not to originate. "The Advocate illumines and interprets only what Jesus has already revealed" (16:14: "he will not speak on his own, but will speak whatever he hears").

## The Filioque

The Spirit "goes forth from the Father" (15:26: *ho para tou patros ekporeuetai*). The Western church added *filioque* ("and the Son") to the Nicene Creed, claiming the Spirit proceeds from both Father and Son — partly grounded in the double sending language of John (the Father sends the Advocate in Jesus' name, 14:26; Jesus sends the Advocate from the Father, 15:26; Jesus sends him after his departure, 16:7). The Eastern church contested this addition; the *filioque* controversy was a major factor in the 1054 schism.
