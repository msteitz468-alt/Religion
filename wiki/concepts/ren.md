# Ren

**Ren** (仁) — "humaneness," "benevolence," "co-humanity" — is the supreme virtue of
[[confucianism|Confucianism]] and the central concern of the [[analects|Analects]]. The character
combines "person" and "two," suggesting that ren is realized **between persons**: the fully human way of
relating to others.

## Meaning

[[confucius|Confucius]] declines to define ren abstractly, instead specifying it situationally: it is
"to love others" (12.22); "to overcome the self and return to **[[li-confucian|ritual propriety]]**"
(12.1); and it is expressed in the negative Golden Rule — "do not impose on others what you yourself do
not desire." Ren is cultivated, not innate-and-automatic; it grows through learning, ritual practice, and
filial relationships outward to all. The **junzi** (exemplary person) embodies it; for Confucius it is so
demanding that he rarely grants anyone has fully achieved it.

## In Mencius and After

**[[mencius|Mencius]]** roots ren in the innate "sprout" of **compassion** (the spontaneous alarm at
seeing a child about to fall into a well), grounding the virtue in human nature itself. In
[[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]] ren is expanded cosmically — "forming one body with all things."

## Cross-Tradition Note

Ren as **other-directed humaneness cultivated through practice** invites comparison with the Christian
command of neighbor-love ([[hesed|ḥesed]], *agape*) and the Buddhist [[bodhisattva|compassion]]
(*karuna*) — but Confucian ren is graded (strongest in the family, extending outward) rather than
universal-by-fiat, and is achieved by **cultivation** rather than commanded or given by grace. A
candidate comparison page.
