# Shabad (the Divine Word)

**Shabad** (Punjabi/Sanskrit: *śabda*, "word, sound") in [[sikhism|Sikhism]] refers to the **Divine Word** as both cosmic creative power and the scriptural hymns (*bāṇī*) through which that Word is mediated. In the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]], the Shabad is understood as the Guru's utterance emanating from [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] and transmitted through the Gurus. Meditating on the Shabad (through hearing, reciting, and contemplating *bāṇī*) is coextensive with [[nam-simaran|Naam Simaran]]. The Granth is sometimes called the **Shabad Guru** — the Guru as divine utterance.

## Shabad in Japji Sahib (Primary Text)

Pauri 5 of [[japji|Japji Sahib]] equates the Shabad with cosmic wisdom (Ang 2):

> The Guru's Word is the Sound-current of the Naad; the Guru's Word is the Wisdom of the Vedas; the Guru's Word is all-pervading. The Guru is Shiva, the Guru is Vishnu and Brahma; the Guru is Paarvati and Lakhshmi. (L259–266)

This is a rhetorical subsumption of Hindu deities and scriptures under the Guru/Shabad principle — not an affirmation of their independent divinity, but an assertion that the Shabad contains all that the Vedas and the deities represent.

Pauri 38 — the culminating metallurgical metaphor — speaks of minting "the True Coin of the Shabad, the Word of God" in the crucible of love (L1735–1755): the entire spiritual journey is the forging of the Shabad into the soul.

Kirtan Sohila (Hymn 3, the Aartee): "The Unstruck Sound-current of the Shabad is the vibration of the temple drums" (L2769–2777, Ang 13) — the cosmic Aartee already occurring in creation, without any external ritual required.

## Shabad and Naam

Shabad and [[naam|Naam]] are closely related and sometimes interchangeable in the Granth. Pauri 19 derives the Naam *from* the Word/Shabad: "From the Word, comes the Naam." The Shabad is typically the *form* of the Guru's utterance (scripture, hymn, divine speech); the Naam is the divine reality that the Shabad conveys and embodies. The Shabad Guru concept — the Granth as the Guru's utterance — rests on this equation.
