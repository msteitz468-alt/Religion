# Consensus Patrum (Appeal to the Fathers)

The patristic and especially fifth-century practice of treating the agreed teaching of the recognised orthodox Fathers as a theological authority, second to and derived from Scripture. As a debating technique it took the form of compiling *florilegia* — dossiers of select quotations from authoritative teachers — to settle disputed questions. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. II.

## The Fifth-Century Expansion

"In the fifth century the practice was greatly extended, explicit and even formal recognition being given to the authority of the succession of venerated teachers. As an offshoot of this the compilation of lists of fathers of unimpeachable prestige, with select quotations from their writings, became a favourite technique in theological debate."

- **[[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]]** counselled the Egyptian monks "to follow in the steps of the holy fathers, since it was they who had preserved the faith handed down from the apostles," and produced "elaborate dossiers of patristic quotations" at the Council of Ephesus (431).
- **[[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret]]** likewise compiled patristic authorities in his *Eranistes*, listing the faith's transmitters as "Ignatius, Eustathius, Athanasius, Basil, Gregory, John and the other luminaries of the world."

## Subordinate to Scripture

Kelly stresses that the Fathers' authority was never conceived as independent of Scripture: "there was no question of their being regarded as having access to truths other than those already contained, explicitly or implicitly, in Scripture." Their authority "consisted precisely in the fact that they had so faithfully and fully expounded the real intention of the Bible writers." Cyril's ultimate appeal was always to "the tradition of the apostles and evangelists and the bearing of divinely inspired Scripture as a whole"; Theodoret's was blunter still — "I yield obedience to Holy Scripture alone." The principle was given its classic formal shape by [[vincent-of-lerins|Vincent of Lérins]].
