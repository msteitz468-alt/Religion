# Baptism

**Baptism** is the first and "door" of the [[sacraments|sacraments]] — the sacramental washing of
regeneration by which a person is incorporated into Christ and cleansed of sin.
[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats it at [[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Tertia Pars Q66–Q71; with
**Confirmation** (Q72) it forms the first stage of Christian initiation.

## Matter, form, and effect (Q66, Q69)

- **Matter**: true water; **form**: "I baptize thee in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and
  of the Holy Ghost." Immersion is the more expressive rite but pouring suffices.
- **Effect**: the remission of **all** sin (original and actual) **and all punishment**, full
  incorporation into Christ, and the imprinting of the indelible **baptismal character**. Baptism
  removes the guilt of original sin but leaves the *fomes* (the inclination to sin) as a trial.

## The three baptisms (Q66 A11–A12; Q68 A2)

Aquinas distinguishes three forms, the latter two "taking the place of" sacramental Baptism when it
cannot be had:

1. **Baptism of water** — the sacrament proper.
2. **Baptism of blood** — martyrdom for Christ.
3. **Baptism of the Spirit / of desire** (*flaminis*) — interior conversion with the *desire* for
   Baptism. A catechumen who dies before baptism, "wanting in reality but not in desire," can be
   saved, "as by fire" (citing Ambrose on Valentinian, and Augustine). Baptism is "necessary for
   salvation in so far as man cannot be saved without, at least, **Baptism of desire**, which 'with
   God counts for the deed.'"

## Ministers and recipients (Q67–Q68)

- **Minister**: ordinarily a priest, but in necessity **anyone** — even a layperson or an
  unbeliever — baptizes validly if they use the Trinitarian form and intend to do what the Church
  does, "for the Baptism is not theirs but [Christ's]."
- **Infant baptism** (Q68 A9): established and necessary; the faith of the **Church** supplies for
  the infant who cannot believe; the unbelief of parents is no bar.
- **Children of Jews/unbelievers against the parents' will** (Q68 A10): Aquinas's much-cited
  position — to baptize the children of unbelievers *before they have the use of reason* and
  *against their parents' will* is **"contrary to natural justice"**, and "it is not the custom of
  the Church" to do so (citing the Council of Toledo). The parent's natural authority over the child
  who cannot yet choose must be respected. This connects to his broader stance on
  [[coercion-of-unbelievers|the coercion of unbelievers]].

## Circumcision as figure (Q70)

Aquinas treats Old-Testament [[circumcision]] as the antecedent of Baptism. He **notes his own
change of opinion** from the *Sentences* commentary, concluding that circumcision conferred grace
"as to all its effects" — but **instrumentally through faith in the future Passion**, not through a
Passion already accomplished, and it imprinted no character and gave grace "less copiously" than
Baptism.

## Confirmation (Q72)

- **Matter**: chrism (olive oil + balm); **form**: "I sign thee with the sign of the cross, and I
  confirm thee with the chrism of salvation, in the name of the Father…"; **minister**: the bishop.
- **Effect**: an increase of grace conferring **spiritual maturity** and strength "for the public
  combat with the enemies of the Faith"; it imprints a **character** and so presupposes the baptismal
  character and is unrepeatable. Given to all the baptized, including women, children, and the dying.

## Justin Martyr's Description (2nd Century)

The earliest detailed patristic description of baptismal practice appears in [[first-apology-justin|Justin Martyr's First Apology]] (Ch. LXI, c. 155 CE), predating all systematic sacramental theology. Justin describes:

- **Preparation**: candidates pray and fast for "remission of sins that are past"; the community prays and fasts with them.
- **Rite**: candidates are brought to water and washed "in the name of God, the Father and Lord of the universe, and of our Saviour Jesus Christ, and of the Holy Spirit" — a triadic formula.
- **Name of God**: the presider says "in the name of" without pronouncing a personal name for the Father, since "no one can utter the name of the ineffable God."
- **Title**: the rite is called **illumination** (*phōtismos*) — "they who learn these things are illuminated in their understandings."
- **Theological rationale**: "Since at our birth we were born without our own knowledge or choice, by our parents coming together... in order that we may not remain the children of necessity and of ignorance, but may become the children of choice and knowledge, and may obtain in the water the remission of sins formerly committed." Baptism re-orients the person from inherited necessity to personal, informed commitment.
- **Prerequisite for Eucharist**: Only the baptized who believe and live according to Christ's teaching may receive the [[eucharist|Eucharist]] (*First Apol.* Ch. LXVI).

Justin's "illumination" language became standard in Eastern Christianity for baptism (*phōtismos* is still the common term in Greek-speaking Orthodoxy).
