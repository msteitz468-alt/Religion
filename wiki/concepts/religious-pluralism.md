# Religious Pluralism

**Religious pluralism** is the position that the major religious traditions are not rivals
in which at most one can be true, but **multiple valid responses to a single ultimate
reality** — each shaped by its own history, language, and culture, and each (so far as can
be judged) roughly equally effective as a path of salvation/liberation. As a technical
position in the **theology of religions** it is the most demanding of the three options in
the standard typology, and is most fully associated with [[john-hick|John Hick]].

## The Threefold Typology (Alan Race)

Alan Race's classification, now standard, sorts responses to the diversity of religions:

- **Exclusivism** — salvation/truth is found in *one* tradition only; others are false or
  at best preparatory. (Karl Barth; conservative confessional theologies of every
  tradition; *extra ecclesiam nulla salus*.)
- **Inclusivism** — one tradition is the fullest truth, but adherents of others may be
  saved *through* it without knowing it. (Karl Rahner's "anonymous Christians"; many
  modern Catholic and Vedantic formulations.)
- **Pluralism** — no single tradition is uniquely normative; all the great traditions are
  authentic, comparably valid responses to the one ultimate reality. (Hick; W. C. Smith;
  Paul Knitter.)

See [[religious-pluralism-debate|the pluralism debate]] for the contest between these
positions.

## Hick's Pluralistic Hypothesis

[[john-hick|Hick]]'s *An Interpretation of Religion* (1989) gives the position its most
systematic philosophical form:

1. **One ineffable ground.** Behind all the traditions stands **[[the-real|the Real]]** —
   the ultimate reality, knowable only as humanly experienced, never in itself.
2. **Neo-Kantian lenses.** Each tradition is a culturally conditioned *interpretive lens*;
   the Real is "schematised" through each community's categories into its personae (Yahweh,
   the Trinity, Allah, Shiva) and impersonae ([[brahman|Brahman]], the [[dao|Tao]], Nirvana,
   [[sunyata|Sunyata]]).
3. **The soteriological criterion.** Traditions are judged by their fruits — the
   transformation **from self-centredness to Reality-centredness**, visible in the saints
   and codified in the cross-traditional **Golden Rule** — not by their unverifiable
   metaphysics. By this test the great traditions appear comparably effective.
4. **The Copernican revolution.** Theology must move from a "Ptolemaic" model (my religion
   at the center) to a "Copernican" one (the Real at the center, all traditions orbiting).
5. **Mythological truth.** Conflicting doctrines that cannot be settled (incarnation,
   reincarnation, the eternity of the world) are reread as *mythologically* rather than
   *literally* true — true insofar as they evoke an apt practical response to the Real.

## Pluralism vs. Perennialism

Pluralism is often conflated with **[[perennialism|perennialism]]** (the "perennial
philosophy" — Aldous Huxley, the Traditionalists, popularly Huston Smith), but they differ:

- **Perennialism** claims a single mystical *essence* directly accessible at the core of
  all traditions; surface differences are inessential husks around one timeless truth.
- **Hick's pluralism** denies any directly knowable common essence — the Real *an sich* is
  beyond experience — and takes the historical and doctrinal differences seriously as the
  varied phenomenal forms of the Real, not as dispensable husks.

Both are targets of the **[[religious-pluralism-debate|particularist]]** critique —
sharpened by [[stephen-prothero|Stephen Prothero]] — which insists the traditions are
genuinely *different* (answering different questions, not climbing one mountain) and that
flattening them is both bad scholarship and a covert theology of its own.

## Cross-Tradition Significance

- Pluralism is itself controversial *within* every tradition: most traditions' confessional
  self-understandings are exclusivist or inclusivist, so pluralism is frequently judged
  heterodox by the very communities it seeks to honor.
- The position has obvious traction in **interfaith** and civic contexts (Hick's Birmingham
  experience) and is sometimes charged with being a liberal-Western, post-Enlightenment
  framework dressed as neutral description.
- It bears directly on every cross-tradition **contradiction** recorded in this wiki (e.g.
  [[crucifixion-in-the-quran|the crucifixion in the Qur'an]], [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the
  nature of Brahman and the self]]): the pluralist reads these as compatible "lenses"; the
  particularist reads them as the irreducible differences that matter most.
