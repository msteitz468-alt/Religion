# Pilgrimage (Bahá'í)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 24426–24487.*

## Sacred Sites

[[the-bab|The Báb]] declared his own house in [[shiraz|Shíráz]] to be a place of pilgrimage. [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] appointed both the Shíráz House and his own former house in [[baghdad|Baghdad]] to be places of pilgrimage, prescribing specific rites of visitation in two Súrihs of Hajj. All male Bahá'ís who were able should seek to visit one of these two sacred sites. Women were exempted from this obligation but are allowed to visit if they wish (lines 24427–24438).

[[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] declared Bahá'u'lláh's Shrine at [[bahji|Bahjí]] to be a third centre of pilgrimage, and declared visiting it a religious obligation for those able to do so. There are no required rituals for such visits (lines 24438–24444).

## Loss of Historical Sites

Pilgrimage to the Baghdad House became impossible following its seizure by local Shi'is in the 1920s. The Shíráz House was destroyed in the aftermath of the Islamic Revolution in Iran. Consequently, the shrines and holy places at the [[bahai-world-centre|Bahá'í World Centre]] are now the only places of Bahá'í pilgrimage (lines 24445–24453).

## Modern Pilgrimage Practice

At present Bahá'ís are able to apply to join organized nine-day pilgrimage groups, during which they visit the Shrines of Bahá'u'lláh and the Báb, and are taken to visit other Bahá'í holy sites. They also commonly attend meetings addressed by members of the Universal House of Justice and International Teaching Centre. The 'pilgrimage season' extends through most of the year except summer months, with visits by successive groups of around one hundred Bahá'ís. Shorter (normally three-day) pilgrimages are also permitted (lines 24454–24469).

During the time of 'Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi, the smaller size of pilgrimage groups and the extended stays gave these visits a religious intensity which their modern successors perhaps lack. Pilgrimage is still considered of enormous importance both as a means of spiritually regenerating the individual pilgrim and providing the Bahá'í World Centre with an additional channel of contact with Bahá'ís from around the world. [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] described the flow of pilgrims as the 'life-blood' of the World Centre (ADJ 4) (lines 24470–24484).

## 'Pilgrims' Notes'

Unauthenticated notes taken by Bahá'ís of their pilgrimage experiences in the presence of 'Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi — particularly of utterances heard from the two leaders — are effectively the Bahá'í equivalent of Islamic *hadíths*. Both 'Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi were insistent that such notes could have no canonical authority — only authenticated texts were authoritative. Shoghi Effendi insisted that notes made of his own remarks were only for the personal use of the pilgrim who heard them; they might be circulated if a source of inspiration but should not be printed, as they were not official pronouncements and as recorded were sometimes inaccurate and misleading (lines 24488–24512).
