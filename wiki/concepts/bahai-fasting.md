# Bahá'í Fasting

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Origins and Legislation

The Báb ordained a nineteen-day fast for his followers during the Babi month of 'Alá'. In the [[kitab-i-aqdas|Kitáb-i-Aqdas]], [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] confirmed both the fast and the calendar, placing the month of fasting immediately before the intercalary days and the new year festival of [[naw-ruz|Nawrúz]]. The fast normally runs from 2–20 March (lines 14232–14242).

## Who Must Fast

Bahá'ís between the ages of fifteen and seventy are required to abstain from food, drink, and smoking between sunrise and sunset (lines 14242–14262).

## Exemptions

Exemptions are granted to: the sick, pregnant and menstruating women, nursing mothers, students, and those engaged in heavy labour or travel. The exemption for travelers is defined as covering journeys of nine hours or more (or two hours by foot), including a period of rest if away from home. Those excused from fasting on account of their work are encouraged to eat frugally and in private, to show respect for the fast (lines 14263–14279).

## Spiritual Purpose

The fast is one of the "pillars of divine law," drawing the believer closer to God. It is intended to be a period of meditation and prayer, during which the individual abstains from worldly desire and seeks to reinvigorate the inner spiritual being of the soul. Fasting is an individual spiritual obligation and responsibility; Bahá'í institutions have no right to enforce it (lines 14329–14341).

At high latitudes, the timing of sunrise/sunset must be adjusted accordingly (line 14342). Those who wish to make religious vows to fast during additional periods outside the month of fasting may do so, but are advised that vows that benefit mankind are better in the sight of God (lines 14319–14328).

## Relationship to Other Religions

The Bahá'í fast is distinguished from Muslim Ramadan in several ways: it is nineteen days (not one lunar month), it is independent of the lunar calendar, and it prohibits smoking as well as food and drink. The voluntary additional fast parallels Islamic and Christian practice of devotional fasting (line 14322).
