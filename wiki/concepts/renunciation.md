# Renunciation (the Determination to Be Free)

The defining attitude of the **medium-capacity** person and the first of the **three principal aspects
of the path** (with [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]] and correct view). In the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim
Chenmo]]** (Vol. I, ch. 22), [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] defines it as the genuine wish to escape all
of cyclic existence, arising from understanding suffering, its origin, and the twelve factors of
[[dependent-origination|dependent-arising]].

## Its Measure

A mere wish is not enough — Sha-ra-wa compares a superficial determination to powder forming "only a
thin layer on the surface of inferior beer." The standard is total disenchantment: like hungry
"northern children" who refuse every form of turnip in disgust, the practitioner must come to think,
of whatever worldly happiness is seen, heard, or remembered, "Everything is suffering; I want nothing
to do with it." Without this genuine disenchantment, great compassion and the uncontrived spirit of
enlightenment cannot arise.

## Not Opposed to the Bodhisattva's Engagement

Tsong-kha-pa reconciles renunciation with the Mahāyāna sūtra passages praising bodhisattvas who "view
cyclic existence as beneficial": those teach **joyous perseverance** in working for others, not
acceptance of karma-driven rebirth. "Not being disenchanted with cyclic existence means not being
disenchanted with accomplishing the good of living beings in cyclic existence" — and per Bhāvaviveka,
bodhisattvas "do not remain here" (seeing saṃsāra's faults) yet "do not remain in nirvāṇa" (caring for
others).
