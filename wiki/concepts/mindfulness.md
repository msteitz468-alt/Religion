# Mindfulness

**Mindfulness** (Pali *sati*; Sanskrit *smṛti*, literally "memory / recollection") is the
seventh factor of the **[[noble-eightfold-path|Noble Eightfold Path]]** (Right Mindfulness,
*sammā-sati*) and the heart of Buddhist meditation: a clear, non-judgmental, present-moment
awareness of body, feelings, mind, and mental objects.

## In the Early Canon

The classical locus is the **Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta** (the "Four Foundations of Mindfulness"):
mindfulness of the **body** (above all the breath), of **feelings** (*vedanā*), of the
**mind** (*citta*), and of **mental objects** (*dhammā*). [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]] devotes
his meditation chapter (*bhāvanā*) to it, stressing that Buddhist meditation is not escapism
or trance but **insight** (*vipassanā*) — the clear seeing of the
[[three-marks-of-existence|three marks]] that liberates. Mindfulness pairs with
**concentration** (*samādhi*) and supports the calming function of *shamatha*.

## Thich Nhat Hanh's Emphasis

For **[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]**, mindfulness is *the* practice — the energy of
present-moment awareness cultivated through **mindful breathing, walking, smiling, and deep
looking**. It is the "sentinel posted at each sense door" that recognizes and disarms habit
energy and strong emotion ("Hello, my habit energy, I know you are there"). Two innovations:

- **The art of stopping.** Mindfulness first *stops* the compulsive momentum of thought and
  reactivity, creating the space for understanding and compassion.
- **Collective mindfulness.** Against a purely private serenity, he insists mindfulness must
  be social and ecological — the basis of **Engaged Buddhism**.

## Secular Adaptation and Controversy

Largely through Thich Nhat Hanh and the *vipassanā* movement (and Jon Kabat-Zinn's
Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction), mindfulness entered Western clinical psychology and
corporate life as a secular technique. This raises a live debate — recorded here, not
adjudicated — over whether mindfulness severed from ethics
([[noble-eightfold-path|*sīla*]]) and the goal of [[nirvana|liberation]] remains Buddhist
"Right Mindfulness" at all, or becomes a value-neutral attention tool ("McMindfulness").
