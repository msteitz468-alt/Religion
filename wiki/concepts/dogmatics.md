# Dogmatics

**Dogmatics** is the discipline within theology that asks about the *content* of the
Church's distinctive talk about God — whether that talk conforms to its proper norm.

## Barth's Definition

[[karl-barth|Karl Barth]] opens [[barth-church-dogmatics-i1|*Church Dogmatics* I.1]] §1 with the thesis (lines
133–134): "As a theological discipline dogmatics is the scientific self-examination of the
Christian Church with respect to the content of its distinctive talk about God."

For Barth, dogmatics is one of three overlapping theological disciplines:

- **Biblical theology** — asks about the *basis* of Christian utterance (Scripture as the
  witness to revelation)
- **Practical theology** — asks about the *goal* of Christian utterance (proclamation and
  its effects)
- **Dogmatic theology** — asks about the *content* of Christian utterance: does what the
  Church says actually conform to what the Church is — namely, Jesus Christ? (lines
  213–217)

## Dogma vs. Dogmas

Barth distinguishes *dogmatics* (the science of *dogma*) from the science of *dogmas*
(specific propositional formulations). Dogmatics is not the task of assembling, repeating,
and transcribing ready-made truths of revelation; the "true content which is sought" is
*dogma* — the norm by which any particular dogmatic statement stands or falls (lines
536–545). This distinguishes his project from both the Roman Catholic account (which
equates dogma with the *magisterium*'s deposit) and older Protestant biblicism (Melanchthon's
*Loci* as systematic arrangement of biblical propositions).

## Dogmatics as Enquiry and as Act of Faith

Barth insists dogmatics has two irreducible characteristics:

1. **Enquiry** (*Fragen*): human knowledge of the divine is always partial, mediated, and
   fallible. It operates as *theologia crucis* ([[theology-of-the-cross|theology of the
   cross]]) — "a laborious movement from one partial human insight to another" (line
   680). It knows even as it seeks (lines 573–574). The *analogia fidei* ([[analogia-fidei|analogy
   of faith]], Rom. 12:6) is the epistemological basis — not a human capacity but a divine
   gift.

2. **Act of faith**: dogmatics presupposes Christian faith — not as the theologian's
   biographical experience or existential decision, but as "the grace of divine
   predestination, the free gift of the Word and Holy Spirit" (line 1078). Without faith
   dogmatics is "idle speculation without any content of knowledge" (lines 881–884).
   Prayer is the human attitude corresponding to this dependence (lines 1192–1236).

## Relationship to Other Positions

- **Roman Catholic dogmatics**: equates dogma with the infallible *magisterium*'s deposit —
  Scripture and apostolic tradition as authoritatively interpreted. Barth rejects this as
  removing the *free divine address* and substituting a permanently available human
  possession (lines 753–781). See [[analogia-entis|*analogia entis*]].
- **Protestant Modernist dogmatics** (Schleiermacher, Bultmann): grounds dogmatics in
  anthropology or existential analysis as the *prius* of faith. Barth rejects as
  "anthropologising theology" (lines 1025, 1803–1808).
- **Evangelical (Reformation) dogmatics**: measures the Church's talk about God against
  the being of the Church — which *is* Jesus Christ — as attested in Scripture. Prolegomena
  to dogmatics are themselves part of dogmatics, not prior to it (lines 1987–1989).
