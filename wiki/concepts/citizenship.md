# Citizenship in Heaven (Philippians)

In [[philippians|Philippians]], Paul frames Christian identity with **civic** vocabulary that, for [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]], is charged by the letter’s setting in the Roman colony of [[philippi|Philippi]]. Two related loci carry the load: the imperative *politeuesthe* (“live as citizens”) at **1:27**, and the noun *politeuma* (“citizenship / commonwealth / governing constitution”) at **3:20**.

## Phil 1:27 — *politeuesthe*

Hansen notes that *politeuesthe* is built on *polis* and denotes discharging the obligations of citizens. Paul’s only use of the verb in the letters was “probably inspired by the pride of Roman citizenship” in the colony. Interpreters divide:

| Option | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Heavenly citizenship | Read 1:27 from 3:20 (TNIV inserts “of heaven” though the word is not in the Greek of 1:27) |
| Earthly civic duty | Bruce Winter: public civic responsibility; do not let 3:20 control 1:27 |
| **Dual citizenship (Hansen via Hawthorne)** | Good citizens of Philippi *and* of the heavenly commonwealth; church as “colony within a colony” |

Hansen’s expanded paraphrase: *As good citizens of Philippi and as good citizens of heaven, live in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ.* Unpacked: stand firm in the one Spirit (Hansen: Holy Spirit, not mere esprit de corps); strive side by side for the faith of the gospel; without being frightened by opponents.

## Phil 3:20–21 — *politeuma*

Hansen: *politeuma* connotes an active constitutive force regulating citizens; “Our governing power, our executive authority is in heaven.” Implication: a **colony of heavenly citizens** on earth (exile/foreigner metaphor; cf. Josephus on Alexandrian Jewish *politeuma*, Philo’s heavenly virtues — possible background, not primary).

**Primary frame for Hansen:** contrast with Roman Philippi’s *politeuma* in **Rome**. Against residents who look to Caesar as “savior of the world,” Paul redirects hope to a **Savior from heaven**, the Lord Jesus Christ — language that **echoes and subverts** imperial titles while drawing their *meaning* from Jewish confession (esp. Isaiah 45, already backdrop of the [[christ-hymn|Christ hymn]]).

Bodily hope: the Savior will transform the body of humiliation to be like his glorious body by the power that subjects all things — continuity of **somatic** existence (not escape from the body). Hansen records the tension with “depart and be with Christ” (1:23) without resolving intermediate-state hypotheses into dogma.

## Theological load

- Ethics: eschatology grounds imitation (3:17–21): two pictures (earthly-minded → destruction; heavenly citizens → Savior and transformation).
- Christology: Savior/Lord titles unite imperial subversion and [[kyrios|YHWH-Lord]] confession.
- Community: dual citizenship underwrites unity and courage under opposition (1:27–30).
