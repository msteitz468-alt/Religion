# Seven Purifications (*Satta-visuddhi*)

The **seven purifications** (*satta-visuddhi*) are the structural framework of the Theravada path
as systematized by [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]] in the [[visuddhimagga|*Visuddhimagga*]], derived
from the **Rathavinīta Sutta** (MN 24), in which the seven purifications are compared to a relay
of chariots: each carries one as far as it can go, then hands one on to the next.

## The Seven Purifications

| # | Pali | Description | Part of Visuddhimagga |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Sīla-visuddhi* | **Purification of virtue** | Part I (Chs. I–II) |
| 2 | *Citta-visuddhi* | **Purification of mind** (concentration / jhāna) | Part II (Chs. III–XIII) |
| 3 | *Diṭṭhi-visuddhi* | **Purification of view** | Ch. XVIII |
| 4 | *Kaṅkhāvitaraṇa-visuddhi* | **Purification by overcoming doubt** | Ch. XIX |
| 5 | *Maggāmagga-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi* | **Purification by knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path** | Ch. XX |
| 6 | *Paṭipadā-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi* | **Purification by knowledge and vision of the way** | Ch. XXI |
| 7 | *Ñāṇadassana-visuddhi* | **Purification by knowledge and vision** (the supramundane paths) | Ch. XXII |

## How They Function as Path Stages

### 1. Purification of Virtue
The foundation. The meditator perfects the monastic code (or lay precepts for laity),
bringing virtue to a pure, unbroken state. Without this, concentration cannot be established —
virtue is the "soil" in which the higher purifications grow.

### 2. Purification of Mind
Access concentration (*upacāra-samādhi*) is the minimum; most meditators are expected to achieve
at least the first material *[[jhana|jhāna]]*. The fully purified mind is the instrument of
clear seeing; concentration without virtue is unstable and the resulting insight unreliable.

### 3. Purification of View
The meditator, using concentration as the basis, resolves what appears to be a "self" into bare
**mentality-materiality** (*nāma-rūpa*): analyzing the four primary material elements (hardness/
fluidity/heat/motion) and identifying how mentality arises from materiality at the six sense doors.
The illusion of a persisting experiencer dissolves into a stream of momentary phenomena.

### 4. Purification by Overcoming Doubt
The meditator traces the causal conditions behind the arising of each moment of
mentality-materiality — seeing that "in the past there was only name-and-form, not a being." Past
and future lives are included by inference. This uproots the view that being arises uncaused or
from a creator.

### 5. Purification by Knowledge and Vision of What Is and Is Not the Path
**Comprehension by groups** (*kalāpa-sammasana*): all formations comprehended as impermanent,
painful, and not-self. The **ten imperfections of insight** (*vipassanupakkilesa*) arise — illumination,
knowledge, rapture, tranquillity, bliss, resolution, exertion, assurance, equanimity, subtle
attachment — and must be recognized as defilements, not attainments. Recognizing the imperfections
and discarding them defines what is and is not the path. **Knowledge of rise and fall** (free from
imperfections) then begins the next purification.

### 6. Purification by Knowledge and Vision of the Way
The eight insight knowledges:
1. Knowledge of rise and fall (free from the ten imperfections)
2. Knowledge of dissolution
3. Knowledge of appearance as terror
4. Knowledge of danger
5. Knowledge of dispassion
6. Knowledge of desire for deliverance
7. Knowledge of reflection
8. Knowledge of equanimity about formations

Plus conformity knowledge (ninth) = "insight leading to emergence." Change-of-lineage knowledge
is the culminating, unassignable stage between the sixth and seventh purifications.

### 7. Purification by Knowledge and Vision
The four **supramundane paths** and their fruits: stream-entry (first path), once-return (second),
non-return (third), Arahantship (fourth). Each path is a single momentary event; each is followed
by two or three fruition consciousnesses; each is followed by reviewing. This purification is the
goal of the entire path.

## The Relay-Chariot Simile
From MN 24: Seven chariots are stationed on a road. One does not travel the entire distance in
a single chariot — one rides the first to where the second is waiting, transfers, and so on to the
destination. So too, virtue is the relay chariot to concentration; concentration to view; and so
on through to knowledge and vision. Each purification is complete in itself and hands one off to the
next; none is the goal, but each is indispensable.
