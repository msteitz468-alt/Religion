# Pro-Nicene Theology

**Pro-Nicene theology** is the term Lewis Ayres ([[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its
Legacy*]], 2004) uses for the mature Trinitarian theology that emerged in the **360s–380s** and
was ratified at the [[arian-controversy|Council of Constantinople (381)]] — the actual
resolution of the fourth-century controversies, as distinct from the [[homoousion|*homoousios*]]
of 325. Its central methodological claim is that **there was no single "Nicene theology" that
survived unchanged from 325**; rather, "pro-Nicene theology was itself constituted by a
collection of overlapping yet distinct theologies" that share assumptions and practices, not
merely creedal terminology. The East/West (Greek/Latin) divide, on Ayres's account, is "of far
less significance than is usually thought" and cannot serve as a fundamental category of
division (see [[de-regnon-paradigm|the de Régnon paradigm controversy]]).

Pro-Nicene theology is best understood as a **theological culture** — "a system of learned
patterns of behaviour (including thought, speech, and human action), ideas, and products" — or,
in Ayres's preferred less-technical phrase, a **"pro-Nicene life of the mind."** Orthodoxy
consists "not so much with open and public 'performance' of one's subscription to the Nicene
creed or its terminology" but with the display of "interconnected principles and combinations
of ideas that enable hearers and readers to recognize an orthodox theology even when
terminology differs."

## Ayres's Three Defining Criteria

Ayres takes **three central principles** to identify a theology as fully pro-Nicene:

1. **A clear person–nature distinction** — "whatever is predicated of the divine nature is
   predicated of the three persons equally and understood to be one" (whether or not articulated
   through consistent technical terminology, e.g. one [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|*ousia*,
   three *hypostaseis*]]).
2. **Eternal generation within the unitary divine being** — "clear expression that the eternal
   generation of the Son occurs within the unitary and incomprehensible divine being."
3. **Inseparable operation** — "clear expression of the doctrine that the persons work
   inseparably" (see [[opera-trinitatis|inseparable operations]]).

For all pro-Nicenes "the Father is presented as first in an order in the Godhead, and as the
source (αἰτία/ἀρχή) of Son and Spirit" — the Father is "greater" only by being the cause, not
at the level of substance.

### What "pro-Nicene" is *not*

Ayres defines the term against two standard framings: (a) the "one original Nicene theology"
narrative (Athanasius defends an original Nicene faith, passed to the Cappadocians, then to the
West) — there is no such unchanged theology, and Athanasius's direct influence on the
Cappadocians "is difficult to prove"; and (b) the **Harnack thesis** that Cappadocian theology
was a "neo-Nicene" retreat from [[athanasius|Athanasius]] into adapted
[[homoiousians|Homoiousianism]] — Ayres follows Ritter (1965) that the Cappadocian account of
unity is "functionally identical" to Athanasius's. The temporal referent is flexible: the
"full flowering" is the 380s, but precursors ([[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary]], Phoebadius of
Agen, [[marius-victorinus|Marius Victorinus]], the later Athanasius) appear earlier and "fall
short in some respects."

## The Three Shared Strategies (Part III)

Ayres distills pro-Nicene theology into three "theological strategies" — patterns of
argumentation that are "the shared core of pro-Nicene Trinitarianism."

**Strategy I — Speaking of unity and diversity in the Trinity.** Reflection on "the paradox of
the irreducible unity of the three irreducible divine persons," always against "the absolute
distinction between God as the only truly simple reality and creation." Its components:
- **[[divine-simplicity|Divine simplicity]]** functions as a *formal feature* (Ayres adopts
  Burrell): it does not describe divine being comprehensibly but governs "the manner in which
  such properties might be attributed to God." Ayres argues "it is in pro-Nicene theology that
  we first see simplicity functioning in" this regulative way.
- **Inseparable operation and appropriation** — every divine act is the act of all three;
  [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]]: "the operation… is one… the oneness of their nature
  must needs be inferred from the identity of their operation." **Appropriation** (attributing
  a common attribute/action to one person) is "a common pro-Nicene principle," not merely
  Augustinian; it "performs" rather than resolves the tension of simplicity.
- **Divine incomprehensibility** and **analogical reserve** — no analogy delivers knowledge of
  the divine essence; creation furnishes "analogical sites" mined complementarily, and analogies
  are displayed to show each other's inadequacy ([[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory Nazianzen]],
  *Or.* 31, runs through and rejects them all). The persons are known only by their **relations
  of origin**.
- The **psychological analogy** is used *against* modern "social Trinity" readings: the soul's
  disunity points to a divine unity that *exceeds* psychological experience.

**Strategy II — Christology and cosmology.** Against R.P.C. Hanson et al., pro-Nicene theology
"did not simply bring an end to 'Logos theology'": the eternal Son remains the
[[logos-christology|image]] that reveals the Father. Salvation occurs "through
[[participation|participation]] in Christ," a union with Christ's incarnate person; and the
created order becomes a **semiotic/figural structure** pointing minds to the incomprehensible
Creator ([[basil-of-caesarea|Basil]], *Hexameron*: "everywhere the dogmas of theology have
been mystically sown into the narrative"). [[augustine|Augustine]]: in the Word, God "is nearer
to us than many things which have been made."

**Strategy III — Anthropology, epistemology, and the reading of Scripture.** "At the heart of
the purification necessary for Christians lies a reordering of human knowing and desiring." This
**dual-focus** anthropology (trained soul governing the body, both ordered to contemplative
*telos*) makes [[deification|purification/deification]] not a *consequence* of Trinitarian
doctrine but "an essential part of those developments." Scripture's σκοπός intrinsically
includes "the journey of the soul in Christ towards union with… the Triune Godhead." Heterodoxy
is reframed as a failure of both attention to mystery *and* a poorly trained soul — making
ascetic discipline (Interlude II, "ascetic portability") integral to orthodoxy.

## Key Figures and Texts

Named pro-Nicenes (Ayres, Epilogue): [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil of Caesarea]],
[[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]], [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory Nazianzen]], Eusebius of
Vercelli, [[ambrose|Ambrose]], [[augustine|Augustine]], the later [[athanasius|Athanasius]],
Didymus the Blind, [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]]; [[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary]]
and [[marius-victorinus|Marius Victorinus]] as Latin precursors. Paradigmatic texts Ayres
recommends: Gregory of Nyssa's *Catechetical Orations* and *Refutation of Eunomius*; Gregory
Nazianzen's *Theological Orations*; Augustine's *Sermons* 52 & 117 and *De Trinitate* 4 & 15;
Rufinus's *Commentary on the Apostles' Creed*.

The decisive technical breakthrough was [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil's]] distinction of shared
*ousia* from individuating *idiomata* (*Contra Eunomium*), "as much epistemological as
ontological," which let him confess *homoousios* (only once in *C. Eun.*, derived from Heb.
1:3) while rebutting the charge that it collapses Father and Son. Pro-Nicene **pneumatology**
extended unity-of-operation arguments to the Spirit, establishing that "the work of
sanctification is the unmediated work of God" — though "on the subject of the Spirit's place in
the Godhead as such little progress is made," and "the later question of the
[[filioque|filioque]] is not an issue" in this period.
