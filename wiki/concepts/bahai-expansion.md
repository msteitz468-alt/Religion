# Bahá'í Expansion

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 12063–12930.*

## Overview

There has been a strong missionary emphasis in both the Bábí and Bahá'í religions (see Teaching), with a determined effort to increase: the number of adherents; the geographical range of their locations; and, in the Bahá'í case, their religious and cultural diversity. By 1995 the Faith was established in 190 countries and 45 dependent territories; over 2,000 tribes and ethnic groups were represented by the 1980s (lines 12063–12080).

## Three Stages of Expansion (lines 12081–12114)

Smith identifies three stages and three 'worlds' of Bábí-Bahá'í expansion:

### (1) Islamic Stage, 1844–c.1892
Bábism began as a sectarian movement within [[shaykhism|Shaykhism]], with initial expansion largely confined within existing Shaykhi communities. Some transcendence of the Shaykhi milieu occurred through the Báb's messianic claims and conversions of prominent non-Shaykhi clerics such as Hujjat and Vaḥíd. The Bábis remained part of the world of sectarian Shi'ism. When [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] revived the movement in Baghdad he simultaneously transformed it, addressing Sufi and Judaeo-Christian themes and presenting beliefs in a style accessible to literate Iranian Muslims without clerical mediation. By 1892 the total number of Bahá'ís may have been in the region of 100,000 (line 12196).

### (2) Internationalization, c.1892–c.1953
During [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]'s leadership (1892–1921) the Bahá'í community broke out of the cultural confines of its Islamic milieu and became a truly international religious movement. This continued under [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]. By 1952 there were Bahá'ís in some 116 countries and major colonial territories; the total number remained c.200,000, with over 90 per cent still Iranian.

**The Faith in the West**: Of key importance was the establishment of the Faith in the West from 1894. The pioneer teacher in the United States was a recently converted Syrian Christian, [[ibrahim-kheiralla|Ibrahim Kheiralla]], who expressed the Bahá'í teachings in a way that appealed to Christian sentiment (lines 12236–12244). 'Abdu'l-Bahá took an active role in the process of reformulation during his Western tours of 1911–13.

**Administration and planning**: The twin developments of Bahá'í administration (begun 1922) and systematic planning (from 1937, see Plans) — led especially by the Western Bahá'ís — enabled organized expansion. By 1952 Bahá'ís resided in 2,425 places with 611 local spiritual assemblies (line 12336).

### (3) Global Stage, from 1953
Since the 1950s the Bahá'í Faith has begun to fulfil the vision of its founders and become a world religion. Total Bahá'ís grew from c.200,000 in the early 1950s to about 5 million by the early 1990s (lines 12343–12346). Key features:
- **Social composition shift**: in 1950, most non-Iranian Bahá'ís came from educated/middle classes; now the majority are drawn from rural and urban masses in the 'Third World' — Latin America, Caribbean, sub-Saharan Africa, and monsoon Asia (lines 12364–12381)
- **Cultural diversity**: much greater range of ethnic groups and cultural expressions of 'being Bahá'í' (lines 12382–12392)
- **Mass teaching methods**: large-scale enrolment of often illiterate rural populations, with much greater use of music (lines 12393–12422)

## Geographical Distribution (lines 12542–12772)

By 1992, 88 per cent of total localities and 85 per cent of assemblies were in the 'Third World'. Africa and South Asia stand out as the regions with the greatest numbers of localities and assemblies (each c.28–31% of world total), followed by Latin America and the Caribbean, South East Asia, and North America. In terms of assemblies per million population, the greatest density is in the Pacific: Oceania (76 assemblies per million) and the Anglo-Pacific (12 per million).

## The Islamic Heartland (lines 12773–12911)

Despite being the Faith's birthplace, Iran now has a comparatively low Bahá'í presence (possibly 0.5–1% of the population), partly due to severe persecution under the Islamic revolutionary regime from 1979. Iranian Bahá'ís have played a massive role in global expansion; up until the 1960s the majority of all Bahá'ís worldwide were Iranians or of Iranian origin. The Iranian Bahá'í diaspora now reaches almost every country.

In the Arab world a modest programme of activity mounted in Egypt and Iraq (with national assemblies established in 1924 and 1931 respectively) sparked opposition from conservative Islamic groups. The Faith has since been banned in several Arab countries.
