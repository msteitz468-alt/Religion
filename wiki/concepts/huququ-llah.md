# Ḥuqúqu'lláh ("The Right of God")

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17449–17544.*

## Definition

Ḥuqúqu'lláh (Arabic: "the Right of God") is a monetary payment to be made to the head of the Bahá'í Faith by all Bahá'ís able to afford it. It was established in the [[kitab-i-aqdas|Kitáb-i-Aqdas]] by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]], who described the payment as a spiritual bounty that brings the individual closer to God and purifies their possessions (l. 17455–17458).

## Terms and Obligations

- Payment is an individual spiritual obligation; no Bahá'í is to be solicited for it (l. 17459–17460).
- It is to be offered "in a spirit of joy" (l. 17461).
- Threshold: payable when a person's property exceeds 19 mithqáls of gold (2.2 troy ounces) in value (l. 17462–17463).
- Rate: 19 percent of the value of all wealth other than one's residence, place of business, and household furnishings (l. 17464–17466).
- Payment is made only once on any particular amount; subsequent payments are owed only on further increments of wealth after necessary expenses are deducted (l. 17467–17471).
- Payment of Ḥuqúq should precede division of a deceased's estate, but after settlement of funeral expenses and debts (l. 17472–17475).
- Those unable to pay are exempt (l. 17475–17476).
- Payment precedes donations to other Bahá'í funds (l. 17476–17478).

## Administration

The payment is made to the Centre of the Faith, who determines how it is disbursed. Expenditures may include promotion of the Bahá'í religion, upkeep of properties, and general charity (l. 17478–17484).

## History

Bahá'u'lláh promulgated the law of Ḥuqúq in 1873 but initially declined to accept payments (l. 17490–17491). In 1878 he appointed Ḥájí Sháh-Muḥammad Manshádí, Amínu'l-Bayán ("trustee of the Bayán"), as the first Trustee of the Ḥuqúqu'lláh, charged with collecting payments from Bahá'ís in Iran (l. 17492–17497). Payments were also made via Zayn'ul-Muqarrabín in Mosul (l. 17498–17500).

Subsequent trustees:
- Ḥájí Amín (Abdu'l-Hasan Ardikání), Amín-i-Iláhí ("trustee of God"; 1881–1928) — posthumously named a [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]] (l. 17502–17510)
- Ḥájí Ghulám-Riḍá, Amín-i-Amín ("trustee of the trustee"; 1928–38) (l. 17503–17506)
- Valíyu'lláh Varqá (1938–55) — appointed Hand of the Cause (l. 17506–17513)
- 'Alí-Muḥammad Varqá (from 1955) — appointed Hand of the Cause (l. 17507–17513)

Payment was initially confined mostly to Bahá'ís of the Middle East (l. 17521–17523). Information was made freely available to Bahá'ís elsewhere in 1985, and the law was made universally applicable in 1992 (l. 17527–17532). A central office of Ḥuqúqu'lláh was established in Haifa in 1991 (l. 17536–17537).

## Relationship to Islamic Law

Ḥuqúq resembles the Shí'í payment of *khums* ("the fifth" of gained wealth, in part payable to representatives of the [[imams|Imáms]]). The term also occurs in the [[the-bayan|Bayán]] (l. 17538–17542).
