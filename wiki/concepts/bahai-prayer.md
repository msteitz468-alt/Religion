# Prayer (Bahá'í)

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Obligatory Prayer (Ṣalát)

In the [[kitab-i-aqdas|Kitáb-i-Aqdas]], Bahá'u'lláh prescribes daily ritual prayer as a primary religious obligation for Bahá'ís from the age of fifteen (the "age of maturity") upwards. Those who are weak from illness or age (over seventy) are exempt. So too are menstruating women, who may instead repeat a specific verse ("Glorified is God, the Lord of Splendour and Beauty") ninety-five times during each twenty-four hour period (lines 24978–24989).

The believer may choose each day to say one of three obligatory prayers:
1. **Short prayer**: to be said between noon and sunset
2. **Medium prayer**: to be said three times — between dawn and noon, noon and sunset, and sunset and two hours after sunset
3. **Long prayer**: may be said at any time, ideally when in "a state of humbleness and longing adoration"

(Lines 24990–24999.)

Prayer is to be preceded by ritual ablutions (*wuḍú'*) and made in the direction of the tomb of Bahá'u'lláh at [[bahji|Bahjí]], which is the Bahá'í [[qiblah|Qiblah]]. Two of the prayers involve ritual movement and prostration (*rak'a*). Prostration may be made on any clean surface. In conditions of insecurity, such as travel, prayer may be omitted, and each missed prayer later compensated for by the repetition of certain verses (lines 24941–24949, 25001–25006).

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] encouraged Bahá'ís to avoid rigidity and ritual in their prayers. Such rituals as there are are few, and should be regarded as symbolic: the worshipper turning towards the qiblah is a physical symbol of turning to Bahá'u'lláh (lines 25017–25027). Those who are uncomfortable making ritual genuflections should instead use the short prayer in which there are no gestures (lines 25028–25030).

Obligatory prayer together with [[fasting|fasting]] is regarded as a means for the believer to draw closer to God, and as a pillar of divine law. It is conducive to humility (lines 25010–25013).

## General Prayer

In addition to daily obligatory prayer, Bahá'ís are encouraged to pray frequently, both as an individual act of turning to God and in their meetings. There are no set forms for such prayers. The purpose of these prayers is to bring people closer to God and Bahá'u'lláh, to help them to purify their own conduct, and allow them to request divine assistance. Prayer does not have to be necessarily prolonged, but the spirit in which it is offered is important. Individual prayer should be offered in private and when one is free from distractions. Collective prayer — in which individuals usually take it in turn to read, chant, or sing prayers — is encouraged (lines 25032–25067).

Administrative meetings of assemblies and committees commonly include prayer, whilst the regular nineteen day [[feast|Feast]] begins with an integral devotional period (lines 25062–25065).

## The Corpus of Prayers

There are many prayers in Persian and Arabic by Bahá'u'lláh and 'Abdu'l-Bahá, widely used. These include prayers to be said on awakening and going to sleep, and during the Bahá'í fast; for travelling, healing, spiritual growth and detachment, protection, forgiveness, assistance and unity; and on behalf of children, parents and spouses. Some of the Báb's prayers have also been translated into English. The original words of all these prayers should be used without alteration. Children should be taught to memorize them (lines 25068–25088).

## Prayer for the Dead

Bahá'u'lláh ordained a specific prayer for the dead, based on a similar prayer of the Báb's. This is to be said preceding the interment of any Bahá'í who has reached "the age of maturity" (fifteen). It is read aloud by one person while the others present stand in silence, and as such is the only Bahá'í congregational prayer (lines 25089–25099).

## Special Prayers

Bahá'ís commonly attach importance to three prayers regarded as having particular power for those in difficulty or sickness:
1. A short prayer of the Báb's, "The remover of difficulties"
2. Bahá'u'lláh's "Tablet of Aḥmad"
3. Bahá'u'lláh's "Long Healing Prayer"

The Islamic "prayer of the signs" (said at times of earthquakes, eclipses, etc.) was annulled by Bahá'u'lláh, who substituted in its place a specific verse, the saying of which is optional (lines 25100–25116).

## Invocation

Bahá'ís are bidden to recite "*Alláh-u-Abhá*" (God is Most Glorious; see [[greatest-name|Greatest Name]]), ninety-five times each day (lines 25117–25122).
