# Five Pillars of Islam (Arkān al-Islām)

The **Five Pillars of Islam** (*Arkān al-Islām*) are the foundational obligatory practices that constitute the outward dimension of the religion, distinct from interior belief ([[iman|Īmān]]). In [[sahih-muslim|Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]], the Five Pillars are stated explicitly in Chapter 6 of *Kitāb al-Īmān* (Book of Faith), with multiple parallel transmissions establishing the canonical list.

---

## Canonical Statement

The most complete formulation is attributed to the Prophet via **Abdullah b. Umar** (Ch. 6, No. 0020):

> "(The superstructure of) al-Islam is raised on five (pillars), testifying (the fact) that there is no god but Allah, that Muhammad is His bondsman and messenger, and the establishment of prayer, payment of Zakat, Pilgrimage to the House (Ka'ba) and the fast of Ramadan." (Ch. 6, No. 0020)

---

## The Five Pillars Individually

### 1. Shahāda — The Testimony of Faith
"Testifying (the fact) that there is no god but Allah, that Muhammad is His bondsman and messenger." (No. 0020)

Alternative formulations in earlier chapters define the same content:
- "Testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the messenger of Allah" (Ch. 1, No. 0001, the Jibrīl hadith)
- "Allah (alone) should be worshipped, and (all other gods) beside Him should be (categorically) denied" (No. 0019)

The Shahāda is identified as "the most excellent" branch of faith and the minimum threshold for entry into Paradise (Ch. 13, No. 0056; Ch. 11, Nos. 0039, 0041).

### 2. Ṣalāt — Prayer
"The establishment of prayer" (Nos. 0018–0020). Chapter 3's heading identifies prayer explicitly as "one of the pillars of Islam."

The obligatory number of prayers is specified elsewhere in Book 1: "Five prayers during the day and the night" (Ch. 3, No. 0007), with the questioner confirming these are the only obligatory ones and voluntary prayers are additional.

Neglect of prayer is equated with apostasy: "Between man and between polytheism and unbelief is the negligence of prayer." (Ch. 36, No. 0146; No. 0147)

The instruction to Muʿādh b. Jabal when sent to Yemen (Ch. 8, No. 0027) orders: first call people to the Shahāda, then "five prayers during the day and the night."

### 3. Zakāt — Almsgiving / Poor-rate
"Payment of Zakat" (Nos. 0018–0020). Zakat is described as obligatory on the wealthy: "it should be collected from the rich and distributed among the poor" (Ch. 8, No. 0027).

The gravity of Zakat is illustrated by Abu Bakr's decision after the Prophet's death to fight those who refused to pay it (Ch. 9, No. 0029): "By Allah, I would definitely fight against him who severed prayer from Zakat, for it is the obligation upon the rich."

### 4. Ṣawm — Fast of Ramadan
"The fast of Ramadan" (Nos. 0018–0021). Appears consistently in all canonical Five Pillars hadiths.

A debated ordering is noted in No. 0018: when asked whether Pilgrimage or the Ramadan fast precedes the other, Ibn Umar replies "the fasts of Ramadan precede the Pilgrimage."

### 5. Ḥajj — Pilgrimage to Mecca
"Pilgrimage to the House (Ka'ba)" or "Pilgrimage to the (House) if you are solvent enough (to bear the expense of) the journey" (Ch. 1, No. 0001; Ch. 6, Nos. 0018–0020).

The conditional clause — financial and physical ability — appears in the Jibrīl hadith (No. 0001) and in the account of the bedouin questioning the Prophet (Ch. 7, No. 0009): "pilgrimage (Hajj) to the House (of Ka'bah) had been made obligatory for him who is able to undertake the journey to it."

---

## Variant Formulations

Three parallel hadiths in Chapter 6 (Nos. 0018–0021) all transmit the Five Pillars from Abdullah b. Umar, with slight variations in wording:

- **No. 0018**: "the oneness of Allah [tawhid], the establishment of prayer, payment of Zakat, the fast of Ramadan, Pilgrimage (to Mecca)" — places *tawhid* rather than the full Shahāda as the first pillar.
- **No. 0019**: "Allah (alone) should be worshipped, and (all other gods) beside Him should be (categorically) denied" — a more polemical formulation of the first pillar.
- **No. 0020**: The full dual Shahāda ("no god but Allah, that Muhammad is His bondsman and messenger") — most complete form.

Chapter 7 (Nos. 0022–0026) records a closely related list given to the delegation of Abd al-Qais: "worship Allah and associate none with Him, establish prayer, pay Zakat, and observe the fast of Ramadan, and pay the fifth part out of the booty." Here a fifth element (Khums, one-fifth of war booty) replaces pilgrimage — reflecting a context of early military community rather than a theological definition of the pillars.

---

## Relationship to Īmān

In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim Book 1, the Five Pillars are consistently associated with **al-Islām** (the outward dimension of the religion) rather than with **Īmān** (interior faith). The Jibrīl hadith (Ch. 1, No. 0001) presents them as the Prophet's answer to the question "What is al-Islām?" — explicitly distinct from his answer to "What is Īmān?" which concerns the six articles of belief.

This Islām/Īmān distinction is reinforced in Ch. 2 (No. 0004): the five practices constitute Islām; the six articles of belief constitute Īmān.

## The Five Pillars in Sahih al-Bukhari

*Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 1–3 (M. Muhsin Khan trans.). All citations by Vol./Book/No.*

### 1. Salat (Prayer) — Bukhari Documentation

**Prayer times established by Gabriel**: The five prayer times were established when Gabriel appeared to the Prophet and led him in all five prayers over two successive days, demonstrating the earliest and latest permissible times for each. "I was ordered to do so." ('Umar bin 'Abdul Aziz confirming the chain: Vol. 1, Book 10, No. 500.)

**Time markers** (from Book 10):

| Prayer | Opening marker | Special notes |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Fajr | When dawn begins | Forbidden until sunrise (Nos. 555–562) |
| Zuhr | Sun's decline from zenith | Can delay in extreme heat |
| 'Asr | Sun still hot and high | Missing intentionally = "lost family and property" (No. 527) |
| Maghrib | Sun disappears from horizon | Offered early; shortly after sunset |
| 'Isha | After disappearance of twilight | Prophet sometimes delayed to midnight |

**Fajr and 'Asr are uniquely emphasized**: Angels change shifts at these two prayers (No. 530). "Certainly you will see your Lord as you see this moon... So if you can avoid missing a prayer before the sun-rise (Fajr) and a prayer before sunset ('Asr), you must do so" (Nos. 529, 547). "Whoever prays the two cool prayers ('Asr and Fajr) will go to Paradise" (No. 548). The Prophet identified prayer at its earliest time as **the dearest deed to Allah**, above kindness to parents and Jihad (No. 505).

**The Adhan — its institution**: Not an original Medinan practice. A communal discussion of bell (Christian) vs. horn (Jewish) led 'Umar to propose a human caller; the Prophet ordered **Bilal ibn Rabah** to stand and pronounce the Adhan (Nos. 577–578). Phrases doubled in Adhan; once in Iqama. Bilal served a two-adhan system for Fajr: his pre-dawn adhan for tahajjud and suhur; Ibn Um Maktum's true-dawn adhan for the fast and prayer (Nos. 591, 594–596). Congregational prayer is 25–27 times superior to prayer offered alone (Nos. 618–621).

**Prayer characteristics** (Book 12 — selected): Opening Takbir with raised hands (Nos. 702–706); Al-Fatiha is obligatory — "Whoever does not recite Al-Fatiha in his prayer, his prayer is invalid" (No. 723); prostration on seven bones (forehead + nose, both hands, both knees, toes of both feet, Nos. 773–776); Tashahhud text given in full (Nos. 794, 797); all positions approximately equal in duration (Nos. 758, 766, 783–784); post-prayer dhikr: 33× each Subhanallah/Alhamdulillah/Allahu Akbar (No. 804).

### 2. Zakat — Bukhari Documentation

*Source: Vol. 2, Book 24 (Nos. 484–578).*

**Theological grounding**: Zakat-refusal earns eschatological punishment. Untithed wealth "will be made like a bald-headed poisonous male snake with two black spots over the eyes" that encircles the owner's neck, citing Qur'an 3:180 (No. 486). Untithed camels and sheep return on the Day of Resurrection to trample their owners (No. 485). Abu Bakr's declaration of war against those who withheld it after the Prophet's death confirms Zakat as an obligation enforceable by the state (No. 536).

**Nisab thresholds and rates** (No. 534 — Abu Bakr's written document to Anas):
- *Silver*: no zakat below 5 Awaq (≈200 Dirhams); rate: one-fortieth (2.5%)
- *Camels*: no zakat on fewer than 5
- *Dates/grain*: no zakat on fewer than 5 Wasqs
- *Horses and slaves owned by a Muslim*: no zakat (Nos. 542–543)
- *Agricultural produce* (No. 560): rain-irrigated land pays one-tenth (*ushr*); well-irrigated land pays one-twentieth

**Distribution** (Nos. 537, 573 — the Prophet's instructions to Muadh going to Yemen): "It is to be taken from the rich amongst them and given to the poor amongst them." The collector must avoid taking the best possessions and must avoid the curse of the oppressed.

**Zakat al-Fitr** (Book 25): Due from every free Muslim, slave, male, and female — one *sa'* of dates or barley, paid before the 'Id prayer. Equivalent to feeding a poor person.

**Prophet's family excluded**: Ahl al-Bayt cannot eat from zakat/sadaqa collected for distribution. The Prophet stopped al-Hasan from eating a charity date: "Don't you know that we do not eat a thing which is given in charity?" (Nos. 562, 568).

### 3. Hajj — Bukhari Documentation

*Source: Vol. 2, Book 26 (Nos. 589–823).*

**Obligatory status and merit**: Hajj is "enjoined by Allah on His devotees" (No. 589). In the ranking of best deeds: belief in Allah and His Apostle first, Jihad second, **Hajj Mabrur third** (No. 594). For women: "The best Jihad (for women) is Hajj Mabrur" (No. 595, Aisha). Complete forgiveness results: "Whoever performs Hajj for Allah's pleasure and does not have sexual relations with his wife, and does not do evil or sins then he will return (after Hajj free from all sins) as if he were born anew" (No. 596).

**Structure of Hajj as documented in Bukhari**:
1. *Ihram at Miqat*: Don Ihram garments, begin Talbiya (text: "Labbaika Allahumma labbaik, Labbaika la sharika Laka labbaik, Inna-l-hamda wan-ni'mata Laka wal-mulk, La sharika Laka" — No. 621)
2. *Tawaf al-Qudum*: Seven circuits of the Ka'ba; Ramal in first three rounds; two rakat prayer at Maqam Ibrahim
3. *Sa'y*: Seven circuits between Safa and Marwa; obligatory, not optional (Aisha's correction of Qur'an 2:158 — No. 706)
4. *Wuquf at Arafat*: The central rite (9th Dhu al-Hijja). Pre-Islamic Quraysh had not gone to Arafat; Qur'an 2:199 corrected this. No fasting on the day of Arafat
5. *Muzdalifa*: Combined Maghrib and Isha; Fajr offered early; weak and women may leave early (Nos. 736–741)
6. *Rami of Jamrat al-Aqaba*: Seven small pebbles, Takbir at each throw; Talbiya ceases here
7. *Sacrifice (Hadi)*: Prophet slaughtered 100 camels; meat and skins distributed in charity
8. *Head shaving*: Prophet prayed for mercy on those who shave (three times), adding those who cut short (Nos. 785–786)
9. *Tawaf al-Ifada*: Lifts all Ihram prohibitions
10. *Rami of remaining Jamarat* (11th, 12th): Seven at each; prolonged supplication after first and second
11. *Farewell Tawaf*: Last act before departure; obligatory for all except menstruating women (No. 810)

**The Farewell Pilgrimage (Hajjat al-Wada')**: On the Day of Nahr the Prophet delivered the definitive sermon: "Your blood, your properties, and your honor are sacred to one another like the sanctity of this day of yours, in this (sacred) town (Mecca) of yours, in this month of yours." He called Allah as witness and charged those present to convey his message to those absent (No. 798, Ibn Umar).

**Ka'ba and Ibrahim**: The Prophet told Aisha the Quraysh had shortened the Ka'ba from Ibrahim's original foundations for lack of funds; he would have rebuilt on those foundations had the converts' faith been more established (Nos. 653–656). The Ka'ba is the first house of worship; pre-Islamic custom of naked Tawaf was stopped by the Prophet (No. 689).

### 4. Sawm (Fasting Ramadan) — Bukhari Documentation

*Source: Vol. 3, Book 31 (Nos. 115–225).*

**Fasting belongs uniquely to Allah**: The most theologically significant hadiths are divine speech (*hadith qudsi*) hadiths:

**No. 128** (Abu Huraira):
> "Allah said, 'All the deeds of Adam's sons (people) are for them, except fasting which is for Me, and I will give the reward for it.' Fasting is a shield or protection from the fire and from committing sins... There are two pleasures for the fasting person, one at the time of breaking his fast, and the other at the time when he will meet his Lord."

**No. 118**: "By Him in Whose Hands my soul is, the smell coming out from the mouth of a fasting person is better in the sight of Allah than the smell of musk." The unpleasant breath of fasting is revalorized as a spiritual perfume.

**The gate of Ar-Raiyan**: "There is a gate in Paradise called Ar-Raiyan, and those who observe fasts will enter through it on the Day of Resurrection and none except them will enter through it" (No. 120). After their entry the gate will be closed permanently.

**Cosmic conditions during Ramadan**: "When the month of Ramadan starts, the gates of the heaven are opened and the gates of Hell are closed and the devils are chained" (No. 123).

**Moon-sighting determines Ramadan**: "When you see the crescent (of Ramadan), start fasting, and when you see the crescent (of Shawwal), stop fasting; and if the sky is overcast then regard the crescent (month) of Ramadan (as of 30 days)" (No. 124). The month may be 29 or 30 days (Nos. 131–136).

**What breaks the fast**: Sexual intercourse (expiation required: free a slave, OR fast two successive months, OR feed 60 poor persons; No. 156–158). Eating/drinking forgetfully does NOT break the fast (No. 154). Breaking immediately at sunset is encouraged (Nos. 175–177); hastening the break is a sign of keeping "on the right path" (No. 178).

**Moral dimension**: "Whoever does not give up forged speech and evil actions, Allah is not in need of his leaving his food and drink" (No. 127). Fasting without moral reformation of speech and conduct is theologically void.

**The 'Ashura fast**: Originally observed by pre-Islamic Quraysh and the Prophet when he arrived in Medina; the Prophet found Medina Jews fasting on 'Ashura and ordered Muslims to fast, saying "We have more claim over Moses than you" (No. 222). When Ramadan was prescribed, 'Ashura became optional (Nos. 116–117, 219–221).
