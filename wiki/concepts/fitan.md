# Fitan (Tribulations and End-Times Signs)

*Fitan* (Arabic: فتن — singular *fitna*, plural *fitan*) means "trials," "tribulations," or "temptations." In Islamic eschatology it designates both (a) the civil wars and moral corruption that will afflict the Muslim community before the Last Hour, and (b) the major signs of the Hour itself. Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 9, Book 88 is the dedicated *Kitab al-Fitan wa Ashrat al-Sa'a* (Book of Tribulations and Signs of the Hour).

## Sources Ingested

- Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 9, Book 88 (Fitan), Nos. 181–268 (approximate) — ingest 2026-06-27

## The Eleven Signs of the Last Hour (No. 237)

The Prophet listed eleven signs before the Last Hour occurs (narrated by Hudhaifa bin Usaid, with several Companions as witnesses):

1. **The Smoke** (*al-dukhan*)
2. **The Dajjal** (the Anti-Christ)
3. **The Beast** (*Dabbat al-Ard*)
4. **The Rising of the Sun from the West** — Q 6:158 cited: "On the day when some of the signs of your Lord will come, no good will it do to a person to believe then"
5. **The descent of Jesus, son of Mary**
6. **Gog and Magog** (*Ya'juj wa Ma'juj*)
7. **Three landslides** (*khasf*): one in the East, one in the West, one in Arabia
8. **A fire driving people to their place of assembly**

(The text numbers eleven total but the exact list in No. 237 includes some overlap with the above.) Critically, **the rising of the sun from the West closes the door of repentance** — once that sign appears, no new belief or repentance is accepted.

## The Dajjal (Anti-Christ)

Bukhari's most detailed descriptions of the Dajjal:
- **One-eyed**: "His right eye will be blind and his eye looks like a bulging out grape" (No. 256, Ibn Umar)
- **Kafir written on his forehead**: "Written between his eyes is *Kafir*" — every believer, literate or not, will be able to read it (No. 260, Anas)
- **Can seemingly kill and revive**: performs a miracle of apparent death and resurrection of a young man, but is stopped the second time (several hadiths)
- **Restricted from Mecca and Medina**: Medina is protected by angles from both plague and the Dajjal (Nos. 201, 246)
- **Appears at Isfahan with Jewish followers**: narrated in the hadiths though varying
- **Physical contrast with Jesus**: In the Ka'ba dream vision (Book 55 No. 649), the Dajjal is described as "very curly-haired, blind in the right eye," whereas Jesus has brown complexion and lank hair — a deliberate visual opposition

## Internal Fitna: Umar as the "Door"

A politically significant hadith (No. 216, Hudhaifa): the Prophet described tribulations flowing like waves toward the Muslim community. Umar ibn al-Khattab stood as "a door" — as long as the door stood, the waves were held back. When Hudhaifa asked whether the door would be opened or broken, the Prophet replied "broken." Hudhaifa understood: once Umar dies, the door to fitna cannot be closed again.

This hadith elevates Umar's caliphate as the barrier against civil war, and implicitly explains the fitna that followed his assassination as the inevitable breaking of the prophetically sealed door. See [[umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]].

## Afflictions from the East

"Afflictions and troubles will appear from the East… from the direction whence the horn of Satan appears" (Nos. 214, 219, Anas and Ibn Umar variants). Najd (the central Arabian plateau) is specifically named in some versions. This hadith has been interpreted historically as referring to the Kharijite movement (which began in Iraq/Persia, east of Medina) and in modern times has been applied in various polemical contexts.

## Euphrates Gold Mountain Prophecy

"The Hour will not come until the Euphrates will disclose a treasure of gold… whoever is present will not take anything from it." The prophecy of a gold mountain emerging from the Euphrates, causing wars over it that kill nine-tenths of the fighters present (Nos. 255–257, Abu Huraira). This remains a widely cited end-times sign in Sunni popular eschatology.

## Fire from the Hijaz

"The Hour will not be established until a fire will come out of the land of Hijaz, and it will throw light on the necks of the camels at Busra." (No. 187, Abu Huraira) — a fire eruption from Arabia visible from Syria. Some scholars have applied this to historical volcanic eruptions in the Hijaz.

## Gog and Magog (Ya'juj wa Ma'juj)

The Bukhari Fitan book contains primarily the "gap in the dam" hadiths: the Prophet said Gog and Magog dig at the dam of Dhul-Qarnayn each day until they can see daylight through it, then stop and say "we will continue tomorrow" — but Allah causes the dam to close overnight. When Allah permits their release, they will say "if Allah wills" and complete the breakthrough. Their full eschatological narrative (flooding the earth, causing chaos) is referenced but not narrated in Bukhari's Fitan book.

## Signs Not in Bukhari

Notably absent from Bukhari's Fitan book (or found only in hadiths from other collections):
- **The Mahdi**: No hadith specifically naming or describing the Mahdi appears in Bukhari's Fitan section — a significant gap given the Mahdi's prominence in later Islamic eschatology
- **Jesus killing the Dajjal at the White Minaret of Damascus**: This scenario appears in Sahih Muslim but not in Bukhari's Fitan book
- **Smoke (*dukhan*)**: Listed in No. 237 but no descriptive hadith develops it in this book

The Mahdi's absence from Bukhari is a point of debate: Sunni scholars note that the Mahdi hadiths are predominantly in Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, and Ibn Majah, not Bukhari or Muslim.

## Apostasy Death Penalty and Caliphate Rules (Book 84 / 89)

Closely connected to the Fitan material:
- The death penalty for apostasy is stated twice: "Whoever changed his Islamic religion, then kill him" (Book 84, No. 57, Ibn Abbas quoting the Prophet); confirmed by Mu'adh's refusal to sit until a Jewish apostate was executed (No. 58)
- The caliphate must remain with the Quraish tribe (Book 89) — a political ruling that bears on legitimacy debates in the succession
- Obedience to rulers is required "as long as they do not order you to commit a sin" (Book 89)
