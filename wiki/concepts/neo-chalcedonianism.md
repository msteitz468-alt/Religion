# Neo-Chalcedonianism

[[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Pelikan's]] term (Vol. I, ch. 5) for the sixth-century interpretation that read the [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedonian Definition]] through a thoroughly **[[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyrilline]]** lens — affirming the two natures while insisting, against any [[nestorianism|Nestorian]]-friendly reading, that the one *hypostasis* is the divine Word and that "one of the Trinity [[theopaschism|suffered in the flesh]]." It is the real doctrinal outcome of the century between Chalcedon (451) and Constantinople II (553): "not Nestorian, not Eutychian, but Neo-Cyrillian."

## Background

Pelikan stresses that Chalcedon "settled very little in the East," providing "the terms for subsequent controversies rather than the solution," and that the post-Chalcedonian conflicts "reopened the trinitarian question" — "the controversy had come full circle."

- **Zeno's Henotikon (482)** — an imperial "act of union" making the Nicene faith (as read at Ephesus, with [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril's]] anathemas) the only binding standard and sidelining Chalcedon; it "failed to appease the Monophysites but managed to precipitate a schism with Rome."
- **The Three Chapters** — [[justinian|Justinian's]] condemnation (544–553) of three Antiochene-leaning items (the person and works of [[theodore-of-mopsuestia|Theodore of Mopsuestia]]; [[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret's]] writings against Cyril; the letter of Ibas of Edessa), whose exoneration at Chalcedon had licensed a "mediating" reading "soft on Nestorianism."
- **Constantinople II (553)** ratified the Cyrilline reading, condemned the Three Chapters (and Origen), and vindicated Chalcedon — but "the christological problem was not settled… much more effectively than it had been at Chalcedon," as the seventh-century monothelite controversy soon showed.

The technical defence of the Neo-Chalcedonian position fell to [[leontius-of-byzantium|Leontius of Byzantium]] and [[leontius-of-jerusalem|Leontius of Jerusalem]] via the [[enhypostaton|enhypostatos]], and culminated in [[maximus-the-confessor|Maximus the Confessor]].
