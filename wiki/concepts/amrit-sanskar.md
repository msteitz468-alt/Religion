*Amrit sanskar* (also called *khande di pahul*, "initiation by the double-edged sword") is the rite of [[khalsa|Khalsa]] initiation. Per [[mcleod-sikhism|McLeod]], among all Sikh life-cycle rites, the rite of Khalsa initiation is the one over which "Tat Khalsa influence … has been complete" — it "certainly retained its existing format, though numerous details were added" over time, and it "demonstrates Tat Khalsa influence at its greatest" (McLeod ~L391–398).

## Eligibility

Khalsa initiation "is open to anyone who lives a worthy life and who affirms belief in the principles represented by the Khalsa. It is not limited to Punjabis" (~L291–293). Those to be initiated must be "old enough to understand the meaning of the ceremony."

## Requirements for Officiants

The ceremony requires at minimum an open copy of the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] and at least seven Amrit-dhari Sikhs, each wearing the Five Ks. Of the seven: one sits with the Guru Granth Sahib; one stands at the door; and the remaining five — the *Panj Piare* ("Five Beloved") — administer the initiation. Men or women may serve in either capacity, though in practice responsibility is "almost always assumed by males" (~L295–300). The five officiants must be physically sound (possessing both eyes, ears, legs, and arms), free from chronic diseases, and must not have been convicted of transgressing the Rahit (~L301–307).

Prior to the ceremony, initiants and officiants should bathe, wash their hair, and appear wearing the Five Ks. No symbols of any other religion may be worn; ear-rings and nose ornaments are not acceptable (~L309–312).

## Preparation of Amrit

The officiants place a large iron bowl on a pedestal, pour in fresh water, and add soluble sweets. They then adopt the "heroic posture" (*bir asari*) — kneeling with the right knee on the ground and the left knee upright. The following compositions are then recited: *Japji*, *Jap*, the Ten *Savayyds*, *Benati Chaupai*, and the six prescribed stanzas from *Anand Sahib*. The reciting officiant keeps his left hand on the rim of the bowl and uses his right hand to stir the *amrit* with a *khanda* (two-edged sword). The other four keep both hands on the bowl, eyes fixed on the *amrit* (~L320–337). One of the officiants then recites *Ardas*.

## The Rite Itself

Candidates adopt the "heroic posture" and cup their hands, right hand over left. Five times a handful of *amrit* is given and drunk; each time the officiant calls out *"Vahiguru ji ka Khalsa! Vahiguru ji ki fateh!"* and the candidate replies in kind (~L343–346). The candidate's eyes are then touched with *amrit* five times and five times it is sprinkled on his/her hair, each time with the same exchange of the greeting. Any remaining *amrit* is consumed by all candidates drinking from the same vessel in turn (~L352–356).

The five officiants then impart the Name of *Vahiguru* by saying in unison the Basic Credal Statement — the *Mul Mantra* — and requiring the initiates to repeat it after them (~L357–360).

## Instruction in the Rahit

One of the Panj Piare expounds to the initiates the meaning of the Rahit. This includes:

1. A promise always to keep the **Five Ks**.
2. Scrupulous avoidance of the **four cardinal prohibitions** (*kurahit*):
   1. Cutting one's hair or having it cut.
   2. Consuming meat slaughtered according to the Muslim rite (*halal* meat, or *kuttha*).
   3. Extra-marital sexual intercourse.
   4. Using tobacco.

Initiants are also urged to have no dealings with initiated Sikhs who cut their hair or smoke; to support the Panth always; to set aside one tenth of their earnings (*das-vandh*) for the Guru's service; and to avoid the Five Reprobate Groups believed to have been denounced by Guru Gobind Singh (~L371–381).

A list of offences against the Rahit that warrant penance (*tanakhah*) is specified; these include: eating from the same dish as a non-initiated or apostate Sikh (*patit*); dyeing one's beard; giving or receiving a cash dowry; and consuming any drug or intoxicant (~L379–381).

## Conclusion

One of the Panj Piare recites *Ardas*; a sixth member sitting with the Guru Granth Sahib takes a *hukam*. Any newly initiated Sikh without a Guru Granth Sahib–derived name is renamed. The ceremony concludes with the distribution of *karah prasad*, all newly initiated receiving from the same dish (~L382–388).

## Penance and Re-initiation

An offence against the Rahit by an Amrit-dhari Sikh is called a *tanakhah*; the offender is a *tanakhdhi*. The local *sangat*, convened before the Guru Granth Sahib, chooses five Panj Piare to investigate and recommend penance — typically manual service to the *sangat* (sweeping the gurdwara, cleaning shoes, washing utensils in the langar). After penance, *Ardas* is recited. A Sikh branded *patit* (apostate) for committing a *kurahit* may confess, promise to desist, receive a penance, and then "seek Khalsa initiation again" (~L659–690).
