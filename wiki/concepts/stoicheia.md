# *Stoicheia* (Elemental Forces/Spirits)

"See to it that no one takes you captive through hollow and deceptive philosophy, which depends on human tradition and the *elemental spiritual forces* (*stoicheia tou kosmou*) of this world rather than on Christ." (Col 2:8)

"Since you died with Christ to the *elemental spiritual forces* (*stoicheia tou kosmou*) of this world, why, as though you still belonged to the world, do you submit to its rules?" (Col 2:20)

The term *stoicheia* (singular: *stoicheion*) is one of the most contested in Pauline studies. It appears in Col 2:8, 20 and in Gal 4:3, 9, and the disputed Heb 5:12. Its meaning affects how one reads both the Colossian opponents and Paul's understanding of the cosmos.

## The Term's Semantic Range

*Stoicheion* in Greek literature refers to:

1. **The letters of the alphabet** (*grammata*) or the basic building blocks of speech/language
2. **The elements of the physical world**: earth, air, fire, water — the four classical elements of Greek cosmology (Philo, *Questions on Genesis* 1.1; Wis 7:17; 19:18)
3. **Rudimentary principles** or basic teachings (Heb 5:12: "the *stoicheia* of the beginning of God's oracle" = elementary doctrine; 2 Pet 3:10, 12: the *stoicheia* dissolved by fire at the eschaton)
4. **Elemental spirits or personal cosmic beings** — this meaning is attested in some magical papyri and late texts but is disputed as a first-century reading

## The Pauline Occurrences

**Galatians 4:3, 9**:
- "So also, when we were underage, we were in slavery under the *stoicheia tou kosmou*" (4:3)
- "But now that you know God—or rather are known by God—how is it that you are turning back to those weak and miserable *stoicheia*? Do you wish to be enslaved by them all over again?" (4:9)

In Galatians, the *stoicheia* represent the pre-Christian condition of both Jews (under the law) and Gentiles (under pagan powers). Paul associates them with "guardians and trustees" (4:2) who administered human life until the fullness of time. The Galatian converts' return to "observing special days and months and seasons and years" (4:10) is a return to the *stoicheia*.

### Moo on Galatians 4:3, 9 (BECNT, 2013)

[[douglas-moo|Moo]]'s preferred meaning of τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου is the **basic material elements of the universe** (dominant lexical sense), which the ancient worldview linked with deities/spirits — so a reference to material elements "would almost certainly include some reference to the deities or spirits" (raw L3826; [[moo-galatians-becnt|source]]). This fits the agitators' focus on circumcision, food, and calendrical observances as religious regimens tied to material realities.

Critically, Moo **denies equating the law with the *stoicheia*** or treating the law as a subset of them: "Paul does not equate the law with the στοιχεῖα; nor does he even suggest that the law is a subset of the στοιχεῖα" (L3828). Rather he **associates** Jews under law and Gentiles under *stoicheia* as both under outmoded religious regimens — without denying the law's divine origin. The older wiki summary that "Paul equates them with" guardians/Torah as elementary slavery overstates the equation; Moo's reading is association of parallel slaveries, not identity of law and *stoicheia*.

**Colossians 2:8, 20**:
In the Colossian context, the *stoicheia* are associated with:
- The "Colossian philosophy" (2:8)
- Human tradition
- Food regulations ("do not handle, do not taste, do not touch," 2:21–22)
- Calendar observance (festivals, new moons, Sabbath, 2:16)
- The ascetic-mystical program of the halakic mystics (2:18, 23)

After the cross and resurrection, believers have *died* to the *stoicheia* (2:20) — they no longer belong to the world system the *stoicheia* govern.

## Interpretive Positions

### 1. Physical Elements (Earth, Air, Fire, Water)

The classical meaning: the *stoicheia* are the constituent elements of the physical world. Submitting to food laws and calendar practices is submitting to the material, physical order as though it retained post-resurrection power over the believer. This reading was common in older scholarship (Bornkamm) and has recent advocates (Rusam).

Problem: this reading struggles to explain why Paul treats them as quasi-personal powers that hold humanity in "slavery" (Gal 4:3).

### 2. Elemental Spirits / Personal Cosmic Beings

The *stoicheia* are personal spirit-beings who govern the natural order and human life — planet-gods, fate-powers, angelic administrators of the cosmos. This reading makes the *stoicheia* synonymous with or subordinate to the [[principalities-powers|principalities and powers]]. The Galatian Torah (law of Moses) and Gentile pagan practices are both forms of submission to these cosmic governors.

This is the interpretation assumed in most NIV/NRSV translations ("elemental spiritual forces") and in many popular commentaries. Arnold (*Colossian Syncretism*, 1995) supports this reading for Colossians, connecting the *stoicheia* to the Phrygian spirit-powers that the opponents' ascetic program was designed to overcome or appease.

### 3. Rudimentary Principles of Religion (Religious ABCs)

The *stoicheia* are the elementary principles or "ABCs" of religion — the basic, preparatory religious regulations (dietary, calendrical, ritual) that marked pre-Christian existence whether Jewish or pagan. They are not personal beings but a *system* — the elementary level of religious life transcended in Christ.

This reading (Betz on Galatians; partly Dunn) emphasizes the parallel between Jewish Torah practice and Gentile pagan practice as forms of the same elementary-level religious bondage.

### 4. Powers as Polluted Structures (McKnight)

[[scot-mcknight|McKnight]]'s reading for Colossians draws on his "polluted structures" understanding of the powers (see [[principalities-powers|Principalities and Powers]]). The *stoicheia* are *worldly structures* — the systemic, sociopolitical forms through which cosmic/angelic rebellion operates. They are neither purely physical elements nor purely personal beings but the institutional-structural manifestations of a cosmos in rebellion against Christ.

On this reading, the halakic mystics' program (food laws, calendar, ascetic visionary practice) belongs to the *stoicheia* because it is a form of participation in the world's structures of religious power and achievement — however Jewish its vocabulary — rather than in the defeated powers of Christ's cross. The Colossians, having *died with Christ* (2:20), have died to those structures.

Key: McKnight connects 2:20 to the baptismal theology of 2:12 — in baptism, believers co-died with Christ, and that death severs their relationship to the *stoicheia* just as death severs any social relationship.

## Significance for the Colossian Letter

The *stoicheia* appear at the structural hinges of the letter's anti-heretical argument:

- 2:8: Warning against the philosophy that serves the *stoicheia* rather than Christ
- 2:15: At the cross, God triumphed over the rulers and authorities (related beings)
- 2:20: Death with Christ = death to the *stoicheia*; therefore do not submit to their rules

Paul's logic: the *stoicheia* represent a form of the world's power over human life. Christ's cross is the definitive break with that power. Submitting to the halakic mystics' program would be a return to *stoicheia*-level existence — a betrayal of the new-creation death and resurrection that baptism enacted.

## Galatians Connection

McKnight draws an explicit connection between Colossians and Galatians 4: "We are thus drawn into the orbit of Gal 4:9–10 because (1) the same laws are mentioned and (2) the stoicheia are also mentioned there and shortly in our text at Col 2:20. The supposed distance of the opponents at Galatia and the opponents at Colossae has now dwindled to a common glance across the street." Both letters present the *stoicheia* as elementary powers that Christ's death has transcended; both criticize the imposition of Torah-based practices on Gentile believers as a regression.
