# Ahura Mazda ("the Wise Lord")

**Ahura Mazda** (Avestan, "Lord Wisdom" / "the Wise Lord"; Middle Persian **Ohrmazd**) is the
supreme God of [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]] — the **one uncreated, wholly good Creator**,
existing eternally and the source of all that is good, including every other beneficent divinity.
Worship of him is *Mazdayasna*, "the worship of Mazda," the religion's name for itself.

## Origin and the Indo-Iranian Background

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], the title *ahura* (Vedic *asura*, "lord")
was borne in the old Indo-Iranian religion by the ethical sovereign-gods, chiefly **Mithra** (lord
of the covenant) and **[[varuna|Varuna]]** (Apam Napat, "Son of the Waters"), the guardians of
**[[asha-druj|asha]]** (Vedic *rta*, cosmic-moral order). Against these stood the amoral
warrior-god **Indra**. [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]], himself a priest of this tradition, took the
"abstract" Ahuric strand to its conclusion: meditating (Boyce argues) on the daily *yasna* and its
doctrine of one original plant, animal, and man, he apprehended a parallel **uniqueness in the
divine sphere** — in the beginning a single beneficent Being, **Ahura Mazda**, the all-wise and
wholly just, from whom all other divine beings emanate.

## Theology

- **Sole good Creator.** Ahura Mazda creates first in the immaterial (*menog*) and then the
  material (*getig*) state — the act of creation called *Bundahishn* (see [[the-three-times|the
  Three Times]]). Only what is good is ascribed to him.
- **Through his Holy Spirit, Spenta Mainyu**, he evokes the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]] (the
  six "Holy Immortals"), forming a **heptad** with himself; through them he creates and sustains
  the world, lesser divinities (the *yazatas*), and humankind.
- **The Adversary.** Co-existing with him from the first, but **not created by him**, is the
  Hostile Spirit, [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] — "equally uncreated, but ignorant and wholly
  malign." This is the root of Zoroastrian **cosmic dualism**: good and evil are two independent
  principles, not two faces of one God.
- **Not omnipotent in the present, but certain to win.** Ahura Mazda foresees that creating the
  good *getig* world makes it a battleground, but knows that **in the end he will destroy evil**
  and achieve a universe wholly good forever — the [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]].

## "Monotheism," Dualism, and the Theodicy

Boyce stresses that Zoroastrian dualism is precisely what lets the faith hold a **wholly good God**
without imputing evil and suffering to his will: pain and wickedness come from the independent
Hostile Spirit, not from Ahura Mazda. Whether the religion is best called **dualist** or a
qualified **monotheism** is a standing classification dispute — the good God is supreme, sole
Creator, and assured of final victory, yet evil is a genuinely separate uncreated power. The
heterodox current [[zurvanism|Zurvanism]] tried to resolve the tension by making **Zurvan** (Time)
the father of *both* Ohrmazd and Ahriman — softening the dualism, and condemned for it.

## Significance and Influence

Ahura Mazda is the prime candidate antecedent for the structured opposition of a good God and a
ranked host against an evil adversary that later pervades Abrahamic angelology and demonology —
recorded, without overclaiming, on
