# Tawhid

**Tawhid** (توحيد, "making one, declaring oneness") is the cardinal doctrine of [[islam|Islam]]: the
absolute oneness, uniqueness, and transcendence of God (*Allah*). It is the content of the first half
of the *shahada* ("there is no god but God") and the theme of Sura 112 (*al-Ikhlas*): "Say: He is God,
the One… He begets not, nor is He begotten, and there is none comparable unto Him."

## Meaning

Tawhid asserts that God is numerically one, without partner (*shirk* — associating partners with God —
is the one unforgivable sin), without parts, and without likeness to creation (*tanzih*,
transcendence). God's unity is the measure against which every other doctrine is judged.

## Cross-Tradition Significance

Tawhid is the sharpest point of contact and conflict with the other monotheisms:

- **vs. Christianity** — the Qur'an explicitly rejects the **Trinity** and the divine sonship of
  Christ as compromising God's oneness (Q 4:171 "say not 'Three'"; Q 5:72–75; Q 112). This sets
  tawhid directly against [[logos-christology|Logos Christology]] and the Nicene *homoousios*
  ([[arianism|Arianism]] is the Christian sub-tradition closest to the tawhid intuition).
- **vs. Judaism** — strong structural affinity with the **[[shema|Shema]]** ("Hear, O Israel: the
  LORD our God, the LORD is one") and with [[maimonides|Maimonidean]] negative theology and the
  rejection of corporeality.
- **vs. Kabbalah** — contrast with the [[sefirot|sefirotic]] differentiation within the divine, which
  a tawhid-based critique would read as compromising oneness (mirroring the internal Jewish tension
  between [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] and rationalist monotheism).
