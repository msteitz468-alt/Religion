# Tawhid

**Tawhid** (توحيد, "making one, declaring oneness") is the cardinal doctrine of [[islam|Islam]]: the
absolute oneness, uniqueness, and transcendence of God (*Allah*). It is the content of the first half
of the *shahada* ("there is no god but God") and the theme of Sura 112 (*al-Ikhlas*): "Say: He is God,
the One… He begets not, nor is He begotten, and there is none comparable unto Him."

## Meaning

Tawhid asserts that God is numerically one, without partner (*shirk* — associating partners with God —
is the one unforgivable sin), without parts, and without likeness to creation (*tanzih*,
transcendence). God's unity is the measure against which every other doctrine is judged.

## Cross-Tradition Significance

Tawhid is the sharpest point of contact and conflict with the other monotheisms:

- **vs. Christianity** — the Qur'an explicitly rejects the **Trinity** and the divine sonship of
  Christ as compromising God's oneness (Q 4:171 "say not 'Three'"; Q 5:72–75; Q 112). This sets
  tawhid directly against [[logos-christology|Logos Christology]] and the Nicene *homoousios*
  ([[arianism|Arianism]] is the Christian sub-tradition closest to the tawhid intuition).
- **vs. Judaism** — strong structural affinity with the **[[shema|Shema]]** ("Hear, O Israel: the
  LORD our God, the LORD is one") and with [[maimonides|Maimonidean]] negative theology and the
  rejection of corporeality.
- **vs. Kabbalah** — contrast with the [[sefirot|sefirotic]] differentiation within the divine, which
  a tawhid-based critique would read as compromising oneness (mirroring the internal Jewish tension
  between [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] and rationalist monotheism).

## Tawhid as Hermeneutic

Beyond doctrine, *tawhid* has been turned into a **method of reading**. [[amina-wadud|Amina Wadud]]
proposes a "**hermeneutics of tawhid**" ([[wadud-quran-and-woman|*Qur'an and Woman*]]): because God
is one, the [[quran|Qur'an]] is a *coherent unity*, so its parts must be read in the light of the
whole rather than atomistically verse-by-verse (see [[tafsir|holistic vs. atomistic tafsir]]). She
also reads the **phenomenon of pairs (*zawj*)** in creation as itself a sign of tawhid: every created
thing is paired, therefore the uncreated Creator alone is unpaired and One (Q 42:11, "nothing is like
Him"). This grounds her egalitarian reading of human creation (see [[adam|Adam, Islamic]]) and her
critique of male-hierarchical exegesis ([[qiwama|qiwama]], [[quran-4-34-male-authority|Q 4:34]]).

## Tawhid in Sahih al-Bukhari (Book 93)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 9, Book 93 (Kitab al-Tawhid), Nos. 469–652. M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

Book 93 is the theological crown of the entire Bukhari collection, positioned last to bracket the work alongside Book 2 (*Kitab al-Iman*). It is the primary Sunni hadith source for **divine attributes** (*sifat*), the **99 Names**, the **vision of Allah in Paradise**, and the definition of **shirk**.

### Tawhid as the Foundation of Mission

The Prophet's instruction to Mu'adh going to Yemen: "Let the first thing to which you will invite them be the Tauhid of Allah." Only after they affirm tawhid: the five prayers, then Zakat (No. 469). Tawhid is the prerequisite for all other Islamic practice. A striking corollary appears at No. 579 (Abu Dharr): "Anyone who died without worshipping anything besides Allah would enter Paradise" — even if he committed theft or adultery. Non-shirk, not moral perfection, is the criterion for salvation.

### The 99 Names of Allah

"Allah has ninety-nine Names, one-hundred less one; and he who memorized them all by heart will enter Paradise. To count something means to know it by heart." (No. 489, Abu Huraira). Bukhari includes the promise but **no enumerated list of the 99 names** — the canonical list derives from other hadith collections (Tirmidhi). "Memorizing" is glossed as knowing them by heart.

### Divine Attributes (Sifat) — Anthropomorphic Passages

Book 93 contains the densest cluster of hadith affirming anthropomorphic divine attributes without accompanying *ta'wil* (allegorical interpretation). Bukhari's editorial strategy is to record these hadiths without interpretation, a practice associated with the Hanbali/Athari approach (*bila kayf* — affirming without asking "how"). These passages are major sites of theological controversy between Athari, Ash'ari, and Mu'tazilite positions.

**Hand(s):** "Allah's Hand is full, and its fullness is not affected by the continuous spending, day and night… His Throne is over the water and in His other Hand is the balance (of Justice)" (No. 508). "On the Day of Resurrection, Allah will grasp the whole Earth by His Hand, and all the Heavens in His right" (No. 509/479).

**Shin (*saq*):** On the Day of Resurrection, believers are tested by Allah appearing "in a shape other than the one which they saw the first time." When they deny Him, "Allah will then uncover His Shin whereupon every believer will prostrate before Him." Hypocrites cannot prostrate (No. 532s, Abu Said). This is the most striking anthropomorphic passage — a key proof-text for the Athari school and a crux for Ash'ari *ta'wil* (interpreting "Shin" as a divine act of revelation, not a literal limb).

**Foot:** "Allah will put His Foot over [Hell] and it will say, 'Qad! Qad! (enough! enough!)'" (Nos. 481, 541, Anas). Hell, ever demanding more, is finally silenced by Allah's Foot.

**Laughter:** Allah "laughs" at a man who fights in His cause wearing only half a coat of armor; "Allah laughed at two men, one of whom killed the other, yet both of them will enter Paradise" (No. 513 area). The divine laughter passages affirm emotional responsiveness in Allah.

**Descent (*nuzul*):** "Our Lord descends to the lowest heaven each night, when one-third of the night remains, and says: Who will call on Me that I may answer him?" (referenced in context of the night prayer's virtue — not explicitly in Book 93 but throughout the collection). Book 93 affirms the principle of divine responsiveness to du'a.

### The Vision of Allah in Paradise (Ru'yat Allah)

The eschatological vision of Allah is the most beloved gift of Paradise — affirmed in Bukhari by the "full moon" analogy:

> "You will see your Lord with your own eyes as you see the moon on the night of a full moon clearly without any trouble in seeing it." (No. 529, Abu Said; No. 531, Jarir; similar: No. 532c)

This vision is the supreme reward, surpassing all material goods of Paradise. The full-moon analogy affirms both the reality and the clarity of the vision.

**Aisha's counter-position (preserved in context, No. 477):** "If anyone tells you that Muhammad has seen his Lord, he is a liar, for Allah says: 'No vision can grasp Him.'" Aisha's denial concerns the Prophet's vision *in this world* (including during the Mi'raj) — not the eschatological vision in Paradise. Bukhari places her hadith immediately before the Paradise-vision hadiths, creating an implicit disambiguation: denying Muhammad saw Allah in his lifetime is compatible with affirming believers will see Allah in Paradise.

### Allah as Time (*al-Dahr*)

"Allah said: The son of Adam hurts Me by abusing Time, for **I am Time** (*al-Dahr*); in My Hands are all things and I cause the revolution of night and day." (No. 583, Abu Huraira). This hadith prohibits cursing Time because Allah identifies Himself with Time. Theologians debated whether this is a literal identification (implying Allah's identity with temporal flow) or a statement of His sovereignty over time. Bukhari includes it without commentary.

### Shirk (Polytheism) as the Worst Sin

"What is the greatest sin?" — "To set up rivals unto Allah while He alone created you" (No. 611, Abdullah, citing the Prophet). This is the Islamic analogue to the first commandment's prohibition on other gods: shirk's gravity lies in falsifying creation's fundamental relation to its Creator. "Never will Allah forgive the setting up of rivals unto Him" (Q 4:48) is the doctrinal statement behind these hadiths.

### The Final Hadith (No. 652)

The entire Bukhari collection ends:

> "Two words which are dear to the Beneficent (Allah) and very light (easy) for the tongue (to say), but very heavy in weight in the balance. They are: **'Subhan Allah wa-bi hamdihi'** and **'Subhan Allah Al-Azim.'"** (Abu Huraira)

The collection that begins with the intention (*niyyah*) hadith closes with the glorification (*tasbih*) of Allah — framing the entire work as a movement from human intention to divine praise.

### Theological Significance: The Athari Position

Bukhari's practice of recording the anthropomorphic attributes *without ta'wil* (interpretation) has been read as an implicit endorsement of the **Athari/Hanbali** position: affirm the attributes as transmitted, without asking "how" (*bila kayf*), and without negating what Allah affirmed of Himself. Ibn Taymiyya and later Salafi/Wahhabi interpreters draw heavily on Bukhari's Book 93. Ash'ari and Maturidi theologians, by contrast, provide allegorical interpretations while accepting the hadiths as authentic. The book thus serves as a primary proof-text in one of Sunni Islam's most persistent internal debates.
