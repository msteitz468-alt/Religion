# Isra and Mi'raj (Night Journey and Ascent)

The **Isra and Mi'raj** (Arabic: الإسراء والمعراج) are two successive events: the *Isra*
(Night Journey) from Mecca to Jerusalem, and the *Mi'raj* (Ascent) from Jerusalem through the
seven heavens to the divine presence. They are narrated in multiple hadiths in [[sahih-al-bukhari]]
and are the Quranic reference point of Q 17:1 ("Glory to Him Who carried His servant by night from
the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque").

---

## The Full Account (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 54, No. 429)

*Narrated Malik bin Sasaa; source: Vol. 4, Book 54, No. 429. M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Al-Buraq and the Journey to Jerusalem

- The Prophet was taken on **Al-Buraq** ("a white animal, smaller than a mule and bigger than a
  donkey"), which "placed its hoof at a distance equal to the range of vision."
- The Prophet was brought to **Bayt al-Maqdis** (Jerusalem / the Farthest Mosque) and tethered
  the Buraq to the ring by the door where the prophets had tethered their animals.
- He led the assembled prophets in prayer — two rak'at.

### The Seven Heavens — Prophets Encountered

Gabriel guided the Prophet upward through seven heavens. At each gate, a guardian angel asked
who approached; Gabriel identified himself and the Prophet; the guardian asked whether the
Prophet had been sent for (*bu'itha ilayhi*); on confirmation, the gate opened with welcome.

| Heaven | Prophet Encountered | Greeting |
|--------|-------------------|---------|
| First | **Adam** | "Welcome O son and a Prophet" |
| Second | **Jesus and Yahya (John the Baptist)** — they are cousins | "Welcome O brother and a Prophet" |
| Third | **Joseph (Yusuf)** | "Welcome O brother and a Prophet" |
| Fourth | **Idris** | "Welcome O brother and a Prophet" |
| Fifth | **Aaron (Harun)** | "Welcome O brother and a Prophet" |
| Sixth | **Moses (Musa)** | Wept — because Muhammad's followers would enter Paradise in greater numbers than Moses's |
| Seventh | **Abraham (Ibrahim)** | "Welcome O son and a Prophet" — Ibrahim was leaning against Al-Bait al-Mamur |

### Al-Bait al-Mamur and Sidrat al-Muntaha

- **Al-Bait al-Mamur** (the Frequented House) in the seventh heaven: 70,000 angels enter and
  pray there daily; they never return after that day — a fresh 70,000 come the next day.
  Ibrahim was leaning against it.
- **Sidrat al-Muntaha** (the Lote Tree at the Boundary): fruits like the clay jugs of Hajr and
  leaves like elephant ears. At its root, four rivers: two hidden (in Paradise), two apparent —
  the **Nile** and the **Euphrates**.
- At the Sidrat al-Muntaha, the Prophet was offered **wine, milk, and honey**; he chose milk.
  Gabriel said: "This is the fitra" (natural religion of Islam).

### The Reduction of Prayers

Allah originally enjoined **fifty prayers daily**. Moses intercepted the Prophet on his
descent: "Your followers cannot bear fifty prayers a day; I tested the Bani Israel with less."
The Prophet returned to Allah multiple times, reducing the number:

> "My followers cannot carry out fifty prayers; go back to your Lord and request for some reduction."

Each reduction brought the Prophet back to Moses, who urged further reduction. After ten
separate reductions of five prayers each, five remained. Allah's final response:
> "There are five prayers and they are all (equal to) fifty (in reward) for My Word does not
> change." (Hadith No. 345, Book 8, in the parallel account narrated by Abu Dhar)

Moses urged the Prophet to return and ask again; the Prophet said he was too ashamed to ask
for more. The five daily prayers (*salat*) thus acquire a specific narrative grounding: they
were fifty, earned Moses's intercession, and retain the eschatological reward of fifty.

## Cosmological Details

From the Mi'raj account and related hadiths in Book 54:

- **The Throne** (*al-'Arsh*): Allah's Throne was over the water at creation (No. 414). Allah
  wrote on His Throne: "My Mercy overpowers My Anger" (No. 416).
- **Seven earths**: Hadiths confirm seven layers of earth (No. 417–418).
- **The sun's prostration**: The sun prostrates under the Throne each night and asks permission
  to rise; one day its prostration will not be accepted and it will rise in the west (No. 421,
  Q 36:38).
- **Malik**: The gatekeeper of Hell (*Malik*, Q 43:77) was shown to the Prophet on the Night
  Journey.

## Aisha's Counter-Tradition: Muhammad Did Not See Allah

A significant theological point within [[sahih-al-bukhari]] itself concerns whether the Prophet
saw Allah on the Night Journey. **Aisha** explicitly denied this:

> "Whoever claimed that Muhammad saw his Lord, is committing a great fault, for he only saw
> Gabriel in his genuine shape in which he was created covering the whole horizon." (No. 457)

She cites Q 6:103 ("No vision can grasp Him") and Q 42:51 (divine speech is through
revelation, not direct vision). This is an **intra-Sunni controversy**: some other traditions
(not in Bukhari) suggest the Prophet may have seen Allah. Aisha's position is the dominant
Sunni view, but it generated debate.

## Theological Significance

1. **The Mi'raj is the origin of the five daily prayers** — their institution is not arbitrary
   legislation but results from a negotiation between Moses's intercession and divine mercy.
2. **Prophetic continuity**: The Prophet meets and greets all major prophets as "brother" or
   "son," establishing the Islamic doctrine that all prophets are in a single line of
   brotherhood.
3. **Cosmic geography**: The Night Journey grounds the Quran's cosmological references —
   the seven heavens, the Throne, al-Bait al-Mamur, and the Sidrat al-Muntaha are all
   directly experienced rather than merely doctrinal.
4. **Jerusalem's sanctity**: The *Isra* establishes the connection between the Sacred Mosque
   (Mecca) and the Farthest Mosque (Jerusalem), undergirding Islam's claim to the Abrahamic
   holy sites.
