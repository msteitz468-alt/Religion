# The Object of Negation (dgag bya)

The **object of negation** (Tibetan *dgag bya*, "that which is to be negated/refuted") is what the
reasoning establishing **[[sunyata|emptiness]]** denies. Identifying it *precisely* is the central
methodological concern of [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s insight section in the
[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]] (Vol. III) and a hallmark of the [[gelug|Gelug]]
[[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|Prāsaṅgika]] system. The governing analogy: "In order to be
sure that a certain person is not present, you must know the absent person. Likewise… you must
carefully identify the self, or intrinsic nature, that does not exist." [[shantideva|Śāntideva]]:
"Without contacting the entity that is imputed / You will not apprehend the absence of that entity."

## The Twin Errors: Over- and Under-Negation

Mis-identifying the object of negation produces one of two opposite failures:

- **Over-negation** (negating too much) — if you "fail to limit the object of negation and overextend
  your refutation," you "lose confidence in the causal progressions of dependent-arising," falling to
  a **nihilistic extreme**. This is the error Tsong-kha-pa attributes to "most who today claim to
  teach Madhyamaka": holding that anti-*svabhāva* arguments also refute conventional cause-and-effect.
  He likens them to the Lokāyata nihilists.
- **Under-negation** (negating too little) — if you "leave some remainder, failing to refute the
  deepest and most subtle core," you "cling to the idea of real things" and fall to an extreme of
  **true existence (eternalism)**. The error here is targeting only a coarse, constructed object (a
  permanent–unitary–independent self, partless particles, the Sāṃkhya *pradhāna*) — leaving the
  **innate** reifying ignorance that actually binds beings untouched. Candrakīrti's **snake/elephant
  simile**: telling a man afraid of a snake in his wall "there is no elephant here" does not dispel
  his fear.

"It is crucial to identify the object of negation carefully, for if it is not identified, you will
certainly develop either a nihilistic view or an eternalistic view."

## The Actual Object of Negation

The precise target is **intrinsic existence** ([[svabhava|svabhāva]], *rang bzhin*): a thing's
existing **"by way of its own intrinsic character," with "ontological status in and of itself,
without being posited through the force of an awareness."** It is the referent of **innate
ignorance** (not merely the acquired, tenet-driven ignorance of philosophers) — "what binds all
living beings." Negating the referent of innate ignorance cuts all tenet-driven reifications "as
though you cut a tree at its root." The object of negation does **not** include mere existence,
production, or cause-and-effect: "we refute that things exist essentially; we do not refute that eyes
and such are dependently arisen results of karma."

Two further refinements from the same section:
- **"Not found under analysis" ≠ "refuted by reason."** Reason analyzing reality seeks intrinsic
  nature and does not find it; this refutes intrinsic existence, not the thing itself (see
  [[two-truths|the two truths]] for the three criteria of conventional existence).
- **Add the right qualifier.** For anything Mādhyamikas posit conventionally, refutations must carry
  a qualifier ("essentially," "intrinsically," "ultimately"); even definitive sūtras do so. Whether
  to add "ultimately" is *not* what distinguishes Svātantrika from Prāsaṅgika (a common Tibetan
  error Tsong-kha-pa rejects).
