# The Ineffable

In [[abraham-joshua-heschel|Heschel]]'s thought, **the ineffable** names the dimension of
reality whose meaning exceeds all expression and conceptual capture — but is *meaning*, not
its absence. "The ineffable … is a synonym for hidden meaning rather than for absence of
meaning. It stands for a dimension which in the Bible is called [[kavod|glory]]." The sense
of the ineffable is "a sense for transcendence, a sense for the allusiveness of reality to
a super-rational meaning."

## Not Ignorance but Cognitive Insight

Heschel insists the ineffable is "an intellectual endeavor out of the depth of reason; it
is a source of cognitive insight" — not a mere confession of ignorance. It is "shared by
all men" and is the ground on which religious language remains valid against both
rationalist dismissal and literalist reduction. Closely related is his treatment of the
**sense of mystery** as an *ontological* category: "the essential mystery of being as
being," found "in a grain of sand, in an atom, as well as in the stellar space" — not a
gap in knowledge to be filled. Heschel even invokes the contested etymology linking *'olam*
("world") to *'alam* ("to hide"): "The world is itself hiddenness; its essence is a
mystery."

## "The Mystery Is Not God"

Crucially, Heschel refuses to *deify* the ineffable mystery: "God is a mystery, but the
mystery is not God." Behind the mystery is mercy and purpose — "we do not deify the
mystery; we worship Him who in His wisdom surpasses all mysteries." This distinguishes his
position from apophatic mysticisms that might be read as worshipping the mystery itself
(compare [[apophatic-theology]]), and from the claim "God is the great unknown," which he
rejects as self-refuting (it "definitely claim[s] to know" God's eternal unknowability).

## Relation to Other Concepts

The ineffable is disclosed through [[radical-amazement]] and [[awe]] and is the object of
[[depth-theology]]'s inquiry. Its biblical name is the [[kavod|glory]]. Heschel's
adverbial, via-negativa language about God (developed alongside [[maimonides|Maimonides']]
*Guide*) follows from it; see [[divine-pathos]].
