# Iḥsān (إحسان)

**Iḥsān** ("excellence," "beauty," "doing what is good") is the third and highest level of Islamic
religious life in the tripartite framework established by the **Jibrīl hadith** in
[[sahih-muslim|Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]] (Book 1, Ch. 1, Nos. 0001–0006). It designates the quality of
worship performed with full awareness of divine presence, transcending mere compliance with
outward law ([[five-pillars|al-Islām]]) and interior doctrinal belief ([[iman|Īmān]]).

## The Jibrīl Hadith Definition

In the foundational Jibrīl hadith (No. 0001), after defining Islām (external acts) and Īmān
(articles of belief), the angel asks: *"Tell me about Iḥsān."* The Prophet replies:

> "That you worship Allah as if you are seeing Him, for though you don't see Him, He, verily,
> sees you." (Ch. 1, No. 0001)

A variant formulation in No. 0006 substitutes *fear* for *worship*: "that you **fear** Allah as
if you are seeing Him" — differing in mood but not in structure. Both versions describe a
subjective state of consciousness rather than a set of prescribed acts.

## Structure: Three Levels of the Religion

The Jibrīl hadith positions Iḥsān as the apex of a three-level hierarchy:

| Level | Term | Scope | Defined as |
|-------|------|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **Al-Islām** | Outward practice | The Five Pillars |
| 2 | **Al-Īmān** | Interior belief | The six articles of faith |
| 3 | **Al-Iḥsān** | Quality of consciousness | Worshipping as if seeing Allah |

The three terms name the same religion viewed from different angles — not three separate religions
or three separate obligations. Every Muslim performs Islām; the goal of Īmān is to make those acts
interior; the goal of Iḥsān is to perform them with unbroken divine awareness.

## Theological Significance

The definition of Iḥsān in the Jibrīl hadith became foundational for **Sufi** and mystical
traditions within Islam ([[sufism|Sufism]]), which interpreted it as mandating *murāqaba*
(constant watchfulness of the divine presence) as the method of spiritual transformation.
Mainstream Sunni theology holds Iḥsān as an aspiration describing the highest quality of
worship rather than a legally separate obligation.

The hadith also closes with the Prophet identifying Jibrīl as the teacher: "He came to you in
order to instruct you in matters of religion" (No. 0001) — framing the entire Islām/Īmān/Iḥsān
framework as divinely revealed teaching, not scholastic construction.
