# Covenant-Breaking (Bahá'í)

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Overview

The violation of the Bahá'í [[covenant-bahai|Covenant]] by setting oneself against the appointed leadership of the Faith. The most prominent example in this range is [[charles-mason-remey|Charles Mason Remey]], who in April 1960 proclaimed himself the second Guardian of the Bahá'í Faith. He and his followers were declared Covenant-breakers by the Hands of the Cause, who reasoned that his claim was unsupported by any formal appointment by [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]], and that according to 'Abdu'l-Bahá's [[will-and-testament|Will and Testament]] the line of Guardians was confined to the male descendants of Bahá'u'lláh (lines 26663–26672).

## Notable Cases in This Range

- **Charles Mason Remey** (1960): proclaimed himself second Guardian; declared Covenant-breaker by the Hands of the Cause (lines 26661–26665). See [[charles-mason-remey|Charles Mason Remey]].
- **Shoghi Effendi's family members**: During the 1940s, Shoghi Effendi excommunicated many members of his extended family — his brother, sisters, cousins and aunts — partly because of their contacts with Covenant-breaking members of Bahá'u'lláh's extended family (lines 28874–28883).
