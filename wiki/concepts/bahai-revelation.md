# Revelation (Bahá'í)

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Definition

Bahá'ís regard the [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestations of God]] as intermediaries between God and humanity. As such, their teachings represent a unique revelation of the divine will, and may be referred to as the "Word of God." Not everything that an individual Manifestation says is to be regarded as revelation; moments of revelation appear to be marked off from everyday life, as in Moses' encounter with the Burning Bush or Muhammad's encounters with the angel Gabriel (lines 26836–26852).

## Process of Revelation

The actual process of revelation remains obscure. It is regarded as distinct from human inspiration. Whilst it may involve visionary experiences (as in Bahá'u'lláh's visions of the [[maid-of-heaven|Maid of Heaven]]) it is not confined to such experiences (lines 26868–26878).

Accounts of the Báb and Bahá'u'lláh indicate that periods of revelation often had a powerful effect on onlookers, with the speed of composition, the melody and vibrancy of the Prophet's voice, and the sound of the reed pen on the paper all exercising a hypnotic attraction. The verses would be uttered without apparent forethought and with great rapidity. Bahá'u'lláh's amanuensis, [[mirza-aqa-jan|Mírzá Áqá Ján]], had to develop a special form of speed-writing ("revelation writing") to take them down. In the space of one hour, a thousand verses might be revealed in this way (lines 26879–26897).

During the early period of his mission, Bahá'u'lláh might continue in this fashion for lengthy periods, day and night, but later he was only able to continue for an hour or so. During this period his body would be in a very energetic state, and he would afterwards be unable to eat. He also on occasion chanted in what was regarded as a special heavenly language (lines 26898–26933).

## Output

Each of the Báb and Bahá'u'lláh produced hundreds of thousands of verses — compared with 6,236 in the Quran — which Bahá'ís regard as indicating the potency of the present era (lines 26862–26867).

Bahá'u'lláh denied having received any formal learning, and stated that whenever he wanted to quote others, a tablet would appear before his face giving all that had been revealed in past holy books (lines 26934–26939).

## Comparison with Islamic Revelation

In the case of Muhammad, "revelation" consisted in the utterance of verses which together make up the successive *súras* of the Quran. In like manner the Báb and Bahá'u'lláh revealed verses, and regarded this act as a primary proof of their divine mission. Only God could produce such verses (lines 26853–26867).
