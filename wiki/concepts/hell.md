# Hell

**Hell** (*infernum*, *gehenna*) is, in Christian eschatology, the state of final and eternal
punishment of the damned. [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats it at the close of the
[[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Supplement (XP Q97–Q99 and the surrounding Appendix material), where the
*Summa* as compiled concludes. The doctrine is the dark counterpart to the [[beatific-vision|vision
of God]] enjoyed by the blessed, and is bound up with his account of [[eternal-punishment|eternal
punishment]] and divine justice.

## The punishment of the damned (Supplement XP Q97)

The damned suffer **multiple punishments**, not fire alone. The **fire of hell is corporeal** — of
the same species as earthly fire — yet acts on the damned (including the incorporeal demons) "as the
instrument of divine justice," binding and tormenting them. The "darkness" is material, the "worm"
that does not die is taken as a spiritual remorse, and the "weeping and gnashing of teeth" partly
metaphorical. The punishments do not all act alike on every region of the lost.

## The will and intellect of the damned (Supplement XP Q98)

Aquinas's psychology of damnation:

- The **deliberate will of the damned is always evil**; the natural will toward good remains but is
  practically corrupted (citing Dionysius, Damascene, Bernard).
- They **repent only indirectly** — not hating sin *as sin*, but hating the punishment it brought
  (Augustine, Aristotle).
- They would **prefer not to exist**, but only *accidentally*, as a relief from misery, not in itself.
- They **hate God** — not in His essence (which they cannot see) but **through His punitive effects**,
  perceiving Him only as the punisher.
- After the Day of Judgment there is **no further merit or demerit**: the evil will is now punishment,
  not new demerit.
- The **knowledge from this life remains** in the separated souls and is a source of **grief**, not
  pleasure — they recall the goods they have lost.

## Divine justice and the eternity of hell (Supplement XP Q99)

The **eternity** of the punishment is upheld as **just**: Christ's word "these shall go into
everlasting punishment, but the just into life everlasting" (Matt. 25:46) sets the two destinies in
parallel; as temporal merit earns eternal reward, so mortal sin — an aversion from the eternal
Good — earns eternal loss. The Supplement's doctrinal body closes here, with "Amen."

## Reception

The reality and eternity of hell is held across the historic traditions ([[catholicism|Catholicism]],
[[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodoxy]], and the Reformers alike), against the **universalism** (*apokatastasis*)
associated with Origen, which Aquinas names and rejects (see also suffrages,
which do not avail the damned). Debates over the corporeality of hell's fire and the precise nature of
its pains continue within and across traditions.
