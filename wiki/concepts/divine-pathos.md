# Divine Pathos (God in Search of Man)

The thesis that gives [[god-in-search-of-man|*God in Search of Man*]] its title:
[[abraham-joshua-heschel|Heschel]] holds that the defining structure of biblical faith is
**not** anthropocentric (man seeking God) but **theocentric** — God actively *seeks* and is
concerned for humanity. "All of human history as described in the Bible may be summarized in
one phrase: God is in search of man. Faith in God is a response to God's question." The
standard scholarly axiom that religion is "man's search for God," with God "silent, hidden
and unconcerned," is for Heschel "incomplete and the axiom false."

## God as Subject and Concern

God is "concern, not only power. God is He to whom we are accountable." This concern is
ontologically prior to human religiosity: "There would be no call to man without a concern
for man." Human religious stirring is itself a divine effect — "the stirring in man to turn
to God is actually a 'reminder by God to man.'" Even repentance is response: Heschel reads
*teshuvah* as both "return" and "answer" — "Return to God is an answer to Him. For God is
not silent."

Correlatively, God is the ultimate **Subject**, never an object of cognition:
"God-awareness is not an act of God being known to man; it is the awareness of man's being
known by God. In thinking about Him we are thought by him." From this follows Heschel's
rule of **adverbial theology**: because nouns "presuppose comprehension," "we have no nouns
by which to describe His essence; we have only adverbs by which to indicate the ways in
which He acts toward us."

## Significance and Tensions

The thesis reframes revelation as divine initiative rather than human achievement (see
[[god-in-search-of-man]] Part II) and the life of mitzvot as *response* (Part III). It
underlies Heschel's rejection of God-as-symbol: "The supreme fact in the eyes of the
prophets is the presence of God's concern for man and the absence of man's concern for
God." Heschel's correlated definition of faith — "an act of the whole person, of mind,
will, and heart … not the same as belief, not the same as the attitude of regarding
something as true" — stands in tension with creedal/propositional formulations such as
[[maimonides|Maimonides']] Thirteen Principles.
