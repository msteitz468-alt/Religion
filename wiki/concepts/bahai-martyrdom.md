# Martyrdom (Bahá'í and Bábí)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 21538–21678.*

## Definition
A martyr is one who suffers death in the cause of their religion — one who becomes a 'witness' for their faith. Martyrdom is a core motif in Shi'ism, particularly linked to the death of the Imam [[husayn|Ḥusayn]] at [[karbala|Karbilá]], glorifying sacrificial action in the cause of truth (lines 21540–21553).

## Bábí Appropriation of the Martyrdom Motif
As tensions between the Bábís and secular and religious authorities increased, the readiness of the Bábís to face martyrdom gave them both courage and religious legitimacy. The struggle at [[tabarsi|Ṭabarsí]] in particular assumed the guise of a re-enactment of Karbalá (SBBR 27, 44–5). This motif continued after most remaining Bábís became Bahá'ís (lines 21600–21610).

## Bahá'u'lláh's Teaching on Martyrdom
Whilst [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] praised those who had given their lives for God's cause, and eulogized martyrdom as greater in God's sight than 'the creation of the universe' (HWA nos. 45–7), he counselled his followers to be cautious in propagating his faith and to observe *taqiyya* (prudent concealment) in identifying themselves as Bahá'ís. They should definitely not seek martyrdom — some Bahá'ís desired to become martyrs, seeing it as a means of expressing self-sacrifice and dedication, even writing to Bahá'u'lláh asking him to grant them this honour. Instead, they should serve and teach the Bahá'í cause. Those who led lives of self-sacrificial dedication might gain the status of martyr even if they died natural deaths (lines 21614–21637).

## Numbers of Martyrs
The number of Bábí martyrs is uncertain. Bahá'í sources commonly refer to a rounded figure of twenty thousand, but a recent estimate places the number at between two and three thousand (MacEoin, 'Bahá'í persecutions'; 'From Bábism' 236–7; 'A note on the numbers'). The true figure may lie between these extremes (lines 21639–21655).

The total number of Bahá'ís killed in Iran before the Islamic Revolution of 1979 is again unknown: MacEoin estimates three hundred or so — though deaths by starvation of dependants of those killed should be added. Since 1979 over two hundred Bahá'ís have been murdered or executed for their beliefs (lines 21656–21664).

## Outside Iran
Whilst there have been many instances of persecution and imprisonment, there have been very few cases of Bahá'ís having been martyred outside Iran. One notable case is Duarte [[vieira|Vieira]] (1966) in what was then Portuguese Guinea. Several dedicated individuals who were not killed for their beliefs were given the status of martyr by Shoghi Effendi, including George [[benke|Benke]], May [[maxwell-may|Maxwell]], and Keith [[ransom-kehler|Ransom-Kehler]] (lines 21665–21678).
