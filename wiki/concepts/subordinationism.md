# Subordinationism

Subordinationism is the ante-Nicene tendency to rank the Son (and Spirit) below the Father in being, status, or function while still affirming their divinity. Kelly stresses that for most pre-Nicene writers this was **not** a heresy but the ordinary way of safeguarding monotheism: the Father alone is *autotheos* ("God in the strict sense") and *agennētos* ("ingenerate"), while the Son is God derivatively. The [[arian-controversy|Arian crisis]] forced the Church to decide which forms of subordination were compatible with the Son's full deity. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. IV–V.

## The Apologists
Because the [[logos-christology|Apologists]] dated the Son's "generation" to his emission for the work of creation, their scheme looks subordinationist by later standards: [[justin-martyr|Justin]] worships the Logos "in a secondary rank" and calls him a "second God" (*deuteros theos*). Kelly reads this as a device to preserve monotheism, not a denial of the Son's divinity.

## Hippolytus, Tertullian, Novatian
[[hippolytus]] and [[tertullian]]: "judged by post-Nicene standards, [their] language has a subordinationist ring," though their intent was to emphasise the Godhead's unity. For Tertullian "the Father is the whole substance, while the Son is a derivation from and portion of the whole" (*portio*, not a division). [[novatian]] held the Son "subject to the Father, less than the Father" — an inferiority springing from the Son's being "by nature derivative."

## Origen
[[origen]]'s is the most systematic ante-Nicene subordinationism, integral to his scheme of [[eternal-son|eternal generation]]: the Father alone is *autotheos*; the Son is a "secondary God" (*deuteros theos*) whose deity is possessed "by participation"; prayer should properly be addressed to the Father alone. Functionally, "the Father's action extends to all reality, the Son's is limited to rational beings, and the Spirit's to those who are being sanctified." His pupil **Dionysius of Alexandria** pressed this so far (calling the Son a "creature," *poiēma*) that he was charged with proto-Arianism and had to issue a defence.
