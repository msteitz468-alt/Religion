# Ṣaḍ-darśana — The Six Orthodox Schools

***Darśana*** ("view, way of seeing") is the standard Indian word for a philosophical system. The
classical map of Hindu philosophy is the **ṣaḍ-darśana**, the **six orthodox (*āstika*) systems** — those
that accept the authority of the [[upanishads|Vedas]] — set against the **heterodox (*nāstika*)** systems
that reject it. This page is the orienting framework for the wiki's Hindu philosophical pages.

*Āstika* and *nāstika* here mark **acceptance of Vedic authority**, not theism: Sāṃkhya and Mīmāṃsā are
āstika yet effectively non-theistic, while Buddhism, Jainism, and Cārvāka are nāstika.

## Dasgupta's Account (Vol. I, Ch. IV)

[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] confirms and grounds this classification, while
cautioning against treating it too schematically. Key points from his "General Observations":

- **The classification.** "The Hindus classify the systems of philosophy into two classes, the *nāstika*
  and the *āstika*." The nāstika ("it is not") views — which neither regard the Vedas as infallible nor
  rest their validity on Vedic authority — are **three**: Buddhist, Jaina, and Cārvāka. The āstika are the
  **six** (*ṣaḍ-darśana*): Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Vedānta, Mīmāṃsā, Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika.
- **Founders and root-texts** (as Dasgupta gives them): **Sāṃkhya** → the mythical *Kapila* (earliest works
  lost); **Yoga** → *Patañjali* (the *Pātañjala Yoga-sūtras*) — Dasgupta treats Sāṃkhya and Yoga as "two
  divergent modifications of an original Sāṃkhya," distinguished mainly by Yoga's admission of *Īśvara* and
  its mystical practices; **Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā** → *Jaimini*, with *Śabara*'s *bhāṣya*, then *Kumārila Bhaṭṭa*
  and his pupil *Prabhākara* (the *bhāṭṭa* and *guru* schools); **Vedānta / Uttara-Mīmāṃsā** → the
  [[brahma-sutras|Brahma-sūtras]] of *Bādarāyaṇa*; **Nyāya** → *Gautama (Akṣapāda)*, with *Vātsyāyana*'s
  *bhāṣya*; **Vaiśeṣika** → *Kaṇāda (Ulūka)*. Dasgupta notes Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika "represent the same system
  for all practical purposes," differing only in stress (logic vs. metaphysics/physics).
- **Why Indian philosophy is written as commentary.** Dasgupta's central methodological claim: the systems
  began as terse *sūtras* — "lecture hints" for those already orally instructed — which then grew **only
  through layered commentary**, each commentator bound to defend and harmonize the founder's words rather
  than propound a new system. "A system in the sūtras is weak and shapeless as a new-born babe," but with
  its commentaries down to the 17th c. "appears as a fully developed man." Hence one **cannot** write a
  chronological history of free-standing thinkers as in Europe; each system must be studied as one
  cumulative whole forged in conflict with its rivals. (He illustrates with the Nyāya commentarial chain:
  Vātsyāyana → Uddyotakara → Vācaspati Miśra → Udayana → …) This is the rationale for the *form* of the
  whole *History*.
- **Four points of agreement** among all systems except Cārvāka: (1) **[[karma|karma]] and rebirth**;
  (2) the doctrine of ***mukti*** — that the beginningless karma-cycle has an end, found *within* the self;
  (3) the doctrine of a permanent **soul** (*ātman/puruṣa/jīva*) — denied only by Buddhism; (4) a shared
  **pessimism about saṃsāra resolving into optimism** about liberation, and a near-common **sādhana**
  (ethical-meditative discipline, largely the Yoga model). See [[moksha|mokṣa]].

This close reading supersedes the earlier note that the six-fold scheme was taken "from general
scholarship": Dasgupta presents the *āstika/nāstika* split and the six founders directly, though he
**warns** the six-fold tidiness is partly retrospective — he treats the schools historically, by their
growth, not as a static list.

## The Six Orthodox Schools (traditionally paired)

| Darśana | Core concern | Wiki status |
|---|---|---|
| **Nyāya** | logic, epistemology (*pramāṇa*), realist analysis | *gap — no page yet* |
| **Vaiśeṣika** | atomism, categories (*padārtha*) of being | *gap — no page yet* |
| **Sāṃkhya** | dualism of *puruṣa* (consciousness) and *prakṛti* (nature); the *guṇas* | *gap — partial via [[maya|māyā]]/Gītā* |
| **Yoga** | Patañjali's practical discipline built on Sāṃkhya metaphysics | *gap — no page yet* |
| **Mīmāṃsā** (Pūrva) | Vedic ritual exegesis, *dharma*, the eternity of the Veda | *gap — no page yet* |
| **Vedānta** (Uttara Mīmāṃsā) | the Upaniṣadic end of the Veda; Brahman and the self | covered: [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]], [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Viśiṣṭādvaita]], [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] |

The standard pairs are **Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika**, **Sāṃkhya–Yoga**, and **Mīmāṃsā–Vedānta**: in each pair one
school supplies the metaphysics and the other its method or application. Of the six, only
**[[hinduism|Vedānta]]** survives as a living philosophical tradition with mass adherence, fragmented into
its rival sub-schools — which is why the wiki's Hindu philosophy coverage is, so far, Vedānta-centric.

## The Heterodox (*nāstika*) Systems

- **[[buddhism|Buddhism]]** — rejects the self (*[[anatta|anātman]]*) and Vedic authority; treated by
  Dasgupta at length in Vol. I (and the wiki's largest non-Hindu Indian cluster).
- **Jainism** — radical pluralism, *anekāntavāda* (many-sidedness), *ahiṃsā*; *gap — no tradition page yet*.
- **[[carvaka|Cārvāka / Lokāyata]]** — materialism, denial of an afterlife and of scriptural authority;
  the standard *pūrvapakṣa* (opponent) foil.

## Why It Matters for This Wiki

The darśana map shows that the wiki's three Vedānta sect pages plus
[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaivism]] are a thin slice of a much larger system. The framework flags the
open coverage gaps (Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā; Jainism, Cārvāka) for future ingests, and
explains the recurring technical vocabulary — *pramāṇa*, *padārtha*, *guṇa*, *prakṛti*, *puruṣa* — that
the Vedānta schools borrow and contest.
