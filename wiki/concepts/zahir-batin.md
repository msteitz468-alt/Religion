# Zahir and Batin (Exoteric and Esoteric)

In Islamic interpretation, **zahir** (*al-zahir*, "the outer/apparent") and **batin**
(*al-batin*, "the inner/hidden") name the two registers of a scriptural text: its plain,
literal, public sense and its concealed, deeper sense accessible to the initiated or the
learned. The axis structures much of [[tafsir|Qur'anic exegesis]] and is developed most fully
in [[sufism|Sufi]] and Shia *ta'wil*. It is one of the hermeneutical frameworks tracked for
[[islam|Islam]].

## In the Tahafut Debate (al-Ghazali)

The zahir/batin distinction is a live weapon in [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]]'s
[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|*Tahafut al-Falasifa*]]. The [[falsafa|philosophers]] read the
Qur'an's promises of **[[bodily-resurrection|bodily resurrection]]**, paradise, and the houris
as *batin* allegory — sensory parables framed by the prophets for the masses, whose real
referent is purely intellectual reward and punishment (Disc. 20, §16). They similarly identify
the Qur'anic "preserved tablet" with the celestial souls (Disc. 16, §10).

Ghazali's reply draws a crucial line: allegorizing (*ta'wil* to the *batin*) is licit **only
where reason demonstrates the literal sense impossible** — as with the anthropomorphic divine
attributes. Bodily resurrection is *not* rationally impossible, so its texts must be held to the
*zahir*; allegorizing them implies the prophets deliberately deceived, which "prophethood is
sanctified high above" (Disc. 20, §§23–25). This selective licensing of esoteric reading is
itself part of why he charges the philosophers with unbelief (*[[takfir]]*) on resurrection.
