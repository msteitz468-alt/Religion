# Pañca-kośa (the Five Sheaths)

The **pañca-kośa** is the Upaniṣadic analysis of the human self into **five concentric "sheaths"**
(*kośa*), through which one passes inward to reach the true [[atman|ātman]]. Its locus classicus is the
**Taittirīya Upaniṣad** (II), and [[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] (Vol. I, Ch. III)
makes it the structure by which the Upaniṣads define "the inmost essence of man."

From outermost to innermost:

1. **Annamaya** — the self "made of food," i.e. the physical body.
2. **Prāṇamaya** — the self as **vital breath** (*prāṇa*), the life-functions.
3. **Manomaya** — the self "consisting of will" / mind.
4. **Vijñānamaya** — the self "consisting of consciousness" / understanding.
5. **Ānandamaya** — the innermost **self as pure bliss**: "Truly he is the rapture; for whoever gets this
   rapture becomes blissful… For whoever finds even a slight difference between himself and this Ātman,
   there is fear for him" (Taitt. II.7).

In Dasgupta's reading the sheaths chart the same inward movement dramatized in
[[prajapati|Prajāpati's]] instruction of Indra (Chā. VIII) — past body, dream, and dreamless sleep to the
bodiless self untouched by pleasure and pain. The bliss of *ānandamaya* is not an *attribute* of the
self: "Bliss is not an attribute of it but it is bliss itself." The scheme later becomes standard in
[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] anthropology.
