# Oikonomia (Divine Economy)

*Oikonomia* (Greek; Latin *dispensatio*), "economy," is the patristic term for God's ordered plan of salvation as worked out in history — and, derivatively, for the distinction of Son and Spirit from the one Father **as disclosed in that plan**. It became the key device of "economic Trinitarianism": the one Godhead is considered both as it exists eternally and as it reveals itself in the economy of redemption. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. IV–V.

## Development of the Term

Kelly traces the word from meaning "God's secret purpose," to the incarnation as the goal of that purpose, and finally to "the distinction of Son and Spirit from the one Father as disclosed in the working out of God's redemptive plan." [[irenaeus]] supplied the technical usage: "by the very essence and nature of His being there is but one God, while at the same time according to the economy of our redemption there are both Father and Son." From him it passed to [[hippolytus]] and [[tertullian]].

## Economic Trinitarianism and Its Limit

For [[tertullian]] the *oikonomia* "distributes the unity into Trinity, setting forth Father, Son and Spirit as three" — three not in substance, power, or status but in *gradus* (grade/*taxis*), *forma*, and *species*. The crucial ante-Nicene limitation Kelly identifies: the Son and Spirit are distinguished as Persons only "as manifested in the order of revelation," the economy; "only later did it come to be applied to the Word and the Spirit as immanent in God's eternal being." [[origen]]'s doctrine of three eternal *hypostases* (rooted in [[eternal-son|eternal generation]]) was the decisive move beyond a merely economic distinction.
