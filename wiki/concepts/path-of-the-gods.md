# Path of the Gods (*Devayāna*)

The **Path of the Gods** (*devayāna*) is the post-mortem journey described in the Upanishads by which
the knower of Brahman travels from the body at death to Brahman. It is contrasted with the **Path of
the Ancestors** (*pitṛyāna*), the path of those who performed Vedic works without liberating knowledge
and who return to rebirth after exhausting their rewards in a limited heavenly sphere.

## The Texts and Their Apparent Conflicts

Multiple Upanishadic passages describe the post-mortem journey but with different staging-posts and
detail:
- **Chāndogya VI,14–15**: the soul passes through elements and worlds on its way
- **Kauṣītaki I,2–3**: describes stages at the moon, then through various cosmic stations
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka VI,2,15**: the path through light, day, bright fortnight, and so on

[[ramanuja|Ramanuja's]] *Śrī-Bhāṣya* (*Brahma Sūtra* IV, Pāda III) harmonizes all accounts into a
**single road beginning with light**.

## The Order of Stages (Rāmānuja's Synthesis)

The soul of the knower passes through:

1. **Light** (*arci*) — the initial station
2. **Day** (*diva*)
3. **Bright fortnight** (*śuklapakṣa*)
4. **Six months of the northern solstice** (*uttarāyaṇa*)
5. **Vāyu** — interpreted not as the wind but as **the world of the gods** (*devaloka*)
6. **Sun** (*āditya*)
7. **Lightning** (*vidyut*) — where a "not-human person" first appears as conductor
8. **Varuṇa**
9. **Indra**
10. **Prajāpati**
11. **Brahman** (*brahmaloka*) — the final destination

The successive stages represent a journey through increasingly refined and elevated cosmic spheres.
**Conducting deities** appear at various points and lead the ascending soul; the soul requires its
**subtle body** (*sūkṣma śarīra*) for this journey, since it must be able to have experiences (including
a colloquy at the moon stage) — a gross body is not possible for a soul that has shed it at death, but
some instrument of experience is required.

## Who Is Conducted by the Deities?

The *Śrī-Bhāṣya* records three positions:

| Teacher | Position |
|---|---|
| **Bādari** | Those meditating on an *effected* Brahman (Hiraṇyagarbha); at the *pralaya* (dissolution) of Hiraṇyagarbha's world, those souls attain the highest Brahman together with their guru |
| **Jaimini** | Those meditating on the *highest* Brahman directly; "Brahman" in the path-texts means the highest |
| **Bādarāyaṇa** (final) | All those NOT depending on symbols are conducted: (1) those meditating on the highest Brahman, and (2) those meditating on the Self as free from *Prakṛti* with Brahman as its Self (*antaryāmin*). **Symbol-meditators are excluded** — their meditation is contaminated by a non-intelligent element (*acit*), and they attain a correspondingly limited result |

The key principle is *tatkratuḥ* (*Chāndogya* III,14): "according to what his thought is, so will he
be." The ultimate referent of the knower's meditation determines the destination.

## Timing of Death

A common textual theme: certain times of death are propitious (day, bright fortnight, northern
solstice) and others are not (night, dark fortnight, southern solstice). Ramanuja interprets this as:
- For those **without knowledge**: the time of death may affect their karmic trajectory
- For the **knower of Brahman**: death at any time leads to the goal; the *Bhagavad Gītā* VIII,23–26
  passage about "two paths" is a description for Yogins to **remember and meditate on** daily as a
  practice, not a timing injunction for the moment of dying

## The Exit from the Body

The knower exits through the **100th+1 artery** (*suṣumnā*), directed toward the crown of the head.
The Supreme Person dwelling in the heart illuminates this path; the soul exits by the Lord's grace and
assistance. The ordinary person may exit through other arteries leading to rebirth.

## The Destination: Non-Division from Brahman

The journey ends in **non-division** (*abheda*) from Brahman — not a local absorption into a
formless unity, but the soul's full realization of itself as Brahman's inseparable mode (body), with
all its true powers manifest. This is eternal communion, not annihilation of the soul's individual
reality. See [[moksha|Moksha]].
