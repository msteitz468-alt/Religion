# Gurmukh / Manmukh

**Gurmukh** ("one who faces the Guru," "Guru-oriented") and **manmukh** ("one who faces the self," "self-oriented") are the two fundamental human types in Sikh soteriology, representing the contrast between the person who has turned toward the [[the-guru|Guru]]'s guidance and the person who remains in the grip of [[haumai|haumai]] (ego/self-centredness).

## McLeod's Slice — Note on Coverage

The terms *gurmukh* and *manmukh* do not appear as explicit technical vocabulary in McLeod's assigned slice (~L5801–8400 / file lines 1–2600). However, the conceptual content they name is fully present and central to McLeod's account of [[guru-nanak|Guru Nanak]]'s doctrine: the two human types are implicit throughout his description of the human predicament (the man blinded by haumai) and the alternative (the devout practitioner who sheds haumai and attunes to the [[nam-simaran|nam]]).

## The Conceptual Contrast (as Present in McLeod's Slice)

McLeod describes the basic human condition as one of blindness: "Mankind, however, is congenitally blind and for most people the vision of the divine, ever present around them, remains forever concealed" (~L508–511). Within the *man* (heart/mind/spirit), "evil exercises its vicious sway, and, seduced by the passions it generates, most are blind to the spiritual reality that lies within and about them. Driven by their evil impulses, they behave in a manner which, in accordance with the law of karma, earns appropriate penalties" (~L518–524). "The fundamental problem is haumai or self-centred concern for all that attracts the proud, the sensual and the selfish" (~L532–535). This is the *manmukh* condition — living oriented toward the self.

The alternative is defined by the devout practice of [[nam-simaran|nam simaran]]: "All who follow the discipline of nam simaran with devout persistence will progressively ascend to levels of spiritual experience which they alone can comprehend" (~L661–664). Those who perceive the Presence of Akal Purakh in creation "receive the means of approach so to appropriate the freedom and eternal bliss that is proffered for all to grasp" (~L505–507). This is the *gurmukh* condition — living oriented toward the Guru and the divine Name.

## Soteriological Significance

The gurmukh/manmukh polarity frames the entire soteriology of Gurmat:
- The **manmukh** trusts in outward forms (temple, mosque, pilgrimage, ceremony) rather than the Guru; remains in haumai; accumulates evil karma; continues the transmigratory cycle.
- The **gurmukh** sheds haumai "and listen[s] in humility" to the Guru's Word (~L599–600); practices nam simaran; attunes to [[hukam|hukam]]; achieves *sahaj* (total peace in mystical union) and liberation.
