# Sramana

*Sramana* (Pali *samana*; "one who strives/toils") denotes the non-Vedic **renouncer** traditions of
ancient India — wandering ascetics who pursued liberation through world-renunciation, austerity, and
mendicancy outside (and often in opposition to) the Brahminical sacrificial order.

## The Sramana Movements

The historically enduring sramana traditions are **[[jainism|Jainism]]** and **[[buddhism|Buddhism]]**,
alongside the now-extinct **[[ajivikas|Ajivikas]]** and other groups active in the same northeast-Indian
milieu in the mid-first-millennium BCE. They share: the institution of "going forth" (world renunciation),
the goal of release from [[samsara|rebirth]], a doctrine of [[karma|karma]], and ascetic discipline
centred on [[ahimsa|non-violence]]. They diverge sharply on the self/soul (the Jain eternal [[jiva|jiva]]
vs. the Buddhist *anatta*) and on fate vs. effort (Ajivika *niyati*).

## In the Jain Account (Dundas)

[[dundas-the-jains|Dundas]] locates Jainism's origins in this renouncer culture, noting the older
self-designation of Jain ascetics as **nigganthas** ("the unbonded/unfettered" — the term by which
[[mahavira|Mahavira]] appears in Buddhist sources as *Nigantha Nataputta*), and treats the
sramana/Brahmin contrast as foundational to understanding why Jainism is **not** a Hindu sect.
