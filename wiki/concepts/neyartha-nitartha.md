# Neyartha and Nitartha

The core **Buddhist hermeneutical distinction** (named in CLAUDE.md's hermeneutical-tracking list) and
the framework the wiki uses to track *how* Buddhist schools read scripture:

- **Nitartha** — teachings of **definitive** meaning, to be taken as the final truth.
- **Neyartha** — teachings of **provisional / interpretable** meaning, true *for a purpose* but
  requiring further interpretation; "meaning-to-be-drawn-out."

## Function

Because the [[gautama-buddha|Buddha]] is held to have taught different things to different hearers
(**upaya**, skillful means — dramatized in the [[lotus-sutra|Lotus Sutra]]), the canon contains
apparently conflicting statements. The neyartha/nitartha distinction resolves this by ranking
teachings: a school treats *its own* defining scriptures as *nitartha* and the others' as *neyartha*.

- [[theravada|Theravada]] uses it sparingly, largely taking the suttas at face value.
- [[mahayana|Mahayana]] uses it heavily: the [[heart-sutra|Prajnaparamita]] and Lotus are *nitartha*;
  the earlier *arhat*-path teaching is *neyartha* (provisional). Different Mahayana schools
  (Madhyamaka, Yogacara) disagree over which sutras are definitive.
- [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] adds a further esoteric layer of interpretation.

## Cross-Tradition Parallel

This is Buddhism's structural counterpart to the other traditions' tools for ranking and harmonizing
scripture: the Islamic *muhkam/mutashabih* and abrogation (*[[qiraat-variant-readings|naskh]]*), the
Jewish [[pardes|PaRDeS]] levels, and Christian senses of scripture ([[typology|the Quadriga]]). A
candidate comparison page on "how traditions rank their own scriptures."
