# Double-Mindedness and Spiritual Wholeness (*Dipsychos* / *Teleios*)

The organizing concern of the [[james|Letter of James]], on [[douglas-moo|Moo]]'s reading (PNTC,
2000), is **spiritual wholeness** — undivided, single-hearted commitment to God — and its opposite,
**double-mindedness**. The two poles are captured by two Greek words James foregrounds:

- ***dipsychos*** ("double-souled," "double-minded") — apparently coined by James (1:8; 4:8); the
  person who wavers between God and the world, "like a wave of the sea, blown and tossed by the
  wind" (1:6). It names a kind of spiritual instability or "schizophrenia" of allegiance.
- ***teleios*** ("perfect," "complete," "mature") — the goal: to be "mature and complete, not
  lacking anything" (1:4; cf. 1:17, 25). Colored by its Hebrew background (the *tāmîm* of Gen. 6:9,
  Noah "complete"), it means integrity/wholeness more than flawless perfection — though Moo warns
  against "lowering the bar," since the standard is God's own holiness.

## The Diagnosis: Dividedness

James's deepest analysis of the human problem, Moo argues, is *dividedness*. It manifests across the
letter:
- **Prayer**: the double-minded doubter cannot receive what he asks (1:6–8).
- **Speech**: the same mouth blesses God and curses people made in God's image (3:9–10).
- **Profession vs. life**: orthodox confession ("God is one," 2:19 — even the demons believe it)
  unmatched by deeds (2:14–26).
- **Loyalty**: the climactic charge of *spiritual adultery* — "friendship with the world is enmity
  with God" (4:4); James calls his readers "adulteresses" (see [[james|James 4:4–10]]).

The antidote is the character of **God himself**, who is *one* and single in purpose (1:5, 17):
believers are to become like God — firm, constant, undivided. This ties the ethic directly to
James's doctrine of God.

## Wholeness as the Letter's Central Concern

Moo resists naming a single "theme" for James but identifies *spiritual wholeness* as its central
*concern*, gathered up in the emotional climax of 4:4–10 (the call to repent of double-minded
worldliness: "Purify your hearts, you double-minded," 4:8). James constructs his exhortations as a
series of stark either/or choices — wisdom from above vs. earthly/demonic wisdom (3:13–18); doing
the word vs. self-deceived hearing (1:22); "pure and undefiled religion" vs. pollution by the world
(1:27). The recurring demand is to stop compromising and give oneself wholly to the Lord.

Moo connects this to Wesley's "Christian perfection": "the giving God all our heart... the devoting,
not a part, but all our soul, body, and substance to God" — while noting James presents wholeness as
a *goal*, recognizing that believers still sin (3:2).

## Background and Reception

- **OT/Jewish**: the call to serve God with an undivided heart (Deut. 6:5, the Shema; Ps. 86:11,
  "give me an undivided heart"); the "two ways" / "two inclinations" (*yetzer*) tradition.
- **Jesus' teaching**: "No one can serve two masters" (Matt. 6:24) — the substratum of James's
  ethic, consistent with his pervasive dependence on the Sermon on the Mount.
- **Early reception**: *dipsychos* recurs prominently in *1 Clement* and *The Shepherd of Hermas*
  (whose *Mandates* are strikingly close to James) — among the earliest evidence for the letter's
  use.
