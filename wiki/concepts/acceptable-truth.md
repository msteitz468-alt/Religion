# Acceptable Truth

**Acceptable truth** is a [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] PR doctrine — attributed to [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]] and recorded in *The Management Series 1970–1974* (p. 116) — holding that public statements should be **tailored to fit the "reality" of the intended audience** rather than conveying full or accurate information.

## The Doctrine

The principle is explicitly presented as a PR technique: determine what the audience will find credible and acceptable, and provide that version of events. Atack summarizes: "Using an acceptable truth at first meant avoiding embarrassing aspects of the truth, and later, more simply, lying."

The doctrine was applied at multiple organizational levels:
- To **public and new members**: presenting Scientology as a self-help system without disclosing the advanced cosmological or financial expectations
- To **media and government**: denying connections between Scientology front groups and the Church itself (e.g., WISE, ABLE, Narconon claiming autonomy)
- To **Church members themselves**: the graded disclosure of doctrine (the Bridge) means each level's members receive only the "acceptable" version of what lies ahead

## Guardian's Office Application: "Plausible Denial"

Within the **Guardian's Office (GO)**, the acceptable truth principle became the technique elsewhere called **"plausible deniability"**: structuring operations so that no single actor could confirm the chain of authorization, and so that the organization could truthfully (if misleadingly) deny knowledge of specific actions. This was how GO operations against critics were organized: compartmented so each participant knew only their piece.

Atack argues this culture became so deep-rooted that "Church representatives perhaps believe the lies they tell about their membership, Hubbard's income, and past misdeeds" — a situation where institutional conditioning has made the distinction between strategic deception and genuine belief difficult to maintain.

## Front Groups

The clearest public application is the **front group structure** — organizations like:
- **ABLE** (Association for Better Living and Education)
- **WISE** (World Institute of Scientology Enterprises)
- **Narconon** (drug rehabilitation)
- **Applied Scholastics**, **Sterling Management**

Each presents an **"acceptable truth"** of its identity: a social-concern organization with no direct Church connection. Atack documents the corporate linkages (Narconon is a subsidiary of ABLE; ABLE is a Scientology entity) and shows the claimed autonomy is false.

## Relationship to "Keeping Scientology Working"

The acceptable truth principle exists in tension with the **"Keeping Scientology Working" (KSW)** doctrine of complete fidelity to Hubbard's exact teachings — one might ask how an organization committed to Hubbard's truth can systematically filter information. Atack's answer is that KSW applies internally (to auditing technology) while acceptable truth governs external relations and member recruitment — the deception is institutionally compartmented.
