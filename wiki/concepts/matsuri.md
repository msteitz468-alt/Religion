# Matsuri (Shinto festival and worship)

**Matsuri** (祭り) is the **festival and act of worship** at the heart of [[shinto|Shinto]] practice — the
communal celebration by which a community **honors, entertains, and renews its bond with its [[kami|kami]]**.
The word comes from *matsuru*, "to serve / to enshrine / to attend upon a deity," and points to Shinto's
character as a religion of **ritual service and relationship** rather than doctrine. (The same root underlies
*matsurigoto*, "government" — to rule and to worship were once one act.)

## The shape of a matsuri

Shinto worship classically moves through a sequence: **purification ([[harae|harae]])** of participants and
space; **invocation** summoning the kami into its seat; **offerings (shinsen)** of rice, sake, fish, and
produce; **norito** (formal prayers of praise and petition); **kagura** (sacred music and dance) and
entertainment to please the kami; the **communal feast (naorai)** sharing the offering with the deity; and the
**sending-off** of the kami. At a great festival the kami is carried out among the people in a portable shrine
(**mikoshi**), processing through the community's streets — the deity **visiting and blessing its territory**.

## Function and types

Matsuri **bind the community (the *ujiko*) to its tutelary kami (the *ujigami*)** and to one another, and mark
the rhythms of the year and life:

- **agricultural** — spring rites for planting and growth, autumn **harvest thanksgiving** (the imperial
  **Niiname-sai**, the emperor's offering of new rice to the kami, is the supreme example);
- **seasonal and calendrical** — New Year, the great purifications;
- **life-cycle** — the **miyamairi** presentation of newborns, weddings.

Matsuri renews **[[musubi|musubi]]** — the generative bond of life and community — and keeps the kami
**present, thanked, and well-disposed**.
