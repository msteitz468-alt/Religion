# Vipassanā Insight Knowledges (*Vipassanā-ñāṇa*)

The **insight knowledges** are the progressive stages of liberating insight (*vipassanā*) in
[[theravada|Theravada]] Buddhism as systematized by [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]] in Chs. XVIII–XXII
of the [[visuddhimagga|*Visuddhimagga*]]. They are the experiential sequence through which the
meditator passes on the way from ordinary perception to the supramundane path.

## Overview Schema

The *Visuddhimagga* presents these within the last five of the [[seven-purifications|seven
purifications]] (purifications 3–7):

**Purification of View (3)** → **Overcoming Doubt (4)** → **Knowledge of What Is and Is Not the
Path (5)** → **Knowledge and Vision of the Way (6)** → **Knowledge and Vision (7)**

## The Insight Knowledges in Sequence

### Purification 3: Purification of View

**1. Delimitation of mentality-materiality** (*nāma-rūpa-pariccheda*) — Direct knowledge of the
arising of mentality from materiality at the six sense doors; the illusion of a persisting "self"
dissolves into bare mental and material phenomena. This is the beginning of insight.

### Purification 4: Purification by Overcoming Doubt

**2. Discerning of conditions** (*paccaya-pariggaha*) — Tracing the causal conditions
(kamma, ignorance, craving) behind the arising of each moment of mentality-materiality; extends
to past and future lives. Uproots the view that beings arise uncaused or by a creator. "In the past
there was only name-and-form, not a being."

### Purification 5: Knowledge and Vision of What Is and Is Not the Path

**3. Comprehension by groups** (*kalāpa-sammasana*) — All formations grouped and seen as impermanent,
painful, not-self. The beginning of insight and the first full-understanding as investigation. This is
the onset of the **ten imperfections of insight** (*vipassanupakkilesa*):

The ten imperfections (arising only in beginners keeping to the right course):
1. *Illumination* (*obhāsa*) — luminous materiality spreading around
2. *Knowledge* (*ñāṇa*) — unerring, keen, like a lightning flash
3. *Rapturous happiness* (*pīti*) — floods the whole body
4. *Tranquillity* (*passaddhi*) — no rigidity or unwieldiness
5. *Bliss* (*sukha*) — exceedingly refined
6. *Resolution* (*adhimokkha*) — extreme confidence
7. *Exertion* (*paggaha*) — neither too lax nor too strained
8. *Assurance* (*upaṭṭhāna*) — immovable mindfulness
9. *Equanimity* (*upekkhā*) — neutrality toward formations
10. *Attachment* (*nikanti*) — subtle, peaceful, clinging to insight without recognizing it as defilement

The imperfections can be taken three ways (false view / conceit / craving) = 30 total. The meditator
must recognize these as "not mine, not I, not my self" and continue. The story of Elder Mahā-Nāga
at Uccavālika illustrates the danger: he mistook the illumination for liberation; Elder Dhammadinna
(an Arahant) flew to question him, tested him, recognized he was still an ordinary man. Only when
Mahā-Nāga recognized this and applied the insight did he proceed to Arahantship.

**4. Knowledge of rise and fall — I** (*udayabbaya-ñāṇa*, immature stage with imperfections) —
lute simile (no store of unarisen states). Formations perpetually renewed, short-lived as dew-drops
or lightning flashes.

### Purification 6: Knowledge and Vision of the Way

The eight knowledges that constitute this purification:

**5. Knowledge of rise and fall — II** (*udayabbaya-ñāṇa*, free from the ten imperfections) — 
sees fifty characteristics per aggregate (five conditioned-arising conditions for rise, five for
fall). He is now a "beginner of insight" in the proper sense.

**6. Knowledge of dissolution** (*bhaṅga-ñāṇa*) — mindfulness brought only to cessation as
destruction/fall/breakup; does not attend to arising or presence. He "sees with insight both the
known and the knowledge." Culminating point of impermanence. Verse: "Aggregates cease and nothing
else exists; / Breakup of aggregates is known as death. / He watches their destruction steadfastly,
/ As one who with a diamond drills a gem." Eight advantages arise from this contemplation.

**7. Knowledge of appearance as terror** (*bhayatupaṭṭhāna-ñāṇa*) — formations in all kinds
of becoming appear as great terror — as lions, tigers, charnel grounds, flaming coal pits. The
knowledge does not itself fear; it merely judges that past formations have ceased, present are
ceasing, future will cease. Two similes: woman whose three sons were beheaded; woman whose ten
children died.

**8. Knowledge of danger** (*ādīnava-ñāṇa*) — no asylum, shelter, place to go, or refuge in any
kind of becoming. Three kinds of becoming as glowing coal pits; five aggregates as murderers with
raised weapons (S IV 174); six internal bases as empty village; six external bases as village-raiding
robbers; seven stations of consciousness and nine abodes burning with eleven fires (S IV 19).
Terror = suffering = worldly = formations; non-arising = safety = bliss = unworldly = Nibbāna.

**9. Knowledge of dispassion** (*nibbidā-ñāṇa*) — becomes dispassionate and takes no delight in
any formation. Three similes: golden swan finding delight only in the seven great lakes; lion finding
delight only in the Himalaya; Chaddanta elephant finding delight only in Chaddanta Lake and Wood.
Mind tends, inclines, leans toward "Non-arising is safety." (Same meaning as terror and danger —
three names for one movement.)

**10. Knowledge of desire for deliverance** (*muñcitukamyatā-ñāṇa*) — mind no longer sticks or
cleaves to any formation; desirous of being delivered from the whole field of formations. Eight
similes: fish in net, frog in snake's jaws, jungle fowl in cage, deer in snare, snake in charmer's
hands, elephant in bog, nāga in Rāhu's mouth, man encircled by enemies.

**11. Knowledge of reflection** (*paṭisaṅkhā-ñāṇa*) — again discerns formations with three
characteristics; sees them also as foul (objectionable, stinking, disgusting, repulsive) — the
ancillary characteristic of pain. Snake-charmer simile: man mistakes snake for fish; on seeing the
three marks, swings the snake around his head to weaken it and flings it away. Meditator "weakens
formations by swinging them with three characteristics."

**12. Knowledge of equanimity about formations** (*saṅkhārupekkhā-ñāṇa*) — abandons both terror
and delight; indifferent and neutral about all formations; "divorces" formations. Divorced-wife
simile. Heart retreats, retracts, recoils from all modes of becoming. Water-drop on lotus leaf;
fowl's feather on fire — does not spread out. Land-finding crow: if Nibbāna is seen as peaceful,
enters only into Nibbāna; if not, occurs again and again with formations as object.

*Note*: Knowledges 10–12 are one knowledge with three names (Paṭis II 64): at the outset =
desire for deliverance; in the middle = reflection; at the end = equanimity.

**13. Conformity knowledge** (*anuloma-ñāṇa*) — the three "impulsions" just before change-of-lineage
(called "preliminary work," "access," and "conformity"). Conforms to functions of truth both in the
eight preceding insight knowledges and in the thirty-seven states partaking of enlightenment.
Righteous-king simile: king conforms to both judges' pronouncements and ancient royal custom by
saying "So be it." This is "knowledge in conformity with truth."

### Between Purifications 6 and 7

**14. Change-of-lineage knowledge** (*gotrabhū-ñāṇa*) — takes Nibbāna as object for the first time;
passes out of the lineage/category/plane of the ordinary man and enters the lineage/category/plane
of Noble Ones. Intermediate — unassignable to either purification. Stream-crossing simile: seizing
a rope on the near bank and leaping with body tending toward the far bank; change-of-lineage
knowledge is the moment of "falling on to the unformed Nibbāna, the bank opposite — staggering,
not yet steady." Three-winds simile: conformity = three winds that blow away clouds; change-of-lineage
= the man with eyes who sees the moon (Nibbāna) now free from clouds.

### Purification 7: Knowledge and Vision

**15–18. Four path knowledges and four fruition knowledges** (eight in total) — path of stream-entry
(1st), once-return (2nd), non-return (3rd), Arahantship (4th), each followed by fruition. At the
moment of path knowledge, four functions occur **simultaneously**: full-understanding (of suffering),
abandoning (of origin), realizing (of cessation), developing (of the path). Three similes: lamp, sun,
boat. The four truths are penetrated in sixteen ways at once. After each path there is a reviewing
of path, fruition, defilements abandoned, defilements still remaining, and Nibbāna (five reviews
each, nineteen total maximum; Arahant has no review of remaining defilements).

## The Triple Gateway to Liberation

At the stage of equanimity about formations and conformity knowledge, the meditator enters one of
three gateways depending on which characteristic is most prominent at emergence:

| Characteristic at emergence | Gateway | Liberation | Noble person type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impermanent (formations as limited/circumscribed) | Signless | Signless liberation | Faith devotee → liberated by faith |
| Painful (stirring up of mind) | Desireless | Desireless liberation | Body witness (→ both-ways liberated) |
| Not-self (seeing as alien) | Void | Void liberation | Dhamma devotee → liberated by understanding |

## The Seven Noble Persons

| Person | Path/Fruition | Characterization |
|---|---|---|
| Faith devotee (*saddhānusāri*) | Stream-entry path | Faith faculty predominant |
| Liberated by faith (*saddhāvimutta*) | Stream-entry fruition through Arahantship | Faith faculty |
| Body witness (*kāyasakkhi*) | Any of eight stages | Concentration faculty |
| Both-ways liberated (*ubhatobhāgavimutta*) | Highest fruition (Arahantship) after immaterial jhānas | Concentration + immaterial jhānas |
| Dhamma devotee (*dhammānusāri*) | Stream-entry path | Understanding faculty |
| Attained to vision (*diṭṭhippatta*) | Stream-entry fruition through 7th stage | Understanding faculty |
| Liberated by understanding (*paññāvimutta*) | Arahantship | Understanding faculty |

## Defilements Eliminated at Each Path

A key doctrinal table from Ch. XXII:

| Path | Fetters permanently eliminated |
|---|---|
| Stream-entry (1st) | False view of personality; doubt; adherence to rules and vows; greed/resentment leading to states of loss |
| Once-return (2nd) | Gross greed for sense desires; gross resentment |
| Non-return (3rd) | All greed for sense desires; all resentment |
| Arahantship (4th) | All five higher fetters: greed for fine-material existence, greed for immaterial existence, conceit, agitation, ignorance |
