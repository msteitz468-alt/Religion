# Interbeing

**Interbeing** (Vietnamese *tiếp hiện*; the English word coined by
**[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]**) is his vernacular rendering of
**[[dependent-origination|dependent origination]]** and **[[sunyata|emptiness]]**: nothing
possesses a separate, independent self; everything "inter-is" with everything else.

## The Teaching

Thich Nhat Hanh's classic illustration: a sheet of paper contains the cloud (rain grew the
tree), the sunshine, the logger, the wheat that fed the logger — "you cannot point to one
thing that is not here." The paper has no separate self (*[[anatta|anatta]]* /
[[sunyata|emptiness]]); it is made entirely of **non-paper elements**. To "inter-be" is to
exist only in mutual dependence. The word is built to hold two truths at once: things
*are* (conventionally), and they have no separate self-existence (ultimately) — the
[[two-truths|two truths]] in a single coined verb.

## Relationship to Classical Doctrine

Interbeing is not a new doctrine but a **re-expression** of the oldest Buddhist ones — the
[[dependent-origination|twelve links]], [[anatta|non-self]], and the
[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] [[sunyata|emptiness]] of [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] — recast in
concrete, ecological, present-moment language suited to lay practice. It grounds Thich Nhat
Hanh's **Engaged Buddhism**: if self and world inter-are, then personal practice and social/
ecological responsibility are inseparable, and "collective [[mindfulness|mindfulness]]"
follows.
