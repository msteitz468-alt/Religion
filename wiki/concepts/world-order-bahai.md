# World Order (Bahá'í)

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

Conceptualization of a future Bahá'í world society outlined by [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]], particularly in his letters of the 1930s (see [[world-order-of-bahaullah-letters|World Order of Bahá'u'lláh letters]]; line 33186).

## The Goal

Bahá'u'lláh's promised Most Great Peace represented a "New World Order" that would replace the defective world order of the present. It would be established as the "practical consequence" of the spiritualization of the world and the fusion of its various peoples; it would represent the "coming of age" of the human race (see [[social-evolution|social evolution]]). The elements of this future world commonwealth include (lines 33193–33253):

- A world community and a consciousness of world citizenship
- A world civilization and culture
- Onset of the "Golden Age" of the Faith (see [[time-bahai|time]])
- A world federal system with "unchallengeable authority" permanently uniting all nations while preserving their relative autonomy
- A world legislature, executive, police force, and adjudicatory tribunal
- Force as the servant of justice; war abolished
- National, racial, and sectarian animosities ceased
- Complete safeguarding of personal freedom and initiative within each nation
- A press giving "full scope to the expression of the diversified views and convictions of mankind"
- A unified global economy, world currency, and unified weights and measures
- Obliteration of extremes of wealth and poverty
- A world civilization, language, script, and literature
- Religion and science reconciled
- Human energies devoted to scientific research, productivity, eradication of disease, prolongation of life, and development of the intellect and moral and spiritual life

## The Process

The present "Age of Transition" was witnessing a "universal fermentation" reshaping humanity. Two forces were at work: an integrative process (represented chiefly by the Bahá'í Faith, but including growing global solidarity and collective security) and a destructive one tearing down barriers to humanity's "destined goal." This titanic spiritual struggle witnessed the "death-pangs" of the old order and "birth-pangs" of the new (WOB 166–71, 191–3; lines 33256–33292). Divine Judgment on institutions opposing the Faith; growth of secularism and moral decline; and breakdown of the existing social and political order were all part of this process (WOB 171–90; lines 33274–33281).

## The World Order of Bahá'u'lláh Letters

A compilation of seven major letters by Shoghi Effendi addressed to the American and Western Bahá'ís (1929–36; lines 33293–33380):

1. *The World Order of Bahá'u'lláh* (27 February 1929) — partly written in response to Ruth White's activities; emphasized the Bahá'í administration as rooted in the writings.
2. *The World Order of Bahá'u'lláh: Further Considerations* (21 March 1930) — noted that the Bahá'í Faith's uniqueness partly lay in its divinely appointed Administrative Order.
3. *The Goal of a New World Order* (28 November 1931) — reviewed economic and other ills since World War I; called for world federation.
4. *The Golden Age of the Cause of Bahá'u'lláh* (21 March 1932) — extolled Bahá'í achievements; introduced non-involvement in politics; appealed for greater support for the Wilmette House of Worship.
5. *America and the Most Great Peace* (21 April 1933) — extolled achievements of the American Bahá'ís.
6. *The Dispensation of Bahá'u'lláh* (8 February 1934).
7. *The Unfoldment of World Civilization* (11 March 1936) — placed the worsening political situation in the context of Bahá'u'lláh's summons to establish the Most Great Peace.
