# Christoformity

A term coined and championed by [[scot-mcknight|Scot McKnight]] (NICNT Colossians, 2018) to describe the shape of Christian moral and spiritual formation in the Pauline letters. McKnight introduces it explicitly as a correction of Michael Gorman's better-known term *cruciformity*.

## The Term and Its Contrast with Cruciformity

**Gorman's cruciformity**: Formation into the cross-shaped pattern of Christ — self-giving, self-lowering love (Phil 2:6–8 as the template). Gorman's reading of Paul's ethics is influential: the believer participates in Christ's death as the pattern for all Christian living.

**McKnight's objection**: Cruciformity is *too narrow*. To limit the template of Christian formation to the cross alone risks a theologia crucis that truncates the Christ-event. Paul's letter to the Colossians makes the whole Christ-event (incarnation, crucifixion, burial, resurrection, ascension, return) the framework for Christian living. "In Christ" and "in the Lord" (the Colossian household code's repeated qualifier: 3:18, 20, 23, 24; 4:1) does not mean only "in the crucified Christ" but in the *whole* risen and reigning Lord.

McKnight: "I cannot agree with the thrust of Michael Knowles... Relying upon the work of scholars like Francis, Knowles frames the ethic of Colossians as follows: being presently 'in Christ' and eagerly awaiting God's final consummation of all things through Christ should lead believers in just the opposite direction — into practical and concrete expressions of their faith. Here I think the emphasis on difference with the so-called opponents disorders the substance of Pauline ethics in Colossians."

## Content

Christoformity is formation into the whole Christ:

- **The cross** (Col 2:13–15; 3:5–11): co-crucifixion with Christ; putting to death what belongs to the earthly nature; the old self stripped off
- **The resurrection** (Col 3:1–4): co-resurrection; seeking the things above; hidden with Christ in God; "Christ is all, and is in all" (3:11)
- **The ascension and reign** (Col 3:1: "where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God"): ethics lived from the vantage point of Christ's cosmic lordship
- **The return** (Col 3:4: "when Christ, who is your life, appears, then you also will appear with him in glory"): eschatological hope as the motivating horizon

In Colossians, *Christoformity* is the grammar of the entire ethical section (3:1–4:6) and especially the household regulations (3:18–4:1). When Paul grounds wives' conduct in "as is fitting in the Lord" (3:18), he is invoking Christoformity — not hierarchy or authority structures — as the governing norm for household relationships. See [[ephesians-household-code|The Ephesians Household Code]] for McKnight's application.

## Christoformity as Ecclesial

A critical dimension: McKnight insists Christoformity is not primarily individual formation. The template is a *community* shaped by individual Christians who indwell it. Pauline ethics is ethics-for-a-church, not virtue cultivation for individuals. The community itself is to embody cosmic reconciliation: "the primary 'virtue,' then, is a community shaped by individual Christians who indwell it (2:6–3:4)."

This contrasts explicitly with Aristotelian virtue ethics (Aquinas–MacIntyre–spiritual-formation movement tradition), which McKnight criticizes as oriented toward individual character development in accountable friendship between social equals. Pauline ethics, he argues, begins with Christology and eschatology focused on the "virtues" of a faithful community embodying cosmic reconciliation.

## In the Context of Paul's Theology (McKnight's Structure)

McKnight's theocentric-christological sequence for Pauline theology:
1. *Christology* — the Damascus-road entry point; Jesus as Messiah, Creator-Lord
2. *Ecclesiology* — the new-creation fellowship embodying cosmic reconciliation in a local community
3. *Missiology* — the church's extension of the mystery (Christ among the Gentiles)
4. *Soteriology* — flows from, rather than grounds, the above

Formation (Christoformity) belongs primarily in the second category: it is the shape of the *ecclesia* living out its identity as an embodiment of cosmic reconciliation.

## Relationship to Related Concepts

- **Theologia crucis** ([[theology-of-the-cross|Theology of the Cross]]): shares the emphasis on the cross but Christoformity insists the resurrection is equally load-bearing for ethics
- **New creation** ([[new-creation|New Creation]]): the new creation is the context in which Christoformity occurs; to put on the new self (Col 3:10) is to be renewed "into the image of its Creator" — i.e., into Christoformity
- **Heis kainos anthrōpos** ([[heis-kainos-anthropos|The One New Human Being]]): the corporate new humanity of Eph 2:15 is the social form of Christoformity — the community that embodies the new creation in Christ
