# The Mesopotamian Underworld (Kur / Irkalla)

The [[mesopotamian-religion|Mesopotamian]] afterlife is one of the **bleakest visions in religious history** —
a dark, dusty underworld called **Kur**, **Irkalla**, or the **"Land of No Return,"** to which **all the
dead** descend, regardless of how they lived.

## The Land of No Return

Below the earth lay a gloomy realm ruled by the dread queen **[[ereshkigal|Ereshkigal]]** (and her consort
**[[nergal|Nergal]]**), entered through seven gates. There the dead lead a shadowy, joyless existence,
clothed like birds in feathers and **eating dust and clay** — "where dust is their food and clay their
bread." There is **no judgment, no reward, no punishment by merit, and no escape**: king and slave alike share
the same dreary fate (a quality of one's afterlife depended mainly on having living descendants to make
funerary offerings, and on the manner of one's death and burial). Even the goddess [[ishtar|Inanna]] could
enter only by stripping away her powers, and could leave only by providing a **substitute** (see [[ishtar|the
Descent of Inanna]]).

## Significance and Contrast

This grim, **un-moralized** afterlife is the sharpest possible contrast with the hopeful, judgment-based
afterlife of [[egyptian-religion|Egypt]] ([[osirian-cult|Osiris]] and the [[maat|weighing of the heart]]). It
also forms the background against which the Hebrew **Sheol** (a comparably shadowy realm) and Gilgamesh's
despairing quest for immortality must be read.
