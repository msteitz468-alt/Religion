# Daotong — Succession of the Way (道統)

**Daotong** (道統) — "succession/lineage of the Way" — is the Confucian claim to an unbroken
transmission (*tong*) of the ancient sages' authentic Way (*dao*) through a specific chain of named
individuals. It is at once a **historical claim** (that the Way was transmitted through specific
persons) and a **legitimation strategy** (that those within the lineage speak with the authority of
the ancient sages).

## Origin: Han Yu

The concept was substantially initiated by **Han Yu** (768–824), Tang-dynasty essayist and fierce
anti-Buddhist polemicist. Han Yu:

1. Articulated the principle that the Confucian Way had a specific historical transmission chain
2. Declared that the chain ended at **Mencius** (4th c. BCE) — after Mencius, the Way was not
   transmitted; no one since has "truly known the Way"
3. Used this to attack Buddhism and Daoism as false traditions with no claim to the Way

Han Yu's lineage ran: Yao → Shun → Yu → Tang → Wen and Wu → Duke of Zhou → Confucius → Mencius.
This left a gap of roughly 1,400 years between Mencius and Han Yu's own day.

## Extension: Zhu Xi's Construction

[[zhu-xi|Zhu Xi]] (1130–1200) radically extended the daotong by inserting **[[zhou-dunyi|Zhou
Dunyi]]** (1017–1073) as the link that bridged the 1,400-year gap between Mencius and the Cheng
brothers — the first thinkers to "truly transmit" the Way since Mencius.

The full lineage as Zhu Xi constructed it:

> Fuxi → Shennong → Huangdi → Yao → Shun → Yu → King Tang → Kings Wen and Wu and Duke of Zhou →
> **Confucius** → Yan Hui → Zengzi → Zisi → **Mencius** → **[1,400-year gap]** →
> **Zhou Dunyi** → **Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi** → (*implicitly*) Zhu Xi

This construction was substantially **retrospective and contested**:
- The Cheng brothers themselves (1032–1107) had been considered, before Zhu Xi, to have revived the
  Way independently — particularly Cheng Hao, who was credited as "having recovered the Way since
  Mencius" without needing Zhou Dunyi as an intermediary.
- Hu Hong (1106–1161) was the first to suggest the connection between Zhou and the Chengs; Zhu Xi
  systematized it into an explicit lineage claim.
- Zhu Xi wrote 22 pieces promoting Zhou Dunyi between 1169 and 1196 to establish this construction.
- According to [[adler-reconstructing-confucian-dao|Adler (2014)]], the daotong construction was
  nearly complete by 1181 and was politically significant: Zhu Xi used the term *zheng tong*
  (orthodox succession), which was normally reserved for legitimate dynastic succession from the
  Mandate of Heaven — a challenge to imperial claims of moral authority.

## Political Implications

**Zheng tong** (正統) in the daotong context challenges imperial authority: if the authentic
transmission of the Way passes through philosophical sages, not emperors, then the sage-scholars
(not the throne) are the custodians of moral legitimacy. Zhu Xi deployed this pointedly during
the Southern Song, when the court was regarded by Daoxue thinkers as having compromised political
principle.

## Canonization

The daotong was institutionalized through the state ceremony:
- **1241**: Emperor Lizong installed five Daoxue figures (including Zhou Dunyi, Cheng Hao,
  Cheng Yi, Zhang Zai, and Zhu Xi himself) in the Confucian temple — effectively granting the
  daotong imperial recognition.
- **1313 (Yuan)**: Zhu Xi's Four Books commentaries became the basis of the civil service
  examinations, meaning that the daotong framework determined the educational curriculum for the
  next ~600 years.

## Hermeneutical Function

The daotong is also a **reading strategy**: texts from within the lineage (the Four Books,
the writings of the Chengs, Zhou Dunyi) are to be read as transmissions of a single, coherent
Way — discrepancies are interpretive problems to be resolved, not evidence of independent positions.
This is Zhu Xi's hermeneutical warrant for reading the *Taijitu shuo* and *Tongshu* as if they
illuminate the Cheng brothers, and vice versa, even though Zhou Dunyi and the Chengs probably had
minimal scholarly relationship.

## Controversies

1. **Lu Jiuyuan's attack** (12th c.): Lu argued that Zhou Dunyi's *wuji* terminology was Daoist
   and that Zhu Xi had misread the *Taijitu shuo*, undermining the very text Zhu used to anchor
   Zhou's daotong position.
2. **The Daoist diagram origin**: If the Taiji Diagram came from Chen Tuan (a Daoist), this
   undermines Zhou's suitability as the Confucian link in the daotong. Zhu Xi denied this.
3. **Selectivity**: Zhu Xi's lineage passes over many significant Confucian thinkers (Xunzi,
   Dong Zhongshu, Han Yu himself) in favor of a narrow transmission that leads to his own school.
