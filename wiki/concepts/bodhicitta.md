# Bodhicitta

**Bodhicitta** (Sanskrit, "awakening-mind" / "mind of enlightenment") is the defining motivation of
the [[mahayana|Mahayana]] [[bodhisattva|bodhisattva]]: the resolve to attain full buddhahood **for
the sake of liberating all sentient beings**. It is the union of **compassion** (*karuṇā*, oriented
to beings) and **wisdom** (*prajñā*, oriented to [[sunyata|emptiness]]), and the engine of the entire
bodhisattva path. Its generation and protection is the explicit subject of
**[[shantideva|Śāntideva]]**'s [[bodhicaryavatara|Bodhicaryāvatāra]].

## Two Kinds (Śāntideva 1.15–16)

Śāntideva distinguishes:

1. **The Mind resolved on Awakening** (*bodhipraṇidhi-citta*) — the aspiration, the wish to become a
   buddha for others' sake (like wishing to set out on a journey).
2. **The Mind proceeding toward Awakening** (*bodhiprasthāna-citta*) — the engaged mind that actually
   undertakes the practices, the six perfections (*pāramitās*) (like actually travelling). One who
   has it is a bodhisattva "in a derived sense."

## Significance

For Śāntideva the Awakening Mind is the supreme alchemy: it "transmutes the impure body into the
priceless jewel of a buddha." It is generated and stabilized through the meditations on the
**[[exchange-of-self-and-other|equality and exchange of self and other]]** (Bodhicaryāvatāra ch. 8):
since there is no real, enduring self ([[anatta|nonself]]), the partiality of self-concern has no
ground, and "the very being of the bodhisattva is the welfare of others." Bodhicitta is the doctrinal
heart of the **lojong** (mind-training) tradition of Tibetan [[vajrayana|Buddhism]].

## Cross-Tradition Note

Bodhicitta marks the distinctive Mahayana reorientation of the goal *away* from individual liberation
(the *arhat*'s nirvāṇa) toward universal, vow-bound altruism — the bodhisattva postponing final
release "as long as space abides." It has been compared, with care, to *agape* and to kenotic
self-emptying in Christian ethics, though grounded in emptiness rather than a personal God.
