# Antaryāmin (The Inner Ruler)

The **Antaryāmin** ("the one who rules from within") is a divine epithet describing [[brahman|Brahman]]
as the **inner controller of all things** — including individual souls — dwelling within every entity
while remaining distinct from and transcendent to it.

## The Proof-Text: Antaryāmin-Brāhmaṇa (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka* III,7)

The foundational passage is the *Antaryāmin-Brāhmaṇa* in *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upanishad* III,7, where the
sage Yājñavalkya describes to Uddālaka Āruṇi "the inner Ruler, the immortal one." The passage proceeds
through a list of cosmic entities — earth, water, fire, atmosphere, wind, sky, sun, moon, space, darkness,
light — and then through the **individual soul** itself:

> "He who, dwelling in the earth, yet is other than the earth, whom the earth does not know, whose body
> the earth is, who rules the earth from within — he is thy Self, the inner Ruler, the immortal one."

The pattern repeats for each entity, ending for every case: **"that is thy Self, the inner Ruler, the
immortal one."**

## Ramanuja's Reading

For [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]] and [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]], the Antaryāmin passage is
the paradigmatic proof of the [[sarira-sariri|body-soul relation]]:

1. Brahman is genuinely **within** each entity (real internal presence, not mere superintendence from
   outside)
2. Each entity is Brahman's **body** (Brahman controls and sustains it as a soul controls and sustains
   its body)
3. The entity **does not know** Brahman — the controlling presence is invisible to what is controlled
4. Brahman is called **"immortal"** (*amṛta*) — the very last word of each clause — affirming that
   Brahman's internal presence in bodies subject to change and imperfection does NOT render Brahman
   subject to those imperfections

The phrase "that is thy Self" does not mean each entity *is* Brahman in the unqualified Advaita sense;
it means Brahman is the innermost reality *of* each entity — as a soul is the innermost reality of its
body.

### The Crucial Theological Point

The Antaryāmin doctrine simultaneously asserts:
- **Immanence**: Brahman is within everything, including individual souls
- **Transcendence**: Brahman remains "other than" (*anyaḥ*) what it inhabits; the entity does not
  know Brahman; Brahman is "immortal" while the bodies are subject to change
- **Sovereignty**: Brahman "rules from within" (*antaraḥ saṃyantā*) — active direction, not passive
  coincidence

This is the alternative to both Advaita (which collapses immanence into identity, dissolving
transcendence) and sharp dualism (which preserves transcendence by placing God entirely outside
creatures).

## The All-Rewards Corollary

From the Antaryāmin doctrine Ramanuja derives the claim that **all rewards flow from Brahman**, even
when proximate deities (Vāyu, Agni, Indra) are the bestowers. Those deities are *bodies* of Brahman;
their bestowal of gifts is Brahman's act through them. *Bhagavad Gītā* VII,21–22 is cited: "I make
that faith unshakable… those desires being ordained by me" — the Supreme Person behind all proximate
causes.

## Advaita's Contrasting Use

The same passage was used by [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] commentators, but to a different end: the
assertion "that is thy Self" is read as teaching the absolute identity of the individual self with
Brahman. The clause "the earth does not know Brahman" is read as pointing to *avidyā* (nescience) as
the only barrier between the individual and the already-identical Brahman. This is the reading Ramanuja
directly opposes — the body-soul definition shows that "thy Self" means *controller of thy body*, not
*identical with thee*.
