# Gurmat

***Gurmat*** ("the *mat* — counsel, mind, teaching — of the Guru") is both the Sikh name for its own
doctrine (McLeod: it "corresponds to the English word 'Sikhism'") and the tradition's hermeneutical
framework. As the wiki's interpretive label for [[sikhism|Sikhism]], Gurmat designates the way the
[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] is read and used:

- **Bāṇī as the living voice of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]].** The Gurus' utterance (*bāṇī*) is not
  commentary on revelation but revelation itself — [[the-guru|the Guru]] speaking. The Granth is
  therefore the Guru, and reading it is encounter, not mere study.
- **Interiority over ritual.** The locus of religion is the *man* (heart/mind); external rite has no
  saving value. The governing discipline is **[[nam-simaran|nām simaraṇ]]**, especially *kīrtan*.
- **The Granth as oracle.** Guidance is sought through the **[[hukam|hukam]]** — a passage opened at
  random and read from the top of the left-hand page — taken daily and at every rite of passage.
- **Anti-criticism stance.** Because the text *is* the Guru, applying historical-critical
  **textual criticism** to the Adi Granth has been treated as an offence punishable by the Akal Takhat
  (see [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] and [[who-is-a-sikh|Who is a Sikh?]]).

In the wiki's cross-tradition scheme of reading-frameworks, Gurmat stands beside the Jewish PaRDeS,
the Christian Quadriga, and the Islamic Zahir/Batin as the **native hermeneutic of Sikhism** — but it
is distinctive in collapsing text and teacher: the scripture is not interpreted *toward* an authority,
it *is* the authority.
