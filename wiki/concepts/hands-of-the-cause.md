# Hands of the Cause of God

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

The Hands of the Cause of God (*Ayádíy-i-Amru'lláh*) are a distinguished institution of eminent Bahá'ís appointed in their rank by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]], [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] (posthumously), and [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]. Their primary functions were the propagation of the Faith and the protection of its integrity (line 16053).

## Early History (Bahá'u'lláh's Era)

The institution was first established by Bahá'u'lláh, who named four individuals to this rank between 1887 and about 1890 (line 15966). They served as channels of communication between Bahá'u'lláh and the Bahá'ís in Iran, and later played a key role in consolidating support for the succession of 'Abdu'l-Bahá; under 'Abdu'l-Bahá's direction they formed the nucleus for the Tehran Spiritual Assembly established in 1899 (lines 15973–15981).

**Named by Bahá'u'lláh (partial list, lines 15994–16004):**
1. Muḥammad-Javád Yazdí, Mullá
2. Nabíl-i-Akbar, Shaykh Muḥammad Qá'iní
3. Mullá Ṣádiq Muqaddas 'Ismu'lláhu'l-Asdaq
4. Mírzá 'Alí-Muḥammad Varqá

'Abdu'l-Bahá did not appoint any further individuals to this rank during his lifetime, but posthumously designated a number of prominent Bahá'ís as Hands (line 15988).

## Shoghi Effendi's Ministry

In his Will and Testament, 'Abdu'l-Bahá authorized the future Guardian of the Faith to appoint Hands of the Cause. Between 1925 and 1952, Shoghi Effendi honoured several Bahá'ís with this rank posthumously (line 16027). In 1951 he began appointing functioning Hands, beginning with an initial group of twelve (24 December 1951), raised to nineteen (29 February 1952) (lines 16031–16034). A further five individuals were appointed between 1952 and 1957 to replace Hands who had died. The total number was raised to twenty-seven in October 1957, shortly before Shoghi Effendi's death (lines 16039–16042).

### Obligations of the Hands (lines 16016–16023)

Per 'Abdu'l-Bahá's Will and Testament, the Hands were to:
- Diffuse the "Divine Fragrances"
- Edify the souls of men
- Promote learning
- Improve the character of all men
- Remain always "detached and devoted from all earthly things"

### Functions and Powers

Most Hands appointed under Shoghi Effendi were assigned responsibilities in the continents in which they resided, notably to assist in achieving teaching plan goals (lines 16044–16046). The Hands were also given responsibility to protect the Faith by exercising vigilance against external and internal enemies (line 16053). To assist them, they were authorized to appoint [[auxiliary-boards|Auxiliary Boards]] (from 1954) (line 16059).

## The Interregnum (1957–1963)

Shoghi Effendi's unexpected death on 4 November 1957 left no designated successor and no will (lines 16149–16156). Gathering in Haifa (18–25 November), the Hands discovered this situation and, in Shoghi Effendi's last general letter he had referred to them as the "Chief Stewards" of the Faith; they acted in this capacity, assuming temporary headship and electing nine of their number as "Custodians" in Haifa (lines 16159–16165).

Meeting in annual conclave, the Hands determined that the best response was the rapid establishment of the [[universal-house-of-justice|Universal House of Justice]], which 'Abdu'l-Bahá's Will and Testament had guaranteed divine guidance and protection. They called for the [[bahai-international-council|International Bahá'í Council]] to become an elected body in 1961, and for the House of Justice itself to be elected in April 1963 (lines 16171–16182). They asked not to be voted for themselves.

The main opposition came from veteran American Hand Charles Mason Remey, President of the International Council, who claimed to be the second Guardian (1960) — a claim rejected by virtually all Bahá'ís (lines 16186–16191).

## Since 1963

The Hands have served the Universal House of Justice since its formation. The House determined in 1964 that it was not possible to appoint further Hands, and sought to relieve the Hands from unnecessary administrative work so they could devote themselves to inspiring Bahá'ís worldwide, while creating new institutions to continue the Hands' functions of propagation and protection: the **Continental Boards of Counsellors** (1968) and the **International Teaching Centre** (1973) (lines 16196–16214). By the end of 1998 only three Hands remained alive (line 16216).

## Significance

The institution of the Hands of the Cause represents the primary mechanism for the global propagation and protection of the Faith in the formative period of its development. The Hands collectively navigated the most critical succession crisis in Bahá'í history following Shoghi Effendi's death, preserving institutional continuity by facilitating the election of the Universal House of Justice.
