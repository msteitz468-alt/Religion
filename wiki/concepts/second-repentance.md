# Second Repentance (Post-Baptismal Penance)

An early-Christian problem: if baptism washes away sin, what becomes of grave sins committed *after*
baptism? The question generated the doctrine of a "second repentance" — a single further opportunity for
forgiveness — and, against it, rigorist denials that grave post-baptismal sin can be remitted at all.

## Clement's Position

[[clement-of-alexandria|Clement of Alexandria]] affirms post-baptismal forgiveness. In
[[quis-dives-salvetur|Quis Dives Salvetur]]: even one who "after the baptismal seal" falls into sin
should not despair — "to every one who turns to God in truth with his whole heart the doors are
opened"; "Of sins already committed God gives remission, but of those that are to come each man
procures his own remission." The legend of the Apostle [[john-apostle|John]] and the robber, who is
"baptized a second time with his tears" and restored, dramatizes the doctrine. A Prodigal Son homily
among Clement's [[clement-fragments|Fragments]] sharpens it polemically against rigorists who would
"resign to destruction" those who sin after baptism. Clement's near-contemporary the *Shepherd of
Hermas* ([[shepherd-of-hermas|Hermas]]) is the other major early witness, teaching one further
repentance after baptism.

## The Rigorist Counter-Tradition

The opposing stream — Montanist rigorism, Tertullian's later position, and above all the mid-third-
century **Novatianist** schism — denied the Church's power to readmit those guilty of apostasy,
murder, or adultery after baptism. The mainstream Church developed instead a graded order of
**penance** (confession, satisfaction, reconciliation) that institutionalized "second repentance,"
eventually as the [[sacrament-of-penance|sacrament of penance]].
