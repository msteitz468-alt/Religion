## Definition

Emanation (*fayḍ* in Arabic philosophical usage) is the Neoplatonic doctrine that the world and all its existents proceed necessarily and eternally from the First Principle (God), as effects follow causes without will, choice, or deliberate act — in the way shadow follows a body or light follows the sun. The doctrine is distinct from creation ex nihilo, in which God freely brings the world from nonexistence into existence by will.

The concept enters Islamic intellectual history primarily through [[al-farabi|al-Fārābī]] and [[avicenna|Avicenna]] (Ibn Sīnā), who systematized it from Plotinian and Aristotelian sources into a hierarchical cosmology of intellects and celestial spheres. [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazālī]] subjects this cosmology to sustained critique in the [[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|*Tahāfut al-Falāsifa*]] (*The Incoherence of the Philosophers*), Discussions 2–3 and related discussions.

## The Philosophers' Emanation Scheme (as reported by Ghazālī)

The fullest exposition of the emanationist cosmology in the *Tahāfut* appears in Discussion 3 (the "third aspect" of God's being called "maker" of the world), where Ghazālī quotes the philosophers' doctrine at length before refuting it.

### The Foundational Axiom: "From One Only One Proceeds"

The governing principle of emanationist cosmology is that the First Principle is absolutely simple and one "in every respect," and therefore only one thing can proceed from it: "from the One, according to them, nothing but that which is one in all respects proceeds" (Discussion 3, para. 2). This axiom creates the problem of how a composite, plural world can flow from a perfectly simple source.

### The Hierarchical Chain

The philosophers' solution is a cascading series of mediating intellects and celestial spheres:

> "From the existence of the First Principle the first intellect emanated, it being a self-subsisting existent, neither body nor imprinted in body, that knows itself and knows its principle." (Discussion 3, para. 43)

From the First Intellect, three things are rendered necessary: a second intellect, the soul of the outermost (ninth) heaven, and the body of that heaven. The second intellect in turn generates a third intellect, the soul and body of the sphere of fixed stars; and so on downward:

- First Intellect → Second Intellect + Soul of 9th Heaven + Body of 9th Heaven
- Second Intellect → Third Intellect + Soul of sphere of fixed stars + its body
- Third → Fourth + soul and body of Saturn
- Fourth → Fifth + soul and body of Jupiter
- … continuing downward through all planetary spheres …
- Tenth (Active) Intellect → matter subject to generation and corruption

"The intellects, after the First Principle, are ten in number, and the spheres nine. The sum of these noble principles, after the First [Principle], is nineteen." (Discussion 3, para. 44)

### How Multiplicity Arises from the First Effect

The philosophers explain the trinity of outputs from each intellect by noting three distinct "meanings" in the first effect: (a) it intellectually apprehends its principle; (b) it intellectually apprehends itself; (c) in itself it is only possible of existence (contingent). From these three aspects proceed respectively an intellect, the soul of the sphere, and the body of the sphere:

> "An intellect proceeds from it inasmuch as it intellectually apprehends its principle. The soul of the sphere proceeds from it inasmuch as it intellectually apprehends itself, whereas the body of the sphere proceeds from it inasmuch as it in itself is [only] possible of existence." (Discussion 3, para. 44)

### God's "Action" as Necessary Emanation, Not Will

Crucially for the philosophers, what emanates from the First does so by "compulsory necessity, inconceivable for God to prevent, in the way the shadow is the necessary consequence of the individual and light [the necessary consequence] of the sun" (Discussion 3, para. 4). The First knows Himself as the principle of all things, and His knowledge is itself the cause of the world's emanation:

> "His knowledge of the whole is the cause of the emanation of the whole from Him … The existing order is a consequent of the intelligible order in the sense that [the former] comes about through [the latter]." (Discussion 5, para. 26)

Will, power, and knowledge in the First are thus all identical with His essence: "Will would be nothing other than Power itself, Power nothing other than Knowledge itself, Knowledge nothing other than the Essence itself." (Discussion 5, para. 27)

## Ghazālī's Critique

Ghazālī's refutation of emanation operates on multiple levels across Discussions 2–3 (and continues structurally in Discussions 4–9).

### 1. Emanation Makes "Action" a Mere Metaphor

Ghazālī insists that genuine agency requires will and choice: "'Agent' is an expression [referring] to one from whom the act proceeds, together with the will to act by way of choice and the knowledge of what is willed." (Discussion 3, para. 4). Since the philosophers deny will to God — the world proceeding from Him "as a necessary consequence, inconceivable for God to prevent" — their use of the terms "maker," "agent," and "handiwork" is metaphorical, not real. He accuses the philosophers of using pious language to "endear yourselves to Muslims. Religion is not fulfilled by uttering expressions devoid of [their real] meaning." (Discussion 3, para. 17)

### 2. An Eternal "Act" Is Contradictory

The philosophers hold the world to be co-eternal with God, but Ghazālī argues that "act" necessarily entails temporal creation out of nothing: "naming that which is not created out of nothing an 'act' is pure metaphor, having no reality." (Discussion 3, para. 29) An eternal, permanently subsisting "effect" cannot properly be called an act.

### 3. The "From One Only One" Axiom Cannot Explain Plurality

Ghazālī levels five distinct objections against the trinitarian output of the First Intellect (Discussion 3, paras. 49–75):

- **First objection**: "Possible of existence" in the first effect — is this identical with, or other than, its existence? If identical, no plurality arises; if other, the same logic entails plurality in the First Principle itself (para. 49–50).
- **Second objection**: The first effect's intellectual apprehension of its Principle — identical with itself, or other? If identical, no plurality; if other, the same must apply to the First, generating plurality there (para. 53–54).
- **Third objection**: If the first effect's self-apprehension is other than itself, emanation should produce a quadruplicity, not a trinity: "itself, its apprehension of itself, its apprehension of its Principle, and its being in itself possible of existence." (para. 62)
- **Fourth objection**: A body of a celestial sphere contains far more complexity (form and matter, a specific size, differentiated poles) than a simple trinity of aspects can explain (paras. 63–67).
- **Fifth objection**: The claimed relationship between the first effect's "possible of existence" and the generation of a celestial sphere is arbitrary: "What is the difference between this and someone who — knowing the existence of a man who is absent, [knowing] that [such a man] is possible of existence … then [goes on to] say: 'The existence of a celestial sphere follows necessarily from [this man's] being possible of existence'?" (para. 75)

### 4. Ghazālī's Positive Counterpoint

Ghazālī declines to offer a full alternative cosmology ("We have not plunged into this book in the manner of one who is introducing [doctrine]," Discussion 3, para. 79), but asserts:

> "What is there to prevent one from saying that the First Principle is knowing, powerful, willing; that He enacts as He wishes, governs what He wills, creates things that are varied and things that are homogeneous as He wills and in the way He wills? The impossibility of this is known neither through rational necessity nor through theoretical reflection." (Discussion 3, para. 79)

The method of emanation, by contrast, "reduces to [the notion] that the first effect, inasmuch as it is possible of existence, [results in the] procession from it of a celestial sphere … This is stupidity, not the showing of a relationship." (Discussion 3, para. 79)

## Hermeneutical Note

Ghazālī throughout Discussions 2–10 argues on the philosophers' own demonstrative (*burhān*) grounds, refusing to appeal directly to scripture against them. His method is internal immanent critique: grant their premises, then show the conclusions are internally incoherent or arbitrary. This is consistent with his stated aim in the *Tahāfut*: "to disrupt their claims" by demonstrating their "inability to prove" their key theses — not to offer a competing positive metaphysics. He names the book *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, "not *The Introduction to Truth*." (Discussion 6, para. 39)

## Dionysian Emanation (*proodos*) — Rolt's "Two Streams"

The Christian-Neoplatonic version of emanation runs not through the Islamic ten-intellect cascade
but through the *proodos* / *epistrophē* ("procession" / "return") of [[pseudo-dionysius|
Pseudo-Dionysius]]. In Dionysius the divine Names are God's "beneficent progressions" (*proodoi*)
outward to creation; the very word **Emanation (*proodos*)** "means an act by which the
Super-Essence goes forth from Itself" while "all the time" remaining "wholly within itself" — an
act Dionysius calls "an **ecstasy of Divine Love**."

[[rolt-dionysius|C. E. Rolt]] (1920) systematizes this into a metaphysics of **two emanating
streams** issuing from the Super-Essential Godhead: a **Universal Stream** (the *autoeinai,
autozōē* — "Very Being," "Very Life," etc.; the abstract "formal law" of the universe, of which
the most universal emanations rank highest) and a **Particular Stream** (mere particularity,
practically identified with **Non-entity**, *to mē on*, the "rough material"). The two "cross,
but do not mingle" timelessly until, at the pre-ordained moment, they mingle and **Time and the
temporal world begin** — matter arising first as "merely existent" (*ousiōdēs*), then ascending
Existence → Life → Sensation → Reason → Spirit. Unlike the necessitarian Islamic schemes (and
unlike Plotinus, for whom Rolt notes creation is "an act of necessity"), Dionysian procession is
framed as an overflow of love, and Dionysius/Rolt deny the cosmos proceeds by necessity from a
will-less First. See [[rolt-dionysius|Rolt's edition]], [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]] (the
*epistrophē*/return), [[apophatic-theology|apophatic theology]], and [[plotinus|Plotinus]].

## In Maimonides (Guide II:11–12)

[[maimonides|Maimonides]] adopts the [[falsafa|falsafa]] emanation scheme into [[rabbinic-judaism|
Jewish]] cosmology, defining *shefa* ("influence/overflow") as the mode of action proper to
incorporeal beings — acting "without contact, distance, or directionality," figured by his
canonical **spring metaphor**: the source "acts continually; and whenever an object is
sufficiently prepared, it receives the effect of that continuous action" (II:12). The
[[separate-intellects|separate Intelligences]] (identified with the angels) emanate from God and
confer being and form on the spheres. He nonetheless **subordinates emanation to creation in
time**: unlike [[avicenna|Avicenna]] and [[al-farabi|al-Farabi]] he denies the cosmos proceeds
*necessarily and eternally*, and faults Aristotle's emanationist compounding of spheres from
simple Intelligences as incoherent (II:22). See [[guide-for-the-perplexed]].
## Averroes Repudiates the Avicennan Cascade

Strikingly, in the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahāfut al-Tahāfut]]* (Disc. 3)
**[[averroes|Averroes]]** — though defending the *falāsifa* — **rejects the ten-intellect emanation
scheme itself** as no part of authentic Aristotelianism: the triple-derivation cascade (each
intellect yielding the next intellect, a sphere-soul, and a sphere-body from its threefold
self-relation) consists of "inventions fabricated against the philosophers by Avicenna, Fārābī, and
others," found among the Peripatetics only in Porphyry. In its place he offers an Aristotelian
picture: the celestial bodies are **rational animals moved "by command"** by their incorporeal
movers, and the First Principle "distributes unity" through the cosmic hierarchy as a king governs
through deputies or a soul governs one animal — answering "from the One only one proceeds" without a
serial decomposition of the intellect. He concedes he "cannot give in this book a demonstrative
proof" of how plurality proceeds from the Monad. See [[separate-intellects]], [[necessary-existent]].
