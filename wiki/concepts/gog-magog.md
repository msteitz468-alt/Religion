# Gog and Magog

Gog "of the land of Magog, prince, chief of Meshech and Tubal" is the archetypal end-of-history invader of Ezekiel 38–39, whose seven-nation coalition attacks restored Israel and is annihilated by Yahweh himself. In later Jewish and Christian (and Islamic — Yaʾjūj wa-Maʾjūj) tradition, "Gog and Magog" became a fixed pair naming the final eschatological enemy. This page is currently grounded in [[block-ezekiel-25-48-nicot|Block, *Ezekiel 25–48* (NICOT)]].

## Identity in Ezekiel (as Block presents the debate)

- Block: "Since this person is never mentioned elsewhere in the OT, we are dependent entirely on this oracle for his identity." He favors deriving Gog from **Gyges of Lydia** (Ashurbanipal's inscriptions), with Magog "a contraction of an original *māt Gūgi*, 'land of Gog'" — while noting "there is no hint that Lydia ever posed a real threat to Judah."
- **Rosh ≠ Russia**: Block calls the popular identification "impossibly anachronistic and based on a faulty etymology, the assonantal similarities … purely accidental"; he reads *rōʾš* as a common noun, "the prince, chief of Meshech and Tubal." Meshech and Tubal are the Anatolian Mushki (Phrygia) and Tabal of Neo-Assyrian sources.
- The seven-nation coalition (Meshech, Tubal, Paras, Cush, Put, Gomer, Beth-Togarmah) is, for Block, a deliberate merism of north and south raising the invasion to "a universal conspiracy"; Gog and company are "perfect symbols of the archetypal enemy."

## Genre and eschatology (Block's positions)

- Against Hitzig, Cooke, and Ahroni, Block argues the Gog oracle is **not apocalyptic**: applying J. J. Collins's apocalypse definition, "none of the revelatory features of apocalyptic [is] present … the apocalyptic approach to the Gog oracle should be abandoned." He reads it as a satirical "literary cartoon strip consisting of eight frames" whose hyperbole "cautions against overliteralism."
- Timing: the invasion presupposes the fulfilled restoration of chs. 34–37; the debacle "does not occur at the end of time. Rather, it marks the beginning of a new era" (39:22).
- 38:17 ("Are you the one of whom I spoke…?") expects, per Block (with Odell, against Zimmerli and Fishbane), a **negative** answer: Gog is *not* Jeremiah's foe from the north — "not about unfulfilled prophecy, but about earlier prophecies illegitimately appropriated."
- Function: "Gog becomes the agent through whom Yahweh declares concretely that the events of 586 B.C. will never be repeated," a Pharaoh-like agent of divine self-revelation.

## Reception

Block's excursus on Gog in Jewish and Christian tradition documents:

- **Targumic**: Targ. Pseudo-Jonathan Num 11:26 (Eldad and Medad prophesy a king from Magog defeated at the end of days, followed by the resurrection of "all the dead of Israel"); Fragment Targum (Gog and Magog fall "by the hand of the King Messiah"); Targ. Ps.-Jon. Exod 40:11 ("Messiah son of Ephraim, by whose hand the house of Israel is to vanquish Gog").
- **3 Enoch 45:5**: both Messiah son of Joseph and Messiah son of David appear with the Gog/Magog wars — see [[two-messiahs|Two Messiahs]].
- **Revelation**: Rev 19:17–21 is "clearly borrowed from Ezekiel's last frame" (the bird banquet), and Rev 20:7–10 names Gog and Magog after the thousand years; Block inventories John's transformations — "the foreign foe becomes a satanic and diabolical force; the divine victory is placed in the hands of the Messiah." See [[millennialism|Millennialism]].
