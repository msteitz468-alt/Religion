# Soul (Bahá'í)

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

In Bahá'í belief, every human being possesses both a physical body and a non-material, rational soul (or human spirit). The nature of this essence is mysterious and beyond human understanding (GWB 158 no. 82, 164–5 no. 83; lines 29635–29637). The soul is both a spiritual and an intellectual reality. It is the first among created things to recognize and love God; one of the "signs of God"; the "harbinger" that proclaims the reality of all of God's worlds (GWB 158–60 no. 82; lines 29638–29643). It is a divine bounty; an emanation from God; an intermediary between the heavenly and lower worlds; the medium for spiritual life (lines 29643–29647).

## Soul and Body

The body is subject to physical limitations (occupying particular space; subject to disease) and decomposes at death; but the soul is unlimited and immortal (SAQ 229, 242; lines 29670–29671). The soul is independent of physical and mental infirmities — these might prevent the soul from revealing its inherent power (like clouds preventing the sun's rays) but do not of themselves affect it (GWB 153–4 no. 80; lines 29672–29677).

The soul comes into existence and its light becomes reflected in the "mirror" of the body at the time of conception (SAQ 151, 239–40; lines 29678–29681). At that moment all souls are pure and equal. It is subsequent life events and decisions that lead to different levels of spiritual development (lines 29681–29686). After death the body decomposes, but the soul enters a new existence, freed from the constraints of its former attachment to the body (SWAB 178–9; lines 29687–29690).

## The Powers of the Rational Faculty

The powers of the "rational faculty" — the "mind" (imagination; conceptualization; reflection; comprehension; memory) — are also an expression of the soul (GWB 163–4 no. 83; SAQ 208–11; lines 29653–29657).

## The Afterlife

The soul is immortal. After the death of the body it continues to progress until it attains God's presence and manifests divine attributes, without limit (LC 204–9; lines 29693–29697). Those souls sanctified from the world will converse with the prophets of God; the dwellers of the highest heaven will circle around them (GWB 155–6 no. 81; lines 29698–29706).

After death each soul will recognize the worth of its own deeds and understand the consequences of its actions. Those who turned to God will experience inexpressible joy; those who lived in error will be filled with fear and consternation; those who rejected God will bemoan their plight (lines 29707–29718). Souls will recognize the accomplishments of other souls at the same level but will not understand those that are higher in rank (GWB 169–70 no. 86; lines 29718–29722). If a murderer has already been punished for his crime in this life, he will not be punished anew in the next (SWAB 179; lines 29723–29725).

## Relationship to Sin and Progress

The soul's attachment to the material world results in [[sin-bahai|sin]], which hinders its progress (lines 29297–29300). If faithful, the soul reflects the divine light and eventually returns to God; if faithless, it becomes a victim of "self and passion" (GWB 158–60 no. 82; lines 29647–29652). The progress of the soul involves pursuit of the [[spiritual-path-bahai|spiritual path]] and the acquisition of [[spiritual-qualities-bahai|spiritual qualities]] (lines 29658–29661).

The Christian doctrine of original sin is rejected: babies are born sinless; it is a person's subsequent behaviour and worldly attachments that separate them from God (SAQ 119–26; lines 29323–29327).
