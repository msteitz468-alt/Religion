# Dukkha

**Dukkha** (Pali; Sanskrit *duḥkha*) is the first of the
[[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]] and the first of the
[[three-marks-of-existence|three marks of existence]] — the pervasive
**unsatisfactoriness** of conditioned existence. Often rendered "suffering," the term is
broader: it covers pain, but also the subtle inadequacy of all impermanent things to give
lasting fulfillment.

## The Three Aspects

[[walpola-rahula|Rahula]] stresses that the First Truth is **not pessimism** but diagnosis,
and distinguishes three senses of dukkha:

1. **Dukkha-dukkha** — ordinary suffering: physical and mental pain, birth, aging,
   sickness, death, grief.
2. **Vipariṇāma-dukkha** — suffering due to **change**: even pleasant states are dukkha
   because they are impermanent and their loss brings pain.
3. **Saṅkhāra-dukkha** — dukkha as **conditioned states**: the deepest sense, that the
   [[five-aggregates|five aggregates]] of clinging that constitute "a being" are themselves
   inherently unsatisfactory because compounded, impermanent, and selfless.

## Dukkha and the Aggregates

The canonical definition culminates: "in brief, the five aggregates of clinging are
dukkha." Dukkha is thus not a pessimistic mood added to life but a structural feature of
clinging to the impermanent [[five-aggregates|aggregates]] as if they were a permanent
[[anatta|self]]. Because dukkha is **conditioned** (it arises from craving), it can be
**unconditioned** — which is the Third Truth, [[nirvana|nirvana]].

## Why It Is Not Pessimism

Both [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]] and [[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bodhi]] insist the teaching is
*therapeutic*: like a physician, the Buddha names the disease (dukkha) precisely in order
to prescribe the cure (the [[noble-eightfold-path|path]]). The same teaching affirms
real, if conditioned, happiness within [[samsara|saṃsāra]] and the unconditioned bliss of
liberation.

**[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]** presses this further, judging the rote slogan
"life is suffering" an over-generalization: suffering is to be *embraced and looked into*
("our suffering is holy if we look deeply into it"), not used to declare existence
worthless. He even removes dukkha from the list of *universal* marks — see
[[three-dharma-seals-is-suffering-universal|the Three Dharma Seals controversy]].
