The *Separate Intellects* (also called *Intelligences* or, in scriptural language, *angels*) are
incorporeal beings that Maimonides adopts from the Aristotelian-Neoplatonic tradition and
integrates with Jewish theology in Part II of the [[guide-for-the-perplexed|Guide for the
Perplexed]], particularly Chapters III–XII and XXI–XXII.

## Definition

The Separate Intellects are purely spiritual, non-corporeal beings that neither reside in bodies
nor are forces distributed through bodies. They are the "intermediate element between God and
this material world" (Ch. III). Each Intelligence is causally ordered below God but above the
material sphere it governs.

## Cosmological Structure: The Chain of Intelligences and Spheres

Maimonides follows the later Aristotelian tradition (as distinct from Aristotle's own uncertainty
about numbers) in positing **nine spheres** and **ten Intelligences**:

> "The later philosophers assumed ten Intelligences, because they counted the spheres containing
> stars and the all-encompassing sphere, although some of the spheres included several distinct
> orbits. There are altogether nine spheres, viz., the all-encompassing sphere, that of the fixed
> stars, and those of the seven planets: nine Intelligences correspond to the nine spheres: the
> tenth Intelligence is the Active Intellect." (Ch. IV)

The chain of emanation from God:

1. **God** creates the **first Intelligence**, the motive agent of the first (outermost) sphere.
2. Each Intelligence is the cause of the next: "the Intelligence which causes the second sphere to
   move has its source and origin in the first Intelligence, and so on" (Ch. IV).
3. The last Intelligence in the series is the **Active Intellect** ("the lowest of the purely
   spiritual beings"), which governs neither a celestial sphere but rather the sublunary world of
   generated forms.

The parallel structure for material bodies: "The series of material bodies similarly begins with
the uppermost sphere, and ends with the elements and their compounds" (Ch. IV).

> "The ruling power emanates from the Creator, and is received by the Intelligences according to
> their order: from the Intelligences part of the good and the light bestowed upon them is
> communicated to the spheres, and the latter, being in possession of the abundance obtained of
> the Intelligences, transmit forces and properties unto the beings of this transient world."
> (Ch. XI)

## The Active Intellect

The tenth Intelligence holds a special role bridging the celestial and the sublunary:

> "The existence of [the Active Intellect] is proved by the transition of our intellect from a
> state of potentiality to that of actuality, and by the same transition in the case of the forms
> of all transient beings. For whatever passes from potentiality into actuality, requires for that
> transition an external agent of the same kind as itself." (Ch. IV)

As the builder impresses the form of a building on matter through the form in his mind, "so the
source of intellect must itself be pure intellect, and this source is the Active Intellect."

The Active Intellect is identified in scriptural terms as "the Prince of the world, frequently
mentioned by our Sages" (Ch. VI). It bestows intellectual forms on prepared matter in the
sublunary world and, through its influence, enables human intellect to actualize.

## The Souls and Intellects of the Spheres

Each sphere possesses three faculties by which it moves:

1. A **soul** — the principle of self-motion (for "only animate beings can move freely," Ch. X).
2. An **intellect** — enabling the sphere to form an idea of its corresponding Intelligence.
3. A **desire** — the sphere's longing to approximate the Intelligence it comprehends.

> "The spheres have a desire to become similar to the ideal comprehended by them. This ideal… is
> simple in the strictest sense of the word, and not subject to any change or alteration, but
> constant in producing everything good, whilst the spheres are corporeal: the latter can
> therefore not be like this ideal in any other way, except in the production of circular motion."
> (Ch. IV)

This explains *why* the spheres rotate perpetually in circles: circular motion is the only
perpetual, perfect action available to a corporeal being that is attempting to approximate an
incorporeal ideal.

Each sphere desires *its own* Intelligence. The variety of spherical motions (velocity, direction)
follows from the variety of the Intelligences comprehended:

> "The ideal comprehended by the one sphere, which completes its circuit in one day, is different
> from that of another sphere which completes its circuit in thirty years: he thus arrived at the
> conclusion that there were as many ideals as there were spheres." (Ch. IV)

## The Four Causes of Sphere Motion

Maimonides summarises the four essential elements that together explain why any sphere moves
(Ch. X):

1. Its **spherical shape** — necessary for continuous circular motion.
2. Its **soul** — only animate beings can move freely.
3. Its **intellect** — enabling conception of the Intelligence it desires to approach.
4. The **Intelligence** itself — the object of desire that sets the sphere in motion.

## Identification with Angels

Maimonides equates the philosophers' *Intelligences* with Scripture's *angels*:

> "The philosophers agree with us that the Intelligences are incorporated beings between the Prime
> Cause and existing things, and that they effect the motion of the spheres, on which motion the
> existence of all things depends… He uses the term 'Intelligences,' and we say instead 'angels.'"
> (Ch. VI)

The term *angel* (Heb. *mal'akh*) means "messenger," and Maimonides extends it broadly:
Intelligences, the spheres, natural forces, and even human psychological faculties are all
*mal'akhim* in different senses:

> "All forces that reside in a body are angels, much more the forces that are active in the
> Universe." (Ch. VI)

He reads Midrashic statements about angels through this philosophical lens: "One angel does not
perform two things, and two angels do not perform one thing" corresponds exactly to the property
of natural forces — each acts in one way only (Ch. VI, citing Bereshit Rabbah).

He grounds the spheres' being "animate and intellectual" in Psalm 19:2 ("The heavens declare the
glory of God"), arguing that the Hebrew verbs *sipper* and *nagad* used together in Scripture
refer exclusively to intellectual beings. "It is a great error to think that this is a mere figure
of speech" (Ch. V).

## Free Will of the Intelligences

Maimonides grants the Intelligences and spheres a form of free will, distinguishing it from
mechanical force: "the spheres and the Intelligences are conscious of their actions, and select by
their own free will the objects of their influence" (Ch. VII). Scripture supports this, he argues,
from the angel's words to Lot ("I cannot do anything," Gen. 19:21) — an angel expressing a limit
on its action that implies conscious choice within its domain.

## Emanation — Terminology and Mechanism

Maimonides addresses the term *influence* (*shefa'*, "emanation") in Ch. XII. The concept is
required because incorporeal beings do not act by contact or at a spatial distance. He uses the
metaphor of a spring:

> "These actions are termed 'influence' (or 'emanation'), on account of their similarity to a
> water-spring. The latter sends forth water in all directions… In a similar manner incorporeal
> beings, in receiving power and imparting it to others, are not limited to a particular side,
> distance, or time. They act continually; and whenever an object is sufficiently prepared, it
> receives the effect of that continuous action, called 'influence' (or 'emanation')." (Ch. XII)

God's creative act toward the Intelligences follows this logic: "the creative act of the Almighty
in giving existence to pure Intelligences endows the first of them with the power of giving
existence to another, and so on, down to the Active Intellect, the lowest of the purely spiritual
beings. Besides producing other Intelligences, each Intelligence gives existence to one of the
spheres" (Ch. XI). Scripture applies the same image to God: "they have forsaken me, the fountain
of living waters" (Jer. 2:13); "for with Thee is the fountain of life" (Ps. 36:10).

## Three Parts of Creation

Maimonides presents a tripartite structure of the Universe (Ch. X–XI):

1. **Pure Intelligences** (angels): incorporeal, neither body nor force in body.
2. **Bodies of the spheres**: corporeal but endowed with permanent forms not subject to genesis
   and destruction.
3. **Materia prima and its compounds** (*transient* beings): the sublunary world, subject to
   constant change.

The four stellar spheres (moon, sun, planets, fixed stars) each govern one of the four elements
below (Ch. X): the moon's sphere sets water in motion; the sun's sphere governs fire; the
planetary spheres, with their retrogrades and stations, govern air; the fixed-stars sphere governs
earth.

## Tension with the Creation Hypothesis

There is a key difference between Maimonides and Aristotle (Ch. VI):

> "Aristotle believes all these beings to be eternal, co-existing with the First Cause as its
> necessary effect; but we believe that they have had a beginning, that God created the
> Intelligences, and gave the spheres the capacity of seeking to become like them."

Under the Mosaic view, the Intelligences and spheres are *created* — God endowed them with their
governing powers — not necessary emanations from an eternal First Cause.

In Chs. XXI–XXII, Maimonides presses further: even on the eternity hypothesis, Aristotle's
emanation scheme is problematic. A *simple* First Intelligence cannot, by the laws of nature,
produce a *compound* sphere (with its own substance and the substance of an embedded star).
"The emanation could not have taken place by the force of the laws of Nature, as Aristotle
contends" (Ch. XXII). This failure of Aristotle's own system strengthens the case for divine
design and will.

## Hermeneutical Note

Maimonides insists (Ch. II note) that his discussions of the Intelligences, the spheres, and
their causes are not mere philosophy: "my intention was… to expound Biblical passages which have
been impugned, and to elucidate their hidden and true sense." The doctrine of the Intelligences
is presented as the key to understanding the *ma'aseh merkavah* (account of the Divine Chariot,
Ezek. 1) and the *ma'aseh bereshit* (account of Creation, Gen. 1–3). This represents the
*sod*-level (esoteric) hermeneutic of Jewish biblical interpretation applied to Aristotelian
cosmology.

## Averroes on the Movers of the Spheres (Tahāfut al-Tahāfut)

In the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahāfut al-Tahāfut]]* (Disc. 3, 14–16),
**[[averroes|Averroes]]** keeps the separate intellects as the **incorporeal movers** of the
celestial spheres but rejects [[avicenna|Avicenna]]'s serial [[emanation]] cascade that derives them
one from another. For Averroes the heavens are **rational animals** that the movers move "by
command," and "intellect" is predicated of these separate substances **analogically** (*per prius et
posterius*), the First being the cause of intellectuality in all the rest (see [[necessary-existent]]).
Against the Sixteenth Discussion he denies that the celestial souls need particular images of
terrestrial events ("the Preserved Tablet"): a single circular motion requires no such imagination,
and heavenly cognition is a unified mode transcending the universal/particular distinction (see
[[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]]). The sublunary "**bestower of forms**" (*dator formarum*) is the
lowest separate intellect, on the Avicennan view he reports.
