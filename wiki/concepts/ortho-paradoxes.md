# Ortho-Paradoxes

**"Ortho-paradoxes"** is [[bart-ehrman|Bart Ehrman]]'s coinage
([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]], ch. 9) for the **paradoxical
affirmations** that emerged as Christian orthodoxy was forced to reject opposing pairs of
heresies *simultaneously* while preserving what each got right. They are, on Ehrman's
account, the product of a historical process rather than logical deduction — "not inherently
contradictory" but paradoxical.

## The Mechanism

Each of the three "Christological dead ends" was, for the orthodox, **right in what it
affirmed but wrong in what it denied**:

- The [[adoptionism|adoptionists]] rightly affirmed Jesus was **human**, wrongly denied he
  was God.
- The [[docetism|docetists]] rightly affirmed Jesus was **divine**, wrongly denied he was
  human.
- The [[gnostics|Gnostic separationists]] rightly affirmed Christ was **both** divine and
  human, wrongly denied he was a **single being**.

Absorbing each correct affirmation while refusing each denial yields the paradoxes.

## The Two Ortho-Paradoxes

1. **Christological** — Christ is fully God *and* fully a man, yet **one being**, not two.
2. **Theological (Trinitarian)** — there are three persons, all of whom are God, yet there
   is only **one God**: "distinct in number but united in essence."

The same logic drove the resolution of the [[arian-controversy|Arian controversy]]: against
both [[modalism|modalism]] (Father = Son) and Arian subordinationism (the Son a lesser,
created divinity), Nicaea affirmed Christ as *[[trinity|homoousios]]* — of one substance with
the Father, yet a distinct person.
