# Christ Hymn (Philippians 2:6–11)

The **Christ hymn** (Phil 2:6–11) is the poetic christological center of [[philippians|Philippians]]: Christ’s preincarnate existence in the form of God, self-emptying and obedience to death on a cross, and God’s exaltation of him with the name above every name so that every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is [[kyrios|Lord]], to the glory of God the Father. In [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]]’s PNTC, the hymn sits at the heart of the ethical section 1:27–2:18 — motive and pattern for unity and humility, not a free-standing dogmatic digression.

## Hansen’s reading (PNTC, 2009)

### Genre and authorship
Hansen engages the near-standard view that 2:6–11 is hymnic (Lohmeyer’s stanza analysis, etc.). He treats the passage as a **narrative of Christ** that the community already shares. On “even death on a cross,” many hold the line to be a Pauline metrical addition; Hansen argues it is **integral** to the hymn’s climax (deepest humiliation), noting that the hymn’s cross is Christ-centered self-emptying rather than Paul’s usual “for us” formula.

### Preexistence
Hansen **affirms** preincarnate existence on **narrative** grounds: the present participle “existing” in the form of God precedes the aorists of emptying, taking, and becoming. The participle alone does not prove preexistence; the continuous sentence down to the cross does. He rejects Dunn-style denials of personal preexistence that rest wholly on an Adam/*eikōn* parallel.

### Form of God (*morphē theou*)
Hansen prefers reading *morphē theou* as the **glory of God** (biblical glory-manifestation tradition) rather than a strict Aristotelian “essence” via Lightfoot — while agreeing with Lightfoot’s **conclusion** of essential equality with God. Parallelism with “to be equal with God” affirms full equality of distinguishable persons; worship of Jesus is inclusion within worship of the one God (christological monotheism at 2:11).

### *Harpagmos*
Preferred sense: equality with God as **already possessed**, **not to be selfishly exploited** (“an advantage to exploit”) — Hoover-line idiom. Rejected or qualified: mere “robbery” claim-of-rights; “something not yet possessed” (Adam grasping); and Martin’s mediating view that equality (= cosmic Lordship) is acquired only at exaltation.

### Kenosis
Hansen’s synthesis: Christ **did not empty himself of divinity**; he surrendered divine rights and cloaked divine glory by **taking the form of a slave** and becoming in human likeness. Emptying is **incarnation** leading to humiliation and death; Isa 53 “poured out” enriches without equating emptying with the death alone. Modified kenotic (divest of prerogatives, not nature) is closer than classical kenotic theory (loss of deity), which Hansen treats as discredited.

### Exaltation and the Name
“Therefore also” — exaltation as gracious vindication/reward **with qualifications** (not merited quid pro quo; *charizomai* gift; revelation of identity already true in form of God). *Hyperypsōsen* = superlative highest place, not “higher than preincarnate status.” Name given = **Lord** (*kyrios*), not the earthly name Jesus (against Moule). Isa 45:23 transferred to Jesus; in Roman Philippi the acclamation **reflects and subverts** “Caesar is Lord,” while the **meaning** of the titles remains Jewish monotheistic (YHWH as only Lord/Savior).

### Ethical bridge (2:5)
Hansen prefers a translation that parallels “think” among yourselves with thinking **in Christ Jesus** (union), but rejects treating ethical exemplar and kerygmatic drama as mutually exclusive: the unique salvation story is available as moral precedent **because** believers already exist in Christ.

### Link to 3:20–21
Hansen holds that 2:6–11 + 3:20–21 together narrate the full path through suffering to glory (incarnation–cross–exaltation–parousia transformation). Verbal parallels lead some to treat 3:20–21 as a hymn fragment; Hansen says the evidence does **not definitively prove** that hypothesis — Paul’s heart may simply reverberate with the hymn (cf. Acts 16:25 jail singing).
