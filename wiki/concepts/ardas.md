Ardas (Punjabi: *ardās*, "petition" or "supplication") is the communal prayer of the [[khalsa|Khalsa]] and the [[sikhism|Sikh]] Panth, recited at the close of congregational worship and at every significant ritual occasion. It is sometimes called "the Khalsa prayer." Ardas is spoken — not chanted or sung — and when recited in a congregational setting it is led by a single speaker, with the congregation responding "Vahiguru!" at designated points.

## Form and Structure

McLeod describes Ardas as consisting of **three clearly defined sections** (~L3003–3036):

**Section 1 — Invocation of the Ten Gurus**: The first section comprises the introductory lines of *Chandi ki Var*, attributed to [[guru-gobind-singh|Guru Gobind Singh]], supplemented by later references to Guru Gobind Singh himself and to the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]. It names all ten Gurus in sequence — Nanak, Angad, Amar Das, Ram Das, Arjan, Hargobind, Hari Rai, Hari Krishan, Tegh Bahadur, and Gobind Singh — asking each to "grant us aid" (~L3042–3056). It concludes by affirming that "the light which shone from each of the ten Masters shines now from the sacred pages of the Guru Granth Sahib" (~L3058–3062), at which the congregation cries "Vahiguru!"

**Section 2 — Remembrance of Past Trials and Triumphs**: The second section recalls the martyrdoms, sacrifices, and heroism of the [[sikhs|Panth]]. It is grouped in thematic clusters, each concluding with a congregational "Vahiguru!" The clusters remember:
- The Cherished Five (Panj Piare), the Guru's four sons, and the Forty Liberated; the devout, the charitable, the brave (~L3063–3071)
- Those Khalsa members who "gave their heads for their faith; who were hacked limb from limb, scalped, broken on the wheel or sawn asunder; who sacrificed their lives for the protection of hallowed gurdwaras, never forsaking their faith; and who were steadfast in their loyalty to the uncut hair of the true Sikh" (~L3072–3084)
- The five *takhats* and all gurdwaras (~L3086–3088)

**Section 3 — Prayer of Petition**: The final section is the actual petition. It includes:
- Petition that Vahiguru may dwell in the thoughts of the entire Khalsa; for protection, victory in battle, and triumph over enemies (~L3089–3100)
- Petition for true knowledge of the faith, blessing of uncut hair (*kes*), spiritual perception, patient trust, abiding faith, and the divine Name; for all to bathe at [[amritsar|Amritsar]] (~L3101–3108)
- Petition for humility of heart and sublimity of understanding; for access to Nankana Sahib and other gurdwaras from which the Khalsa has been separated (~L3109–3116) — a reference to sites in Pakistani Punjab following partition
- A flexible intercession slot: "refer here in appropriate words to the purpose for which the gathering or congregation has assembled" (~L3120–3122)
- Request for forgiveness of errors in reading scripture, fulfillment of tasks, and the company of the devout
- Closing couplet: "Nanak prays that the Name may be magnified; By your grace may all be blessed" (~L3128–3129)

The prayer concludes with three communal affirmations: **"Vahiguru ji ka Khalsa! Vahiguru ji ki fateh! Sat Sri Akal!"** (~L3130–3131)

## Fixed and Variable Elements

Only the **first eight lines** (the opening invocation of the Gurus) and the **concluding couplet** are unalterable. The wording of the second section varies: "Different versions of the second section are current in printed form, and personal or panthic intercessions are commonly introduced into the third section during the actual reciting of the prayer" (~L3022–3026). An approved text of Ardas has been published in *Sikh Rahit Maryada*, but "the right of individuals to vary the wording is specifically acknowledged" (~L3028–3036).

## Function and Context

Ardas is spoken by the leader of a congregation "except when it is included in personal devotions" (~L3004–3005). Its congregational dimension is central — the "Vahiguru!" responses punctuating the second section constitute a collective affirmation of Sikh identity, martyrdom memory, and divine presence.

As an authorized element of the [[gurmat|Sikh Rahit Maryada]], Ardas is one of the mechanisms by which the [[sikhs|Panth]] maintains its collective religious and historical identity across diaspora and homeland communities alike. It is the only composition in McLeod's appendix of scriptural translations in which the terms "Akal Purakh" and "Vahiguru" appear; McLeod omits them from all other translations for metrical reasons but retains them here (~L1339–1341).

## Key Theological Content

- Names all ten Gurus, culminating in identification of the Guru Granth Sahib as the living Guru
- Invokes the [[five-ks|Five Ks]] identity implicitly via "uncut hair of the true Sikh"
- Names the five takhats (including Akal Takhat at Amritsar) as places of Panth-wide devotion
- References Nankana Sahib (Guru Nanak's birthplace, in Pakistan) as a site of longing — reflecting the 1947 partition
- The petition affirms that the divine Name (*nam*) is the supreme gift, consistent with [[nam-simaran|Nam Simran]] theology throughout the Guru Granth Sahib
