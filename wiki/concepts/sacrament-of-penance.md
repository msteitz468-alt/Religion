# The Sacrament of Penance

**Penance** is the sacrament for the remission of sins committed after [[baptism]] — "the second
plank after shipwreck" (Jerome). [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats it across the end of the Tertia
Pars ([[summa-theologica|*ST*]] III Q84–Q90, where the *Summa* breaks off at his death) and the
**Supplement** (XP Q1–Q28), compiled posthumously from his *Scriptum* on the Sentences. It is the
one sacrament whose **matter** is the acts of the recipient himself.

## Penance as sacrament and as virtue (TP Q84–Q85)

- **As a sacrament** (Q84): its **matter** is the three acts of the penitent — **contrition,
  confession, satisfaction**; its **form** is the priestly absolution, "I absolve thee." It requires
  no imposition of hands. It is **necessary for salvation** (conditionally) for post-baptismal mortal
  sin, instituted in the New Law (the keys given to Peter), and — unlike Baptism, Confirmation, and
  Orders — it imprints no character and is therefore **repeatable** (the Novatians, who denied
  repetition, are named and refuted).
- **As a virtue** (Q85): penance is a species of **commutative justice** (rendering to God what is
  due for the offense), seated in the will, not a theological virtue. It originates from servile fear
  and is brought through hope and charity to filial fear.

## Effects: remission of sin (TP Q86–Q89)

All mortal sins are forgivable (the "sin against the Holy Ghost" = final impenitence); one mortal sin
cannot be pardoned without all; **temporal punishment can remain after the guilt is forgiven** (the
key difference from Baptism, which remits all punishment) — grounding the later doctrine of
satisfaction and [[indulgences]]. Venial sin requires only "virtual" displeasure and is remitted by
sacramentals (holy water, the Lord's Prayer, a bishop's blessing). Forgiven sins do **not** simply
return, though subsequent ingratitude "virtually" revives their debt. Penance **restores the lost
virtues** and the dignity of divine sonship (though not lost virginity); charitable works "deadened"
by mortal sin are **revived** by penance, while works done without charity cannot be quickened.

## The three parts in detail (Supplement XP Q1–Q16)

- **Contrition** (XP Q1–5): "assumed sorrow for sins, together with the purpose of confessing and
  making satisfaction." It is true sorrow of the will plus purpose of amendment; its object is one's
  own actual sins. **Attrition** (imperfect sorrow, from servile fear) is distinct from contrition
  and does not itself become it. Sufficiently intense contrition, drawing "infinite power from
  charity," can remit all temporal punishment.
- **Confession** (XP Q6–Q12): necessary by divine law for mortal sin after Baptism, and by the
  positive precept of **Lateran IV (1215)**. It must be made to a **priest with jurisdiction**
  (unique among the sacraments), be **entire** (all recalled mortal sins) and ordinarily oral and in
  person. The **seal of confession** is **absolute**: the priest "knows it as God knows it, not as
  man," and no authority — not even the Pope, nor a threat of excommunication — can compel its
  disclosure "by word, sign, or any other way."
- **Satisfaction** (XP Q12–Q16): an act of "vindictive justice" the penitent applies to himself,
  measured by proportionate equality (*satis*). It reconciles Anselm's "rendering God due honor" with
  the remedial aim of uprooting the causes of sin. Its **threefold means** — **almsgiving, fasting,
  prayer** — answer the three concupiscences of 1 John 2:16 (of the eyes, of the flesh, the pride of
  life). One person may satisfy for another **as to the debt of punishment** (in charity) but not as
  to the medicinal remedy; greater charity means less punishment is required.

## The power of the keys, excommunication (Supplement XP Q17–Q24)

The **power of the keys** (Matt. 16:19) is threefold: of *authority* (the
Trinity), of *excellence* (Christ), and of *ministry* (priests). The ministerial key has two parts —
**knowledge/discretion** (judging worthiness) and **power/jurisdiction** (the absolution). Following
Peter Lombard (a view Aquinas later revised), the keys *dispose* toward the remission of guilt while
**God alone causes it**, but they directly remit part of the temporal punishment. **Excommunication**
is medicinal, in minor (barring the sacraments) and major (barring communion) forms; it is imposed by
bishops of their own authority, by parish priests only on commission.

## Reception and divergence

This account — penance as a grace-conferring sacrament requiring priestly absolution, with the
guilt/punishment distinction underwriting satisfaction, [[purgatory]], and indulgences — is normative
for [[catholicism|Catholicism]] (confirmed at Trent). The **Protestant** Reformers denied penance
sacramental status, rejected the necessity of auricular confession to a priest and the entire
satisfaction/indulgence apparatus, and located forgiveness in faith and absolution as the
proclamation of the gospel. [[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodoxy]] retains confession and absolution but
without the developed satisfaction/indulgence scheme.
