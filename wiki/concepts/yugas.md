# The Four Yugas

The **Yugas** are the four **Ages of the world** in Hindu cosmology — a scheme of declining time named, as
[[wendy-doniger|Doniger]] notes (*[[doniger-the-hindus|The Hindus]]*, Ch. 2), after the **four throws of
the dice**. Counterintuitively, the *number* of the Age rises as the quality of life and the Age's length
*fall*:

1. **Kṛta Yuga** (the "Winning Age") / **Satya Yuga** ("Age of Truth") — the throw of **four**; the
   Greeks' Golden Age: humans virtuous and long-lived.
2. **Tretā Yuga** ("Age of the Trey") — the throw of **three**; less perfect.
3. **Dvāpara Yuga** ("Age of the Deuce") — the throw of **two**; "things fall apart."
4. **Kali Yuga** — the dice-throw of **snake eyes** (one): the present **Iron / Losing Age**, "when people
   are no damn good and die young, and barbarians invade India." Crucially, "unlike the other Ages, it is
   *now*, it is real."

The four are often imaged as the **four legs of dharma**, visualized as a cow who stands on four legs in
the Winning Age and "totters on one leg in the Losing Age."

## Linear *and* Cyclical Time

Doniger's key point: Indian time is **both linear and cyclical**. It is linear "as in Greece" (the ages
steadily decline) but, **unlike Greece**, also cyclical — "the end circles back to the beginning again."
Each Kali Age ends in a doomsday **dissolution** (*pralaya*) of fire and flood that destroys the cosmos and
then becomes the primeval flood from which it is re-created. This circularity is bound up with
**[[samsara|reincarnation]]** — "the circularity of the individual soul."

## The Flood and the Submarine Mare

The end-of-Yuga dissolution joins the great **flood myth**: in the oldest Indian version (the **Śatapatha
Brāhmaṇa**, c. 800 BCE), the first man **Manu** ("the Indian Adam") saves a little fish that grows huge
(later identified as an incarnation of **Viṣṇu**) and is in turn saved by it from the flood — "Manu alone
remained here." In the cosmic version the fire comes from the **submarine mare** at the bottom of the
ocean, whose flames (born of the fires of sexual desire and its ascetic repression) the flood both bridles
and unleashes at doomsday. A "remnant or seed" of good people always survives to repeople the next world.
Doniger links the flood myth to the possible historical flooding of the [[indus-valley-civilization|Indus
cities]] — while warning that modern "catastrophic flood" theories may themselves be "merely the latest
variants of the myth of the flood."
