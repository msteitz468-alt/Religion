# The Noble Eightfold Path

The **Noble Eightfold Path** (Pali *ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga*) is the fourth of the
[[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]] — the *magga*, the way leading to the
[[nirvana|cessation of suffering]]. It is the Buddha's **Middle Way** (*majjhimā
paṭipadā*) between the extremes of sensual indulgence and self-mortification, set out in
his first sermon (the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*).

## The Eight Factors

1. **Right View** (*sammā-diṭṭhi*) — understanding the [[four-noble-truths|Four Truths]],
   [[karma|kamma]], and [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]].
2. **Right Intention** (*sammā-saṅkappa*) — renunciation, good-will, harmlessness.
3. **Right Speech** (*sammā-vācā*) — no lying, divisive, harsh, or idle speech.
4. **Right Action** (*sammā-kammanta*) — no killing, stealing, sexual misconduct.
5. **Right Livelihood** (*sammā-ājīva*) — earning without harm.
6. **Right Effort** (*sammā-vāyāma*) — abandoning and preventing the unwholesome,
   arousing and maintaining the wholesome.
7. **Right Mindfulness** (*sammā-sati*) — clear awareness of body, feelings, mind, and
   mental objects (the *satipaṭṭhāna*).
8. **Right Concentration** (*sammā-samādhi*) — the meditative absorptions (*jhānas*).

## The Threefold Training

The eight factors are conventionally grouped into the **three trainings** (*ti-sikkhā*),
the spine of the whole path:

- **Sīla** (ethical conduct) — factors 3–5;
- **Samādhi** (mental discipline / concentration) — factors 6–8;
- **Paññā** (wisdom) — factors 1–2.

[[bhikkhu-bodhi|Bodhi]]'s anthology places the path under the **supramundane right view**
that orients the practitioner toward [[nirvana|liberation]], distinct from the *mundane*
right view aimed only at a [[samsara|fortunate rebirth]].

## "Right" and the Two Truths

The word translated "right" (*sammā*) means "complete / correct / in accordance with
reality," not merely morally upright. Notably the path is described as **noble** (*ariya*)
because it is the practice of those bent on awakening; the [[mahayana|Mahayana]]
[[heart-sutra|Heart Sutra]] negates "no path" (*na mārga*) at the ultimate level — not to
abolish practice but to deny the path's inherent existence under [[sunyata|emptiness]].
