# Going for Refuge (the Three Jewels)

Refuge in the **Three Jewels** — Buddha, Dharma (teaching), and Saṅgha (community) — is "the excellent
door for entering the teaching." In the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** (Vol. I, chs. 11–12),
[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] grounds it in two **causes**: fear of the lower realms, and conviction
that the Three Jewels can protect from them — "If they remain mere words, so will your refuge."

## Why the Buddha Alone Is a Worthy Refuge

Only one who is "free of all fears, who is skilled in the means of freeing others, who has great,
impartial compassion … is worthy of being a refuge. Since only the Buddha has these qualities, and
the divine creator and so on do not, he alone is the refuge." Refuge is taken by knowing the Three
Jewels' good qualities and distinctions, by commitment (the Buddha as **teacher**, the Dharma as the
**actual refuge**, the Saṅgha as **companions**), and by not seeking other ultimate refuges. The
"real refuge is the jewel of the teaching, because once you have attained it, you are liberated from
fear."

## The Precepts of Refuge

Tsong-kha-pa gives both a set from Asaṅga's *Compendium of Determinations* (rely on excellent
persons; hear the teaching; attend properly; practise conformingly — and: guard the senses, take up
the precepts, be compassionate, make periodic offerings) and the **oral tradition's** six precepts:
three proscriptive (do not take ultimate refuge in worldly deities; abandon harm to beings; do not
befriend those who repudiate the Three Jewels) and three prescriptive (revere images of the Buddha,
even poor ones, as the Teacher; treat scriptures with total respect; honour the Saṅgha and even those
who merely wear the robes). Plus six general precepts (repeated refuge, offering the first portion of
food, establishing others, supplicating in all activities "forsaking any other worldly methods,"
refuge thrice by day and night, and never forsaking the Jewels "even in jest or at the cost of your
life").
