# Generosity (Dāna)

The first of the **[[six-perfections|six perfections]]**. In [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s
**[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** (Vol. II), generosity is defined following Asaṅga's
*Bodhisattvabhūmi* as "the intention accompanying bodhisattvas' disinterested non-attachment to all
their possessions and their body, and, motivated by this, the physical and verbal actions of giving."

## A State of Mind

Its perfection does not depend on relieving the world's poverty — "Bringing the perfection of
generosity to completion is not contingent on removing beings' poverty." It is accomplished by
destroying "stingy clinging" and conditioning the mind to give away one's body, resources, and roots
of virtue. [[shantideva|Śāntideva]]: "Generosity is perfected, it is said, / Through the attitude of
giving away to all beings / All your possessions … / Therefore, generosity is a state of mind."

## Three Kinds

1. **The gift of the Dharma** — teaching the sublime teaching without error (and the arts).
2. **The gift of fearlessness** — protecting beings from humans, animals, and the elements.
3. **Material gifts** — both actually giving things and the **purely mental** generosity of imagining
   vast gifts offered to all beings (which "increases merit immeasurably with little difficulty").

Renunciate bodhisattvas emphasize the gift of teachings; lay bodhisattvas, material gifts. Giving
one's actual body is withheld "while your compassionate attitude is impure."

## The Casuistry of Giving (Ch. 10, "How to Give")

A detailed case-law drawn from the *Bodhisattvabhūmi* governs the **manner** of giving (give with
one's own hands, at the right time, respectfully, smiling, without harming others — thirteen faulty
manners are to be eliminated) and the limits of giving: what **not** to give (the body before
compassion matures or for trifling purposes; poison, weapons, traps, or political power given for
harm; texts to those seeking only to criticize) and the recipients. When confronted by a poor and a
rich petitioner at once, give to the poor first.

## Obstacles

The faults of **stinginess** (Sha-ra-wa: "I am not talking … about the benefits of giving; I am
talking … about the faults of tightfistedness"); seven wrong motivations (denial of karmic results,
arrogance, seeking fame, discouragement, partiality, expecting return, expecting fruition); and four
hindrances (not being used to giving, declining fortune, attachment, not seeing the goal).
