# Return (*Ra'fa*) — Bahá'í Concept

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Definition

*Return* (Ar.: *raj'a*) is a Shí'í doctrine according to which the appearance of the [[mahdi|Mahdí]] (the Hidden Imám) would be accompanied by the return of the Imáms and their followers. In Babi-Bahá'í belief it has become a more general concept, whereby more or less the same dramatis personae reappear in each religious dispensation to re-enact the same roles: the [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestation of God]]; his forerunners who announce his coming; and his chief disciples and opponents (lines 26775–26788).

## Application in Babi Theology

For the Báb — as Mahdí — the Prophet Muhammad, his daughter Fáṭima, the Imáms and their "Gates" (*abwáb*), together with those who believed in them, had all now returned, and were identified with the [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] ([[mulla-husayn|Mullá Husayn]] as Muhammad; Mullá 'Alí Basṭámí as the Imám 'Alí; etc.). Each was the return of the *reality* of the former personage. Similarly, for the Bábís the Babi struggle at [[tabarsi|Tabarsí]] was a re-enactment of Karbalá (lines 26788–26805).

## Application in Bahá'í Theology

[[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] claimed that he was the Báb "arrayed in his new attire," and noted that each Manifestation could claim that he was the "return" of all former prophets, and that those who were first to accept his call were the return of those who had been similarly distinguished in previous dispensations (KI 99, 102) (lines 26805–26813).

[[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] explained the Christian identification of John the Baptist with the Jewish prophet Elijah (Elias) as an expression of the return of the same qualities and perfections (SAQ 132–4). "Return" referred to inner perfections and not to the reincarnation of individual souls, a belief he regarded as absurd (SWAB 183) (lines 26816–26824).

## Distinction from Reincarnation

This strong rejection of metempsychosis (*tanásukh*) contrasts with the views of some of the more extreme Bábís. Bahá'í theology is clear: the soul comes into existence at the moment of conception and continues in some form of non-material existence after death. It does not return to earth. "Return" refers to the return of spiritual *qualities* and *roles*, not of individual souls (lines 26821–26827, 26373–26386).
