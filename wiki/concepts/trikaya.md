# Trikāya (the Three Buddha-bodies)

**Trikāya** (Sanskrit, "three bodies"; Tibetan *sku gsum*) is the Mahayana/Vajrayana doctrine of
the three **dimensions** (*kāya*) of a buddha. It is structurally central to the
[[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]], which maps the three to the stages of dying and the
three graded opportunities for liberation.

## The Three Bodies

- **Dharmakāya** (*chos-sku*, Buddha-body of Reality) — the ultimate, uncreated, empty yet
  naturally radiant nature of enlightened mind; "spacious like the sky." Realised at the moment
  of death, in the [[inner-radiance|inner radiance of the ground]].
- **Sambhogakāya** (*longs-sku*, Buddha-body of Perfect Resource) — luminous, immaterial forms of
  pure enlightened energy (the [[peaceful-and-wrathful-deities|peaceful and wrathful deities]]).
  Realised in the [[bardo|bardo of reality]].
- **Nirmāṇakāya** (*sprul-sku*, Buddha-body of Emanation) — visible, usually physical manifestation
  arising for the benefit of beings (e.g. Śākyamuni; a *tulku*). Realised, in the bardo scheme, in
  the bardo of rebirth.

In [[dzogchen|Dzogchen]] the three are said to be effortlessly present in stabilised
[[rigpa|intrinsic awareness]] itself: its emptiness is the dharmakāya, its radiance the
sambhogakāya, its unimpeded arising the nirmāṇakāya. (The Nyingma sometimes add a fourth and
fifth body.)
