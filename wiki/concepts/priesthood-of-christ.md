# The Priesthood of Christ

The **priesthood of Christ** is the doctrine that [[jesus|Christ]] is the true and eternal High
Priest who reconciles God and humanity by offering himself. In the NT the claim is developed most
fully in the [[hebrews|Epistle to the Hebrews]]. [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats it in
the [[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Tertia Pars Q22, drawing heavily on Hebrews; it is
the Christological ground of his [[atonement|theology of the Passion]] and of the
[[transubstantiation|eucharistic]] sacrifice. Evangelical commentary densification below is from
[[gareth-lee-cockerill|Cockerill]]’s NICNT reading of Hebrews (fidelity re-ingest 2026-07-18).

## Cockerill (NICNT) — Hebrews: all-sufficient High Priest

Attributed to [[gareth-lee-cockerill|Gareth Lee Cockerill]] ([[cockerill-hebrews-nicnt|Hebrews NICNT, 2012]]). Digests R3, R5, R6, R7, R8. All interpretive claims are Cockerill’s.

### Main theme and structure

For Cockerill, the eternal Son as all-sufficient High Priest is the **main theme** of Hebrews, announced at 2:17–18 after the purification-then-session seed of 1:3 (R3). Macro-section 4:14–10:18 presents that high priesthood as resource and urgency for perseverance (R5 L2386). Four parts in two pairs: 4:14–5:10 + 5:11–6:20 introduce and prepare; 7:1–28 + 8:1–10:18 expound legitimacy/eternity and all-sufficient sacrifice (R5 L2388–2390). Father–Son conversation pattern: ch. 7 (Father addresses the eternal exalted High Priest) answers in 8–10 (Son’s incarnate obedience, esp. Ps 40 in 10:5–10) as 1:1–14 answers in 2:5–18 (R5 L2394). High priesthood is **intrinsic** to the Son’s identity as Savior and Revealer — not a mere rhetorical device; “Aaron is the metaphor; the Son is the true ‘Great High Priest’ (4:14)” (R2 L1693).

### Great High Priest; sympathy; throne of grace (4:14–5:10)

“Great high priest” intensifies superiority over Levitical priests; perfect participle “who has gone through the heavens” = access as present reality (R5 L2414–2416). Greek “sympathy” means **active assistance** — grace of forgiveness and victory over temptation — not Aaronic “deal gently” from shared sinfulness (R5 L2426; 2468–2472). Tempted “in every way… without sin”: lifelong perfect obedience, not limited to fear of suffering (R5 L2428–2432). Bold approach to the throne of grace: under the old order only the high priest approached the Ark-throne yearly in fear; through Christ’s obedient sacrifice that throne is a true mercy seat (R5 L2434–2438). Ps 2:7 + Ps 110:4 merge sonship and priesthood: legitimacy alongside Aaron, exposure of Aaron as antiquated, sufficiency because he is Son (R5 L2492–2494). “Having been perfected” = **vocational perfection** as Savior through accepted obedience → right-hand session; Source of eternal salvation for “those who obey him” (R5 L2534–2540). Forerunner Jesus opens the way inside the veil (6:19–20); “High Priest forever after the order of Melchizedek” resumes the deferred theme (R5 L2728–2734).

### Legitimacy and eternity (Heb 7)

Exposition of Ps 110:4 via Gen 14 (see [[melchizedek|Melchizedek]]). Perfection unattainable under the Levitical priesthood = definitive access to God; change of priesthood necessarily a change of the law as means of approach (R6 L2826–2836). Non-Aaronic: “our Lord has **arisen** from Judah” (not “descended”) — even in incarnation he receives no authority from ancestry (R6 L2838–2846). Priest “according to the **likeness** of Melchizedek” by “power of an **indestructible life**” — eternal life of God in the Son’s Person, not first possessed only at resurrection (R6 L2848–2862). Divine oath makes him **Guarantor** (not merely Mediator) of a better covenant (R6 L2882–2890). Many priests prevented by death vs. **inviolable** priesthood of the one who “remains”; able to save completely those who come to God through him; intercession = postsacrificial representation, not endless petitioning after a still-unfinished offering (R6 L2896–2920). Once-for-all self-offering vs. daily/double need of old high priests (R6 L2948–2954). “A Son having been perfected forever” integrates eternal sonship, incarnation, obedience, self-offering, exaltation, and present session (R6 L2956–2964).

### Symphony 8:1–10:18: sanctuary / sacrifice / covenant

Cockerill’s structural metaphor: three movements (8:1–13; 9:1–22; 9:23–10:18), each sanctuary → expanding sacrifice → covenant (R2 L1621; R7 L2974). One self-offering functions three ways: Day-of-Atonement sin-removal; high-priestly consecration authorizing heavenly entry; covenant-establishing sacrifice making him Mediator/Guarantor (R7 L2980).

**Main point (8:1–2):** “We have such a high priest who has sat down… minister of the Sanctuary and the true Tent that the Lord… has pitched.” Session is not exaltation-language alone but the eternal Son who achieved his seat by obedient self-offering — present available reality (R7 L2988–2998). Against Philo/Platonism: Sanctuary is the **place of God’s presence** entered by the incarnate/exalted Son, not a world of ideas; “true Tent” = heaven itself as Most Holy Place only (no two-part heavenly temple) (R7 L3004–3010). See [[tabernacle|Tabernacle]].

**Once-for-all and session (9:25–10:14):** Entered once for all by own blood, not goats/calves; eternal redemption; does **not** carry blood into heaven — entered “by means of” blood shed on the cross (R7 L3172–3178). Blood of Christ cleanses conscience from dead works to serve the living God (R7 L3180–3200). Third movement: once-for-all vs year-after-year; “abolition of sin” (singular = principle/force) at the climax of the ages (R8 L3290–). Ps 40:6–8 (10:5–10) is the **heart** of the sacrifice symphony: obedient human life offered on the cross; “body you prepared”; “we have been made holy… once for all” (R8). Standing priests (job never done) vs. one who **sat down forever** by one sacrifice; Ps 110:1a fulfilled, 1b pending at second coming; present intercession = mediated grace (R8 L10:11–14).

**High Priest and New-Covenant Mediator** are two sides of one ministry: High Priest cleanses the heart; Mediator removes Old-covenant condemnation; inseparable because the New Covenant writes law on cleansed hearts (R8 on 9:15; 10:15–18). See [[new-covenant|New Covenant]].

### Benefits and pastoral aim

Full repertoire: deliverance from covenant curse, forgiveness, cleansing, law on the heart, present access, ultimate entrance (R2 L1693–1695). Appeal depends on authentic cleansing and true entrance; draw near (4:14–16; 10:19–25) is the means of perseverance. Apostasy severs from the once-for-all provision — “Where, now, can they go for deliverance?” (R5 L2646–2648).

## Aquinas's account (ST III Q22)

- **It is fitting that Christ be a priest** (A1): "the office proper to a priest is to be a mediator
  between God and the people" — bestowing divine things on the people and offering the people's prayers
  and satisfaction to God. See [[christ-as-mediator]].
- **Christ is both priest and victim** (A2): uniquely, the one who offers and the offering are the
  same — "Christ offered the most perfect sacrifice… and hence it was fitting that He should be both
  priest and victim" (*sacerdos et hostia*). This is the hinge of the [[atonement]] as **sacrifice**.
- **The effect is the expiation of sins** (A3): cleansing both "the stain of sin and the debt of
  punishment."
- **It pertains to Christ himself** (A4): not as needing expiation (He had no sin) but in that his
  human nature was glorified through his sacrifice.
- **It is eternal** (A5): not in the offering (made once) but in the **consummation** — the
  perpetual intercession by which those for whom the sacrifice was offered attain its end.
- **After the order of [[melchizedek|Melchizedek]]** (A6): not the Levitical/Aaronic line. Aquinas
  reads Melchizedek (Gen. 14; Heb. 7) as the type prefiguring Christ's priesthood — by his offering of
  **bread and wine** (figure of the Eucharist), his being **outside the one tribe** of Levi, and his
  being "without genealogy," i.e. eternal.

## Significance and reception

Christ's priesthood grounds the Catholic theology of the Mass as the re-presentation of the one
sacrifice of Calvary (see [[transubstantiation]]) and the ministerial priesthood of [[holy-orders]] as
a participation in Christ's. It is shared in substance with [[eastern-orthodoxy|Eastern Orthodoxy]];
the **Protestant** Reformers affirmed Christ's unique high priesthood and his eternal intercession but
**rejected** the sacrificing ministerial priesthood and the eucharistic sacrifice they took it to
imply, stressing instead the priesthood of all believers and the once-for-all sufficiency of Calvary
(Hebrews).
