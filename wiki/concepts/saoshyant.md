# The Saoshyant (the coming Savior)

The **Saoshyant** (Avestan, "one who will bring benefit," the future Benefactor) is the
[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] **eschatological savior** who appears at the end of the age to lead
the final defeat of evil and inaugurate [[frashokereti|Frashokereti]], the renovation of the world.
It is the religion's principal contribution to the history of **messianic** expectation.

## Development of the Idea

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], [[zarathustra|Zoroaster]] in the Gathas
speaks of the *saoshyant(s)* — "those who will bring benefit," seemingly including himself and his
followers as agents of the world's healing — and appears to have expected the consummation **soon**.
As that hope was deferred, later tradition developed the figure into a **definite coming savior**,
and ultimately **three** future Saoshyants, born across millennia from Zoroaster's own
miraculously preserved seed (kept in a lake, to quicken virgin mothers). The **last Saoshyant**,
**Astvat-ereta** ("he who embodies righteousness"), presides over the general resurrection and the
final ordeal.

### The Name and the Young Avestan Development (Rose)

[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]] derives *saoshyant* from the future
participle of *su-* "to swell, energize, be strong" (cf. *spenta*): "**one who will be
strong / will revitalize**." In the [[gathas|Gathas]] it appears six times — singular
(perhaps [[zarathustra|Zarathustra]] himself) and plural (several future benefactors).
Young Avestan texts both **multiply** the saoshyants (victorious priests, Haoma-venerators,
fighters against the Lie) and **single out three**: the **Farvardin Yasht** names a final
one, **Astvat-ereta** ("through whom asha *becomes corporeal*," echoing the Gathic *astvat
ashem*), and the **Zamyad Yasht** (Yt 19) shows him arising from **Lake Kansaoya** (Lake
Hamun in Sistan) wielding the hero's weapon, to **raise the dead and make existence
"indestructible"** — a developed bodily-resurrection schema centuries before the Sasanian
period. In later Iranian hope the awaited savior is **Behram Warzawand** ("the victorious
miracle-worker").

## Role at the End

- The Saoshyant leads the **last great battle** in which the Yazatas annihilate the Daevas and the
  legions of darkness.
- He presides over the **raising of the dead** and the **general Last Judgment** that divides all
  righteous from all wicked (see [[chinvat-bridge|the Chinvat Bridge]] for the prior individual
  judgment).
- Through the **river of molten metal** and the last *yasna* offered by [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]
  and the [[amesha-spentas|Amesha Spentas]] — yielding the "white haoma" that confers immortality —
  the blessed are made deathless and [[angra-mainyu|Angra Mainyu]] is destroyed forever.

## Significance and Influence

A **single (or serial) eschatological deliverer**, virgin-associated in birth, who comes to defeat
evil decisively and raise the dead, is among the most frequently cited candidate antecedents for
later **Jewish messianism** and the **Christian** expectation of a returning savior — the very
heart of the [[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|influence question]], recorded
without overclaiming. The Saoshyant binds together the [[the-three-times|linear-time]] schema, the
[[frashokereti|final renovation]], and bodily [[resurrection-of-the-dead|resurrection]] into a
coherent redemptive climax.
