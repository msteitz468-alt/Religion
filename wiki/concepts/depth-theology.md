# Depth-Theology

**Depth-theology** is [[abraham-joshua-heschel|Abraham Joshua Heschel]]'s name for the
method of [[god-in-search-of-man|*God in Search of Man*]]. Where ordinary theology examines
the *content* of believing — creeds, dogmas, doctrines — depth-theology examines the **act
of believing**: the pre-conceptual, pre-creedal substratum and the moments of immediacy out
of which faith arises. "The theme of theology is the content of believing. The theme of the
present study is the act of believing."

## Method

Depth-theology seeks "to ascertain not so much what the person is able to express as that
which he is unable to express" — "the insights that no language can declare." It rests on
several methodological commitments developed in Part I of the book:

- **Recovering the questions.** "The primary task of philosophy of religion is to
  rediscover the questions to which religion is an answer." An answer without a living
  question is "devoid of life."
- **Situational over conceptual thinking.** "Conceptual thinking is an act of reasoning;
  situational thinking involves an inner experience; in uttering judgment about an issue,
  the person himself is under judgment." Its beginning "is not doubt, detachment, but
  amazement, awe, involvement" — the philosopher is "a witness, not an accountant."
- **Reading the Bible in its own categories.** "Hebrew thinking operates within categories
  different from those of Plato or Aristotle"; the Bible "represents a *way of thinking*,"
  not a sum of doctrines, so a philosophy of Judaism must not translate it into Greek,
  Kantian, or positivist terms. Heschel faults both Spinoza (Bible as naïve myth) and
  [[philo-of-alexandria|Philo]] (Bible as Greek philosophy in disguise) for flattening
  biblical distinctiveness.
- **Preconceptual thinking.** "The living encounter with reality takes place on a level
  that … is responsive, immediate, preconceptual, and presymbolic." Concepts are "second
  thoughts"; philosophy of religion "must be an effort to recall and to keep alive the
  meta-symbolic relevance of religious terms."

## Relation to Other Concepts

Depth-theology takes [[radical-amazement]], [[awe]], and the sense of [[the-ineffable]] as
its data. It underwrites Heschel's "elliptic thinking" (philosophy and religion as two foci
of one ellipse) and his epistemological reversal, "we praise before we prove." It is the
methodological counterpart to his central thesis of [[divine-pathos|God in search of man]].
