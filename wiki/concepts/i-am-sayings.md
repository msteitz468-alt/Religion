# I Am Sayings (*Egō Eimi*)

The distinctive Johannine formula by which Jesus identifies himself. The Greek *egō eimi* (I am) appears in two forms in the Gospel of John: **absolute** (without a predicate, "I am") and **predicated** (with a complement, "I am the X"). Both forms are without parallel in the Synoptic tradition and represent the sharpest expression of John's high Christology.

## The Absolute Form

The absolute *egō eimi* — "I am," with no predicate — functions as an echo of the divine name. In the LXX (Septuagint), the divine self-disclosure of Exodus 3:14 and the "I am he" formula of Deutero-Isaiah (LXX Isa 41:4; 43:10, 25; 46:4; 48:12) are rendered with *egō eimi*. When Jesus uses the formula absolutely, the resonance is with YHWH's self-identification.

Key instances:

- **8:24**: "Unless you believe that *I am*, you will die in your sins."
- **8:28**: "When you lift up the Son of Man, then you will know that *I am*, and that I do nothing on my own."
- **8:58**: "Before Abraham was, *I am*." — the clearest divine claim; immediately his hearers pick up stones.
- **13:19**: "I tell you now, before it happens, so that when it happens you may believe that *I am*."
- **18:5–6**: When Judas and the soldiers come to arrest Jesus, he says "*I am*" and they fall to the ground — an involuntary recognition of the divine presence.

The transition to Jesus' use of the emphatic first person is structural in John. [[j-ramsey-michaels|Michaels]] notes that in chs. 1–3, the emphatic "I" belongs to John the Baptizer ("I am not the Christ," 1:20; "I am a voice," 1:23), not to Jesus. Jesus first uses the absolute *egō eimi* at 4:26 (to the Samaritan woman), and from ch. 4 onward it becomes his characteristic mode of self-disclosure.

## The Predicated Form

Seven predicated "I am" statements with a complement:

| Reference | Statement |
|-----------|-----------|
| 6:35, 51 | "I am the bread of life" |
| 8:12; 9:5 | "I am the light of the world" |
| 10:7, 9 | "I am the gate/door for the sheep" |
| 10:11, 14 | "I am the good shepherd" |
| 11:25 | "I am the resurrection and the life" |
| 14:6 | "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" |
| 15:1, 5 | "I am the true vine" |

Each predicated statement is embedded in a discourse or narrative context that develops its meaning. They collectively name Jesus as the exclusive source of life, knowledge, access to God, and salvation — replacing and fulfilling the institutions and figures of Israel's story (manna, pillar of fire, shepherd-king, Torah, vine of Israel).

## Structural Role in John

The "I Am" sayings work in tandem with the **signs** (*sēmeia*). The seven signs are: (1) water to wine (2:1–11); (2) healing of the royal official's son (4:46–54); (3) healing of the man at Bethesda (5:1–15); (4) feeding of the five thousand (6:1–14); (5) walking on water (6:16–21); (6) healing the man born blind (ch. 9); (7) raising of Lazarus (ch. 11). The signs display; the discourses interpret. The feeding (sign 4) is followed by the "bread of life" discourse; the healing of the blind man (sign 6) is preceded by "I am the light of the world"; the raising of Lazarus (sign 7) is preceded by "I am the resurrection and the life."

This sign-discourse pairing is unique to John and has no Synoptic equivalent.

## Divine Name Background

The absolute *egō eimi* in John 8:58 ("Before Abraham was, *I am*") is particularly striking because it uses *ēn* (imperfect, was) for Abraham's past existence but *eimi* (present) for Jesus — a grammatical claim to eternal, timeless self-existence identical to the divine name. The hostile response (stoning, 8:59; 10:31; 11:8) confirms the Jewish interlocutors understand this as a divine claim, not mere self-identification.

The same formula at 18:5–6, when the soldiers fall to the ground at *egō eimi*, recalls theophanies throughout the Hebrew Bible in which the divine presence prostrates human beings (cf. Ezek 1:28; Dan 8:17).

## Relation to the Synoptics

The Synoptic Jesus announces "the kingdom of God" and speaks with "exousia" (authority) that astonishes crowds. The Johannine Jesus announces *himself* as the source of life, light, truth, and the way to the Father. Michaels: "What Jesus reveals from the Father is simply that he is the Revealer, sent from the Father. In so doing he reveals the Father — which is not so different from saying that he reveals the kingdom of God." The difference is that the implicit becomes explicit: the "I" of "But I tell you" in the Sermon on the Mount becomes the magisterial "I am" of John 8:58.

## Controversy: Divine Identity or Prophetic Formula?

Some scholars (e.g., Bultmann in a different way) have argued that the absolute *egō eimi* is simply a formula of self-identification without divine resonance — "It is I." The divine name background is thus disputed. However, the narrative contexts (8:59; 10:31; 18:5–6) strongly support the divine name interpretation: the reactions are those of people who understand a divine claim, not a mere identification.
