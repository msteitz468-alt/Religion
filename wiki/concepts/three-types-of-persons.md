# The Three Types of Persons

The organizing architecture of the *lam rim* graded path, inherited by [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s
**[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** from [[atisha|Atiśa]]'s *Lamp for the Path*. All of the Buddha's
teaching is sorted into the paths of three ascending capacities (*skyes bu gsum*):

1. **Small capacity** (*skyes bu chung ngu*) — those who, turning from this life, "diligently strive
   to attain solely the joys of cyclic existence" — a fortunate rebirth (high status) in future lives.
2. **Medium capacity** (*skyes bu 'bring*) — those who, disenchanted with all cyclic existence,
   "diligently strive just for their own peace" — individual liberation (the śrāvaka/pratyekabuddha
   goal).
3. **Great capacity** (*skyes bu chen po*) — the [[bodhisattva|bodhisattvas]] who, understanding their
   own suffering, "sincerely want to extinguish all the sufferings of others" — buddhahood for all,
   via the [[six-perfections|perfections]] and (optionally) the mantra vehicle.

## All Scripture Subsumed; Lower Stages as Branches of the Mahāyāna

The schema is both a taxonomy of practitioners and a **hermeneutic**: every teaching is reframed as a
*stage* within one integrated path rather than a separate, lower vehicle. Crucially, "the stages of
the path for the person of great capacity also include the paths for the other two types of persons
in their entirety. Thus, these two paths are parts, or branches, of the Mahāyāna path." The
small/medium practices a great-capacity aspirant undertakes (refuge, karma, renunciation) are
**prerequisites** for [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]], not detours — Tsong-kha-pa warns that losing this
integration would leave the three paths "separate and unrelated."

Its two purposes: it deflates the presumption of claiming great capacity without the lower
realizations, and it benefits practitioners of every level. The staged order (high status →
liberation → omniscience) is argued from Nāgārjuna, Asaṅga, Āryadeva, and Mātṛceṭa, not merely
asserted. The definitions also draw on Asaṅga's *Compendium of Determinations* and Vasubandhu's
*Abhidharmakośa*.
