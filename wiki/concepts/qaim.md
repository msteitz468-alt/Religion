# Qá'im

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Definition

Qá'im (Ar.: "the one who will arise [from the family of Muhammad]") is a Shí'í term for the [[mahdi|Mahdí]] and one of the titles of the Hidden Imám (see [[shi-ism|Shí'ísm]]). According to Babi and Bahá'í belief, [[the-bab|the Báb]] was the Qá'im/Mahdí (lines 25757–25763).

The concept is central to the doctrine of [[bahai-return|return]] (*raj'a*) in Babi-Bahá'í theology: the appearance of the Qá'im was expected to be accompanied by the return of the Imáms and their followers. The Báb identified his followers — the [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] — with those returning figures (lines 26777–26801).
