# Covenant (Bahá'í)

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims grounded in lines 9926–10167 of that source.*

The Arabic term is *'ahd* (agreement, covenant). For Bahá'ís the Covenant is the doctrinal mechanism by which the unity of the Faith is assured and schism prevented (line 9981–9982). 'Abdu'l-Bahá described it as 'the most great characteristic' of Bahá'u'lláh's religion (line 9980), 'the pivot of the oneness of mankind' (line 9983), and a fortress to protect the Bahá'ís (line 9986).

## Two Forms of the Covenant (lines 9931–9946)

### The Greater Covenant
The covenant between each [[manifestation-of-god|Manifestation of God]] and his followers regarding the promise of the next Manifestation, expressed in [[progressive-revelation|prophecy]]. In the Western tradition, each Manifestation in the series Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, the Báb, and Bahá'u'lláh prophesied the appearance of the next, and their respective followers were duty-bound to investigate the claims of the following Manifestation.

### The Lesser Covenant
The covenant a Manifestation makes concerning his immediate successor, to whom followers should turn and obey. Historical precedents cited are the appointment of the Apostle Peter by Jesus, and of the Imám 'Alí by Muhammad. In both cases the covenant was neither written nor sufficiently explicit, resulting in Christianity and Islam becoming 'riven by contending sects' (lines 9954–9959).

By contrast, the Bahá'í Lesser Covenant is both explicit and in written form (lines 9960–9962):
- Bahá'u'lláh appointed 'Abdu'l-Bahá as Centre of his Covenant in the [[book-of-the-covenant|Book of the Covenant]] (Kitáb-i-'Ahd)
- 'Abdu'l-Bahá's [[will-and-testament|Will and Testament]] directed Bahá'ís to follow [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]
- 'Abdu'l-Bahá also promised divine guidance and protection for the as-yet-unelected [[universal-house-of-justice|Universal House of Justice]]

Those who reject any link in this chain of succession are regarded as [[covenant-breakers|Covenant-breakers]] (line 9974–9976).

## The General Covenant (lines 9994–10002)
There is also a more general covenant by which God bestows his bounties on humanity and in return demands recognition of his messengers and obedience to his laws. Faithfulness to this covenant requires a distinctive pattern of behaviour marked by morality and piety.

## Book of the Covenant (Kitáb-i-'Ahd) (lines 10003–10045)
Bahá'u'lláh's last testament, written in Arabic in his own handwriting, given to 'Abdu'l-Bahá during his final illness (May 1892). It was read to witnesses and a large gathering of Bahá'ís on the ninth day after Bahá'u'lláh's passing. Key provisions:
- Explicitly appoints 'Abdu'l-Bahá (the *ghusn-i-a'zam*, Most Great Branch) as successor
- Directs Bahá'u'lláh's family and the Afnán relatives of the Báb to turn to 'Abdu'l-Bahá
- Bahá'u'lláh's second surviving son, Mírzá Muḥammad-'Alí (*ghusn-i-akbar*, Greatest Branch), is subordinate to 'Abdu'l-Bahá
- Conflict is forbidden; God's domain is the hearts of men; rulership is entrusted to kings
- The rulers (*umará*) and learned (*'ulamá*) among the Bahá'ís are Bahá'u'lláh's trustees

## Covenant-Breakers (lines 10046–10167)
Bahá'í term (Arabic: *náqiḍín*) for those who break (*naqḍ*) the Covenant by denying the legitimacy of 'Abdu'l-Bahá, Shoghi Effendi, or the Universal House of Justice, or wilfully opposing their authority. The term also refers to [[azalis|Azalí Bábís]]. It does not apply to those who simply leave the Faith.

'Abdu'l-Bahá declared his half-brother Mírzá Muḥammad-'Alí and his followers to be Covenant-breakers, expelling the larger part of Bahá'u'lláh's family from the Faith (lines 10082–10095). He also expelled Dr Ameen U. Fareed (Amínu'lláh Fáríd) for financial misconduct (lines 10096–10106).

After 'Abdu'l-Bahá's death (1921), the principal opposition movements were:
- In the United States: Ruth White and Ahmad Sohrab (lines 10134–10139)
- In Egypt: a short-lived opposition movement (line 10141)
- In Iran: Aváríh apostatized and attacked the Faith in his writings (lines 10142–10144)
- Shoghi Effendi expelled a widening circle of his relatives who contracted marriages with existing Covenant-breaking members of Bahá'u'lláh's family (lines 10146–10153)
- After Shoghi Effendi's death (1957): C.M. Remey claimed to be the second Guardian; his movement subsequently splintered (see [[remeyite-groups|Remeyite Groups]]) (lines 10155–10161)

Globally, the Bahá'í community has maintained its unity (line 10167).
