# Diyu (the Chinese underworld)

**Dìyù** 地獄 ("earth-prison") is the **underworld of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]]** — a
**subterranean court-and-prison system** where the souls of the dead are **judged, punished, purified, and sent
back to rebirth**. It is the **judicial branch of the [[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]**, and a
striking example of folk religion's **syncretism**: a fusion of an indigenous bureaucratic afterlife with the
Buddhist machinery of **karma, hells, and reincarnation**, organized in Daoist–Buddhist form.

## Structure

- Diyu is governed by **[[yan-wang|Yan Wang]] (Yánluó Wáng)**, "King Yama," adapted from the Indian
  **[[yama|Yama]]** — the chief magistrate of the dead.
- It is organized as **Ten Courts**, each under a **Yama-King**, through which a soul passes in sequence. Each
  court tries specific offenses and assigns specific **punishments** (the lurid hells of boiling, freezing,
  the tongue-ripping pool for liars, the mountain of knives, etc.) — penal, judicial, and **temporary**.
- After judgment and expiation, the soul reaches the **last court** and the goddess **Mèng Pó**, whose **broth
  of forgetfulness** erases the memory of the past life, before crossing the bridge to **reincarnation** on the
  **Wheel of Rebirth**.

## Belief and practice

Diyu is **not eternal damnation** but a **purgatorial, correctional passage**: punishment fits the crime, is
finite, and ends in rebirth. The living can **aid the dead** through merit — Buddhist and Daoist rites,
offerings, and **spirit-money** burned to fund the soul's passage and bribe its way through the courts. The
**City God (Chenghuang)** delivers the newly dead to Diyu, linking the [[household-gods-cult|local
bureaucracy]] to the courts below.
