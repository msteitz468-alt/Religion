# Dao

The **Dao** ("Way") is the central concept of [[daoism|Daoism]]: the nameless, formless source and
pattern from which all things arise and according to which they move. The [[daodejing|Daodejing]] insists
it is fundamentally **ineffable** — "the Dao that can be told is not the eternal Dao" — and prior to all
distinctions, even being and non-being.

## Range of Meaning

- As **cosmic source/ground** — that from which the "ten thousand things" are generated ([[wang-bi|Wang
  Bi]]: the Dao as *wu*, non-being, the root of being).
- As **the course/pattern of nature** — the spontaneous way things go when uncontrived ([[ziran|ziran]]),
  which the sage follows by [[wu-wei|wu-wei]].
- (In **[[religious-daoism|religious Daoism]]**) as a reality to be **embodied** through cultivation, the
  ground of immortality.

Note the term is shared with **Confucianism**, where *dao* means rather the right **moral-social Way** of
the sages — a revealing contrast (the cosmic-spontaneous Dao vs. the ethical-cultural Dao).

## Cross-Tradition Comparison

The ineffable Dao is a prime comparandum for the wiki's other "beyond-name" ultimates: apophatic
[[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] (Kabbalah), Buddhist [[sunyata|emptiness]], and *nirguna* [[brahman|Brahman]]
(Advaita) — all resist predication, yet the Dao is distinctively **immanent and naturalistic** (a way of
*nature*, not a transcendent God or a void). A strong candidate for a comparison page on ineffable
ultimates.
