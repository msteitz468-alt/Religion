# The Six Perfections

The **six perfections** (*ṣaṭpāramitā*) are the trainings that constitute the engaged
[[bodhisattva|bodhisattva]] path, undertaken once [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]] has been generated:

1. **Generosity** (*dāna*)
2. **Ethical discipline** (*śīla*)
3. **Patience** (*kṣānti*)
4. **Joyous perseverance** (*vīrya*)
5. **Meditative stabilization** (*dhyāna*)
6. **Wisdom** (*prajñā*)

They are the principal subject of the second half of the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]**'s
great-capacity path. **[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]** treats the first four (and an overview of the
last two) in Volume Two, following the Indian sources — above all
**[[shantideva|Śāntideva]]**'s [[bodhicaryavatara|Bodhicaryāvatāra]] and Asaṅga's
*Bodhisattvabhūmi*.

## The Definite Number, Order, and Nature of the Perfections

Following Maitreya's *Ornament for the Mahāyāna Sūtras* (as read by Asaṅga and Haribhadra),
[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] gives the six as a closed and ordered set, justified on several grounds —
e.g. in terms of the **three trainings** (ethics = ethical discipline, with generosity as
precondition and patience as aid; concentration = meditative stabilization; wisdom = wisdom; joyous
perseverance pervading all three). The **order** is one of arising (each perfection is the
precondition of the next), of inferior-to-superior, and of coarse-to-subtle.

A "perfection" (*pāramitā*) is defined as a **state of mind perfected by habituation**, not an
external accomplishment: "the physical and verbal aspects of generosity are not the main thing; the
main thing is the mental aspect" — generosity is perfected by destroying stinginess, "Therefore,
generosity is a state of mind" (Śāntideva). The same localizing move governs each perfection.

The six unite the **factor of method** (the collection of merit — generosity, ethics, patience) and
the **factor of wisdom** (the collection of sublime wisdom); both are required, per the *Teaching of
Vimalakīrti*: "Wisdom that is not imbued with method is bondage; … method not imbued with wisdom is
bondage." On this basis Tsong-kha-pa refutes the **Hva-shang** position that emptiness-meditation
alone suffices (see [[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]] and [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]). Wisdom is the
"eye" that completes the other five: without it "these five perfections … are blind."

## Each Perfection (per the Lamrim Chenmo, Vol. II)

1. **Generosity (dāna)** — non-attached giving of material things, fearlessness, and the Dharma; its
   perfection lies in the attitude, governed by a detailed casuistry (drawn from Asaṅga's
   *Bodhisattvabhūmi*) of what, when, how, and to whom to give — and what *not* to give. See
   [[generosity-dana|generosity]].
2. **Ethical discipline (śīla)** — "an attitude of abstention that turns your mind away from harming
   others"; three kinds: restraint (the prātimokṣa vows / abstention from the ten nonvirtues),
   gathering virtue, and acting for beings. The restraint discipline is the foundation that "includes
   and reaches to the other two."
3. **Patience (kṣānti)** — see [[patience-kshanti|patience]]: disregarding harm, accepting suffering,
   and certitude about the Dharma; carried by Śāntideva's arguments against anger.
4. **Joyous perseverance (vīrya)** — "delight in virtue"; armor-like, gathering-virtue, and
   acting-for-beings forms; developed by four powers (aspiration, steadfastness, joy, relinquishment)
   against the three lazinesses. See [[joyous-perseverance-virya|joyous perseverance]].
5. **Meditative stabilization (dhyāna)** — "a virtuous, one-pointed state of mind"; treated only in
   overview here, the full method deferred to the śamatha section (Volume Three).
6. **Wisdom (prajñā)** — discernment of the ontological status of objects; three kinds (ultimate,
   conventional/the five sciences, and how to act for beings); full treatment of the wisdom of
   selflessness deferred to the vipaśyanā section (Volume Three).

> Tsong-kha-pa's own lament (Vol. II): "Nowadays, from among the six perfections … there exist in
> slight measure the stages of the practice of meditative stabilization, but the stages of the
> practice of the other five perfections have disappeared."

## Relation to the Four Ways to Gather Disciples

The six perfections mature the bodhisattva's *own* buddha-qualities; the **four ways to gather
disciples** (saṃgrahavastu — giving, pleasant speech, meaningful conduct, and concordance of one's
own conduct) mature *others*. Together they are "the active expression of a bodhisattva's virtuous
qualities" (*Bodhisattvabhūmi*).
