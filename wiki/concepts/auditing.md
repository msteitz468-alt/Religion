# Auditing

**Auditing** is the central practice of [[scientology|Scientology]] — a form of one-on-one counseling in
which an **auditor** asks a set sequence of questions to guide the subject (the "preclear") to locate and
discharge [[engram|engrams]] and other spiritual barriers. It is to Scientology what prayer, ritual, or
meditation are to other traditions: the core technology of salvation.

## The E-meter

Auditing uses the **E-meter** (electropsychometer), a device the preclear holds (two cans) that measures
small changes in electrical resistance; the auditor reads its needle as an indicator of "charge" on a
subject — where an engram or upset lies. The Church regards the E-meter as a confessional/spiritual
instrument (a legal characterization important to its US religious status).

## The Bridge to Total Freedom

Auditing is organized as a graded, sequential path — the **Bridge to Total Freedom** — with precisely
defined levels and prices. Major milestones:

- **Clear** — the state of having erased the reactive mind ([[engram|engrams]]).
- The **Operating Thetan (OT) levels** — confidential upper levels (OT I–VIII and beyond) in which the
  freed [[thetan|thetan]] regains spiritual abilities; **OT III** contains the **Xenu** cosmology and the
  auditing-away of "body thetans."

## Hermeneutic — Standard Tech

Auditing must be delivered as **"standard tech"** — exactly as [[l-ron-hubbard|Hubbard]] specified, with no
deviation ("**Keeping Scientology Working**"). Altering the procedure ("squirreling") is the cardinal sin,
and the boundary between the [[church-of-scientology|Church]] and the [[free-zone-scientology|Free Zone]].

## Urban on Auditing

Urban (*The Church of Scientology*, Princeton 2011) traces the E-meter's origin to **Volney Mathison's** device, which Hubbard adapted from around 1951–52. After a dispute, Hubbard and Mathison split in 1954. Hubbard rebranded the E-meter as a "religious artifact used as a spiritual guide in the church confessional" (1973 policy letter) — a deliberate reclassification to insulate it from FDA medical-device regulation, one of the most explicit examples of the "religion angle" in operational practice.

The E-meter as confessional was critical to the **Security Check / Sec Check** — interrogation sessions ("Have you ever enslaved a population?"; "Did you come to Earth for evil purposes?") that used E-meter responses as lie-detector-style indicators of hidden transgression. These continued under the name "integrity processing" after Fair Game was officially canceled.

The financial structure of auditing: the full Bridge reportedly costs a minimum of **$300,000–$400,000** to complete. In 1993, the Church's total assets were estimated at $398 million, with $300 million earned in a single year.
