# Theoria (Antiochene Exegesis)

*Theoria* (θεωρία, "contemplation/insight") is the technical term of the Antiochene exegetical school for the capacity to perceive, **in addition to** the historical facts set out in a biblical text, a spiritual reality to which those facts were designed to point. It was the Antiochene alternative to [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]]: a disciplined form of [[typology]] that refuses to let the spiritual sense dissolve the literal-historical one. Drawn primarily from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. III.

## Theoria vs. Allegory

Where [[allegorical-reading|allegory]] treats the text as "a mere symbol…the literal, historical sense, if it is regarded at all, plays a relatively minor role," *theoria* keeps the history intact. Severian of Gabbala drew the line sharply: "It is one thing to force allegory out of the history, and quite another thing to preserve the history intact while discerning a *theoria* over and above it."

**[[diodore-of-tarsus|Diodore of Tarsus]]**, the school's chief theorist, set the rule:

> "We do not forbid the higher interpretation and *theoria*, for the historical narrative does not exclude it, but is on the contrary the basis and substructure of loftier insights…. We must, however, be on our guard against letting the *theoria* do away with the historical basis, for the result would then be, not *theoria*, but allegory."

## The Three Conditions

For *theoria* to operate legitimately (as against arbitrary allegorising), Kelly distills three conditions:

1. the **literal sense** of the narrative must not be abolished;
2. there must be a **real correspondence** between the historical fact and the spiritual object;
3. the two objects must be **apprehended together**, though in different ways.

## Chrysostom's Schema and Definition

**[[john-chrysostom|Chrysostom]]**, the school's great practical exegete, divided Scriptural statements into three classes: (a) those admitting a "theoretic" addition to the literal sense; (b) those to be understood solely literally; (c) those admitting only a non-literal (allegorical) meaning. His classic definition of a type — "a prophecy expressed in terms of things" (*hē dia pragmatōn prophēteia*) — captures the Antiochene insistence that the spiritual sense is anchored in real events.

## Representatives

- **[[diodore-of-tarsus]]** — chief theorist; applied typology (Cain → synagogue, Abel → Church, the spotless lamb → Christ).
- **[[theodore-of-mopsuestia]]** — the most austere, drastically reducing the directly Messianic Psalms and prophecies.
- **[[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret]]** — applied the principles "more flexibly," readier to recognise prophecy in the Psalms.
- **[[john-chrysostom]]** — the practical representative; literal preference with occasional figurative reading.
