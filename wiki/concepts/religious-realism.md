# Religious Realism vs. Non-Realism

A foundational dispute in the philosophy of religion over **what religious language is
doing**: does it *assert truths about a reality that exists independently of us*
(**realism**), or does it *express attitudes, regulate a form of life, or articulate values
without referring to any transcendent reality* (**non-realism**)? [[john-hick|John Hick]]
devotes a major part of *An Interpretation of Religion* to defending **critical realism**
against the non-realists, because [[religious-pluralism|pluralism]] only makes sense if the
traditions are genuinely *about* something — [[the-real|the Real]].

## The Positions

- **Naïve realism** — religious statements straightforwardly describe transcendent
  realities much as ordinary statements describe the world ("God exists, is good, acts in
  history" — taken at literal face value). The default of most pre-modern and confessional
  theology.
- **Critical realism** (Hick's position) — religious language *intends* and *refers to* a
  mind-independent reality, but our access to it is always mediated, interpreted, and
  conditioned by our concepts and culture. We know the Real *as humanly experienced*, never
  *an sich*. Reference is real; literal adequacy is not.
- **Non-realism** (religious anti-realism) — religious language does **not** refer to any
  transcendent reality; its meaning is exhausted by its human function.

## The Non-Realists Hick Argues Against

- **Ludwig Feuerbach** — theology is anthropology; "God" is the projected, idealized
  essence of humanity. The paradigmatic reductive non-realism.
- **R. B. Braithwaite** — religious assertions are disguised *moral* commitments; "God is
  love" announces an intention to live an agapeistic way of life, plus associated stories
  one need not believe.
- **J. H. Randall** — religious symbols are non-referential but functionally real,
  evoking and clarifying shared human ideals.
- **D. Z. Phillips** (Wittgensteinian) — religion is a self-contained "language game" /
  form of life; asking whether God "exists" outside that practice is a category mistake.
- **Don Cupitt** — the "Sea of Faith" non-realism: God is a unifying human ideal, a
  regulative fiction, not a being. Explicitly anti-realist theology from *within*
  Christianity.

A near-neighbour worth flagging: **[[karen-armstrong|Karen Armstrong]]**, in *A History of
God*, repeatedly frames God as a *symbol deliberately created by the imagination* ("God did
not really exist — and yet 'he' was the most important reality") and commends Cupitt's
program. She does not formally adopt non-realism, but her developmental-experiential
position sits on the realism/non-realism fault line — and is the chief reason Hick's
critical realism and Armstrong's mystical-pragmatic account, though both apophatic, pull
apart. See [[karen-armstrong|Karen Armstrong]] and [[religious-pluralism-debate|the
pluralism debate §6]].

## Why It Matters for Pluralism

Hick's whole project hangs on critical realism. If the non-realists are right, the
traditions are not *responses to* anything transcendent, and there is nothing for them to
be plural responses *to* — pluralism collapses into a comparative study of human projection
(closer to Feuerbach than to Hick). Hick therefore argues that the realist intention is
intrinsic to religious practice (worshippers *mean* to address a real God, not to perform a
language game) and that, absent decisive defeaters, believers are rationally entitled to
trust their religious experience as cognitive — analogous to our entitlement to trust
sense experience of the physical world.

The irony the critics press: Hick's *own* Real *an sich*, of which nothing literal can be
said, comes uncomfortably close to the non-realism he opposes — a realism so thin it risks
emptiness. See [[the-real#The Standard Objection|the standard objection to the Real]] and
[[religious-pluralism-debate|the pluralism debate]].

## Cross-Tradition Significance

- The realism/non-realism axis cuts across traditions: it reappears wherever a tradition
  develops a strongly **apophatic** or **demythologized** strand ([[sunyata|Madhyamaka
  emptiness]], *nirguna* [[brahman|Brahman]], negative theology, Bultmannian
  demythologization) — each of which can be read in a realist or a non-realist key.
- It frames a recurring modern question this wiki meets repeatedly: when a tradition's
  literal claims are historically or scientifically doubted, is the right move to abandon
  reference (non-realism) or to relocate it (critical realism / "mythological truth")?
