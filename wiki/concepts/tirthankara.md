## Definition

A *tīrthaṅkara* (Sanskrit; Prakrit *titthayara*) is a "fordmaker" — a liberated teacher who founds a *tīrtha* (ford or crossing), understood as the fourfold community of ascetics and lay people that serves as "a spiritual ford (*tīrtha*) for human beings over the ocean of rebirth" (Dundas, p. 21). The term is usually rendered in western scholarship as "fordmaker." The [[jains|Jains]] believe these teachers activate the Three Jewels — right faith, right knowledge, and right practice — which are held to be uncreated and eternally valid (Dundas, p. 21).

The fordmakers are the supreme religious authority in [[jainism|Jainism]], which is non-theistic: no divine or supernatural being is implicated in the creation or oversight of the cosmos (Dundas, p. 21). The fordmakers themselves, after death, are liberated souls dwelling at the apex of the universe in a state of pure consciousness, and they cannot intervene in worldly affairs — yet they remain the central objects of Jain devotion.

## The Twenty-Four Fordmakers

During each downward motion (*avasarpiṇī*) and each upward motion (*utsarpiṇī*) of the cosmic time-wheel, exactly twenty-four fordmakers appear in succession. The current era's twenty-four run from [[rshabha|Ṛṣabha]] (the first, also called Ādinātha) to [[mahavira|Mahāvīra]] (the twenty-fourth and last). Their physical dimensions and life-spans decrease as the cosmic era descends toward its nadir: the first fordmaker is massive and lives for near-incalculable periods; the twenty-third ([[parshvanatha|Pārśva]]) lived a hundred years; [[mahavira|Mahāvīra]], the twenty-fourth, lived seventy-two years (Dundas, pp. 20–22).

The list of twenty-four as a formal chain first appears in the [[kalpasutra|Kalpasūtra]], which gives extended biographies only for Ṛṣabha, Nemi, Pārśva, and Mahāvīra (Dundas, pp. 23, 40). The full list may have been "concocted about the second or first centuries BCE, in partial imitation of the lists of Vedic seers found in the Upaniṣads, as a means of validating the Jain community as a self-aware religious group through extending the origins of the promounders of the doctrine back into the past" (Dundas, p. 40). Iconographic emblems that distinguish individual fordmakers (e.g., Mahāvīra with a lion; Pārśva with a cobra canopy) were gradually introduced from the late fourth century CE onwards (Dundas, p. 40–41).

## The Pattern of a Fordmaker's Career

The biographies of all fordmakers share an essentially identical pattern:

1. **Birth**: Always into a family of the warrior (*kṣatriya*) class. The birth is presaged by fourteen auspicious dreams seen by the mother (Dundas, p. 22, 37).
2. **Awakening**: The gods (themselves subject to rebirth and unable to attain enlightenment in their divine state) awaken the future fordmaker to his destiny (Dundas, p. 21).
3. **Renunciation**: He renounces the householder's life and becomes a wandering mendicant (*śramaṇa*).
4. **Austerity**: He undertakes an obligatory period of physical and mental austerities, burning off karma accumulated over innumerable existences. His "uniquely powerful physical structure" facilitates this (Dundas, p. 21).
5. **Enlightenment** (*kevala-jñāna*): He attains full omniscience — "transcendent knowledge and vision which give direct and simultaneous access to all forms of reality in the universe in every temporal and spatial dimension" (Dundas, p. 35).
6. **Preaching** (*samavasaraṇa*): He creates a community and preaches the doctrine. The gods construct a circular open-air preaching arena (the *samavasaraṇa*) and magically create replicas of him facing the other directions so all can hear (Dundas, pp. 34–35).
7. **Death and liberation**: He dies in meditation and his soul, freed from the body, travels to the apex of the universe to abide "in a state of bliss and pure consciousness along with the other liberated souls" (Dundas, p. 21).

## The Ford and the Community

The community (*tīrtha*) which the fordmaker establishes comprises four groups: monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen. This fourfold community is itself the "ford" — the institution that enables practitioners to cross the ocean of [[samsara|saṃsāra]]. An early source records a prophecy that the *tīrtha* will outlast Mahāvīra by 21,000 years before eventually declining to extinction, to be reawakened only in the next cosmic era (Bh 20.8; cited in Dundas, p. 13).

## The Fordmaker as Great Man (*Mahāpuruṣa*)

The fordmakers, and especially Mahāvīra, are described according to the Indic model of the *cakravartin* (universal emperor) and the *mahāpuruṣa* (Great Man). Their births are presaged by fourteen auspicious dreams seen by the mother (objects such as a parasol and white elephant that indicate future greatness). The fordmaker bears auspicious marks on his body — notably the wheel imprinted on the sole of his foot — as signs of his spiritual sovereignty. The wheel mark is interpreted as making the fordmaker "equivalent to the ancient Vedic gods" (Dundas, p. 37). In the Jain understanding, however, the fordmaker is a "cakravartin, albeit not of the temporal world but rather of the spiritual realm" (Dundas, p. 37).

## The Preaching Assembly (*Samavasaraṇa*)

The *samavasaraṇa* is the archetypal assembly at which each fordmaker delivers his first sermon. As elaborated in the Universal History (*Mahāpurāṇa*), it is a circular open-air arena reconstructed by the gods each time, with a pavilion and sacred tree at its centre surrounded by concentric balustrades. The fordmaker faces east; replicas created by the gods face the other three directions. Humans, gods, and animals all gather in harmony to hear the preaching of non-violence (AvNiry 562; cited in Dundas, p. 35). "Every Jain temple is regarded as being a replication of the fordmakers' preaching assembly, and entry to this sacred space is thus envisioned as bringing about contact with what is implied in the true teachings" (Dundas, p. 35).

## Fordmakers and the Ganadharas

Each fordmaker converts a set of chief disciples (*gaṇadharas*), who become "leaders of the troop" and interpret and transmit the fordmaker's teachings. The difference between a fordmaker and an enlightened disciple (*kevalin*) is that "the former requires no teacher and develops a more advanced type of karma" (AvNiry 657; cited in Dundas, p. 40). The gaṇadharas are always originally brahmans who are converted through debate. See [[mahavira|Mahāvīra]] for details of the eleven gaṇadharas of the current era.

## Key Text-Concept Connections

- **[[kalpasutra|Kalpasūtra]]**: First text to formally link Mahāvīra with a chain of twenty-three predecessors; gives biographies of Ṛṣabha, Nemi, Pārśva, and Mahāvīra.
- **Ācārāṅga**: Oldest biographical material; describes Mahāvīra's ascetic career as prototype of all fordmaker careers.
- **Universal History** (as elaborated by Jinasena, Gunabhadra, Silāṅka, Hemacandra): Situates each fordmaker within the immense temporal framework of the cosmic wheel.
