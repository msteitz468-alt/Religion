# Rigpa (Intrinsic Awareness)

**Rigpa** (Tibetan *rig-pa*, Sanskrit *vidyā*) is **pure, intrinsic awareness** — the central term
of [[dzogchen|Dzogchen]]. In its primary usage it names "the fundamental innate mind in its natural
state of spontaneity and purity, beyond… the subject-object dichotomy" — the direct opposite of
**fundamental ignorance** (*ma-rig-pa*).

## Sems vs. Rig-pa

Dzogchen draws a decisive distinction between:

- **Sems** — ordinary, gross, *dualising* mind (*rnam-shes*, dualistic consciousness); and
- **Rig-pa** — pure awareness, free of the subject-object split, pervaded by
  [[inner-radiance|inner radiance]].

Chapter 4 of the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]], "The Introduction to Awareness:
Natural Liberation through Naked Perception," is a **pointing-out** (*ngo-sprod*) text whose whole
purpose is the direct introduction to rigpa. It describes awareness, examined nakedly, as "radiant…
free from the presence of an observer, manifestly stark and clear, completely empty and uncreated…
without duality of radiance and emptiness." In it the three [[trikaya|buddha-bodies]] are
inseparably present: its emptiness is the dharmakāya, its radiance the sambhogakāya, its
unimpeded arising the nirmāṇakāya.

## A Term With Many Names

The text lists synonyms used across the traditions for this one nature: "self" (eternalists),
"selflessness of the individual" (Śrāvakas), "mind" (Cittamātra), Perfection of Discriminative
Awareness (*Prajñāpāramitā*), Nucleus of the Sugata
([[buddha-nature|tathāgatagarbha]]), [[mahamudra|Great Seal]], the Unique Seminal Point, the
Expanse of Reality (*dharmadhātu*), and the Ground-of-all (*ālaya*). Chapter 4 critiques the
graduated vehicles (Madhyamaka, Kriyā/Yoga-tantra, Mahāyoga/Anuyoga) as falling short by
"polarising the non-dual reality," and counsels: "Do not meditate at all, since there is nothing
upon which to meditate."
