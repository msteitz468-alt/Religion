# Greatest Name

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition and Islamic Background

According to Islamic belief, God has ninety-nine names (compassionate, merciful, etc.). In esoteric tradition it is believed there is also a hundredth — the "greatest name" — which has special potency (lines 15385–15388). For Bahá'ís this greatest name (*ism-i-a'ẓam*) is **bahá** ('glory', 'splendour'), as used in the religious title of their founder, [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] ('Glory of God') (lines 15390–15392).

## Usage in Bahá'í Practice

'Abdu'l-Bahá referred to *bahá* as a name of comfort and protection (lines 15393–15396). Expressions using this name or derivatives of it are commonly used by Bahá'ís. These include:

1. **Alláh-u-Abhá** ('God is Most Glorious') — an invocation Bahá'ís are bidden to repeat ninety-five times each day as a devotional practice (lines 15402–15404), and which is widely used as a greeting.

2. **Yá Bahá'u'l-Abhá** ('O Glory of the Most Glorious') — appears in a calligraphic device designed by [[mishkin-qalam|Mishkín-Qalam]], which many Bahá'ís set as a wall decoration (lines 15406–15413).

3. **The "ringstone symbol"** — a configuration of the Arabic letters 'b' and 'h', featured on Bahá'í rings (lines 15413–15419).

The name should be used with proper dignity and reverence: not on stationery or as a car sticker, for example, and neither should it be used on gravestones (lines 15420–15424).

## Numerical Symbolism

According to the [[abjad|Abjad]] numerical system, *bahá* has a numerical equivalence of 9, hence the frequent use of the number nine in Bahá'í symbolism (lines 15425–15428).
