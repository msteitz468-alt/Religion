# Extreme Unction

**Extreme Unction** (the "last anointing"; in modern Catholic usage the **Anointing of the Sick**) is
the sacrament administered to the gravely ill by anointing with consecrated oil. [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]
treats it in the [[summa-theologica|*ST*]] Supplement (XP Q29–Q33), one of the seven
[[sacraments|sacraments]].

## Aquinas's account (Supplement XP Q29–Q33)

- **A true sacrament** (Q29), not a mere sacramental; one sacrament despite the several anointings;
  instituted by Christ (promulgated through the apostles, James 5:14). Its **matter** is consecrated
  **olive oil**; its **form** is **deprecatory** ("Through this holy unction…") rather than assertoric
  — precisely because, unlike the character-imprinting sacraments, its bodily effect is **not
  guaranteed**.
- **Effect** (Q30): the principal effect is the remission of the **remnants of sin** (the spiritual
  debility left by sin), and consequently of any guilt still found; **bodily healing** is a secondary,
  conditional effect ("if it be expedient"). It imprints **no character** and is therefore
  **repeatable**.
- **Minister** (Q31): the ordinary **priest** (not deacons or laymen).
- **Recipient** (Q32): only the **gravely ill who have the use of reason** — not the healthy, infants,
  or the permanently deranged; the five senses and the feet are anointed.
- **Repetition** (Q33): repeatable, including within the same prolonged illness if the danger of death
  returns, since it produces no permanent effect.

## Reception

For [[catholicism|Catholicism]] and [[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodoxy]] this is one of the seven
sacraments; the Protestant Reformers rejected its sacramental status.
