# Abhidharma

**Abhidharma** (Sanskrit; Pali *Abhidhamma*, "higher / further Dharma") is the analytical-systematic
strand of Buddhist philosophy that "aims at filling out the metaphysical details behind the Buddha's
core teachings of nonself, impermanence, and suffering." It is also the **third basket** of the
[[tipitaka|canon]] (after the Sutta and Vinaya), and the system [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s
[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] takes as its principal target.

## The Core Framework

Several Abhidharma schools arose (Sarvāstivāda, Theravāda, Sautrāntika, etc.), differing on details
but sharing a set of presuppositions reconstructed by Siderits & Katsura:

1. **Two truths** — every statement is either **conventionally** true (acting on it leads to
   successful practice) or **ultimately** true (it corresponds to reality without presupposing any
   *conceptual fiction*). See [[two-truths|the two truths]].
2. **Only dharmas are ultimately real.** A **dharma** is a simple, impartite existent — an
   indivisible particle, a momentary occurrence of color, a pain-sensation, a moment of
   consciousness. Composite wholes (a chariot, a person) are **conceptual fictions**: useful
   aggregations the mind imposes, reducible without remainder to facts about dharmas.
3. **Dharmas arise by [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]]** and are therefore mostly
   impermanent.
4. **Dharmas have [[svabhava|intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*)]]** — and *only* dharmas do; each dharma
   has exactly one. *Svabhāva* is the mark of the ultimately real.
5. **Suffering is overcome by realizing the ultimate truth** — seeing that the "I" is a fiction and
   that what is real is just a causal series of dharmas.

This is the **person-empty-but-dharmas-real** position: the self is deconstructed into the five
*skandhas* (see [[anatta|nonself]]), but the analysis bottoms out in really-existing dharmas.
[[paul-williams|Williams]] glosses the project vividly: it is the attempt to redescribe ordinary
statements ("on my alms round I was given a strawberry") "with maximum possible exactitude" — replacing
the fictional "I" and "strawberry" with a list of the impermanent psychological and physical factors
and the causal relations actually obtaining. Its great systematic monument is Vasubandhu's
*Abhidharmakośa*.

## Why It Matters for the Wiki

Abhidharma is the indispensable *foil* for [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]. Nāgārjuna accepts the
Abhidharma definition of the ultimately real — *if* anything is ultimately real, it is a dharma with
*svabhāva* — and then argues that **nothing** meets it: the dharmas are as empty as the chariot. In
the wiki's terms, Abhidharma supplies the metaphysical vocabulary (two truths, dharma, *svabhāva*,
conceptual fiction) that Madhyamaka borrows and turns. The dispute over whether the dharmas are real
is the central fault line of Indian Buddhist philosophy.
