# Maya

**Maya** is one of the most contested terms in [[hinduism|Hindu]] metaphysics, ranging in meaning from
"the creative power of God" to "cosmic illusion." How a school construes maya is a litmus test of its
whole position.

## The Range of Meaning

- **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]**: maya is the principle by which the one [[brahman|Brahman]] *appears*
  as a plural world and individual selves; it is neither fully real nor wholly unreal
  (*anirvachaniya*). The world is real-as-experienced but sublated upon liberating knowledge — the
  classic image is the **rope mistaken for a snake**. This "world as illusion" reading is Advaita's most
  famous and most attacked claim.
- **[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]]** and **[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]]**: emphatically
  **reject** maya-as-illusion. For Ramanuja and Madhva, maya is simply **God's real creative power**;
  the world and souls are fully real. [[ramanuja|Ramanuja's]] "seven great objections" (*saptavidha
  anupapatti*) against the Advaitic maya are a landmark of Indian philosophical polemic.

## Cross-Tradition Note

Advaitic maya is sometimes compared to the Buddhist [[sunyata|emptiness]] (both relativize the
phenomenal world), and the resemblance fueled the charge that [[shankara|Shankara]] was a
"crypto-Buddhist." But maya presupposes a real underlying Brahman, whereas emptiness denies any inherent
ground — so the comparison marks a contrast as much as a likeness.
