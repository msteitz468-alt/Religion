# Confirmation (Chrismation)

The post-baptismal rite of initiation — anointing with chrism (essential in the East) and/or the laying on of hands (emphasised in the West) — by which the Holy Spirit is conferred or "perfected" in the newly baptised. The Latin term *confirmatio* first appears in a canon of the first Council of Orange (441). Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. XVI.

## Theological Content

Its core meaning is the gift of the [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]]. [[cyril-of-jerusalem|Cyril of Jerusalem]]: the blessed oil becomes "the chrism of Christ, capable of producing the Holy Spirit." Innocent I: episcopal consignation is "the medium by which he bestows the Paraclete." Because both [[baptism]] and confirmation seemed to confer the Spirit, the period struggled to distinguish them; the emerging solution made confirmation the rite that "strengthens," "fortifies," or "perfects" (*teleiōsis*) the baptised. **Faustus of Riez**'s influential homily put it memorably: "In baptism we are regenerated for life, but after baptism we are confirmed for the struggle… in baptism we are nourished, but after baptism we are strengthened."

The East kept anointing as the essential matter; the West retained both chrism and the imposition of hands, increasingly reserving the latter to the bishop.
