# Kyrios (Lord)

*Kyrios* (Greek κύριος, "Lord") is one of the earliest exalted titles applied to
Jesus. The same word served in antiquity for a slave's **master**, a student's
**teacher**, and for **God** (it renders the divine name YHWH in the Septuagint).
[[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]] ([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]])
argues that during Jesus's lifetime the disciples called him *kyrios* in the social
sense ("master-teacher"); after the [[resurrection|resurrection]] the term was
**escalated to divine Lordship** — at the preliterary tradition in **Acts 2:36**
("God has made him both Lord and Christ"), Jesus "actually was ruling as Lord of the
earth, because he had been exalted to this new status by God … at the resurrection."

The **Philippians 2** "Christ Poem" caps the title's elevation: at his exaltation "every
tongue [will] confess that Jesus Christ is *Lord*" — an allusion to **Isaiah 45:22–23**,
where the worship of "every knee" belongs to YHWH alone. The transfer of this divine
acclamation to the exalted Jesus is, for Ehrman, the point at which "Jesus has been
granted the status and honor and glory of the One Almighty God himself." See the
disputed origins of the title in [[kyrios-christology-origins|Kyrios Christology Origins]].

### Hansen (PNTC) on Phil 2:9–11 and 3:20 (2026-07-18)

Attributed to [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]] ([[hansen-philippians-pntc]], [[christ-hymn]]). The name given at exaltation is **Lord**, not the earthly name Jesus; *kyrios* carries the LXX/YHWH register via Isa 45. In Roman [[philippi|Philippi]] the acclamation “Jesus Christ is Lord” **reflects and subverts** imperial “Caesar is Lord,” while the *meaning* of the titles remains Jewish monotheistic. At 3:20–21 the same double register applies to **Savior** and Lord: Caesar as “savior of the world” vs the Savior from heaven. Hansen’s “christological monotheism”: worship of Jesus is extension of the worship of the one God, to the glory of the Father (2:11). Positions sit alongside Ehrman/Hurtado without adjudication — see [[kyrios-christology-origins]].
