# Ziran

**Ziran** ("self-so," "of-itself-so") is the Daoist ideal of **spontaneity and naturalness** — things
being and behaving as they spontaneously are, without external compulsion or contrivance. It is the
quality the [[dao|Dao]] exhibits and that **[[wu-wei|wu-wei]]** preserves.

## Meaning

The [[daodejing|Daodejing]] says "the Dao follows ziran" (ch. 25) — even the Way "models itself on what
is so-of-itself," i.e. on uncontrived spontaneity. To live by ziran is to act from one's own nature and
to let other things follow theirs, the opposite of forcing, scheming, or imposing artificial standards.
For the sage and the ideal ruler, honoring ziran means **minimal interference** so that people and things
flourish in their own way.

## Significance and Contrast

Ziran is the Daoist value that most sharply opposes the **Confucian** program of deliberate moral and
ritual cultivation (*[[analects|li]]*, the artful shaping of character): where Confucianism *cultivates*,
Daoism *un-contrives*. This Confucian–Daoist tension over nature vs. culture is one of the central debates
of Chinese thought (a candidate comparison/controversy page).
