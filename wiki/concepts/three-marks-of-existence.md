# The Three Marks of Existence

The **three marks** (Pali *tilakkhaṇa*) are the three characteristics that, in
[[buddhism|Buddhism]], are true of all conditioned phenomena. Insight (*vipassanā*) into
them is the wisdom that liberates:

1. **Anicca** — **impermanence**: everything conditioned arises and passes away; nothing
   compounded endures.
2. **Dukkha** — **unsatisfactoriness**: because they are impermanent, conditioned things
   cannot provide lasting satisfaction (see [[dukkha|dukkha]]).
3. **Anatta** — **non-self**: nothing conditioned possesses a permanent, independent
   self or essence (see [[anatta|anatta]]).

## The Logical Chain

The marks are ordered as an argument the suttas repeat: **"what is impermanent is dukkha;
what is dukkha is non-self."** Because the [[five-aggregates|five aggregates]] are seen
to be in constant flux (anicca), clinging to them brings dukkha, and because they neither
endure nor lie under one's control they cannot be a [[anatta|self]] (anatta). A scope
distinction is built in: **anicca** and **dukkha** are predicated of all *conditioned*
(*saṅkhāra*) things, whereas **anatta** is predicated of **all** *dhammas* — including the
unconditioned [[nirvana|nibbāna]], which is *anatta* though not impermanent or dukkha.

## Contested: Is Suffering the Third Mark, or Nirvana?

⚠️ **The list above is disputed.** While the *anicca / dukkha / anatta* triad is standard in
[[theravada|Theravada]] (and is the version [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]] transmits),
**[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]** argues that **suffering does not belong** among the
*universal* marks: impermanence and nonself are true of *all* things (a table, a flower),
but suffering is not — it arises only from wrong attachment. On his reading the authentic
**Three Dharma Seals** are **impermanence, nonself, and [[nirvana|nirvana]]** (citing the
Northern *Saṃyukta Āgama* and [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]). The divergence tracks a real split
in the textual record (Pali vs. Northern/Āgama transmission) and is staged, unadjudicated,
at [[three-dharma-seals-is-suffering-universal|the controversy page]].

## Significance

The three marks are the shared doctrinal core across every Buddhist school and the direct
object of insight meditation: seeing them dismantles the craving and the self-view that
power [[samsara|saṃsāra]]. They mark the sharpest contrast with the Hindu *ātman*/*brahman*
traditions, which affirm precisely the permanent, blissful Self that **anatta** denies.
