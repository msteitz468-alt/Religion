# Harae and kegare (purification and pollution)

**Harae** (祓, purification) and **kegare** (穢れ, pollution/defilement) form the **central ethical-ritual
polarity of [[shinto|Shinto]]** — taking the place that the **good/evil** or **sin/righteousness** axis holds
in the monotheisms. For Shinto, the fundamental religious problem is **not moral guilt but impurity**: to
approach the **[[kami|kami]]** one must be **clean**, and the work of religion is to **remove pollution and
restore purity and harmony**.

## Kegare — pollution

**Kegare** is a **ritual, quasi-physical defilement** (not primarily a moral fault), contracted above all
through contact with:

- **death** (the gravest pollution), **blood**, **disease**, and decay;
- **wrongdoing** and disorder (*tsumi*), which are conceived less as guilt than as a **stain** to be wiped away.

Kegare **repels the kami** and brings misfortune; it is the reason death and Shinto are kept apart (funerals in
Japan are overwhelmingly **Buddhist**, while births, weddings, and shrine life are Shinto).

## Harae — purification

**Harae** is the removal of kegare, restoring purity. Its forms:

- **misogi** — purification with **water** (rinsing mouth and hands at the shrine *temizuya*; full immersion in
  river, sea, or under a waterfall);
- **ōharae** — the "great purification," a communal rite (twice yearly) cleansing the nation's accumulated
  *tsumi*;
- **harai** by a priest waving the *haraegushi* (a wand of paper streamers) or sprinkling salt.

## The mythic charter

The paradigm is in the **[[kojiki|Kojiki]]**: after **[[izanagi-and-izanami|Izanagi]]** flees the polluting
land of the dead (**Yomi**), he **bathes to wash off its defilement** — and from this very act of *misogi* the
greatest kami are born, **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** from his left eye, **[[tsukuyomi|Tsukuyomi]]** from his
right, **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]** from his nose. **Purification is thus literally creative** — the source of the
sun itself. **Salt-purification** (e.g. before sumo) and the ideal of **makoto** (sincerity, a "bright, pure
heart") extend the principle into daily life.
