# The Theological Virtues, Gifts, and Fruits (Aquinas)

[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] treats the supernatural perfections of the Christian — the theological
virtues, the gifts of the Holy Spirit, the beatitudes, and the fruits — at
[[summa-theologica|*Summa Theologiae*]] I-II, Q62–70. These complete his account of the
[[cardinal-virtues|natural virtues]] and depend on [[grace]].

## The three theological virtues (Q62)

**Faith, hope, and charity** are called **theological** for three reasons: their **object is God**
himself, they are **infused by God alone**, and they are **made known only by divine revelation**
(Q62, A1). In the order of *generation* faith precedes hope, and hope precedes charity; in the order
of *perfection* **charity is first** — "the form of them all" (Q62, A4).

## Infused vs. acquired virtue (Q63)

Besides virtues acquired by habituation there are virtues **infused by God** that differ *in species*
from the acquired ones, because their formal object and rule differ: infused temperance is measured by
the divine rule ("I chastise my body," 1 Cor. 9:27), acquired temperance by human reason and bodily
health (Q63, A3–4).

## The theological virtues observe no mean (Q64)

Moral virtue lies in a mean between excess and defect; but the theological virtues, whose **rule is
God himself — who is infinite — admit no excess**: "never can we love God as much as He ought to be
loved" (Q64, A4). Any "mean" applies to them only accidentally, relative to us.

## The connection of the virtues (Q65)

The moral virtues are connected through **prudence**; the *infused* virtues are additionally connected
through **charity**. Charity cannot exist without the (infused) moral virtues, nor they without
charity; faith and hope can exist *inchoately* without charity but are not **perfect** virtues until
informed by it (Q65).

## What remains in heaven (Q67)

Faith and hope are intrinsically imperfect — faith involves non-vision, hope involves not-yet-
possession — so both **pass away** in glory, giving place to vision and possession. **Charity
remains** identically (its wayfaring imperfection is only accidental): "Charity never falleth away"
(1 Cor. 13:8).

## Gifts, Beatitudes, Fruits (Q68–70)

- **The seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit** (Q68): wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge,
  piety, fear of the Lord. They differ from the virtues in their *principle of motion* — the virtues
  perfect us to follow the movement of **reason**, the gifts dispose us to be moved promptly by the
  **Holy Spirit** — and are necessary because we possess the theological virtues only imperfectly.
- **The Beatitudes** (Q69) are *acts* (not habits) flowing from the virtues and gifts, answering the
  three false notions of happiness (sensual, active, contemplative).
- **The twelve Fruits of the Holy Spirit** (Q70, from Gal. 5:22–23) are the delightful end-products of
  the Spirit's work; every beatitude is a fruit, but not conversely.

## The virtues treated in particular (II-II)

Where I-II treats the theological virtues in general, the **Secunda Secundae** treats each in
particular, together with its gifts, its opposed vices, and its precepts.

### Faith (II-II Q1–16)

- **Object (Q1).** The **formal object** of faith is the **First Truth** (God); everything else
  believed — Christ's humanity, the sacraments, creatures — comes under faith only "in relation to
  God," as medicine considers all things in relation to health (Q1, A1). On the side of the thing
  believed the object is simple; on the side of the believer's act it is **complex, a proposition** —
  yet "the act of the believer does not terminate in a proposition, but in a thing" (Q1, A2).
  **Nothing false can come under faith** (Q1, A3). The **articles** are distinct points of unseen
  truth; their *substance* has not increased over time, only their explicitness, so the ancients
  believed implicitly what later believers hold explicitly (Q1, A7). Defining new creedal symbols
  belongs to the **Sovereign Pontiff** (Q1, A10, citing Lk. 22:32).
- **Act (Q2).** To believe is, with Augustine, **"to think with assent"** (*cum assensione
  cogitare*) — the intellect "cleaves firmly to one side" as in science, yet **without seeing** its
  object (Q2, A1). The threefold formula **credere Deo / credere Deum / credere in Deum** (believing
  God as authority / that God is / moving toward God as end) names one act under three aspects (Q2,
  A2). The intellect is determined to assent **not by reason but by the will moved by grace**; hence
  faith is **meritorious** (Q2, A9). Reason *preceding* the will to believe diminishes merit; reason
  *consequent* to it is a sign of greater merit (Q2, A10).
- **The virtue itself (Q4).** Unpacking Heb. 11:1 ("the substance of things to be hoped for, the
  evidence of things that appear not"), faith resides in the **intellect** (its object is the True),
  and **charity is its form** — *fides caritate formata* — quickening the act toward its end (Q4,
  A1–3). **Living (formed) and lifeless (unformed) faith are one and the same habit**; lifelessness
  is accidental, like childhood to a man (Q4, A4). Only living faith is a true virtue (Q4, A5).
  Faith is the **first of the virtues** (Q4, A7) and is **more certain than science** *simpliciter*
  (from its cause, the Divine Truth that cannot err) though less certain *quoad nos* (its matter
  exceeds our grasp) (Q4, A8).
- **Subjects (Q5).** Angels before confirmation and man before sin had faith; **demons** have a
  compelled, non-meritorious "faith" — "the devils believe and tremble" (Q5, A2). A heretic who
  obstinately denies **one** article has **no faith at all** in the rest, since faith adheres to all
  articles by the single mean of the First Truth as proposed by the Church (Q5, A3).
- **Cause and effects (Q6–7).** Faith is **infused by God** as to both proposal and interior assent
  — against the **Pelagians**, the beginning of faith is not from free will alone (Q6, A1). Faith
  produces **fear** (servile from lifeless faith, filial from living) and is the **first beginning
  of the heart's purification** (Q7).
- **Vices and gifts.** Unbelief (Q10), **heresy** (Q11), and **apostasy** (Q12) are the vices
  opposed to faith — see [[coercion-of-unbelievers]] for Aquinas's charged conclusions on each.
  **Blasphemy** (Q13) and the six species of the **sin against the Holy Ghost** — despair,
  presumption, resisting known truth, envy of another's spiritual good, impenitence, obstinacy
  (Q14) — round out the treatise.
- **The cognitive gifts.** The **gift of understanding** (Q8) is *intus legere*, "reading within" —
  a supernatural light penetrating to the heart of what is believed; it corresponds to the beatitude
  of the **clean of heart**. The **gift of knowledge** (Q9) gives sure judgment about **created**
  things in relation to faith (where **wisdom** judges by the highest cause, God); it corresponds to
  the beatitude of **those who mourn**. See [[fourfold-sense]] and [[wisdom-literature]] for related
  cognitive frameworks.

### Hope (II-II Q17–22)

- **In itself (Q17).** Hope is a theological virtue whose **proper object is eternal happiness** —
  "we should hope from Him for nothing less than Himself" (Q17, A2). Its object is a future good,
  **arduous but possible** by Divine assistance. It is lawful to hope **in** a creature only as a
  secondary, instrumental cause through whom God helps — hence intercession of the saints — never as
  the first cause (Q17, A4). Like the other theological virtues, hope observes **no mean** toward its
  principal object (one cannot trust God too much) (Q17, A5). Faith precedes hope; in the order of
  perfection charity perfects it — "we hope chiefly in our friends" (Q17, A7–8).
- **Subject and certainty (Q18).** Hope is in the **will** (Q18, A1). It is **voided in the
  blessed** (happiness is present, not future) and **absent from the damned** (who know their state
  is inescapable); it belongs to wayfarers, including souls in purgatory (Q18, A2–3). Its certainty
  is not its own but **by participation in the certainty of faith** (Q18, A4).
- **The gift of fear (Q19).** Aquinas's most elaborated taxonomy distinguishes **worldly** fear
  (turning *from* God for fear of worldly loss — always evil), **servile** fear (turning to God for
  fear of punishment — substantially good, its *servility* evil), **filial/chaste** fear (fear of
  the **fault** of offending or being separated from God), and **initial** fear (between the two, in
  beginners). The **gift** of fear is filial fear (Q19, A9). Filial fear **increases** with charity
  while servile fear's servility is **cast out** (Q19, A10), and filial fear **remains in heaven**
  transformed into wonder at God's supereminence (Q19, A11). "The fear of the Lord is the beginning
  of wisdom" — as its *effect*, where faith is its beginning as *essence* (Q19, A7).
- **Opposed vices (Q20–21).** **Despair** conforms the appetite to a false opinion that God refuses
  pardon; it can exist **without unbelief** (the universal faith-judgment retained while the
  particular estimate is corrupted), and though unbelief and hatred of God are graver *in species*,
  despair is the **most dangerous** sin *for us* — "to despair is to fall into hell" — since without
  hope men rush headlong into sin (Q20). It arises chiefly from **sloth**. **Presumption** —
  inordinate trust in Divine mercy, hoping for pardon without repentance — is the sin against the
  Holy Ghost properly, arising from **pride**; it is less grave than despair, "for it is more proper
  to God to spare than to punish" (Q21).
