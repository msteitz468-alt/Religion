# Hukam

**Hukam** (Punjabi: *hukam*, from Arabic *hukm*, "command/order") is the divine Order or Will in Sikh theology — the cosmic harmony that pervades and underlies all creation, expressed in both physical and psychical dimensions. Perceiving and attuning to the hukam is the metaphysical goal that [[nam-simaran|nam simaran]] aims to realize.

## McLeod's Account

McLeod presents hukam as the intelligible face of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] in creation. The *shabad* (Word) is "the message inscribed in creation," and [[guru-nanak|Guru Nanak]] describes it: "Look around you and look within. Both around and within you will perceive the divine Order (*hukam*), a harmony expressed in the physical and psychical creation, which reflects the divine harmony of Akal Purakh" (~L607–610).

The soteriological imperative follows directly: "In order to secure liberation one must attune one's whole life to that harmony expressed as the divine Name. This purpose one achieves by means of the regular, disciplined practice of *nam simaran* or 'remembrance of the Name'" (~L611–616).

## Hukam in Institutional Practice

The term *hukam* is also used institutionally. After Guru Gobind Singh's death, the voice of the Guru Panth can be expressed in ***hukam-namas*** ("letters of command") issued by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC), "claiming to be the voice of the Guru Panth, but such an occasion is a rare event" (~L1030–1034). Guru Gobind Singh himself "issued *hukam-namas* or 'letters of command' to particular individuals and *sahgats*" (~L1556–1557).

A contested *hukam-nama* allegedly from Guru Gobind Singh to followers in Kabul references the Five Ks, "but this *hukam-nama* is most unlikely to be genuine" — it lacks the Guru's seal and contradicts other early sources (~L1989–1998).

## Hukam in Japji Sahib (Primary Text)

Pauri 2 of Japji Sahib gives the fullest primary-text treatment of Hukam (Ang 1, Thind/Khalsa translation):

> By His Command, bodies are created; His Command cannot be described. By His Command, souls come into being; by His Command, glory and greatness are obtained. (L88–90)

> Everyone is subject to His Command; no one is beyond His Command. O Nanak, one who understands His Command, does not speak in ego. (L116–122)

The last line is decisive: understanding Hukam dissolves [[haumai|haumai]] (ego-speech). The Hukam framework thus resolves Pauri 1's opening question — "how can we become truthful?" — by showing that the solution is not more effort but attunement to the divine Order that is already operating. The entirety of creation (high/low stations, blessings, wandering, bodies, souls) is within Hukam; no being stands outside it.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

Hukam is the cosmic expression of [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]]'s nature, made perceptible through creation and made audible through the *shabad* (spoken by the eternal [[the-guru|Guru]]). The human predicament — [[haumai|haumai]] — is precisely the failure to perceive and submit to hukam, remaining instead locked in self-will. [[nam-simaran|Nam simaran]] is the discipline by which the practitioner progressively subordinates haumai to hukam, achieving the bliss of *sahaj* (total union with the divine).
