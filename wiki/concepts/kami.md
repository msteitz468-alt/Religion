# Kami

**Kami** (神) are the **sacred powers** at the heart of [[shinto|Shinto]] — the gods, spirits, and numinous
presences whose reverence *is* "the way of the kami" (*Shintō*). The term resists translation: "god,"
"spirit," "deity," and "numen" all capture part of it. Kami are reckoned beyond counting — the
**yaoyorozu no kami**, the **"eight million"** (i.e. innumerable) gods.

## What counts as a kami

The classic definition is **Motoori Norinaga's** (in his *Kojiki* commentary): a kami is **anything whatsoever
that inspires awe** — "not only… deities of heaven and earth… but also birds, beasts, trees, plants, seas,
mountains, and so forth. Whatever seemed strikingly impressive, possessed the quality of excellence, or
inspired a feeling of awe was called kami." Thus kami include:

- **great cosmic deities** — the sun-goddess **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]**, the storm-god **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]**,
  the creators **[[izanagi-and-izanami|Izanagi and Izanami]]**;
- **nature and place** — particular mountains, waterfalls, the wind, the sea, ancient trees, unusual stones
  (the kami often *dwells in* a natural object, a *shintai*);
- **abstract generative forces** — fertility, growth, the **[[musubi|musubi]]** power itself;
- **ancestors, emperors, and remarkable humans** — even deified by enshrinement (the emperor as *arahitogami*;
  [[hachiman|Hachiman]] as the deified Emperor Ōjin).

## Their nature

Kami are **immanent, not transcendent**: they live alongside humans in the world, are **finite and
particular**, and are **neither omnipotent nor perfectly good**. They have **two aspects** — a gentle,
blessing side (*nigi-mitama*) and a violent, wrathful side (*ara-mitama*) — and must be **honored, thanked,
and placated** to keep their benevolence. There is **no absolute good-vs-evil dualism** among them; the kami
themselves can rage (Susanoo) and be soothed. One approaches them only in a state of **purity**
([[harae|harae]]); pollution (*kegare*) repels them.
