# Rehabilitation Project Force (RPF)

The **Rehabilitation Project Force (RPF)** is a **punitive labor and re-indoctrination program** for [[sea-org|Sea Organization]] members deemed to have committed serious infractions, shown low production, or manifested "out-ethics" behavior. It is one of the most controversial elements of Scientology's organizational structure, described by critics as a forced-labor program and by the Church as a rehabilitative religious retreat.

## Origin

The RPF was introduced in **1973** by [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]] while he was recovering from a **motorcycle accident in Tenerife** (November 1973, broken arm and ribs). It was reportedly conceived as a mechanism for dealing with Sea Org members who had failed in their assignments without simply expelling them.

A **"RPF's RPF"** — a sub-program for those who violated RPF rules — was created in **1974**.

## Conditions

Documented conditions (from court testimony, ex-member accounts, and journalism), as recorded by Atack, Wright, and others:

- Members perform **manual labor** for extended hours
- Must **run rather than walk** at all times
- May **not speak unless spoken to** by a non-RPF member
- Receive a **reduced diet** and restricted quarters
- Subject to **mandatory auditing** and "study" sessions
- May not leave without authorization; departures without permission constitute "blowing"

**Gerry Armstrong** described the RPF facilities on the Apollo as a "roach-infested cargo hold."

**"Happy Valley"** (circa 1982): a variant at an Indian reservation location in the California desert where senior executives (including [[david-mayo|David Mayo]]) were required to run around poles for approximately five hours daily. The desert heat was described as reaching 117°F at nearby La Quinta.

**Vicki Aznaran** was confined at Happy Valley and escaped on **April 9, 1987** by walking onto the **Sobova Indian Reservation**, where she was assisted by Native Americans.

## Scholarly Dispute

The RPF is a central exhibit in the academic debate over Scientology:

- **J. Gordon Melton** and other scholars sympathetic to Scientology: the RPF is "a monastic retreat" analogous to religious penances in established traditions
- **Stephen Kent**: the RPF constitutes **coercive control** and may meet international definitions of forced labor; published detailed academic analysis of conditions
- **Catherine Wessinger** and **Lorne Dawson**: disputed Kent's most severe characterizations
- **Ex-members** (Armstrong, Headley, Many, Aznaran): described conditions as abusive and effectively imprisoning

Courts have not definitively resolved the classification question.

## Clearwater Hearings Testimony (May 1982)

At the City of Clearwater hearings, **David Ray** testified to RPF conditions at Flag. This testimony is among the earliest public documentation of the program's specific practices.

## Significance in the "Religion" Debate

The RPF is frequently cited in arguments that the Sea Org operates as a **labor organization rather than a religious order**. The combination of minimal pay, restricted movement, mandatory labor, and punitive "rehabilitation" resembles (per critics) features of labor coercion rather than voluntary religious commitment.
