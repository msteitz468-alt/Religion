# Taurobolium

> **Concept page.** A **bull-sacrifice baptism** — "the rite in which a bull is slain and its
> flowing blood pours over the initiate in a kind of baptism" (glossary,
> [[laeuchli-groh-mithraism-in-ostia|*Mithraism in Ostia*]]). Those who received it were the
> *tauroboliati*.

## Proper Home: the Cult of Magna Mater

The taurobolium belongs properly to the cult of [[magna-mater-cybele|Magna Mater/Cybele]] (and
Attis), not to Mithraism. At [[ostia|Ostia]] the Ostia team is explicit: "**the *tauroboliati*
of Ostia were Magna Mater initiates and not Mithraic ones**." No Mithraic *Pater* inscription
mentions baptism by blood, and the small, numerous Ostian [[mithraism|mithraea]] were physically
too small and too many for so expensive a sacrifice.

## The Mithraic Mis-attribution

A persistent popular tradition — represented in the wiki by
[[haywood-mithraism-freemasonry-ancient-mysteries|H. L. Haywood's essay]] — makes the taurobolium
"the very center and climax of the Mithraic ritual." This **conflates** Magna Mater's blood-bath
with Mithraism's [[mithras|Mithras]]-slays-the-bull iconography (the **tauroctonos**, a distinct
thing). The wiki records Haywood's version as an error.

Laeuchli adds a developmental nuance: "by the time Mithra reached Ostia **the taurobolium had
become symbolic**," paralleling "the spiritualization of the Old Testament" — the bloody act
transmuted into a symbolic, salvific image. Only in a 4th-century "Mithraic fundamentalism" do
inscriptions for actual bull-slaying reappear (all post-AD 370).
