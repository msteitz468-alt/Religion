# Kevala-jnana (Omniscience)

Kevala-jnana is the Jain term for the omniscient knowledge that constitutes full enlightenment. Dundas summarises the Jain view as "enlightenment as the result of a process of self-cultivation in which an individual gradually progresses with the aid of a variety of religious practices from a state of inadequate perception of the world to the attainment of certain higher forms of knowledge … until, with the elimination of all negative factors, there arises omniscient knowledge which is totally unique and unconditioned" (lines 2–8).

## Nature of the Claim

The mainstream Jain position is that omniscience means **literal, simultaneous, total knowledge**: "the ability to know and see everything in the universe at all times and in all possible modifications simultaneously, in other words a complete unfettering of the mind" (lines 15–18). Dundas notes that "such a claim for a human being was a grand one, and at the outset unique in India, for the omniscience which the Buddhists originally attributed to their founder related to knowledge only of that which was specifically conducive to spiritual advancement" (lines 18–21).

The fifth *anga* of the Svetambara scriptures (the "Exposition of Explanations") illustrates the breadth of Mahavira's omniscience: "local knowledge, the actions of gods, the previous and future births of human beings, the unseen ontological categories and creatures living in earth, water, air and fire" (lines 25–27).

## Epistemic and Polemical Function

Kevala-jnana grounds Jain doctrinal authority: "All Jain doctrinal categories, whether ontological, metaphysical, ethical or cosmological, are ultimately validated by Mahavira's immediate and unmediated experience of the totality of reality" (lines 33–36). The omniscient teacher is "totally trustworthy (*apta*) and faultless and his teaching had full authority, against which the claims of other sects and schools were flawed and incomplete" (lines 36–39).

Conversions in Jain narrative literature are depicted as being effected by the omniscient teacher's ability "to describe minutely and give shape to entities which would otherwise remain invisible to the unenlightened" (lines 30–32).

Because the claim is unfalsifiable, opponents subjected it to "intense scrutiny" (lines 41–42). The Jain response — accepted by around the sixth century CE — was that no one since the disciple Jambi had achieved enlightenment, and that omniscience was unattainable during the fifth and sixth spokes of the current world era. This "had the advantage of enabling the Jains to dispose easily of one of the main objections of their opponents, namely that nobody had ever encountered a fully omniscient person" (lines 50–53). Proof of such persons lay instead in "a scriptural tradition which described such omniscient teachers and communicated the otherwise inaccessible truths revealed by them" (lines 57–59).

## Relationship to Karma

Kevala-jnana arises only when all harming (*ghatiya*) karmas are eliminated. Specifically, the "knowledge-covering" karma (*jnanavaraniya*) is described by Abhayadeva Siri as "a cloud covering the sun of omniscience" — it prevents "the functioning of the developed mental capabilities of the jiva including the omniscience which is otherwise natural to it" (lines 499–505). Omniscience is thus not acquired but recovered: "All of these jivas are in their purest state identical and fully and equally endowed with the qualities of bliss, energy and omniscience" (lines 285–287); karma obscures these innate qualities.

## The Mystical Reading (Kundakunda)

The early Digambara writer [[kundakunda|Kundakunda]] "argue[s] for the mystical nature of this experience and suggest[s] that it is primarily directed internally towards realisation of the individual's inner nature" (lines 10–12), rather than being outward knowledge of all things. For Kundakunda, "the soul cannot be in any meaningful way modified by contact with karmic matter and therefore, on the level of the certain standpoint, the sole object of omniscience is the soul itself" (*NiyS* 159–66, line 885). This represents a minority but highly influential strand within Jainism.

## Soteriological Sequence

Kevala-jnana (enlightenment) is distinct from final *moksha*. An enlightened person (*kevalin*) may still live for a period, walking, preaching and meditating; "no new karma is bound by these activities nor is it possible in this state to carry out acts of violence, even involuntarily" (lines 693–694). Final deliverance requires also the cessation of the four non-harming karmas. See [[moksha]] and [[karma]] for the full sequence.
