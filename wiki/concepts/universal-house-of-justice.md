# Universal House of Justice

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Definition

The Universal House of Justice (Arabic: *Bayt al-'Adl-i-A'zam*) is the supreme ruling body of the Bahá'í Faith, established in 1963 (line 31533). It is the apex of the [[bahai-administrative-order|Bahá'í Administrative Order]].

## Textual Basis

Bahá'u'lláh referred to the future establishment of a House of Justice whose members ("Trustees") were assured of divine inspiration (TB 68; line 31544). The House would follow his teachings for the training of peoples, upbuilding of nations, safeguarding of human honour, and should be "a shelter for the poor and needy", promote the Lesser Peace, choose a universal language and script, and have charge of Bahá'í charitable endowments (KA 35, TB 70, 89, 125–130; lines 31535–31558). It would determine matters not already settled in the writings, and could legislate in response to changing conditions (lines 31559–31563).

'Abdu'l-Bahá emphasized the authority of the future "Universal" or "Supreme" House of Justice: it would be under Bahá'u'lláh's protection, inspired by the Holy Spirit, its decisions "of God", "the source of all good and freed from all error" (see [[infallibility-bahai|infallibility]]). Opposition to it would constitute opposition to God (lines 31569–31578). Members were to be elected by representatives of the various national Bahá'í communities through their "secondary Houses of Justice" (the national spiritual assemblies). For reasons 'Abdu'l-Bahá said would later become apparent, membership was to be confined to men (lines 31585–31595). Its head would be the Guardian (lines 31593–31594).

Shoghi Effendi stressed the complementarity of the Guardianship and the (then unelected) House of Justice: "to ensure the continuity of divinely appointed authority; safeguard the unity of the Faith; and maintain the integrity and flexibility of the Bahá'í teachings" (WOB 148; lines 31596–31604).

## Establishment

Both 'Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi considered establishing the House during their lifetimes but declined. In 1950/51, when there were nine national assemblies (with two more about to be formed), Shoghi Effendi established the [[international-bahai-council|International Bahá'í Council]] as a precursor (MBW 7–8; lines 31606–31623). After Shoghi Effendi's unexpected death in 1957, the [[hands-of-the-cause|Hands of the Cause]] assumed direction of the Faith, announcing elections for April 1963. Electors were members of all fifty-six national assemblies then extant (lines 31624–31634).

Since then the Universal House of Justice has acted as supreme Bahá'í administrative body and, in the absence of a living Guardian, as permanent head of the Faith (lines 31635–31636). Its constitution was adopted 26 November 1972; nine men are elected every five years at an international convention (representing 165 assemblies by 1993; lines 31638–31644).

## Membership

By 1998, sixteen men in total had served. The original nine elected in 1963 included: Hugh Chance (US), Háshmand Fathe-Azam (Iran), Amos Gibson (US), Lutfu'lláh Hakím (from International Bahá'í Council), David Hofman (Britain), H. Borrah Kavelin (IBC), 'Alí Nakhjavání (IBC), Ian Semple (IBC), and Charles Wolcott (IBC). Three of the original nine still served as of 1998 (lines 31649–31694).

Members do not hold special status or authority as individuals; the House exercises its leadership as a collectivity (lines 31733–31738).

## Relationship to the Guardianship

In the *Dispensation of Bahá'u'lláh* (1934) Shoghi Effendi had stressed the complementarity and "essential nature of the guardianship for the integrity of the Faith" (WOB 148, 150; lines 31743–31750). Shortly after its establishment, the House stated that there was no way in which further Guardians could be appointed (October 1963); its own authority and infallibility were not dependent on the presence of a Guardian (lines 31751–31769).

## Developments Since 1963

Under the Universal House of Justice's leadership:

- **Expansion**: massive growth of the worldwide Bahá'í community, five international teaching plans, four new Houses of Worship, bringing the global total to seven (lines 31783–31803).
- **Community development**: literacy, education, role of women and youth, family life, socio-economic development; made HÚqúqu'lláh applicable worldwide (1992); published the *Kitáb-i-Aqdas* (lines 31805–31817).
- **Iran**: responded to the sustained persecution following the Islamic Revolution of 1979; resettled refugees, replaced Iranian funding sources, garnered media attention (lines 31819–31834).
- **Administration**: ruled that no further Hands of the Cause would be appointed (November 1964); created the [[continental-boards-of-counsellors|Continental Boards of Counsellors]] (1968) and [[international-teaching-centre|International Teaching Centre]] (1973); increased Auxiliary Board membership (lines 31836–31857).
- **World Centre**: constructed its own seat in Haifa (1975–83), worked on the [[arc|Arc]] buildings (from 1992) and terraces of the Shrine of the Báb (lines 31858–31882).
- **Proclamation**: launched two global proclamation campaigns (1967 on the centenary of Bahá'u'lláh's letters to rulers; 1985 with *The Promise of World Peace*); greatly expanded Bahá'í involvement with the United Nations through the [[bahai-international-community|Bahá'í International Community]] (lines 31883–31915).

## Writings

The House has preferred directive guidance over legislative innovation. Its guidance takes the form of letters to communities, institutions, and individuals — volumes of which are published and regarded as divinely empowered. It has published its constitution (1972), compilations of Bahá'í writings on many topics, and commissioned translations (including the *Kitáb-i-Aqdas*, 1992). By 1983 over 60,000 items of letters by Bahá'u'lláh, 'Abdu'l-Bahá, and Shoghi Effendi had been collected (lines 31938–31995).
