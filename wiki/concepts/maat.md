# Ma'at

**Ma'at** is the central concept of [[egyptian-religion|ancient Egyptian religion]] — at once **cosmic order,
truth, justice, balance, and rightness** — the principle on which the entire universe, society, and morality
depend. Its opposite is **isfet** (chaos, falsehood, injustice). Ma'at is both an abstract principle and a
**goddess**, the daughter of [[ra|Ra]], depicted as a woman with an ostrich **feather** on her head.

## Order Against Chaos

The cosmos was created *out of* chaos and is forever threatened by its return; **maintaining ma'at** — keeping
the sun rising, the Nile flooding, society just, and falsehood at bay — is the great task of the gods and,
above all, of the **pharaoh**, whose whole legitimacy rests on "doing ma'at." [[ra|Ra's]] nightly battle with
the chaos-serpent **[[apep|Apep]]** is ma'at versus isfet on the cosmic scale; honest dealing and just
judgment are ma'at on the human scale.

## The Weighing of the Heart

Ma'at is also the standard of the **afterlife judgment**: in the [[book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]], the
heart of the dead is **weighed against the feather of Ma'at** in the Hall of Two Truths. A heart balanced
with the feather — a life lived in truth and justice — wins eternal life; a heart heavy with sin is devoured
(see [[osirian-cult|the Osirian cult]]). This is one of the earliest visions of **moral judgment after
death**.

## Cross-Tradition Note

Ma'at — divine **order and justice** that gods and kings must uphold, and against which the dead are
judged — is a rich comparandum for the Hebrew **[[covenant|covenant]] justice** and *tsedeq*, the Vedic
**rita** (cosmic order), the Chinese **[[dao|Dao]]**, and Greek *dikē*. A candidate comparison page on cosmic
order across traditions.
