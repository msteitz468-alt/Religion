# Modes of Being (*Seinsweisen*)

**Modes of being** (*Seinsweisen*, singular *Seinsweise*) is [[karl-barth|Karl Barth]]'s preferred terminological substitute for the classical word "person" (*persona*, *hypostasis*) in the doctrine of the Trinity. It appears in §9 of the [[barth-church-dogmatics-i1-s8-12|*Church Dogmatics* I.1]] and is the most distinctive formal innovation in Barth's trinitarian doctrine.

## The Problem with "Person"

Barth argues that the term *persona* was never "adequately clarified when first introduced into the Church's vocabulary" (*CD* I.1, ll. 2867–69). The term's ambiguity runs in two directions:

- In the Greek tradition, *πρόσωπον* risked sounding Sabellian (three masks behind which God is concealed as something other), so the East preferred *ὑπόστασις*; but that term sounded to the West like *substantia* — three substances, i.e., tritheism.
- Augustine already admitted that speaking of *tres personae* is simply *necessitas loquendi* — "not in order to say that the three in God are precisely *personae* but in order to say with the help of the term *personae* that there are three in God" (*De Trinitate* V, VII; ll. 2916–18). He called them *tres nescio quid* — three "I know not what."

The modern injection of *personality* (self-consciousness, individual will) into the concept was worst of all: applying this to each of the three would produce "three divine I's" and thus "the most extreme expression of tritheism" (ll. 2652–54). Neo-Protestant theology oscillated between this tritheistic use and a Sabellian reduction of the three to purely phenomenological modes of a single divine personality (ll. 3062–79).

## Barth's Proposal

Barth proposes *Seinsweise* as "not absolutely, but relatively better and more simply and clearly" expressing what the tradition meant by "person" (ll. 3113–16). The statement: "God is One in three ways of being, Father, Son and Holy Ghost, means … that the one God, i.e. the one Lord, the one personal God, is what He is not just in one mode but … in the mode of the Father, in the mode of the Son, and in the mode of the Holy Ghost" (ll. 3125–30).

The term is grounded in the Greek *τρόπος ὑπάρξεως* (mode of existence) or Latin *modus entitativus* (Quenstedt; ll. 3131–33), and in the Eastern term *ὑπόστασις* understood as *subsistentia* (mode of being of an existent), not as *substantia* (nature/essence). Barth can cite Calvin (*subsistentia in Dei essentia*), Wollebius, the Leiden Synopsis, and Burmann for versions of the same concept (ll. 3143–70).

## What the Concept Does (and Does Not) Say

The concept insists that God's "modes of being are not to be exchanged or confounded" (l. 3173): the Father cannot simply be the Son or the Son the Spirit; the modes are "irremovable" (l. 3185). At the same time, *Seinsweise* refers always to the *one* divine essence and one divine "personality" (in the modern sense): "we are not speaking of three divine I's, but thrice of the one divine I" (ll. 2658–59). The concept is deliberately less expressive than "person" with respect to the richness of the three, but more expressive with respect to what *makes* each mode what it is — namely, the mode's distinctive *relation of origin* to the other two modes.

The deeper rationale: "the truly material determinations of the principle of threeness in the unity of God were derived … not from an analysis of the concept of person, but from a very different source … This other source" — the doctrine of **relations** — "we prefer to let … rank as the primary one even externally, and therefore by preference we do not use the term 'person' but rather 'mode (or way) of being'" (ll. 3108–14).

## Reception

The term anticipates the concept of *Subsistenzweisen* (subsistent modes) in later twentieth-century trinitarian theology. Critics (e.g., some Roman Catholic responses to Barth) argued that *Seinsweisen* tilts toward Modalism by weakening the genuine distinction of the three; Barth countered that his account explicitly guards against both tritheism and modalism through the doctrines of [[perichoresis]] and [[opera-trinitatis|*opera ad extra indivisa*]].
