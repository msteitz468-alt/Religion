# Bahá'í Holy Days

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Legislative Basis

In the [[kitab-i-aqdas|Kitáb-i-Aqdas]], [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] ordained two "Most Great Festivals" for Bahá'ís to celebrate: the anniversaries of the Báb's declaration of mission in 1844 and his own declaration in the Garden of Ridván in 1863 ("the King of Festivals"). He also identified [[naw-ruz|Nawrúz]] (New Year) and the birthdays of the Báb and himself as holy days, and directed that these "Twin Birthdays" (occurring on the first and second days of the Muslim month of Muḥarram respectively) be accounted as one (lines 16727–16744).

During Bahá'u'lláh's lifetime Bahá'ís also commemorated the anniversary of the Báb's martyrdom. The anniversary of Bahá'u'lláh's passing was subsequently commemorated as a holy day. 'Abdu'l-Bahá did not wish Bahá'ís to celebrate his own birthday (which coincided with the anniversary of the Báb's declaration), but allowed them to celebrate the Bahá'í Covenant of leadership and his own succession, appointing a date for this 180 days after Bahá'u'lláh's passing. The anniversary of his own passing was also later commemorated. [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] did not permit any dates connected to his own life to be marked as holy days (lines 16745–16765).

## Calendar of Major Holy Days (lines 16766–16812)

| Date | Occasion |
|------|----------|
| **21 March** | Nawrúz (New Year) |
| **21 April – 2 May** | The Riḍván Festival (twelve days); the 1st, 9th, and 12th days are holy days on which work is suspended |
| **23 May** | Declaration of the Báb; commemorated at about two hours after sunset on the twenty-second |
| **29 May** | Ascension of Bahá'u'lláh |
| **9 July** | Martyrdom of the Báb; commemorated at noon |
| **20 October** | Birth of the Báb |
| **12 November** | Birth of Bahá'u'lláh |

**Minor holy days** (work not suspended):
| Date | Occasion |
|------|----------|
| **26 November** | Day of the Covenant |
| **28 November** | Passing of 'Abdu'l-Bahá; commemorated at about 1 o'clock in the morning |

## Observances

The Riḍván Festival commemorates Bahá'u'lláh's arrival at the Garden (1st day), the arrival of his family (9th day), and his final departure (12th day). The 1st day of Riḍván should be celebrated at 3 o'clock in the afternoon, marking the time of Bahá'u'lláh's arrival (lines 16775–16781).

Shoghi Effendi emphasized the importance of Bahá'ís abstaining from work on the major holy days, unless specifically disallowed under the terms of their employment, as an important means of gaining public recognition of the Faith. Bahá'í parents should similarly seek to gain permission for their children not to attend school on these days (lines 16785–16797).

There is no set format for the various commemorations; Bahá'í communities organize their own observances. Devotional programs of prayers and specific writings for the holy day — particularly Bahá'u'lláh's Tablet of Visitation for the day of his passing and for the martyrdom of the Báb — are commonly organized. Public celebrations and communal arts are often held on Nawrúz and the birthday of Bahá'u'lláh (lines 16829–16843).

In the Middle East, the "Twin Birthdays" are celebrated according to the Islamic lunar calendar; elsewhere, according to the Gregorian calendar (lines 16821–16828).

## Notes on Format

Shoghi Effendi discouraged Bahá'ís from celebrating the holy days of other religions among themselves (lines 16797–16800). He also encouraged them not to celebrate his own birthday or other dates connected to his own life as holy days (line 16764).
