# Qiblah (Bahá'í)

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Definition

The *qiblah* (Ar.) is "the point of adoration" to which believers turn in prayer. For Muslims this is the Ka'ba in Mecca (lines 25913–25916).

## History in the Babi-Bahá'í Tradition

In the Arabic [[the-bayan|Bayán]], the Báb changed the qiblah to "He Whom God Shall Make Manifest," stating that it would move as he moved. [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] confirmed this change in the [[kitab-i-aqdas|Kitáb-i-Aqdas]], identifying his own "Most Holy Presence" with "the Centre round which circle the Concourse on High." After his death the qiblah became his tomb at [[bahji|Bahjí]] (lines 25916–25927).

## Practice

Bahá'ís are directed to face the qiblah when they perform their daily obligatory prayer (*ṣalát*), but all other prayers may be said facing any direction ("Whichever way ye turn, there is the face of God": Qurán 2:115). In practice, the prayer of visitation for Bahá'u'lláh is also commonly said facing the qiblah. The bodies of the dead should be buried so that they "face" the qiblah, i.e. have their feet pointing towards it (lines 25927–25939).

## Contrast with Islamic Practice

The shift from Mecca to the tomb of Bahá'u'lláh represents a deliberate break with Islamic practice, signalling the independence of the Bahá'í revelation. The Báb's intermediate formulation — pointing toward the coming Manifestation rather than a fixed geographic location — is itself doctrinally innovative within the Islamic/Babi context.
