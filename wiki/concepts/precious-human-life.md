# Precious Human Life (Leisure and Opportunity)

In the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** (Vol. I, ch. 7), [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] opens the path
proper with the contemplation of the rare and valuable human birth endowed with **leisure** (*dal ba*)
and **opportunity** (*'byor ba*) — the indispensable basis for practising the Dharma.

## Leisure: the Eight Non-Leisures

Leisure is freedom from eight states lacking the conditions for practice (Nāgārjuna's *Friendly
Letter*): rebirth with wrong views, where no buddha has come, in the three lower realms, as a
barbarian in a borderland, as a long-lived deity, or as one "stupid and mute." "In these eight states
there is no time for acts of virtue."

## Opportunity: Ten Endowments

Five **self-pertaining** (from Asaṅga's *Śrāvaka Levels*): being human, born in a central region,
with complete faculties, with "reversible karma" (not having committed the five deeds of immediate
retribution), and faith in the source (discipline). Five **other-pertaining**: a buddha has come, has
taught the Dharma, the teaching endures, has followers, and there is "caring for others."

## Great Purpose and Rarity

The human life is "the supreme seed of glorious enlightenment" (Āryaśūra) and the only basis on which
vows may first be taken — even dying deities wish for human rebirth. Yet it is extraordinarily hard to
obtain: those reborn from the miserable realms into happy ones are "similar in number to the dust
particles on the tip of a fingernail" (*Bases of Discipline*); it is as unlikely as a blind sea
turtle surfacing into a floating yoke ([[shantideva|Śāntideva]]). Rare in its **causes** (pure
ethical discipline, generosity, and stainless aspirational prayers) and its **effects**, it must
therefore be seized for practice now — the gateway to a "lean toward practice" that, per Geshe Döl-wa,
"the practice of all other teachings follows."
