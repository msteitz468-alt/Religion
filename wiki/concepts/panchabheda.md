# Pañca-bheda — The Fivefold Difference

***Pañca-bheda*** ("fivefold difference") is the defining doctrine of [[madhva|Madhva's]]
[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita Vedānta]]: reality is constituted by **five eternal, real differences** that can
never be dissolved. It is the precise inverse of [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita's]] non-difference
(*abheda*) — where Śaṅkara holds that all apparent distinction is [[maya|māyā]] superimposed on one
[[brahman|Brahman]], Madhva holds that difference is the very nature (*svarūpa*) of things and is
beginningless.

## The Five Differences

Per [[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] (Vol. IV, "Defence of Pluralism"), the five are
the difference between:

1. **God and the soul** (*īśvara–jīva*)
2. **God and matter** (*īśvara–jaḍa / prakṛti*)
3. **One soul and another** (*jīva–jīva*)
4. **Soul and matter** (*jīva–jaḍa*)
5. **One material thing and another** (*jaḍa–jaḍa*)

These differences are **eternal**: "the differences in eternal things are eternal, and in non-eternal
things non-eternal." For Madhva, difference is not a relation added to things but identical with their
being — *bhedas tu sarva-vastūnāṃ svarūpaṃ naijam avyayam* ("difference is the very own, imperishable
nature of all things").

## The Underlying Ontology: Svatantra and Asvatantra

Beneath the fivefold scheme is a **twofold** division of all reality into the **independent**
(*svatantra*) — God alone, i.e. [[brahman|Brahman]] = **Viṣṇu** (Nārāyaṇa / Vāsudeva) — and the
**dependent** (*asvatantra*) — souls and matter, which are fully real but never self-standing. The
fivefold difference articulates how the dependent reals stand eternally distinct both from God and from
one another.

## Consequences

- **No identity in liberation.** Because the soul–God difference is eternal, [[moksha|mokṣa]] can never be
  absorption or identity; it is graded, blissful **service** of Viṣṇu, the souls remaining distinct and
  even ranked by merit *within* liberation.
- **A hierarchy of souls, and eternal damnation.** Souls fall into three classes — those *fit for
  emancipation* (*mukti-yogya*), those bound to transmigration, and (in Dasgupta's words) "the demons,
  ghosts and the like," the worst *cursed eternally in hell* (the later Mādhva term is *tamo-yogya*, not
  used by Dasgupta). This **doctrine of eternal damnation** is near-unique in Indian thought and follows
  directly from the eternity of difference.
- **Hermeneutics.** The fivefold difference forces Madhva's reading of the canon: difference-texts are
  literal, the great identity-texts ([[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|*tat tvam asi*]]) figurative or
  re-parsed toward distinction.

## Contrast Across the Schools

| School | Status of difference |
|---|---|
| [[advaita-vedanta\|Advaita]] | difference is **unreal** ([[maya\|māyā]]); only non-dual Brahman is real |
| [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta\|Viśiṣṭādvaita]] | difference is **real but inseparable** (souls/matter are God's body) |
| [[dvaita-vedanta\|Dvaita]] | difference is **real, eternal, and fivefold** (*pañca-bheda*) |
