# Nikah (Islamic Marriage)

*Nikah* (Arabic: نكاح) is the Islamic marriage contract — a legal and religious covenant between a man and a woman. In Sunni law, it is simultaneously a civil contract and an act of worship (*ibada*), since it fulfills the prophetic *sunna*. The major Sunni source for marriage law is [[sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]], Vol. 7, Book 62.

## Sources Ingested

- Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 7, Book 62 (Marriage), Nos. 1–177 — ingest 2026-06-27

## Marriage as Sunna

The Prophet explicitly forbade celibacy and castration as religious choices:

> "Are you the same people who said so-and-so? By Allah, I am more submissive to Allah and more afraid of Him than you; yet I fast and break my fast, I do sleep and I also marry women. So he who does not follow my tradition in religion, is not from me." (Vol. 7, Book 62, No. 1, Anas bin Malik)

Marriage is presented as the normative Prophetic path; abstaining from it is a deviation from the *sunna* even if motivated by piety. The Prophet recommended marriage specifically to young men as a guard against illegal sexual activity: "Whoever among you is able to marry, should marry, because it helps him lower his gaze and guard his modesty" (No. 4, Ibn Masud).

## The Mahr (Dowry)

The *mahr* (bride-gift, obligatory payment from groom to bride) is a required element of the marriage contract. Bukhari records a range of acceptable amounts:

- **Iron ring**: acceptable as minimum when groom has nothing of value, provided it is genuine (No. 62 area)
- **Qur'an memorization**: a woman may be given in marriage with a portion of Qur'an as the teaching mahr (a recognized precedent)
- **Manumission of a slave woman as mahr**: the Prophet manumitted Safiyya and made her manumission her mahr (No. 57 area)
- **Named cash amounts**: vary by Companion's resources; the text does not impose a fixed minimum

The mahr belongs exclusively to the wife and cannot be reclaimed except in khul' (wife-initiated divorce), where the woman voluntarily returns it.

## Conditions for Valid Marriage

Sunni law derived from Bukhari requires:
1. **Wali (guardian)**: the woman's male guardian (father, brother, or other *mahram*) must give consent; the Prophet stated "There is no marriage without a guardian"
2. **Witnesses**: two adult Muslim witnesses are required
3. **Mahr**: even a nominal gift must be specified
4. **Offer and acceptance (*ijab wa qabul*)**: explicit contractual formula

## Prohibited Combinations

Several categories of prohibited marriages appear explicitly:
- **Two sisters simultaneously**: forbidden (Nos. 36–38, citing Q 4:23)
- **A woman and her paternal or maternal aunt simultaneously** (Nos. 36–38)
- **Foster-milk relations**: parallel to blood-kin prohibitions when the milk was the infant's sole food (No. 36 area)
- **Ash-Shighar** (exchange marriage without mahr — i.e., "I give you my sister/daughter if you give me yours with no mahr"): explicitly forbidden (No. 39 area)
- **Prohibited degrees of kinship**: marriage within blood-kin categories listed in Q 4:22–24

## Orphan Guardians and Q 4:3

Aisha explains the Qur'anic verse "if you fear that you shall not be able to deal justly with the orphan girls, then marry other women" (Q 4:3): "This Verse has been revealed in connection with an orphan girl under the guardianship of her guardian who is attracted by her wealth and beauty and intends to marry her with a Mahr less than what other women of her standard deserve." (No. 2, Ursa from Aisha). The verse corrects guardians who exploit their position; they must give the orphan her full mahr or marry other women instead.

## The Prophet's Own Marriages

- Nine wives at the time of his death (Nos. 5–7); one wife (Maymuna, per No. 5) for whom there was no night-turn rotation
- The night-turn (*nawba*) system was maintained by lot; one wife might receive more days for extraordinary circumstances (a new bride received seven nights if a virgin, three if a matron — No. 48 area, Sa'ad)
- He was exempt from the general four-wife limit (Q 33:50) and from the strict equal-rotation requirement (Q 33:51), though he strove to maintain fairness

## Mut'a (Temporary Marriage)

A significant Sunni/Shia controversy documented in Bukhari:
- **Earlier permission**: Nos. 13o (Ibn Masud) and 52 state that the companions were permitted temporary marriage (*mut'a*) during campaigns in the early period of Islam
- **Abrogation at Khaybar**: Ali explicitly narrated that "the Prophet forbade the temporary marriage at the time of Khaybar and the eating of donkeys' meat" (No. 50, Ali)
- **Ibn Abbas's minority view**: Ibn Abbas reportedly preserved a permissive view (No. 51), which the compiler notes
- **Sunni position**: abrogated at Khaybar; haram; the Ali narration is authoritative
- **Shia position**: mut'a was abrogated and re-permitted multiple times; remains halal; the prohibition was introduced by Umar, not the Prophet

See [[fadak-inheritance]] for the broader Sunni/Shia pattern of disputed Companion narrations.

## Rights and Duties of Spouses

From Book 62:
- **Wife as "guardian" of husband's house and children** — accountable for that trust
- **Wife cannot fast optional fasts without husband's permission** when he is present (No. 120 area)
- **Refusal of husband's bed**: "If a man invites his wife to his bed and she refuses and causes him to sleep in anger, the angels will curse her till morning" (No. 121, Abu Huraira)
- **Husband's generosity**: spending on wife and family counts as *sadaqa* (No. 263 area — see [[nikah]])
