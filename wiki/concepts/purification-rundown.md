# Purification Rundown (and Narconon)

The **Purification Rundown** is a [[church-of-scientology|Scientology]] program developed by [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]] that combines exercise, sauna, and megadose niacin (vitamin B3) to purportedly eliminate drug residues and environmental toxins stored in body fat. It is described by the Church as a spiritual prerequisite: one cannot be fully audited until the body is purified of drug residues that would otherwise distort the E-meter's readings and impede spiritual progress.

## The Program

Per Wright (*Going Clear*) and standard Church materials:
- Extended daily sauna sessions (up to five hours)
- Running
- Large doses of niacin (beginning at 100 mg and escalating to 5,000 mg/day or more)
- Oil supplements (to replace fats in which toxins are theorized to be stored)
- Strict vitamins and mineral protocol

Hubbard's book *Clear Body, Clear Mind* (1990) serves as the primary text. The Church claims the program "is the most effective rehabilitation technology" for drug treatment, and the IAS listed it as a major Scientology accomplishment.

## Narconon

**Narconon** is the Church-affiliated drug rehabilitation program based on the Purification Rundown. It operates semi-independently, does not typically disclose its Scientology affiliation in recruiting materials, and has been used as an entry point into Scientology for recovering addicts. Multiple countries and states have investigated Narconon's medical claims and/or lack of proper disclosure.

Kirstie Alley, who has spoken publicly about Scientology, became involved after Narconon treated her cocaine addiction.

## "Hubbard Detoxification Day"

After September 11, 2001, the Church ran the "New York Rescue Workers Detoxification Project," applying the Purification Rundown to first responders. The program was publicized as the "most effective rehabilitation technology" for environmental toxin exposure. Medical authorities disputed its efficacy for this purpose.

## Medical and Scholarly Assessment

The Purification Rundown has no accepted scientific basis. Medical critics note:
- Niacin at prescribed doses causes "flushing" (skin reddening), which Hubbard described as evidence of toxin release — mainstream medicine attributes it to histamine release
- There is no mechanism by which sauna sessions remove stored drug residues at clinically significant levels
- Megadose niacin at the levels used carries risks (liver damage)

The Church disputes these assessments. Recorded without adjudication.
