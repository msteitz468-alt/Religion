# Moksha

**Moksha** ("release, liberation") is the supreme goal (*purushartha*) of [[hinduism|Hindu]]
soteriology: freedom from **samsara**, the beginningless cycle of death and rebirth driven by **karma**.
It is liberation *from* conditioned existence and *into* the highest reality — but what that release
consists of depends entirely on one's metaphysics of [[brahman|Brahman]] and [[atman|atman]].

## Doniger's Genealogy of Moksha (Modern Critical — *The Hindus*, Ch. 7)

[[wendy-doniger|Doniger]] (*[[doniger-the-hindus|The Hindus]]*, Ch. 7) traces *moksha* through **four
stages** of expansion, as a word that means *springing free* — "the release of an arrow from a bow or of a
prisoner from a jail":

1. **Freedom from death** — the earliest Upaniṣadic meaning, still firmly Vedic: the sacrificial patron
   *frees himself* from death and climbs to heaven. "Complete freedom (BU 3.1.3–6)." An entirely
   intrasacrificial concept.
2. **Freedom from rebirth** (*saṃsāra*) — freedom not just from death once, but from the grinding *series*
   of re-deaths (see [[samsara|saṃsāra]]). This is the fully developed Upaniṣadic concept.
3. **Freedom through renunciation** (*saṃnyāsa*) — in the later Renunciation Upaniṣads: "The ascetics who
   have full knowledge of the Vedānta are purified by the discipline of renunciation. In the worlds of
   brahman… they become fully immortal and fully freed (MU 3.2.6)."
4. **Freedom from the values of Vedic Hinduism itself** — the man freed from desires no longer accumulates
   karma; "he does not think, 'I did something good' or 'I did something bad'" — he is **beyond good and
   evil** (BU 4.4.22).

**The neti neti description.** Brahman/moksha, as the positive goal, is never positively described: "Brahman,
ineffable, can be described only in the negative: 'Not like this, not like that' (*neti neti*) (BU 4.5.15)."

**The householder/renunciant tension.** Moksha introduces an interloper into the three worldly aims
(*trivarga*) of dharma, artha, and kama (see [[dharma|Dharma]]). Doniger records five Hindu solutions to
the tension: (1) *sequence* — the four stages of life (*ashramas*), live the householder life first; (2)
*symbiosis* — the renouncer blesses the householder who feeds him; (3) *compromise* — periodic fasting,
limited renunciation within the householder life; (4) *identification* — do your worldly duty perfectly and
gain the merit of renunciation (the *Bhagavad Gita* solution, Krishna to Arjuna); (5) *mutual hierarchy* —
each side believes it outranks the other, and both are right within their own framework.

## Emancipation in the Upaniṣads (Dasgupta, Vol. I, Ch. III)

In the earlier [[upanishads|Upaniṣads]], [[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] writes,
**mukti is not a new acquisition** — "not a product, an effect, or result of any action, but it always
exists as the Truth of our nature." We are "always emancipated and always free," and *seem* bound only
because we do not know the self; therefore "the true knowledge of self does not lead to emancipation but
*is* emancipation itself." Bondage and rebirth persist only while the self identifies with its desires;
when it knows itself as "the highest essence and principle of the universe," desire ceases and with it
rebirth. Dasgupta frames the whole doctrine through the **Naciketas–Yama** dialogue of the Kaṭha
Upaniṣad — Naciketas refusing Yama's offered wealth and pleasures to demand instead the knowledge of what
survives death — and Yājñavalkya's image of phenomenal existence dissolving in the self "like a lump of
salt in water." This is the direct seed of [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]'s *jīvanmukti*.

## What Liberation Is, by School

- **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]**: moksha is **knowledge** (*jnana*) — the dispelling of ignorance and
  the realization that atman already *is* Brahman; possible while still embodied (*jivanmukti*).
- **[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]]**: moksha is **eternal loving communion** with the
  personal God, attained through **bhakti** and self-surrender — union, not identity.
- **[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]]**: moksha is **graded, blissful service** of God in his presence, the
  soul remaining eternally distinct (and, for Madhva, not open to every soul).
- **[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaivism]]**: moksha is **[[pratyabhijna|recognition]]** (*pratyabhijñā*) —
  the dawning awareness that one **already is** Śiva, the one free consciousness; bondage was only
  self-forgetting, so liberation reveals (not negates) the world as Śiva's real self-expression.

The **paths** (*yogas*) — knowledge ([[jnana-karma-samuccaya|*jnana*]]), action
([[karma-yoga|*karma*]]), and devotion (*bhakti*) — are harmonized in the [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]
— though *how* they relate to liberation is itself disputed.

## Cross-Tradition Comparison

Moksha as *release from rebirth* parallels the Buddhist **nirvana** ([[four-noble-truths|Four Noble
Truths]]) and Jain liberation, and contrasts with the Abrahamic goal of **resurrection / eternal life
with God** ([[resurrection-body|resurrection]], [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]]) — a once-lived life
judged, not an escape from recurrence. A candidate comparison page on liberation-vs-resurrection.
