# Moksha

**Moksha** ("release, liberation") is the supreme goal (*purushartha*) of [[hinduism|Hindu]]
soteriology: freedom from **samsara**, the beginningless cycle of death and rebirth driven by **karma**.
It is liberation *from* conditioned existence and *into* the highest reality — but what that release
consists of depends entirely on one's metaphysics of [[brahman|Brahman]] and [[atman|atman]].

## What Liberation Is, by School

- **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]**: moksha is **knowledge** (*jnana*) — the dispelling of ignorance and
  the realization that atman already *is* Brahman; possible while still embodied (*jivanmukti*).
- **[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]]**: moksha is **eternal loving communion** with the
  personal God, attained through **bhakti** and self-surrender — union, not identity.
- **[[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]]**: moksha is **graded, blissful service** of God in his presence, the
  soul remaining eternally distinct (and, for Madhva, not open to every soul).

The **paths** (*yogas*) — knowledge (*jnana*), action (*karma*), and devotion (*bhakti*) — are
harmonized in the [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]].

## Cross-Tradition Comparison

Moksha as *release from rebirth* parallels the Buddhist **nirvana** ([[four-noble-truths|Four Noble
Truths]]) and Jain liberation, and contrasts with the Abrahamic goal of **resurrection / eternal life
with God** ([[resurrection-body|resurrection]], [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]]) — a once-lived life
judged, not an escape from recurrence. A candidate comparison page on liberation-vs-resurrection.
