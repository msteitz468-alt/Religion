# Nadar (Divine Grace)

**Nadar** (also *nazar*, *kirpa*, *prasad*) is the [[sikhism|Sikh]] theological concept of **divine grace** — the unmerited favor through which [[akal-purakh|Akal Purakh]] draws souls to [[naam|Naam]] and liberation. In the [[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]], nadar is indispensable: no matter how much effort a person exerts, union with God ultimately depends on divine looking (*nadar* = "glance/look"). The Mul Mantar's closing phrase "by Guru's Grace (*Gurprasaad*)" encodes this principle. Nadar is not earned by caste, ritual, or asceticism; it overrides all social hierarchies.

## Nadar in Japji Sahib (Primary Text)

The *Mool Mantar* closes with "Gur Prasaad" ("By Guru's Grace") — establishing grace as the axiomatic prerequisite for knowing God, embedded in the Granth's foundational theological statement.

Pauri 4 (Ang 2): "By the karma of past actions, the robe of this physical body is obtained. By His Grace, the Gate of Liberation is found" (L214–222). Karma and Nadar are distinguished: karma provides one's birth and circumstance; only Nadar opens the path to liberation.

Pauri 7 (Ang 2): The decisive statement of grace-priority: even perfect worldly reputation across four cosmic ages is worthless without God's Nadar.

> still, if the Lord does not bless you with His Glance of Grace, then who cares? What is the use? (L326–328)

> O Nanak, God blesses the unworthy with virtue, and bestows virtue on the virtuous. No one can even imagine anyone who can bestow virtue upon Him. (L337–344)

This last verse states the asymmetry clearly: Nadar flows downward (God to human), never upward. No human act creates an obligation on God.

Pauri 24: "By His Glance of Grace, He bestows His Blessings." Pauri 38: "Such is the karma of those upon whom He has cast His Glance of Grace. O Nanak, the Merciful Lord, by His Grace, uplifts and exalts them." (L1756–1764, Ang 8)

The *Kirtan Sohila* (Hymn 5, Mehl 5): those "blessed to sing God's Praises" — the blessing is not earned; the language throughout is passive (blessed, given, awakened), emphasizing divine initiative.
