# Rita

**Rita** (Vedic Sanskrit *ṛta*, "that which is properly joined; order, truth") is the central ordering
concept of [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]] — the **cosmic, ritual, and moral order** that governs the
regular course of nature (the sun's path, the seasons, the dawn), the correct performance of the **sacrifice**,
and **truth and right conduct**. The opposite is *anṛta* (disorder, falsehood).

## The Order the Gods Uphold

Rita is not created by the gods but is the order they themselves **guard and embody** — above all
**[[varuna|Varuna]]** (with Mitra), the great sovereign who watches that rita is kept and punishes the lie.
The cosmos, the ritual, and morality are a single fabric of rightness: the **sacrifice performed in
accordance with rita maintains the cosmic order**, and truthful, ordered human life is part of the same
universal harmony.

## The Seed of Karma (Dasgupta)

[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] (Vol. I, Ch. II) makes *ṛta* the developmental root of
the **Law of [[karma|Karma]]**. Following Macdonell he notes the word marks order in three registers at
once — natural, **moral** (truth and right), and **religious** (sacrifice and its unalterable law of
producing effects). When this idea of an *inviolable cosmic law* was joined to the Brāhmaṇic conviction
that **sacrificial works mechanically produce their own results** — and to the notion that a person's
enjoyments and sufferings are necessarily tied to his deeds — the outcome was the doctrine of karma and
[[samsara|transmigration]]. In this reading *ṛta* is the **first germ of karma**: results follow acts by
an order built into reality, not by any god's grace.

## Cross-Tradition Note (Indo-European / Indo-Iranian)

Rita is the **Indo-Iranian cousin of Zoroastrian [[zoroastrianism|*asha*]]** (truth/order, opposed to the
Lie) — the two are the same ancient concept, sharply illuminating the shared Indo-Iranian heritage. It is
also comparable to Egyptian **[[maat|ma'at]]**, Chinese **[[dao|Dao]]**, and Greek *dikē*, and it is the
conceptual ancestor of the later Hindu **dharma**. A strong candidate for a cross-tradition comparison page on
**cosmic order**.
