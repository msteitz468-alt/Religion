# Adoptionism

**Adoptionism** is the Christology that **Jesus was by nature a human being whom God
adopted (or appointed) to divine sonship** — "not by nature divine," with no
pre-existence, no virgin-birth implication in its earliest forms, "not of the same kind
of 'essence' as God himself." It is the frozen, later form of the earliest
[[exaltation-christology|exaltation Christology]]; [[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]]
([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]]) notes that theologians' label
"adoptionism" carries a condescending ring ("*only* an adopted son") and prefers
"exaltation Christology" for the underlying view.

Ehrman highlights the **Roman logic of adoption** (citing Michael Peppard): an adopted son
often gained *higher* status than a biological one, chosen for merit and inheriting all the
father's wealth, status, and power (Octavian/Augustus vs. Caesarion). Applied to Jesus: "He
received all of God's power and privileges. He could defy death. He could forgive sins …
He was for all intents and purposes God."

## As a Condemned Position

As mainstream Christianity moved to [[incarnation|incarnation Christology]] in the later
first and second centuries, adoptionism was branded a **heresy** — one of the three
"Christological dead ends" (it "denies the divinity" of Christ; the others deny his
[[docetism|humanity]] or his [[modalism|unity]]).

- The **[[ebionites|Ebionites]]** — Jewish Christians who kept the Law; held Jesus "a man
  in a like sense with all" humanity, justified (like anyone keeping the Law as Jesus did)
  to become a "Christ."
- The **Theodotians** (Roman Adoptionists), following the shoemaker-theologian Theodotus:
  Jesus born of a virgin but "a (mere) man," empowered by the Spirit at his baptism — some
  said he became divine then, others only at the resurrection. They claimed their view was
  "the doctrine taught by the apostles themselves and by most of the church in Rome until
  the time of Bishop Victor"; Ehrman comments they "may well have had a point" historically.

Ehrman notes the orthodox charge that adoptionists *altered* manuscripts is not borne out
by the evidence — surviving variants more often show **orthodox** scribes adjusting texts
toward incarnational readings.
