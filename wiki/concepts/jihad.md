# Jihad (Struggle / Holy Warfare)

**Jihad** (Arabic: جهاد, "struggle, striving, effort") is one of the central concepts in Islamic
theology and law, denoting both interior spiritual striving and exterior armed conflict in defense
or expansion of the Islamic community. The word's root (*jahada*) means to exert effort or struggle.

## Typology

Islamic tradition distinguishes at minimum two senses:

- **Greater Jihad** (*al-jihad al-akbar*): the interior struggle against the self, passions, and
  sin — the most cited meaning in Sufi traditions and popular piety.
- **Lesser Jihad** (*al-jihad al-asghar*): armed struggle for the sake of God — the primary
  meaning in classical fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence) and the dominant usage in Sahih al-Bukhari.

## In Sahih al-Bukhari (Book 52 — Kitab al-Jihad)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Book 52 (Nos. 41–323). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Jihad's Rank among Deeds

**No. 41** (Abdullah bin Masud): Asked what the best deed is, the Prophet replied: (1) prayer at its
stated times, (2) dutifulness to parents, (3) Jihad in Allah's Cause. This hierarchy — salat first,
then parental obedience, then Jihad — is significant: Jihad is not the absolute highest obligation.

**No. 248** (Abdullah bin Amr): A man asked permission to join Jihad. The Prophet: "Are your parents
alive?" On hearing yes: "Then exert yourself in their service." Parental duties can override Jihad.

**No. 43 / No. 127–128** (Aisha): "The best Jihad (for women) is Hajj Mabrur." Jihad's meaning is
re-gendered: women's highest form is the spiritual discipline of pilgrimage.

### Intention Determines Validity

**No. 65** (Abu Musa): "He who fights that Allah's Word (i.e. Islam) should be superior, fights in
Allah's Cause." Fighting for booty, fame, or show does not qualify as Jihad in Allah's Cause — the
intention (*niyyah*) must be purely for divine supremacy.

**No. 196** (Abu Huraira): "I have been ordered to fight with the people till they say, 'None has
the right to be worshipped but Allah,' and whoever says, 'None has the right to be worshipped but
Allah,' his life and property will be saved by me except for Islamic law, and his accounts will be
with Allah." The Shahada as the trigger for protected status — connecting Jihad directly to
[[tawhid]].

### Paradise for Martyrs

**No. 46** (Abu Huraira): "Allah guarantees that He will admit the Mujahid in His Cause into
Paradise if he is killed, otherwise He will return him to his home safely with rewards and war booty."

**No. 48** (Abu Huraira): "Paradise has one-hundred grades which Allah has reserved for the
Mujahidin who fight in His Cause, and the distance between each of two grades is like the distance
between the Heaven and the Earth. So, when you ask Allah for something, ask for Al-Firdaus which is
the best and highest part of Paradise."

**No. 53** (Anas): "Nobody who dies and finds good from Allah… would wish to come back to this
world… except the martyr who, on seeing the superiority of martyrdom, would like to come back to
the world and get killed again."

**No. 54** (Abu Huraira): The Prophet himself expressed his desire to be martyred, resurrected, and
martyred again repeatedly: "By Him in Whose Hands my life is! I would love to be martyred in Allah's
Cause and then get resurrected and then get martyred…"

**No. 82** (Abu Huraira) — **Expanded martyrdom**: "Five are regarded as martyrs: They are those
who die because of plague, abdominal disease, drowning or a falling building etc., and the martyrs in
Allah's Cause." The category of martyrdom extends beyond the battlefield to deaths from disease,
disaster, and accident.

### Rules of Warfare

**Invitation to Islam before battle** (No. 192 / 253, Anas): The Prophet gave Ali the flag at
Khaibar with the instruction: "Be patient, till you face them and invite them to Islam and inform
them of what Allah has enjoined upon them. By Allah! If a single person embraces Islam at your hands,
that will be better for you than the red camels." Prior invitation is required before combat.

**Adhan as identity marker** (Nos. 193–195, Anas): "Whenever Allah's Apostle attacked some people,
he would never attack them till it was dawn. If he heard the Adhan (call for prayer) he would delay
the fight." At Khaibar: not hearing the adhan at first light authorized immediate attack. The adhan
functions as the legal marker of a Muslim community under protection.

**Prohibition on targeting women and children** (Nos. 257–258, Abdullah / Ibn Umar): "During some
of the Ghazawat of the Prophet a woman was found killed. Allah's Apostle disapproved the killing of
women and children." Standing prohibition on intentional killing of non-combatants.

**Tension with night-raid rule** (No. 256, As-Sab bin Jaththama): The Prophet was asked about night
attacks that might endanger pagan women and children. He replied: "They (i.e. women and children) are
from them (i.e. pagans)." This hadith stands in direct tension with Nos. 257–258 and generated
extensive classical jurisprudential discussion on collateral harm.

**Prohibition on burning** (No. 259, Abu Huraira): "It is none but Allah Who punishes with fire, so,
if you find them, kill them." Burning as punishment is exclusively divine and forbidden to humans.

**Apostasy** (No. 260, Ibn Abbas): "If somebody (a Muslim) discards his religion, kill him." Death
penalty for apostasy, without fire.

**Deception in warfare** (Nos. 267–269, Abu Huraira / Jabir): "War is deceit" — repeated three
times from different narrators, establishing deception in military strategy as permitted. Verbal
deception for assassination (Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf, Nos. 270–271) was explicitly authorized by the
Prophet.

**Obedience to commanders** (No. 203, Ibn Umar): "It is obligatory for one to listen to and obey
(the ruler's orders) unless these orders involve one disobedience (to Allah)." Qualified obedience.

**No. 210 / 266** (Abu Huraira): "Do not wish to meet the enemy, but when you meet (face) the enemy,
be patient." Seeking out battle is not recommended; if engaged, patience is obligatory.

### Ghulul (Misappropriation of Booty)

**No. 307** (Abu Huraira): *Ghulul* — theft from the common booty before distribution — is a major
eschatological sin. The offender will carry the stolen item on his neck on the Day of Resurrection
(a stolen sheep bleating, a stolen horse neighing, stolen gold and silver). The Prophet said he would
be unable to intercede for such a person.

### End-Times Jihad

**Nos. 176–177** (Abdullah bin Umar / Abu Huraira): "You will fight with the Jews, and the stone
behind which a Jew will be hiding will say, 'O Muslim! There is a Jew hiding behind me, so kill him.'"
An eschatological Jihad tradition embedded within the book, recorded without editorial comment.

**Nos. 175** (Um Haram / Anas): "Paradise is granted to the first batch of my followers who will
undertake a naval expedition." Eschatological rewards for specific anticipated future military ventures.

### Historical Episodes Documented in Book 52

- **Battle of Badr**: Archers' orders; Prophet's prayer in tent; Abu Sufyan questioned about surviving
  leaders; 140 enemy losses recorded.
- **Battle of Uhud**: Archers' fatal disobedience (No. 276 — the decisive narrative); Prophet wounded;
  Fatima washing blood; Abu Talha shielding the Prophet; Anas bin An-Nadr with 80+ wounds.
- **Battle of Khandaq (Trench)**: Gabriel ordering the Prophet to Bani Quraiza; Muslims digging;
  miraculous food supply; Bani Quraiza judgment (Sad bin Muadh's ruling endorsed by the Prophet).
- **Battle of Khaibar**: Ali given the flag; eye-trouble cured by the Prophet's saliva; invitation-
  to-Islam requirement before battle; adhan rule at dawn.
- **Battle of Hunain**: Prophet on white mule: "I am the Prophet in truth, I am the son of Abdul
  Muttalib"; Abu Sufyan holding reins; army retreated but Prophet stood.
- **Sariya of Asim bin Thabit** (No. 281): Khubaib's two rak'at before execution — established the
  practice of voluntary prayer before martyrdom for all Muslims under death sentence.
- **Killing of Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf**: Prophet authorized verbal deception to gain access.
- **Bani Quraiza judgment**: Sad bin Muadh ruled warriors to be killed and women/children enslaved;
  Prophet endorsed this as "the judgment of the King Allah."
- **Heraclius letter** (No. 191): Full extended hadith with Abu Sufyan's interview; Heraclius's
  internal acknowledgment of the signs of prophethood; text of the prophetic letter (Q. 3:64 cited).
