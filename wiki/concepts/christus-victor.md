# Christus Victor (The "Classic" Theory of Atonement)

The soteriological motif — named by Gustaf Aulén and championed by [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Jaroslav Pelikan]] (Vol. I, ch. 3) — in which salvation is above all **Christ's victory over death and the devil**. Pelikan argues it "was more important in orthodox expositions of salvation and reconciliation than Western dogmatics has recognized," and is rightly called "the classic theory of the atonement."

## The Motif

Its great expositor is [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]], building on Genesis 3:15 and Matthew 12:29 (binding the strong man): "He fought and was victorious; for he was man doing battle for the fathers, and by his obedience utterly abolishing disobedience. For he bound the strong man, liberated the weak, and by destroying sin endowed his creation with salvation." It is bound up with [[recapitulation|recapitulation]]: the second Adam undoes the first. [[origen|Origen]] too taught that Christ "by his death had begun the overthrow of the devil's dominion." Not only the resurrection but "especially his passion and death" belong to the victory.

## Related Themes

- **[[ransom-to-whom|Ransom to the devil]]** — the patristic consensus that the *lytron* of Mark 10:45 was paid to the devil (Origen explicit; rejected later by Gregory of Nazianzus). A sub-form of the victory motif.
- **Descent into hell** — see [[descent-into-hell]]: in the West the harrowing of hell took over the triumph-over-the-devil function the Greek fathers assigned to death and resurrection.

## A Cluster, Not a System

Pelikan stresses that early soteriology was a **cluster of images, not a doctrine**: it is impossible "to ascribe exclusive or even primary force to any one theory." Three rough atonement themes (Christ as teacher/example; as sacrifice/satisfaction; as victor) correspond to three definitions of salvation's content — **revelation of truth, forgiveness and justification, immortality and deification** — with a deeper unresolved question beneath them: did Christ's work *accomplish* the reconciliation, or *disclose* a reconciliation "there all along"? See [[atonement]], [[redemption]], [[apotheosis-divinization|deification]].
