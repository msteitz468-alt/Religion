# Messenger of the Covenant

"The messenger of the covenant" (*malʾaḵ habb'rîṯ*) is a phrase occurring only in
[[malachi|Malachi]] 3:1, at the heart of the book's most contested passage. It stands
amid a cluster of three figures whose identities and interrelations are a classic
crux: **"my messenger"** who prepares the way, **"the Lord" (*hāʾāḏôn*)** who suddenly
comes to his temple, and **"the messenger [angel] of the covenant."** Coverage here is
from [[pieter-verhoef|Verhoef]]'s NICOT commentary; the readings are attributed.

## The Text (Malachi 3:1)

"Behold, I will send **my messenger**; he will prepare the way before me. Then suddenly
(*piṯʾōm*) he will come to his temple, **the Lord** you are seeking, **the messenger of
the covenant**, in whom you delight."

The verse answers the taunt of 2:17 ("where is the God of justice?") with the
announcement of a coming that is a purifying judgment (3:2–5).

## Verhoef's Distinctions

Verhoef separates the three figures rather than collapsing them:

- **"My messenger" = a human forerunner.** The notion of a forerunner clearing the
  road before a king (Isa. 40:3; eastern royal custom) appears only here and in
  Malachi 3:23–24. Verhoef surveys the identifications — a heavenly angel (Rashi,
  Kimchi), Messiah ben Joseph (Ibn Ezra), prophets in general, the prophet Malachi
  himself — and concludes most interpreters rightly take him as **a human being
  identified with [[elijah|Elijah]]** (per 3:23–24). Following the NT (Matt. 11:10;
  Mark 1:2; Luke 1:76; 7:27), many identify him with [[john-the-baptist|John the
  Baptist]]; Verhoef proposes **"a pyramid of forerunners" with its pinnacle in John
  the Baptist.**
- **"The Lord" (*hāʾāḏôn*) = God himself,** who comes to his temple; Verhoef rejects
  reading this figure as the Messiah or as the forerunner (v. 5 makes clear God is the
  one who comes for judgment).
- **"The messenger of the covenant" = identified with the Lord, not the forerunner.**
  The relationship parallels that of the "angel of the Lord" elsewhere (Gen. 16; Exod.
  3), who is both identified with and distinguished from God. This messenger is
  "destined to dispense both the covenant blessing and the covenant vengeance," and
  the context "favors judgment" — like Israel wrongly longing for the Day of the Lord
  in Amos 5:18–20.

The [[covenant]] in view is "the one God made with Israel." Verhoef reads the prophecy
as uniting "first and second comings of Christ, with judgment stressed in both."

## Competing Readings (as positions)

- **R. T. France** holds all three figures — "my messenger," "the Lord," and "the
  messenger of the covenant" — are different descriptions of one messenger of Yahweh.
- **Kraetzschmar / Van der Woude** read "the angel of the covenant" as the tutelary
  (guardian) angel of the congregation, with "covenant" meaning the covenant community
  (comparing Dead Sea Scrolls usage).
- The **traditional christological reading** (Jerome, Calvin) makes the coming Lord
  the triune God or Christ; Verhoef judges this "more a dogmatic than an exegetical
  conclusion" while affirming the christological fulfillment on other grounds.

## Reception

The passage is foundational for the Jewish and Christian expectation of an
Elijah-forerunner (see [[elijah]]) and, in the NT, is applied to John the Baptist as
the one who "prepares the way." The purification of the [[levites|sons of Levi]] as a
refiner's fire (3:2–4) links the messenger's coming to the reform of the priesthood.
