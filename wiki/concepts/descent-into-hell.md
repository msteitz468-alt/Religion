# The Descent into Hell (Harrowing of Hell)

The doctrine — eventually a clause of the Apostles' Creed — that between his death and resurrection Christ "descended into hell" (Hades/Sheol). Drawn from [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Jaroslav Pelikan]] (Vol. I, ch. 3).

## Development

Pelikan traces a widening of meaning:
- The **earliest** (Syriac) references may have meant simply Christ's death and burial.
- [[justin-martyr|Justin]] and [[irenaeus|Irenaeus]] read it as **the liberation of the Old Testament dead** — Christ descending "to preach his own salvation" to the patriarchs in their graves (Irenaeus cites a passage he alleges the Jews expunged).
- **1 Peter 3:19** ("the spirits in prison") was variously applied, sometimes to pagans who had never heard Christ.
- It entered the **Western creed no earlier than c. 370**, and "became a controversy only in the Middle Ages and the Reformation."

## Function

In the West the descent took over the function the Greek fathers assigned to the death-and-resurrection itself: **the triumph over the devil and his legions** — making it a form of the [[christus-victor|Christus Victor]] motif. It is closely tied to [[bodily-resurrection|resurrection]] and the patristic understanding of [[sheol|Sheol/Hades]].
