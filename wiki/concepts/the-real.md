# The Real

**"The Real"** (Latin *Realitas*; chosen by [[john-hick|John Hick]] over "the Ultimate,"
"Ultimate Reality," or "the One") is Hick's term for the **single transcendent ground of
all the religious traditions** — the postulated reality to which the gods and absolutes of
the world's faiths are humanly conditioned responses. It is the metaphysical keystone of
[[religious-pluralism|religious pluralism]].

## The Real *an sich* vs. the Real as Humanly Experienced

Hick's central distinction, borrowed from **Kant**, is between:

- **The Real *an sich*** (in itself) — the *noumenal* ground, beyond the range of human
  concepts. Of it almost nothing can be literally said: it is not personal *or*
  impersonal, not one *or* many, not good *or* evil, not substance *or* process. Even
  these negations are provisional. It is **ineffable** in a strict sense.
- **The Real as humanly experienced** — the *phenomenal* manifestations that arise at the
  interface between the Real and a given community's "lens": the gods, absolutes, and
  ultimates of actual religious life.

Hick presses Kant's *noumenon/phenomenon* distinction into the epistemology of religion:
just as, for Kant, the mind's categories shape the raw input of the world into the
phenomenal world we experience, so the conceptual and cultural categories of each
tradition shape the impact of the Real into its experienced deity or absolute. He grounds
this in a wider principle he traces back to Aquinas ("things known are in the knower
according to the mode of the knower") and to the Sufi al-Junayd ("the colour of the water
is that of its container").

The traditions themselves, Hick notes, already draw a version of this distinction:

| Tradition | The Real *an sich* | The Real as experienced |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita Vedanta | *nirguna* [[brahman|Brahman]] (without attributes) | *saguna* Brahman / Ishvara |
| Mahayana / Pure Land | *dharmata* Dharmakaya | *upaya* Dharmakaya — Amida |
| Christian mysticism | the Godhead beyond God (Eckhart) | the personal Trinity |
| Kabbalah | [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] | the [[sefirot|sefirot]] |

## Personae and Impersonae

The Real is experienced under two basic ranges of category:

- **Personae of the Real** — the personal deities encountered in I–Thou worship: Yahweh,
  the heavenly Father, Allah, Vishnu, Shiva, Krishna. These are "joint products of
  transcendent presence and earthly imagination" — formed in interaction with their
  worshipping communities, and (like Yahweh in the Hebrew Bible) historically *developing*
  over time. A persona is real *within* the relationship, not a separate consciousness
  alongside the Real.
- **Impersonae of the Real** — the non-personal absolutes of the contemplative traditions:
  [[brahman|Brahman]], the [[dao|Tao]], Nirvana, [[sunyata|Sunyata]], the Dharmakaya.

The personal and non-personal are, Hick suggests, **complementary** — analogous to light
behaving as wave or particle under different experimental conditions. When approached in
the mode of I–Thou encounter the Real *is* personal; when approached in non-personal
contemplative awareness it *is* impersonal — not in itself, but in that relationship.

## The Standard Objection

The Real is the most criticized element of Hick's system. If literally *no* substantial
predicate applies to the Real *an sich* — not goodness, not personality, not oneness — then
(critics such as Keith Ward, Gavin D'Costa, and William Rowe argue) it is **indistinguishable
from nothing at all**, and cannot ground Hick's confident value-claim that the traditions
are genuinely "in touch" with it or are turning their adherents toward it. The neo-Kantian
move, the charge runs, either says too little to do any work, or quietly smuggles in
content (e.g. that the Real is benign) that its own apophaticism forbids. See
[[religious-pluralism-debate|the pluralism debate]].

## Cross-Tradition Significance

- The Real is a deliberately **apophatic** construct and invites comparison with negative
  theology generally — Pseudo-Dionysius, the Cappadocians, Maimonides' *via negativa*, and
  especially [[sunyata|Sunyata]] and *nirguna* [[brahman|Brahman]] (which Hick treats not
  as the Real itself but as *impersonae*, the Real as experienced in particular
  traditions).
- It reframes every named ultimate in this wiki — [[tawhid|Allah]], the Trinity,
  [[brahman|Brahman]], [[dao|the Dao]], [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] — as a phenomenal manifestation
  rather than a competing candidate for "the" ultimate.
