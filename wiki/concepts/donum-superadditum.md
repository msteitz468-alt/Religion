# Natural Endowment and the Superadded Gift (*Donum Superadditum*)

The distinction — which [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Pelikan]] (Vol. I, ch. 6) presents as the climax of the Western settlement on nature and grace — between what human nature *possesses by creation* and the **saving grace that is a "superadded gift"** (*donum superadditum*) of God's unmerited generosity, mediated through the church and its sacraments. Pelikan traces the later medieval-scholastic formula to its root in [[augustine|Augustine]] himself.

## The Distinction in Augustine

Augustine separated two senses of "grace": (a) **natural grace** — "that grace by which we are distinguished from cattle," including the natural distinctions of intelligence and beauty; and (b) the good gift "that pertains to a holy life," which "did not come from nature, but was superadded by God." His clinching formulation: "**The capacity to have faith, as the capacity to have love, belongs to men's nature; but to have faith, even as to have love, belongs to the grace of believers.**"

This honoured the genuine [[natural-law|natural endowments]] even of the heathen (against any Manichaean denigration of nature — "although there was a fault present in nature, yet nature was not itself a fault") while insisting that saving grace is wholly gratuitous and added from above.

## Significance

The formula "set the terms for the development of medieval doctrine": the scholastic *donum superadditum* (original righteousness as a gift added to a complete human nature) and the whole nature/grace architecture of Thomism descend from it. It is the constructive counterpart to the [[semi-pelagianism|Semi-Pelagian]] settlement at the [[semi-pelagianism|Council of Orange (529)]] — "the natural endowments of man… were not to be minimized, but saving grace was a superadded gift."
