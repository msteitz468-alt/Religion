# Apokatastasis (Universal Restoration)

*Apokatastasis* (ἀποκατάστασις, "restoration") is the doctrine — classically [[origen|Origen]]'s — that all rational creatures, even the damned and the Devil, will ultimately be purified and restored to God, so that "the end will be identical with its beginning" and God will be "all in all." Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. XVII.

## Origen's Doctrine

The doctrine rests on two principles: the **freedom of the will** and the **goodness of God**. All punishment, including the pains of hell, must therefore be medicinal and finite — God's chastisement working "by discipline, persuasion and instruction," not force. Origen's eschatology "culminates" here; even the Devil, "it appears, will participate in the final restoration" (a corollary Origen later denied, but which "the logic of his system required"). Kelly is careful to note Origen did *not* mean a pantheistic absorption — the distinction between God and created spirits remains. [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]] took it up, envisaging "the eventual purification of the wicked… and the final restoration of all things, the Devil himself included."

## Reception

By the fifth century "the stern doctrine that sinners will have no second chance after this life, and that the fire which will devour them will never be extinguished, was everywhere paramount" (see [[eternal-punishment]]). Apokatastasis was eventually condemned. See the [[universal-salvation-controversy]].
