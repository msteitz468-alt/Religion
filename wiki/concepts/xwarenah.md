# Xwarenah (the divine glory / kingly fortune)

**Xwarenah** (Avestan *xwarenah*; Middle Persian *xwarrah*; New Persian **farr** or
*farr-i izadi*) is the [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] concept of a radiant **divine glory,
fortune, or charisma** — a numinous power bestowed by [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]] that
confers legitimacy, success, and a kind of luminous "grace." It is one of the most
pervasive ideas in Iranian religion and kingship, recurring (per
[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]]) from the [[gathas|Gathas]] through the
Achaemenid monuments and Sasanian investiture art down to Ferdowsi's *Shah Nameh* and
Shi'a thought.

## Range of Meaning

- A **personal endowment**: first used in the Gathas of **Jamaspa**; more broadly, the
  glory that marks the favored and the righteous.
- A **kingly fortune** (*kavaem xwarenah*, the "Kayanian glory"): the divine sanction
  that makes legitimate rule possible. It can be **won and lost** — when [[yama|Yima]]
  sins, the *xwarenah* is said to fly away "in the form of a bird" (Zamyad Yasht), and
  in the **Karnamag** of Ardashir it races after the new king as a **ram**, abandoning
  the doomed last Parthian king.
- A **cosmic glory** described in the **Zamyad Yasht** (Yt 19) as "the mighty, gleaming
  glory created by Mazda," contested between the forces of good and evil and ultimately
  secured for the coming [[saoshyant|Saoshyant]].

## In Iranian Kingship and Art

The bond between glory and rule is the visual program of Persian monarchy. On
[[persians|Achaemenid]] tomb reliefs and at Bisutun a **winged figure/disc** hovers over
the king — variously read as Ahura Mazda, the king's [[fravashi|fravashi]], or the
*xwarenah* itself (popularly the *fravohar*). On **Sasanian** investiture reliefs Ahura
Mazda hands the king the **ring of investiture**, symbol of the *xwarrah*; the **ram,
boar, and bird-with-pearl** are its emblems on crowns and seals. "Church and state were
born of one womb," says the *Letter of Tosar* — and *xwarrah* is the theological hinge of
that **religiocracy**.

## Significance and Comparisons

*Xwarenah* fuses **legitimacy, righteousness, and luminosity** into a single transferable
substance, making Iranian kingship an explicitly religious office and tying the fate of
the realm to the moral order ([[asha-druj|asha]]). It survives Islamization as **farr**
in the *Shah Nameh* and is reapplied in Shi'a thought to the line of Ali (*farr-i izadi*).
As a "divine glory" motif it invites comparison with the Hebrew
**[[divine-glory-kabod|*kabod*]]** (the radiant glory of YHWH) and Greek *doxa*, though
the distinctively Iranian element is its attachment to **kingship and personal fortune**.
