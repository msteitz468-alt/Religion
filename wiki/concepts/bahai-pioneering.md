# Pioneering (Bahá'í)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 24513–24596.*

## Definition
Bahá'ís who have left their own homes and established residence in another locality or country to propagate the Bahá'í Faith are considered 'pioneers' (the term is [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]'s) (lines 24515–24520).

## Charter and Early History
In his *Tablets of the Divine Plan* (1916–17), [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] called on North American Bahá'ís to propagate the Bahá'í teachings throughout the world. A few responded, but it was only during the first American Seven Year [[bahai-plans|Plan]] (1937–44) that there was a systematic endeavour to establish the Bahá'í Faith outside North America through the dispatch of pioneers to Latin America (lines 24521–24531).

## The Ten Year Crusade and Knights of Bahá'u'lláh
Pioneering became greatly emphasized during the international Ten Year Crusade (1953–63), the pioneers of that plan being given the honorific title 'Knights of Bahá'u'lláh' for settling in virgin territories. Pioneering has remained an important part of subsequent plans under the Universal House of Justice (since 1963). The House appointed Continental Pioneer Committees in 1965 to help with the placement of the increasing number of pioneers (402 in that year world-wide) (lines 24532–24547).

## Scriptural Basis
[[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] praised those who had left their homes to teach his Cause. They would be strengthened by the power of the 'Faithful Spirit', and accompanied by 'a company of our chosen angels'. Such action was 'the prince of all goodly deeds' (GWB 333 no. 157) (lines 24548–24554).

'Abdu'l-Bahá praised the blessings of 'homelessness and adversity in the pathway of God'; called on those who propagated the Faith to be fearless and have no thought for themselves; and assured them of divine assistance. They should learn the languages of the places they went to, and be filled with the love of God (LC 570; TDP) (lines 24555–24564).

## Shoghi Effendi's Guidance
Shoghi Effendi stated that pioneering was the most important Bahá'í activity that anyone could perform, particularly to areas where there were as yet no Bahá'ís. Such pioneers should persevere and not abandon their posts until the Faith was well established (the example of Marion [[jack-marion|Jack]], who stayed in her pioneer post in the face of tremendous hardship, was lauded). Pioneers should try to disperse rather than congregate in one locality, so as to have more contact with local populations. Ideally they should be self-supporting. If unable to support themselves they could be assisted from Bahá'í [[funds-bahai|Funds]], but this should not be regarded as a permanent arrangement. Those unable to pioneer might 'deputize' (i.e. subsidize) someone else to do so on their behalf. Pioneers should keep in close contact with the relevant Bahá'í administrative body (lines 24565–24591).
