# Religion as a Virtue (Aquinas)

In [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]], **religion** (*religio*) is not faith or piety in the modern loose
sense but a precise **moral virtue annexed to justice** — the virtue by which we render God the
worship that is his due ([[summa-theologica|*ST*]] II-II Q81–100). Because we can never repay God in
full equality, religion falls short of strict justice, yet it is "the chief of the moral virtues,"
standing nearest the [[theological-virtues|theological virtues]].

## Religion in itself (Q81)

Aquinas gives the three classical etymologies — Cicero's *relegere* (to re-read/ponder), Augustine's
*re-eligere* (to re-choose God) and *religare* (to re-bind) — and concludes that religion "denotes
properly a relation to God" (Q81, A1). It is a **special moral virtue**, distinct from the
theological virtues: **God is its end, not its object** — its acts (worship) are directed *to* God,
but it is about the *acts of worship* rather than about God himself (Q81, A5). Its **interior acts**
(devotion, prayer) are primary; its **exterior acts** (adoration, sacrifice, oblations, vows) are
secondary signs that arouse and express the interior worship (Q81, A7). The proper act of religion is
**latria** — the worship owed **God alone**, sharply distinct from the **dulia** owed to creatures
(see [[cardinal-virtues]]).

## Interior acts: devotion and prayer (Q82–83)

- **Devotion** (Q82) is "the will to give oneself readily to things concerning the service of God"; it
  is caused by **meditation** — on God's goodness and on one's own defects — and its chief effect is
  **spiritual joy**, with a secondary sorrow. Christ's humanity is its great occasion.
- **Prayer** (Q83) is the most developed question. Prayer is an act of the **reason** (not the
  appetite): "the unfolding of our will to God." We do **not pray to change God's eternal disposition**
  but "**that we may obtain what God has appointed to be fulfilled by our prayers**" (Gregory). We pray
  **to God** alone for fulfilment, but rightly ask the **saints** to intercede. The **Lord's Prayer**
  is "the most perfect of prayers," its seven petitions ordered from the end (God's glory) through the
  means to the removal of obstacles. Prayer has three effects — **merit, impetration, and spiritual
  refreshment** — and is infallibly heard when one asks, **for oneself, things necessary for
  salvation, piously, and perseveringly.**

## Exterior acts: adoration, sacrifice, vows (Q84–88)

- **Adoration** (Q84) — interior and bodily reverence; bodily signs are "connatural to man."
- **Sacrifice** (Q85) belongs to the **natural law** generically (every people offers sacrifice), with
  its specific matter set by positive/divine law; it is due to **God alone**. Aquinas distinguishes
  **sacrifice** (something done to the offered thing), **oblation** (a mere offering),
  **first-fruits**, and **tithes** (Q86–87).
- **Vows** (Q88) — a vow is a deliberate **promise made to God** concerning a better good; it is an act
  of latria, and acting under a vow is **more meritorious** (it offers the will itself). The **solemn
  vow** (taken in Holy Orders or religious profession) of continence is "absolutely indispensable."
  The Sovereign Pontiff holds the fullest power to dispense or commute vows. (Vows lead into the
  treatise on the religious state — see [[states-of-life]].)

## The vices opposed to religion (Q89–100)

- **Oaths** (Q89) require three conditions — **judgement, justice, and truth**; an oath is a remedy
  for human distrust, not a good to be sought; **perjury** (Q98) is always a mortal sin against God.
- **Superstition** (Q92) is the vice of **excess** in worship — not in *how much* but in *to whom* and
  *in what manner*. Its species (Augustine) are: undue worship of the true God, **idolatry**,
  **divination**, and superstitious **observances**.
- **Idolatry** (Q94) is "the gravest sin" in its genus, for it sets up a false god; Aquinas reviews the
  ancient "three theologies" (mythical, civil, natural) and assigns idolatry both human causes
  (disordered affection, pleasure in images, ignorance of God) and a completive **demonic** cause.
- **Divination** (Q95) — astrology, augury, omens, necromancy, lots — is unlawful when it seeks
  knowledge of **free acts or hidden contingents**, which only God knows; astrology is licit only for
  **natural** effects (weather, harvests). Diviners "succeed" because most people follow their
  passions, and because of **demonic interference** (Augustine). The **notory/magic arts** and
  **amulets** (Q96) involve a tacit or explicit **compact with demons** and are condemned; sacred
  words may be worn only if they contain no invocations or unknown characters and one's hope rests in
  God alone. **Tempting God** (Q97), **sacrilege** (Q99, ranked by the holiness of the thing
  violated), and **simony** (Q100 — the buying or selling of spiritual things, which "cannot be
  sold") complete the vices against religion.

## Cross-tradition note

Aquinas's *religio* — worship as a discrete virtue of justice toward God, with interior acts (prayer,
devotion) grounding exterior acts (sacrifice, vows) — offers a sharp comparison with the worship and
sacrifice concepts of other traditions; see [[sacrifice]], [[yajna]], and [[five-pillars]].
