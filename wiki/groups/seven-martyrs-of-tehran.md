# Seven Martyrs of Tehran

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Overview

Seven Bábís executed for their beliefs in 1850 (19–20 February). A Babi plot against the life of the chief minister, Amín Nizám, was alleged (but not widely believed), and a group of suspected Bábís arrested. Most chose to avoid identifying themselves as Bábís and were released, but seven remained resolute and were beheaded. As European envoys noted, the execution was in public, and replaced the earlier practice of private executions in the presence of the sháh (lines 28258–28268).

The seven killed were all men of high social status, and their execution attracted considerable attention. They were:
1. Hájí Mírzá Sayyid 'Alí, the Báb's maternal uncle and guardian, a leading merchant of Shíráz
2. Mírzá Qurbán-'Alí of Bárfurúsh, a prominent spiritual guide (*murshid*) in the Ni'matu'lláhí Sufi order
3. Hájí Mullá Ismá'íl Qumí (cleric)
4. Áqá Sayyid Husayn Turshízí (cleric)
5. Hájí Muhammad-Taqí Kirmání (merchant)
6. Sayyid Murtadá Zanján (merchant)
7. Muhammad-Husayn Maraghi'í (government official)

(Lines 28269–28292.)

## Significance

The Báb referred to them as the "seven goats" of Islamic tradition who would walk before the promised Qá'im on the Day of Judgement (lines 28288–28291). Other groups of seven martyrs are lauded in Bahá'í history, notably the "Seven Martyrs of Yazd" (1891) and the "Seven Martyrs of Hurmuzak" (1955) (lines 28295–28298).
