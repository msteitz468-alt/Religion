# Amorites

## Identity and Nomenclature

"Amorite" renders Akkadian *Amurru* ("the West") and names a large, complex group of
**Northwest-Semitic** speakers, the Amorite language most likely originating in
northeastern Syria. The Middle Bronze Age (2000–1600 BCE) in northern Syria and
Mesopotamia is called the **"Age of the Amorite kingdoms,"** since most major cities
were then ruled by kings with Amorite names (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1).

Older scholarship cast the Amorites as crude desert nomads who overran Sumer and
toppled Neo-Sumerian civilization. Coogan corrects this: while there was a
substantial pastoralist element, large parts of the Amorite tribes were **sedentary**
— villagers and city-dwellers long established in Mesopotamian society. The clans who
seized power were already fully urbanized.

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **Mesopotamian history**: Amorite dynasts ruled [[mari|Mari]], Babylon (Hammurapi),
  and Yamhad (Aleppo). Shamshi-Adad I of Upper Mesopotamia belonged to an Amorite
  tribe of northern Syria.
- **The Bible**: "Amorite" is used loosely for the pre-Israelite population, both
  generally (sometimes interchangeably with "Canaanite") and specifically for the
  **Transjordanian kingdom of Sihon** (Num. 21.21–31), conquered en route to Canaan.
  Amos 2.9 recalls the Amorite "whose height was like the height of cedars."

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Israelite/Jewish**: the Amorites are one of the standard pre-Israelite "nations"
  of the land; Sihon and Og become paradigmatic defeated kings, rehearsed in
  liturgy (Ps. 135–136).
- **Critical scholarship**: distinguishes the historical MB Amorites of the cuneiform
  record from the biblical "Amorites," a later catch-all term for prior inhabitants;
  the highland Transjordanian "Amorite" kingdoms of Numbers 21 show **no Late Bronze
  occupation** archaeologically, undercutting a literal 13th-c. conquest there.

## Historical and Archaeological Context

Our chief window is the ~25,000 tablets from [[mari|Mari]] on the middle Euphrates
(palace of Zimri-Lim, destroyed by Hammurapi c. 1760 BCE), supplemented by archives
from Tell Leilan (Shubat-Enlil), Alalakh, and elsewhere. They illuminate Amorite
tribal structure (the Hanean confederation; Simalite/northern vs. Yaminite/southern
branches) and the symbiosis of pastoralists with town-dwellers — a model Coogan
applies to the **patriarchal way of life** in Genesis (see [[abraham]]). Kingdom of
**Amurru** later became a strategic prize between Egypt and Hatti in the Amarna age.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

In the Pentateuch's geography of promise, the defeat of the Amorite kings Sihon and
Og marks the threshold of the land — the first conquests, the proof that Yahweh gives
nations into Israel's hand. "Amorite" thus carries the weight of the doomed-nation
motif alongside [[canaanites|Canaanite]].

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

The Mari evidence reshaped 20th-c. debate over the "patriarchal age," once anchored to
an "Amorite migration." Coogan (with most scholars) now treats such parallels as
*illustrative*, not probative, of the historicity of Genesis. Related:
[[canaanites]], [[hurrians]], [[mari]], [[hammurapi|Hammurapi]], [[abraham]].
