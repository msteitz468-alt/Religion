# Edomites

## Identity and Nomenclature

**Edom** ("red") was the kingdom of Israel's southeastern neighbor, also called
**Seir**, in the rose-red mountains east of the Arabah and south of the Dead Sea. In
the biblical genealogy the Edomites are **"the sons of [[esau|Esau]],"** [[jacob|Jacob's]]
twin brother (Gen. 36) — an etiology that frames them as Israel's nearest **kin** and
sharpest **rival** ("brother Jacob," Obad. 10). In the Greco-Roman period the southern
group is called **Idumea** (Idumeans).

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **Genesis**: the Esau/Jacob rivalry — birthright and blessing (Gen. 25, 27, 33, 36).
- **Numbers/Deuteronomy**: Edom refuses Israel passage in the wilderness; yet Israel
  is told not to abhor an Edomite, "for he is your brother" (Deut. 23.7).
- **Obadiah, Lamentations 4, Psalm 137, Ezekiel 35**: Edom's **betrayal at the fall of
  Jerusalem (586 BCE)** — looting, blocking fugitives, rejoicing — is the most reviled
  act in the exilic record, *more* than the Babylonians themselves (Cogan, Coogan
  Ch. 7). Obadiah's whole 21-verse oracle is Edom's condemnation.
- **Ezra-Nehemiah**: by the Persian period **Idumea** lies south of [[jerusalem|Judah]],
  settled by Edomites pushed northward out of their old territory by Arab tribes (the
  **Kedarites**) — Hebron itself is "now in Edomite territory" (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8).

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Israelite/Jewish**: the **brother-enemy** par excellence — kinship intensifies the
  sense of betrayal. Edom becomes a cipher for the hostile, treacherous neighbor; in
  later Jewish tradition "Edom" is even reapplied to Rome.
- **Critical/archaeological**: settled Edom develops late (little Late Bronze
  occupation in southern Transjordan), with Edomite material culture (Khirbet Qitmit,
  En Haseva) marking their push into the Negeb at the end of the Judean monarchy.

## Historical and Archaeological Context

Centuries-long strife between Judah and Edom centered on the **southern Negeb and the
Red Sea port of Elath/Eilat** and its trade routes (an Arad ostracon warns "otherwise
Edom will arrive there"). After 586 the Edomites occupied parts of southern Judah; in
the Persian period this becomes the province/region of **Idumea**, its capital unknown.
The Edomites were themselves displaced eastward by advancing **Arab (Kedarite)**
tribes.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Edom embodies the theme of **fraternal betrayal** within the family of nations:
because Edom is "brother," its treachery at Judah's lowest moment carries unique moral
weight, generating poetic justice oracles (Obadiah). The Esau/Jacob narrative also
anchors the broader biblical pattern of younger-over-elder election.

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

In Second Temple and rabbinic literature "Edom" becomes a symbolic name for oppressive
empire (eventually Rome). **[[herod-the-great|Herod the Great]]** was of [[idumeans|Idumean]]
descent — a fact later used to question his legitimacy as "king of the Jews."

In the **[[kabbalah|Kabbalah]]** of the [[zohar|Zohar]] (end-of-Genesis material) this
brother-enmity is raised to cosmology: **[[esau|Esau]]/Edom is the worldly face of the
[[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]** (the "Other Side"), and Esau's heavenly Prince is **Samael**.
Edom thus becomes the archetypal demonic-historical adversary whose eschatological
judgment (Isa 63; Obad.) inaugurates the messianic redemption — the final breaking of the
Other Side. This is the mystical culmination of the "Edom = oppressive empire" trope.
