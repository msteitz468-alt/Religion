## Identity and Nomenclature

The Homoians are the theological party whose defining slogan was that the Son is "like" (*homoios*) the Father, or "like according to the Scriptures," avoiding all technical *ousia* terminology. Ayres traces their emergence to the Sirmium 357 meeting, where a small gathering produced a "manifesto" condemning use of *ousia*/*substantia* altogether:

> "But as for the fact that some, or many, are concerned about substance (*substantia*) which is called *ousia* in Greek, that is, to speak more explicitly, *homoousion* or *homoiousion*, as it is called, there should be no mention of it whatever, nor should anyone preach it." (Hilary, *Synod.* 11–241)

Ayres notes that "strong ambivalence to Nicaea, or a wish to ignore its terms, has turned to direct opposition. This text demonstrates growing clarity among some theologians that resulted in the emergence of 'Homoian' theology". He cautions that "ecclesiastical parties are complex and often fluid entities—and the Homoians were particularly so". For some scholars the Homoian party cannot properly be said to exist until the Dated creed of 359; Ayres argues against such a strict dating given the slow emergence of likeness language through the decade (footnote 313).

The Homoians should not simply be identified with [[arianism|Arians]]: Ayres emphasizes that many theologies in this period were "non-Nicene more than anti-Nicene; only in the 350s do we begin to trace clearly the emergence of directly anti-Nicene accounts".

## Key Proponents

- **Acacius of Caesarea** (bishop c.340–365): Eusebius of Caesarea's successor; a "powerful figure" with "significant influence with Constantius"; presided over the Constantinople 360 council.
- **Eudoxius**: bishop of Antioch from 357, then bishop of Constantinople 360–70; initially supported the most radical subordinationist Homoians (including [[aetius|Aetius]]) but later broke with them and became influential with Emperor Valens (footnote 315).
- **Valens** and **Ursacius**: western figures who helped draft the Sirmium 357 manifesto and pressed the Dated creed at the Council of Ariminum.

The Homoian "leadership of this alliance was always diverse... bishops of different stripes united by the desire to find a solution to the ongoing controversy that would rule out any theologies seemingly tainted with Marcellan emphases. Homoians are found in east and west, but Greek- and Latin-speaking Homoians follow different lines of development. In particular, Latin Homoians managed to contain some of the distinctions that resulted in distinct groupings in the east".

## Key Documents

1. **Sirmium 357 "manifesto"**: Probably composed by Ursacius and Valens, possibly with Potamius of Lisbon; proscribed all *ousia* language. The seminal Homoian document.
2. **The Dated creed**: Drafted at Sirmium before the twin councils. Asserts the Son "like the Father in all respects, as the Holy Scriptures also declare and teach," while rejecting *ousia* language as unscriptural and a source of "disturbance". Known as the "Dated creed" because it bore a date in its prologue — a detail pro-Nicene writers later ridiculed.
3. **Creed of Niké/Constantinople**: The definitive Homoian settlement. Text preserved at Athanasius, *Synod.* 30. Key clause: "we say that the Son is like the Father as the Holy Scriptures say and teach". All *ousia* and *hypostasis* language explicitly excluded. The phrase "in all things" (from the Dated creed) was dropped; *homoios* without qualification is the final formula.

## Theological Position

Homoians affirmed a Son "like" the Father "according to the Scriptures," but "all further technical terminology was avoided—although a clear subordination emphasis was understood to be implied by 'like'". They opposed *homoousios* as Marcellan, and *homoiousios* as insufficiently distinct. They also opposed the Heteroousian/Anomoean position that the Son is *unlike* the Father in essence, finding it an embarrassing extreme — though Ayres notes the Homoian alliance was internally fractured on this point.

Ayres describes the Homoian programme as "strongly and intentionally subordinationist," and the radical wing was perceived by some as leading inexorably to the [[heteroousians|Heteroousian]] position of [[aetius|Aetius]] and [[eunomius|Eunomius]].

## Relation to the Other Trajectories

- **vs. Homoiousians**: [[homoiousians|Basil of Ancyra]] and his party insisted on *homoiousios* — likeness *in essence* — seeing the Homoian "like according to the Scriptures" as theologically insufficient.
- **vs. Heteroousians**: The [[heteroousians|Heteroousians]] (Aetius, Eunomius) pushed subordinationism further, asserting the Son is *unlike* the Father in essence; some Homoians supported them before breaking.
- **vs. pro-Nicenes**: [[athanasius|Athanasius]] and western bishops increasingly used Nicaea and *homoousios* as a counter-standard. The 357 manifesto provoked Phoebadius of Agen's *Contra Arianos* and [[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary of Poitiers]]'s *De synodis*.

## Historical Rise and Decline

Homoian theology crystallized through the series of councils pushed by [[constantius-ii|Constantius II]] in the 350s. Its political apex came at the twin councils of Ariminum (west) and Seleucia (east) in 359, and at the Constantinople council of early 360 that ratified the Homoian creed and deposed Basil of Ancyra's supporters. However, "this 'victory' was achieved in the face of widespread resistance... The victory was thus a Pyrrhic one whose main effect may have been to promote increasingly clear thinking about the issues that divided the Homoians from a whole variety of different trajectories and thinkers: Athanasius; many western theologians; the Homoiousians". Despite this, the creed "remained the imperially sanctioned statement of orthodoxy for almost two decades (especially clearly in the east)".

## Influence and Legacy

The Homoian settlement defined the terms against which [[pro-nicene-theology|pro-Nicene theology]] crystallized in the 360s–380s. Several bishops exiled under Constantius's Homoian policies — including [[hilary-of-poitiers|Hilary of Poitiers]] and Eusebius of Vercelli — returned to the west as significantly more developed thinkers and shaped Latin pro-Nicene theology. Ayres presents the Homoian victory as, paradoxically, one of the conditions enabling the subsequent pro-Nicene synthesis.
