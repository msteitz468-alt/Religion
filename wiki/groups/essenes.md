# Essenes

## Identity and Nomenclature

The etymology of "Essenes" is contested — proposals include derivation from Aramaic
*hasayya* (pious ones), or from *asah* (doers of the law). The Essenes are not named in the
NT but are described by Josephus, Philo, and Pliny the Elder as a major Jewish sect living
an ascetic communal life, often near the Dead Sea.

The **identification of the Qumran community** (authors of the [[dead-sea-scrolls-vanderkam|Dead Sea
Scrolls]]) with the Essenes is widely accepted in modern scholarship but not unanimously so.
[[unterman-talmud|Unterman]] (Ch. IX) accepts this identification.

## Primary Textual Appearances

The Essenes left no NT footprint — they are described only in external sources:
- **Josephus** (*Jewish Antiquities* 18; *Jewish War* 2): Three "philosophical schools"; the
  Essenes most admired for virtue; communal property; strict purity; deterministic; rejected
  Temple animal sacrifice (or at least were excluded from it); sent offerings but did not sacrifice.
- **Philo** (*Every Good Man Is Free*; *Hypothetica*): ~4,000 Essenes; lived in villages, not
  cities; vegetarian (disputed); rejected slavery and commerce.
- **Pliny** (*Natural History*): A community "above En-gedi" near the Dead Sea — the clearest
  geographical pin.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls** (if the Qumran identification is accepted): The Community Rule (*1QS*),
  Damascus Document (*CD*), War Scroll, Thanksgiving Hymns, etc.

## Tradition-Specific Portrait

### Unterman's Analysis

[[unterman-talmud|Unterman]] (Ch. IX) characterizes the Essenes as the third major party,
distinct from both Pharisees (democratic, public) and Sadducees (aristocratic, Temple-based):

- **Ascetic, communal, pacifist**: Withdrew from public life into desert communities. Held
  property in common (no private property). Non-violent.
- **Vegetarian**: Unterman describes them as vegetarian — not all sources confirm this for
  all Essene groups.
- **Contemplative and deterministic**: Unlike the Pharisees (free will + providence), Essenes
  held a more deterministic theology.
- **Copied the Dead Sea Scrolls**: Unterman identifies the Essenes as the authors/custodians
  of the Dead Sea Scrolls.

### Modern Scholarly Portrait

The Qumran community (if Essene) understood itself as the true covenant people, living in
the wilderness in fulfillment of Isaiah 40:3 ("prepare the way of the Lord in the wilderness").
They practiced strict purity laws, initiated members through ritual immersion, and awaited
an eschatological war (*The War Scroll*). The **Teacher of Righteousness** (see
[[teacher-of-righteousness|Teacher of Righteousness]]) was their founding or authoritative
figure, opposed by the "Wicked Priest."

The Community Rule describes communal property, rank by purity, expulsion procedures, and a
theology of predestination with two spirits (Spirit of Truth and Spirit of Falsehood).

## Theological Significance

The Essenes represent a **sectarian strand** within Second Temple Judaism that:
- Rejected the Jerusalem Temple as corrupt (or at least its current priesthood)
- Emphasized ritual purity and covenantal separation from the rest of Israel
- Maintained a deterministic theology and an expectation of eschatological judgment
- Preserved and produced extensive scriptural interpretation

Their disappearance after 70 CE (likely destroyed by Rome during the Jewish War) means they
left no living successor tradition, though their texts survived in the Qumran caves.
