# Kedarites

## Identity and Nomenclature

The **Kedarites** (Kedar / Qedar) were a powerful **North Arabian tribal confederation**,
named in the genealogies as descendants of **Kedar, son of [[abraham|Ishmael]]** (Gen.
25.13). They are the best-documented of the Iron Age and Persian-period **Arabs**, the
caravaneers of the incense routes — heirs to the role the Bible earlier assigns to the
**[[midianites|Midianites]]** and later to the **Nabateans** (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3 notes
the continuity: "whether known as Midianites… or later as Arabs and Kedarites, or later
still as Nabateans, the merchandise… remained basically the same").

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **Genesis 25.13**: Kedar among the twelve sons of Ishmael.
- **Isaiah 21.16–17; 42.11; 60.7**: oracles and visions involving Kedar's flocks, glory,
  and rams ("all the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered to you").
- **Jeremiah 49.28–33**: oracle against **"Kedar and the kingdoms of Hazor"** that
  Nebuchadrezzar struck.
- **Ezekiel 27.21**: Kedar trades rams, lambs, and goats with **[[byblos|Tyre]]**.
- **Psalm 120.5**: "Woe is me, that I dwell… among the tents of Kedar" (the remote desert).
- **Nehemiah 2.19; 6.1–6**: **Geshem (Gashmu) the Arab**, a Kedarite leader, opposes
  Nehemiah's rebuilding of Jerusalem.

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Israelite/Jewish**: the quintessential **tent-dwelling desert nomads** and traders of
  the eastern and southern fringe — sometimes hostile (Geshem), sometimes the wealthy
  flocks gathered to Zion in eschatological vision (Isa. 60).
- **Critical/historical**: a real and influential power. **Assyrian** kings (Esarhaddon,
  Ashurbanipal) extracted gold, gems, camels, and aromatics from Kedarite kings (e.g.
  **Yautha**); **Persian**-period inscriptions from **Tell el-Maskhuta** (Egypt) and
  **Dedan** (al-ʿUla, Hijaz) name Kedar and the leader **Geshem** and his son (Leith,
  Coogan Ch. 8).

## Historical and Archaeological Context

By the Persian period the Kedarites controlled **northwest Arabia, southern Transjordan
(former Edom), the Negeb and Sinai, and the coast around Gaza** — and **pushed the
[[edomites|Edomites]] northward** out of their ancestral land into what became
[[idumeans|Idumea]] (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8). **Herodotus** reports that these Arabs were
**allies, not tributaries**, of the Persian king; a **Kedarite king supplied camel-borne
water skins** for **[[persians|Cambyses']]** desert crossing into Egypt in 525 BCE. Silver
bowls dedicated by "Qainu son of Geshem, king of Kedar" survive from Tell el-Maskhuta.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Kedar represents the **Ishmaelite desert world** at Israel's southern and eastern edge —
both the threatening wilderness ("tents of Kedar") and, in Deutero-Isaiah, the nations'
wealth flowing to a restored Zion. As Geshem the Arab, the confederation joins Sanballat
and Tobiah as the external opposition to the post-exilic restoration.

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

The Kedarites bridge the **Midianite → Arab → Nabatean** caravan continuum and, via
Ishmael, figure in later genealogies of the Arabs. Their displacement of Edom reshaped the
map that produced **[[idumeans|Idumea]]**. Related: [[midianites]], [[edomites]],
[[idumeans]], [[persians]], [[assyrians]], [[abraham]], [[byblos]], [[gaza]].
