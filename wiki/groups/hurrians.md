# Hurrians

## Identity and Nomenclature

The **Hurrians** were a non-Semitic, non-Indo-European population whose language
spread across northern Syria and Mesopotamia, and as far south as Canaan, at the end
of the Middle Bronze Age. Their most significant political expression was **Mitanni**
(also Hanigalbat), a state in northeast Syria with a Hurrian population and an
Indo-Aryan–named ruling elite, which by the early 15th century BCE controlled the
northern half of the Near East (Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1; Redmount, Ch. 2). The biblical
**"Horites"** are plausibly a memory of Hurrian presence.

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **The Nuzi tablets** (c. 1550–1400 BCE, east of the Tigris): several thousand
  Hurrian legal and economic documents long invoked as parallels to the **patriarchal
  narratives** — adoption of a slave as heir (Gen. 15.2–3), a barren wife's surrogate
  (Gen. 16), household gods (Gen. 31). Coogan notes most proposed parallels have been
  *disproven*; the valid ones (e.g., brother-arranged marriage requiring the woman's
  assent — cf. Rebekah, Leah, Rachel) are merely illustrative, since such customs
  continued into the first millennium when Genesis was written.
- **Possible biblical "Horites"/"Hivites"**: pre-Israelite groups of the land and of
  Seir (Gen. 14.6; 36).

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Critical scholarship**: Mitanni is one of the six Late Bronze "great kingdoms"
  (with Egypt, Hatti, Kassite Babylon, Assyria, Mycenae). Its capital Washukani is
  still unidentified; it is known mostly from *other* archives. The Amarna letters'
  northern correspondence is full of "Hurrianisms."
- **Biblical tradition**: the "Horites" are simply one more pre-conquest people; no
  developed theological portrait.

## Historical and Archaeological Context

Mitanni reached its peak under Saushtatar (after 1470 BCE), clashed with Egypt's
Thutmose III over Syria, then made peace sealed by royal marriages (Tushratta's
daughter to Amenhotep III). Its downfall came when the Hittite king **Suppiluliumas**
sacked Washukani and absorbed northern Syria; thereafter Assyria and Hatti fought over
the Mitannian remnant until it vanished as an independent power. See [[hyksos]] and
[[sea-peoples]] for the wider Late Bronze power-struggle and collapse.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Limited within the biblical text itself, but historically central: the Hurrian/Mitanni
world is part of the cosmopolitan Late Bronze order whose collapse (c. 1200 BCE)
created the conditions for Israel's emergence ([[israelites]]). The Nuzi debate is a
case study in the *rise and fall of "archaeological confirmation"* of the patriarchs.

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

The mid-20th-c. "Nuzi parallels" were a pillar of confidence in a second-millennium
patriarchal age; their collapse (documented by Coogan) is part of the broader shift
toward dating the Genesis traditions to the first millennium. Related: [[amorites]],
[[canaanites]], [[abraham]], [[documentary-hypothesis]].
