# Núrí Family

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23750–23874.*

## Overview
The family of [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]]'s father, Mírzá 'Abbás Núrí (Mírzá Buzurg; d. 1839), and by extension of Bahá'u'lláh and [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]. A family tree is given in *BKG* facing p. 205 (lines 23750–23755).

## The Family of 'Abbás Núrí

'Abbás had four wives and three concubines, by whom he had a total of fifteen children, twelve of whom survived him. Bahá'u'lláh was the third child by the second wife, Khadíjih; [[subh-i-azal|Subh-i-Azal]] was the only child by the first concubine. Some of the siblings became Bahá'ís, some Azalis, others remained Muslims. Bahá'u'lláh's own siblings also included three children by his mother's first marriage. His companions-in-exile included his younger full brother, Mírzá Músá, and a half-brother, Mírzá Muḥammad-Qulí. The family was well connected by marriage to other high-ranking members of Iranian society, including Mírzá Áqá Khán Núrí, the second of Náṣiru'd-dín Sháh's chief ministers (lines 23756–23780).

## The Family of Bahá'u'lláh

Bahá'u'lláh married three wives and had eight children who survived childhood.

**First marriage** (1835) to Ásíyih Khánum ([[navvab|Navváb]]; 1820–86): Seven children born, only three survived to adulthood: 'Abbás ([[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]; 1844–1921), Fáṭimih ([[bahiyyih-khanum|Bahíyyih Khánum]]; 1846–1932), and [[mirza-mihdi-purest-branch|Mihdí]] (1848–70).

**Second marriage** (1849) to Fáṭimih Khánum, 'Maḥd-i-'Ulyá' (1828–1904), one of Bahá'u'lláh's cousins: Six children, four survived — a daughter Samadíyyih (b. 1856/7) and three sons: [[mirza-ali-covenant-breaker|Muḥammad-'Alí]] (1853/4–1937), Diyá'u'lláh (1864–98), and Badí'u'lláh (1867–1950).

**Third marriage** to Gawhar Khánum, sister of Mírzá Mihdí Káshání (date unknown, occurred in Baghdad): One daughter, Furughíyyih. Unlike the other wives, Gawhar remained in Baghdad after Bahá'u'lláh's departure, only rejoining him after his exile to [[akka|Akká]].

Six of the children married — to relatives of prominent Bahá'ís, including in two cases to the offspring of Bahá'u'lláh's brother Mírzá Músá. There were fifteen grandchildren. Bahíyyih chose to remain unmarried, whilst Mihdí died in his youth. Apart from 'Abdu'l-Bahá's own immediate family, the other family members followed Muḥammad-'Alí in his rejection of 'Abdu'l-Bahá's leadership after Bahá'u'lláh's death and were declared [[covenant-breakers|Covenant-Breakers]] (lines 23782–23834).

## The Family of 'Abdu'l-Bahá

'Abdu'l-Bahá had one wife, [[munirih-khanum|Munírih Khánum]], whom he married on 8 March 1873. Nine children were born, but five died in childhood (two boys and three girls). Four daughters survived:

- **Ḍíyá'íyyih** (d. 1951), married Mírzá Hádí Shírází Afnán (d. 1955) in 1313/1895. Five children (later surnamed Rabbání): **[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]]** (eldest son); Rúḥánguiz; Mihrángíz; Ḥusayn; and Riyáḍ.
- **Ṭúbá** (1880–1959), married Mírzá Muḥsin Afnán (1863–1927). Four children (surnamed Afnán): Rúḥí (1899–1971); Thurayyi; Suhayl; and Fu'ád (d. 1943).
- **Rúḥá**, married Mírzá Jalíl Iṣfáhání, son of Mírzá Muḥammad Ḥasan, the King of Martyrs. Four children (surnamed Sháhid): Maryam; Muníb; Zahrá; and Ḥasan.
- **Munavvar** (d. 1971), married Mírzá Aḥmad, son of Mírzá 'Abdu'r-Raḥím Yazdí. No children.

Six of the children married relatives. Four of these marriages were with descendants of Bahá'u'lláh's daughter Furughíyyih who were declared Covenant-Breakers — a major factor leading [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] to ultimately expel the majority of his immediate relatives from the Faith (lines 23836–23874).
