# Phoenicians

## Identity and Nomenclature

**"Phoenician" is simply the Greek word for Canaanite** — specifically the coastal
[[canaanites|Canaanites]] of the Lebanese and Syrian littoral, organized in
independent city-states: **[[byblos|Byblos (Gubla)]], Sidon, Tyre, Arvad, Beirut**.
The Bible usually calls them **"Sidonians."** They are not a separate people from the
Canaanites but the surviving, seaward-facing branch of Canaanite culture after the
Late Bronze collapse.

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **1–2 Kings**: **Hiram of Tyre** supplies cedar, gold, and craftsmen for Solomon's
  Temple and palace and joins his Red Sea trade (1 Kings 5; 9–10). **Jezebel**,
  daughter of the king of Sidon, marries Ahab and promotes **Baal and Asherah** worship
  — the great religious conflict of Elijah's day.
- **Ezekiel 26–28 / Isaiah 23**: extended oracles against **Tyre**, "merchant of the
  peoples," exporting the wealth of Arabia (Ezek. 27).
- **The Amarna letters** ([[byblos|Rib-Addi of Byblos]] writes 67 of them) and the
  **tale of Wen-Amun** show the coastal cities' Late Bronze–Iron Age commerce.

## The Alphabet

Coogan's volume credits **Canaan, c. 1700 BCE**, with inventing the **alphabet** —
"one of the most important innovations in the history of civilization." Because
*Phoenician* is the Greek word for Canaanite, this is "the beginning of what is
popularly known as the **Phoenician alphabet**, the ancestor of every Western alphabet"
(Pitard, Coogan Ch. 1). The Phoenicians were its great transmitters across the
Mediterranean.

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Israelite/Jewish**: ambivalent — valued trading partners and Temple-builders, but
  the source (via Jezebel) of Baalism and the prophetic polemic against it. Tyre
  becomes a symbol of proud commercial wealth doomed to fall.
- **Critical scholarship**: the Phoenicians are the **primary conduit of Hellenism**
  to the Persian-period Levant and the dominant culture of the entire coastal plain
  and Galilee — far more than inland Judah and Samaria, which "avoided heavy
  Phoenicianizing" (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8).

## Historical and Archaeological Context

In the Persian period the Phoenician city-states dominated the Levantine coast, their
fleets serving the Persian navy (e.g., in the conquest of Egypt), absorbing Cypriot
and Aegean/Greek elements and spreading them inland. Galilee became "heavily
Phoenician, densely settled, and prosperous." Their colonies (Carthage, etc.) carried
Canaanite religion and the alphabet across the Mediterranean.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

The Phoenicians personify the **double face of Canaan** for Israel: indispensable
partners in building the house of Yahweh, yet bearers of the rival cult of Baal and
Asherah. The Elijah–Jezebel conflict (Baal of Tyre vs. Yahweh) is the dramatic
center of this tension; cf. the religion uncovered at [[ugarit]].

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

The alphabet, Mediterranean trade, and the transmission of Hellenism are their
enduring legacy; Tyre's oracles (Ezek. 28) later fed Jewish and Christian reflection
on pride and "the prince of Tyre." Related: [[canaanites]], [[byblos]], [[ugarit]],
[[sea-peoples]], [[persians]], [[1-2-kings]].
