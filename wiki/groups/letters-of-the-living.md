# Letters of the Living (Ḥurúfu'l-Ḥayy)

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 20853–20930.*

## Identity and Nomenclature

The Letters of the Living (*Ḥurúfu'l-Ḥayy*) were the first eighteen disciples of the Báb (l. 20855). The group name reflects their cosmic function as "forerunners" (*sábiqún*) — the first members of the human race to respond to God's pre-eternal covenant (l. 20924–20927). In Shí'í tradition the *sábiqún* are identified with Muḥammad, the Imáms, and Fátima (l. 20927–20928), and the Báb reapplied this identity to his first followers.

5. Mullá Khudá-Bakhsh Qúchání, later known as Mullá 'Alí (l. 20877–20878)
6. \*Mullá Ḥasan Bajistání (l. 20879)

## Geographical and Social Origins

Of Nabíl's list: six were from Khurásán (nos. 1–6); five from Ádharbáyján (nos. 10–14); three from Qazvín (nos. 15–17); two from Yazd (nos. 7–8); and one each from Mázandarán (no. 18) and India (no. 9) (l. 20912–20917). Most were of relatively humble social origins and had been followers of the Shaykhi leader Sayyid [[kazim-rashti|Kázim]] (l. 20917–20919). Nine were killed during or immediately following the Shaykh Ṭabarsí conflict (l. 20920–20921).

## Theological and Narrative Significance

As a group, the Letters of the Living were given a cosmic role by the Báb, identified with the *sábiqún* (forerunners) — the first of the human race to respond to God's pre-eternal covenant (l. 20922–20928). They were also to have an organizational function as the first *waḥíd* ("unity") in a proposed hierarchy of nineteen-member groups of believers (l. 20929–20930; see also the Bábí concept of numbers).

The Báb gave each of the Letters honorific titles; the best known are [[quddus|Quddús]] ("Holy"), [[tahirih|Táhirih]] ("Pure"), and Vaḥíd ("Unique") (l. 17004–17007). [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] similarly gave honorific titles to many of his early followers (l. 17008–17012).
