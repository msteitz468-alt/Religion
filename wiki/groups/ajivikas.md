# Ajivikas

The Ajivikas were an early Indian **[[sramana|sramana]]** (renouncer) order contemporary with
[[jainism|Jainism]] and [[buddhism|Buddhism]], led by **[[makkhali-gosala|Makkhali Gosala]]**.

## Identity and Doctrine

Their defining teaching was **niyati** (fate/destiny): the doctrine that all events are rigidly
predetermined and human effort cannot alter the soul's allotted course through rebirth. No Ajivika
scriptures survive; the order is known almost entirely through the hostile testimony of its rivals.

## Reception in Jain Sources (Dundas)

[[dundas-the-jains|Dundas]] stresses that the surviving portrait — preserved in the Jain canon — is
**polemically distorted**. The early *Sayings of the Seers* nonetheless show that Makkhali Gosala was
once treated as an authoritative teacher *alongside* [[mahavira|Mahavira]], with whom he spent six years
as a companion before a bitter break. Dundas further suggests that "inconsistencies in Jain karma theory
... could be explained with reference to what little is known of Ajivika doctrine" — e.g. the soul-colour
(*lesya*) theory may be an Ajivika import into [[karma|Jain karma doctrine]].
