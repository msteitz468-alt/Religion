# Moabites

## Identity and Nomenclature

**Moab** was the Iron Age kingdom on the high plateau **east of the Dead Sea**, between
the **Arnon** (its northern frontier with Israelite Gilead) and the **Zered**. In the
epic sources the Moabites are the **"kindred" / "people of Chemosh,"** after their
sovereign deity (Num. 21.29; Jer. 48.46) — paralleling Israel-of-Yahweh, Ammon-of-Milcom,
Edom-of-Qaus (Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). The J source's etiology (Gen. 19.30–38) makes
**Moab the firstborn son of [[abraham|Lot]]** by his elder daughter and **[[ammonites|Ammon]]**
the second, casting both as incestuously-born fraternal **"cousins" of Jacob** — a social
charter for the formative Transjordanian polities.

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **Genesis 19**: the Lot etiology pairing Moab and Ammon.
- **Numbers 21–25**: King **Balak** hires **[[balaam|Balaam]]** to curse Israel (Num.
  22–24); the apostasy of Israelite men with Moabite/Midianite women at Baal-peor (Num.
  25). The conquest narrative claims Israel took Heshbon and **Dibon** — but excavation
  shows these sites had **no Late Bronze occupation**, undercutting the itinerary (Stager,
  Coogan Ch. 3).
- **Deuteronomy 23.3–6**: no Moabite admitted "to the assembly of the LORD" — yet **Ruth
  the Moabitess** becomes great-grandmother of **[[david|David]]**, a pointed counter-text.
- **Judges 3**: the fat king **Eglon** of Moab oppresses Israel until Ehud's assassination.
- **2 Kings 3** & the **Mesha Stela**: King **Mesha's** revolt against the house of Omri
  (see below).
- **Isaiah 15–16, Jeremiah 48, Amos 2**: extended oracles against Moab.

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Israelite/Jewish**: an ambivalent neighbor — at once the despised oppressor (Eglon,
  Baal-peor) and, through **Ruth**, woven into the Davidic and thus messianic line. Later
  tradition wrestles with the tension between Deut. 23 and Ruth.
- **Critical/archaeological**: Moab emerges as a settled kingdom only in the Iron Age; the
  **Mesha Stela** (c. 840 BCE) is among the longest royal inscriptions from the region and
  a primary witness to Moabite religion, language (close to Hebrew), and the **"house of
  David"** reading.

## Historical and Archaeological Context

The **Mesha Stela** (Moabite Stone, found at Dibon/Dhiban in 1868) commemorates Mesha's
throwing off Israelite control: it names **Omri** as "king of Israel," credits the Omrides
with having earlier subdued Moab "all the way south to the Arnon," reports Mesha's
recapture of territory, devotes Israelite towns to the god **Chemosh** in a *herem* exactly
parallel to Israel's own holy-war language, and — on a recent reading — also mentions the
**"house of David"** (Stager / Cogan, Coogan Chs. 3, 7; cf. the [[dan|Tel Dan]] stela).
The stela also locates Gadites in former Reubenite land, a datum in the [[israelites|tribal]]
geography debate.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Moab embodies the **near-kin enemy** whose god Chemosh is the structural mirror of Yahweh,
making Moab a foil for Israel's covenant identity. Yet the **Ruth** narrative subverts the
exclusionary Deuteronomic law, folding a Moabite woman into the royal genealogy — a
standing scriptural argument over the boundaries of the covenant people.

## Influence on Later Traditions and Controversies

Moab fuels the *herem* discussion (Chemosh's *herem* on the Mesha Stela closely parallels
[[herem-canaanite-conquest|Israel's conquest ban]]), the "house of David" epigraphic debate,
and the perennial Ruth-vs-Deuteronomy tension over Gentile inclusion. Related:
[[ammonites]], [[edomites]], [[israelites]], [[midianites]], [[balaam]], [[ruth]],
[[david]], [[dan]], [[herem-canaanite-conquest]].
