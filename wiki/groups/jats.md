# Jats (Sikh Jats)

The **Jats** are the agrarian landowning caste that forms the **numerical and political core of the
Sikh [[sikhs|Panth]]** — more than 60% by McLeod's reckoning. Low in the classical *varṇa* hierarchy
yet dominant in the villages of the Punjab, Jats began entering the Panth in numbers under
[[guru-hargobind|Guru Hargobind]] and came to define the [[khalsa|Khalsa]] after 1699, when Brahman
and Khatri Sikhs largely declined initiation (agent 2 ~L870–895).

*(Scaffolded 2026-06-27 from [[mcleod-sikhism|McLeod]] Chs 11–13.)*

## Significance for the Panth

- **Constituency of the Khalsa.** The martial, landholding Jat ethos is woven into McLeod's account of
  the Panth's militancy — alongside Khalsa martial nurture, the Jat warrior tradition, and the British
  "martial races" recruitment that required Khalsa symbols of Army recruits.
- **Caste practice vs. doctrine.** Though Sikhism rejects vertical caste, Jats observe *zat* endogamy
  and *got* exogamy in marriage — the chief instance of the caste-vs-creed tension (see
  [[who-is-a-sikh|Who is a Sikh?]]).
- **Land, migration, and gender.** The Jat custom of dividing patrimony equally among **sons** drove
  both diaspora migration (holdings shrinking below viability) and the structural subordination of
  women, since "power must reside with the males because they are the holders of the land" (agent 6).
