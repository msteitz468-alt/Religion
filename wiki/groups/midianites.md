# Midianites

## Identity and Nomenclature

The **Midianites** were a confederation of tent-dwelling pastoralists and
caravaneers whose heartland lay east of the Gulf of Aqaba in **northwestern Arabia**
(medieval Arab geographers' "land of Midian"; today the **Hijaz**). Their
confederation included the **Kenites** (Hebrew *qayin* / Aramaic *qayyan*,
"metalsmith"), and at times the **Amalekites** (see new dedicated [[amalekites|Amalekites]] page for the Zoharic Sitra Aḥra portrait), **Ishmaelites**, and **Cushan**
(Stager, Coogan Ch. 3). The Egyptians grouped such desert tent-dwellers under the
rubric **[[apiru-habiru|Shasu]]**.

## Primary Textual Appearances

- **Exodus**: [[moses|Moses]] flees to Midian, marries Zipporah daughter of the
  Midianite priest **Jethro** (also Reuel/Hobab), and experiences the burning-bush
  theophany of **Yahweh** at the mountain of God. Jethro advises Moses on a judicial
  system "already in use among the Midianites" (Exod. 18).
- **Numbers**: the **Baal of Peor** apostasy and **Phinehas** spearing the Israelite
  and Midianite woman (Num. 25) — the turn from kinship to enmity.
- **Judges**: the camel-riding **Midianite raiders** as Israel's archenemies in the
  **Gideon** cycle (Judg. 6).
- **Genesis**: Midian as a son of [[abraham|Abraham]] by Keturah (Gen. 25) — making
  Midianites kin to Israel; Midianite/Ishmaelite caravaneers buy Joseph (Gen. 37).

## Tradition-Specific Portraits

- **Friendly kin → enemy**: the benign Moses-era relationship (priest Jethro,
  shared meal "before God," guidance through the wilderness) is set before c. 1100 BCE;
  thereafter the Gideon wars make Midian the archetypal raider. Coogan argues the
  *benign* traditions must be authentic and early — no one would invent a Midianite
  father-in-law for the great lawgiver during the period of hostility.

## The Midianite/Kenite Hypothesis on Yahwism

A central claim of Coogan Ch. 3 (following Cross, Eduard Meyer): **Yahwism may have
Midianite/Kenite antecedents.** Evidence:
- The name **Yahweh** and his sacred mountain located in Arabia (Seir, Edom, Paran,
  Teman, Cushan), not Sinai — per the earliest Hebrew poetry ([[judges|Song of
  Deborah]], Judg. 5.4–5; Deut. 33.2; Hab. 3.3–7).
- Egyptian topographical lists of **Shasu** territories name both the **"land of the
  Shasu: Seir"** and the **"land of the Shasu: Yhw3 (Yahweh)."**
- The Kenites as **metalsmiths** (cf. Cain/Tubal-cain) and the Moses connection.

The chronological/geographical fit of **"Midianite painted pottery"** (Hijaz ware,
made at Qurayyah and distributed through the Arabah copper districts — Timna, Punon)
corroborates the era. Coogan keeps the link **tentative** pending more knowledge of
Midianite religion. See [[divine-name]].

## Theological and Narrative Significance

The Midianites stand at the hinge of Israel's religious origins: in-laws of Moses,
possible source of the divine name, and then — once the caravan trade they protected
collapsed — transformed into the raiders of Judges. Their dual role (kin and enemy)
mirrors the wider biblical pattern for Israel's neighbors (cf. [[edomites]]).

## Controversies

The Midianite/Kenite origin of Yahwism is a major, contested hypothesis (see
[[divine-name]], [[israelites]]). Related: [[moses]], [[abraham]], [[edomites]],
[[apiru-habiru]], [[gideon]], [[jethro]], [[phinehas|Phinehas]].
