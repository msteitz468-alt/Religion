## What the source is

Bruce K. Waltke's *The Book of Proverbs: Chapters 15:30–31:31* (New International Commentary
on the Old Testament; Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2005) is the second of two volumes of a single
commentary planned as one work and split by length. A publisher's note records that the
Introduction in Vol. 1 covers the entire book; this volume is commentary alone, running from
Collection IIB (15:30–16:22) through Collection VII (the sayings of Lemuel) and the concluding
acrostic on the valiant wife (31:10–31). Waltke supplies his own translation with detailed
text-critical notes, then comments unit-by-unit up a hierarchy from proverb pairs to collections.

This is a **fidelity re-ingest** completing the Waltke/Proverbs footprint: Vol. 1 (chs 1–15) was
re-ingested 2026-07-18 with its own source page and ledger; Vol. 2 was flagged there as a
separate, un-re-ingested work. This page supplies the source apparatus for the second volume.

## Hermeneutical frame

The whole-book Introduction (method, ANE, authorship, theology, poetics) is in Vol. 1; this volume
enacts that method unit-by-unit. What the second volume most displays:

1. **Poetics as unit-marking.** Waltke demarcates units by catchword, janus verse, inclusio, and
   chiasm, then reads the arrangement as meaning-bearing (e.g. the "breached wall" inclusio around
   the "seven perverted types," 25:28–26:28; the righteous/wicked framing couplets making 29:1 the
   center of chs 28–29).
2. **Conservative text-criticism.** He defends "the more difficult MT" against LXX paraphrase and
   *metri causa* emendation (e.g. retaining "silver dross" against the Ugaritic "glaze" reading at
   26:23; keeping *maśśāʾ* "oracle" against Delitzsch's "king of Massa").
3. **Prophetic terms with a wisdom nuance.** *qesem* (16:10), *ḥāzôn* (29:18), and *maśśāʾ* (30:1;
   31:1) all denote the sage's inspired revelation rather than mantic/prophetic vision.
4. **ANE comparison under theological control.** The Amenemope borrowing in Collection III is
   limited (22:16–23:11) and reframed by trust in the covenant God.
5. **Reformed canonical / typological Christology.** The surrogate king anticipates the Messiah;
   Agur's "son" (30:4) is typical Israel fulfilled in Christ; Woman Wisdom / valiant wife frame
   the book around the fear of the LORD.

Register discipline for this ingest: Waltke's interpretive and theological claims are attributed to
him (or to scholars he engages — Wolters, McCreesh, Van Leeuwen, Meinhold, Malchow, Whybray,
Delitzsch, McKane); text-critical and structural facts he reports are recorded as his findings;
the LXX's rearrangement of chs 30–31 is recorded as a reception fact.

## Volume synthesis

Waltke's second volume completes the wiki's fullest worked example of grammatico-historical method
disciplined by canonical-theological conviction, and it shifts the center of gravity from the
prologue's programmatic theology (Vol. 1) to the *editorial artistry* of the collections. Its most
consequential contribution is to show that the later collections are read as **composed**, not
merely compiled: catchword, janus, inclusio, and chiasm mark units whose sequence carries
argument, and the "men of Hezekiah" (25:1) are treated as active editors who profiled chiasms and
built the "Manual for Future Monarchs" of chs 28–29 around righteous/wicked framing couplets. This
gives the wiki its most developed account of Proverbs' composition history and a concrete,
attributed basis for associating Solomon with the proverbs "as early as 700 b.c."

Three themes are sharpened for the wiki. First, the **Amenemope** relationship — Collection III's
dependence on the Egyptian instruction — is stated with unusual precision: dependence is limited to
the first eleven sayings (22:16–23:11) and theologically controlled by trust in the covenant God,
a model of adaptation-under-control that parallels Hartley's "more in format than in substance"
verdict on Job and now anchors the [[wisdom-literature|Wisdom Literature]] page. Second, the
book's two named **non-Solomonic authors**, [[agur|Agur]] and [[lemuel|Lemuel]], receive their own
pages: Agur's confession supplies the wiki's sharpest statement of the sage's epistemic
self-limitation (agnosticism resolved only by special revelation — the same anti–natural-theology
move that grounds the whole book), and Lemuel's collection preserves the corpus's only extended
maternal royal instruction. Third, the closing acrostic on the [[valiant-woman|valiant wife]]
completes the book's **feminine frame**: Waltke reads her as a historical exemplar who incarnates
wisdom, deliberately distinct from the symbolic [[woman-wisdom|Woman Wisdom]] of the prologue —
the two figures framing the book around the fear of the LORD (31:30).

A standing tension worth recording: Waltke's confessional commitments (Solomonic authorship
defended on historical-reliability grounds; MT priority against LXX rearrangement; a typological
Christology reading the surrogate king and Agur's "son" toward Christ) are held throughout in his
attributed voice, not promoted to wiki voice, and the critical positions he rejects (the
blasphemy-emendation of Agur; the Massaite readings; the allegorical reading of the valiant wife)
are preserved as positions. Together with Vol. 1 this completes a fidelity re-ingest of the whole
of Waltke on Proverbs.
