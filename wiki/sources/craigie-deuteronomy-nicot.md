# The Book of Deuteronomy (NICOT) — Peter C. Craigie

## What the source is

Peter C. Craigie's *The Book of Deuteronomy* (1976) in the **New International Commentary on the Old Testament** (NICOT; series editor R. K. Harrison; Eerdmans). Craigie (University of Calgary; d. 1985) offers a confessional evangelical exposition with an original relatively literal Hebrew translation and extensive ANE (Hittite treaty; Ugaritic) comparative notes. Dialogue with critical scholarship without adopting Wellhausen/Noth as controlling frames.

## Hermeneutical frame

Craigie reads Deuteronomy as a **literary covenant-renewal document** cast in ANE **suzerain-vassal treaty form**, recording Moses’ hortatory addresses on the plains of Moab before conquest and succession. Methodological presupposition (Preface + Appendix I): Deuteronomy is Word of God, not pure human invention; historical-critical tools are used under that constraint, not as the final court of meaning. Dating argument is cumulative: second-millennium treaty form (historical prologue; Kitchen’s four distinctions) supports Mosaic-era substance without *proving* it; Weinfeld’s Esarhaddon/VTE curse parallels are engaged and resisted as controlling. Theology: covenant as initiating divine love requiring reciprocal *ʾahavah*; law maintains relationship; land is gift contingent on faithfulness; curses are solemn contingent warnings (not already-inevitable prophecy). NT applications (Christ as curse; prophet like Moses; Christian close of ch. 34) are explicit evangelical reception, always attributed.

**Load-bearing attributed moves (verified in digests):** whole-book treaty map (C-A-14); treaties support but do not prove Mosaic date (C-A-22); Josiah = *terminus ad quem* / rediscovery, not composition purpose (C-A-45; Additional Note on centralization/Shechem); Shema *ʾeḥad* + love as book-center (C-C-22–25); *ḥerem* as covenant-protection (C-C-29–33); prophet like Moses primary succession + christological Acts application (C-D-47–51); VTE direct-borrowing rejected (C-F-19); exile contingent not inevitable (C-F-43); ch. 34 Christian pointer to Jesus as more-than-prophet (C-G-38).

## Volume / work synthesis

Craigie’s NICOT *Deuteronomy* is a confessional evangelical commentary whose controlling claim is that the fifth book of Moses is **essentially a unified Moab covenant-renewal document** structured on the classical Near Eastern vassal-treaty pattern. The Hebrew title *ʾēlleh hadde ārîm* (“these are the words”) is preferred to the Greek “second law”: the work is hortatory speech given literary covenant form, not a second, rival law corpus. Liberation from Egyptian vassaldom is transposed into exclusive vassaldom under YHWH; treaty form is therefore theologically apt, not a borrowed shell.

On composition and date, Craigie reports the full critical fluidity (Moses to Exile) and states two live options under the treaty consensus: Mosaic-era (or soon after) versus seventh-century. He prefers Kitchen/Kline’s second-millennium formal distinctions (historical prologue; witnesses placement; balanced blessings; consistent order) while explicitly warning that parallels **support but do not prove** Mosaic dating. Against Josianic *composition*, he treats 2 Kings 22–23 as rediscovery (*terminus ad quem*), notes that centralization is result rather than Deuteronomy’s theoretical purpose, and stresses that the only named sanctuary region is near Shechem (Ebal/Gerizim)—hard to reconcile with a document written to authorize Jerusalem-only reform. Orthographic updating of living prose is allowed; legal content is judged compatible with Mosaic-age ANE parallels (Hammurabi et al.). Exile curse catalogues are contingent sanctions natural to land-gift theology, not *vaticinium ex eventu* as controlling; an optional exilic gloss is conceded only for isolated phrases such as “as it is this day” (29:27).

Theologically the law sits inside **initiating divine love** and commanded reciprocal love (6:4–5 as structural center). General stipulations (chs. 5–11) expound Decalog and allegiance; specific stipulations (12–26) apply that allegiance to sanctuary, kingship, prophecy, war, family, and social justice; blessings/curses and the life/death choice (27–30) seal decision before witnesses of heaven and earth. Continuity (31–34) deposits Torah as witness, appoints Joshua, teaches the Song, blesses the tribes, and ends with Moses’ unknown grave and the epitaph that no prophet like Moses arose—Craigie’s Christian close points forward to Jesus as more than a prophet (Heb 3), the new-exodus mediator of the New Covenant.

Relative to other ingested NICOT/OT voices, Craigie is more form-critically invested in treaty dating than [[marten-woudstra|Woudstra]] and more explicitly christological at 18:15 and 34:10–12 than many of the series’ literary-theological volumes.
