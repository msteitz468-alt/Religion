# Hamilton, *The Book of Genesis: Chapters 1–17* (NICOT, Eerdmans 1990)

## What the source is

Volume 1 of Victor P. Hamilton’s two-volume commentary on Genesis in the New International Commentary on the Old Testament (NICOT) series. Evangelical grammatical-historical commentary: original translation, verse-by-verse philological notes, literary-structural observations, ANE comparative notes, and per-unit “New Testament Appropriation” sections. The **Introduction covers the entire book of Genesis**; Volume 2 (chs. 18–50) is commentary-only (publisher’s note, raw ~L245–249). Invitation from R. K. Harrison; encouragement from Dennis Kinlaw (Asbury); editor Gary Lee; wife Shirley typed the bulk of the manuscript.

## Hermeneutical frame

Attributed to Hamilton (Introduction + commentary practice), with loci from the re-ingest digests:

1. **Canonical-evangelical final form** — “Theology must be based on the text as we have it, not on what it might once have been” (Intro §IV; C-V1R1). Holistic text required for theology; promissory speeches not treated as secondary late insertions.
2. **Mosaic authorship decoupled from evangelical conviction** — “We do not believe that an evangelical view of Scripture is necessarily wedded to the Mosaic authorship of Genesis… ‘Composite’ is not a synonym for ‘errant’” (Intro §III; C-V1R1-43). Post-Mosaic contemporization (“until this day”) acknowledged; recent literary/rhetorical work tends to support essential unity.
3. **Full engagement with DH / form / tradition history** — Detailed survey of Astruc → Wellhausen → Gunkel → Noth → Cross/Van Seters/Rendtorff; Cassuto praised as most convincing anti-JEDP case; Kitchen ANE anomaly critique noted. Source identification legitimate but must not displace canonical theological coherence.
4. ***Tôlĕdôt* architecture** — Ten *’ēlleh tôlĕdôt* as the book’s “lines of demarcation”; all as **superscripts** (not Wiseman–Harrison colophons); 2:4a introduces 2:4bff., with 1:1–2:3 as prologue; five narrative / five genealogical; vertical vs horizontal genealogies; generation → degeneration → regeneration arc.
5. **Literary-narrative criticism** — Alter/Sternberg-type attention to structure, irony, and characterization as theologically significant.
6. **ANE comparative** — Shared motifs (Enuma Elish, Gilgamesh, Atrahasis, Ugarit, Nuzi) used for cultural plausibility and **theological distinctiveness** (monotheistic / demythologizing polemic), not for reducing Genesis to myth.
7. **Register split** — Exegesis proper is historical-philological; each major unit closes with confessional **New Testament Appropriation** — kept distinct in wiki attribution.

## Volume synthesis

Hamilton’s Volume 1 is the NICOT series’ full-scale evangelical entry on Genesis: a whole-book Introduction plus continuous commentary through the end of Abraham Cycle Part I (Gen 17). Its load-bearing contribution is not a novel outline but a **method package**: final-form theology that refuses both precritical flatness and Wellhausenian dismantling of promissory structure; *tôlĕdôt* as literary-theological architecture (superscripts, 2:4a, Babylonia–Canaan–Egypt geography); and ANE comparison that heightens rather than relativizes monotheistic distinctives.

Across the primeval history Hamilton presses complementary poles rather than source contradiction (1:1–2:3 divine image/authority vs 2:4ff. creaturely limitation; absolute *berēʾšît*; *ʿēzer kĕnegdô* as equal counterpart). The Fall–Flood–Babel arc is moral and relational degeneration answered by election. On the flood he contrasts Genesis’ moral etiology with Atrahasis overpopulation and Gilgamesh whim, while noting that universal language can still leave room for debate with Gen 13’s local *kol-hāʾāreṣ*. Sons of God (6:1–4) receive three classical options without dogmatic closure. Table of Nations is read as chiastic whole, not a scrapyard of J/P.

The Abraham opening (12–17) is the theological hinge: sevenfold promise; Lot separation; Melchizedek; Gen 15 unilateral pieces-ritual and 15:6 faith/*ḥāšaḇ*; Gen 16 Hagar as ANE-surrogacy narrative with *El Roi*; Gen 17 as **reaffirmation** (Hiphil *qûm*, eternal covenant) rather than P’s bilateral replacement of ch. 15 — circumcision as *amen* sign, not entrance condition. Within the wiki, this re-ingest replaces the 2026-06-04/07 footprint’s unanchored paraphrase with quote-loci density and a proper source page/ledger. Volume 2 completes the patriarchal narrative (Akedah, Jacob, Joseph/50:20).
