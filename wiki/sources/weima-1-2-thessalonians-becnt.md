# Weima, *1–2 Thessalonians* (BECNT, 2014)

## What the source is

Jeffrey A. D. Weima's *1–2 Thessalonians* is a volume in the **Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New Testament** (BECNT), edited by Robert W. Yarbrough and Robert H. Stein (Baker Academic, 2014; ebook 2014). Weima (PhD, Wycliffe College, University of Toronto) is Professor of New Testament at Calvin Theological Seminary; he cochaired the SNTS "Thessalonian Correspondence" seminar and earlier published *An Annotated Bibliography of 1 & 2 Thessalonians* (with Stanley E. Porter, Brill, 1998) and a shorter Zondervan Illustrated Bible Backgrounds treatment (2002). The BECNT format supplies, for each unit: literary analysis (character, boundaries, structure), author's translation, verse-by-verse exegesis and exposition, additional notes (text-critical and grammatical), and occasional excursuses.

Register discipline for this ingest: the **body text** is Weima's own exegesis and exposition; the **Additional Notes** and collected **Notes** carry text-critical and bibliographic detail; the **Introduction** states his positions on the city, church founding, authorship, and literary method. All interpretive, theological, and historical-reconstruction claims are attributed to Weima (or to the scholars he cites), never promoted to bare wiki voice on the strength of this single source.

## Hermeneutical frame

Weima reads both letters as **real letters** governed by Greco-Roman epistolary conventions rather than classical rhetorical handbooks alone. Each unit is analyzed under "Literary Analysis" for character (thanksgiving, paraenesis, apostolic parousia, disclosure formula, etc.), boundaries, and internal structure. Method is **grammatico-historical** within the BECNT evangelical frame, with sustained **socio-historical** attention to Thessalonica's free-city status, politarchs, Cabirus cult, and imperial ideology. A standing **pastoral-consolatory** lens treats both letters primarily as comfort and correction for a persecuted congregation, not as eschatological timetables.

## Volume / work synthesis

Weima’s BECNT *1–2 Thessalonians* is a sustained **epistolary-literary** commentary: every unit is framed by literary character, boundaries, and structure before grammatico-historical exegesis. The Introduction is not disposable background — it supplies the civic, cultic, and imperial setting (Via Egnatia free city; politarchs; Cabirus; imperial cult; religious pluralism) against which Weima reads conversion “from idols,” affliction from “fellow citizens,” and the political charge implicit in another *kyrios* and *parousia*. Authorship is treated as a two-letter problem: 1 Thessalonians is undisputed (with a serious internal fight only over 2:13–16), while 2 Thessalonians is defended as Pauline against the post-Trilling cumulative case, with the standing-temple presupposition of 2:4 as a strong pre-70 control.

On 1 Thessalonians the commentary’s pastoral through-line is defense-then-exhortation: restore trust in the missionaries (2:1–3:13: workshop integrity, nursing-mother and father metaphors, apostolic parousia, Timothy’s mission) so that the congregation will hear holiness, brotherly love, quiet work, and eschatological comfort (4:1–5:22). The load-bearing eschatological unit is 4:13–18, which Weima reads as comfort for a fear that the dead are *disadvantaged* at the parousia; *apantēsis* as Hellenistic formal reception makes the movement Christ-toward-earth with escort, not a secret pre-tribulation rapture to heaven. 5:1–11 then distinguishes the day of the Lord as thief for outsiders — including the Roman “peace and security” slogan — from readiness for “sons of light,” before congregational order and the letter closing.

On 2 Thessalonians the same consolatory method governs denser apocalyptic material. Chapter 1 promises eschatological reversal under persecution; chapter 2 corrects the claim that the day of the Lord *has come* by a prerequisite sequence (apostasy and man of lawlessness; mystery already at work; restrainer still present), then comforts the elect. Weima’s preferred restrainer identification is the archangel Michael via Daniel 10–12, advanced with deliberate tentativeness (“whisper, not shout”) after surveying Rome, law-and-order, gospel/Paul, Spirit/church, Satan, and Giblin’s false prophet. Chapter 3 returns to the *ataktoi* with third-pass discipline framed by imitation of Paul’s labor and the 3:17 autograph as **authoritative presence**, not merely anti-forgery authentication.
