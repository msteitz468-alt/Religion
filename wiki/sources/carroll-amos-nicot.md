## What the source is

M. Daniel Carroll R. (Rodas), *The Book of Amos*, New International Commentary on the Old Testament (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2020). Full-scale evangelical critical commentary on Amos — combining Hebrew philology, eighth-century historical and socioeconomic reconstruction, final-form literary analysis (including chiastic structures), and sustained attention to social ethics and reception (early Judaism, NT, Latin American / contemporary appropriation). Carroll’s earlier monograph *Contexts for Amos* (1992) and Guatemalan / Latino formation frame the volume’s distinctive engagement with liberation theology and Two-Thirds World readings without capitulating to them.

The volume has: Author’s Preface; a substantial **Introduction** (I Historical Background; II Authorship; III Composition, Structure, and Poetics; IV Religion and Theology; V Amos in Early Judaism and the NT; VI The Message of Amos for Today; plus VII The Text); and verse-by-verse **Text and Commentary** on Amos 1:1–9:15, with four major commentary sections and named excursuses (OAN introduction; doxologies; woe cry; visions introduction).

## Hermeneutical frame

Carroll reads Amos as a **principled synchronic / final-form literary-theological** work largely from the prophet himself, set against a plausible **eighth-century** sociohistorical backdrop (ca. 765–750). He records multi-stage redaction models (Wolff six stages; Jeremias four editions; Schart; Persian Yehud literati) but does not adopt them as the governing frame — historical fit + literary coherence + skepticism about pan-Deuteronomism drive the method. Instruments: **poetics** and **rhetorical / speech-act criticism** (deliberative aim; doom can function as warning); **Geertzian interpretive anthropology** (religion authorizes the social construction of reality); and a **Latin American / Two-Thirds World ethical horizon** forged against, not simply within, liberation theology.

Register note: this is a commentary — all interpretive, philological, and theological claims are **Carroll’s** and are recorded in **attributed voice** on the wiki pages. The volume’s own translation, textual notes, excursuses, and exposition are its body; the general-editor’s preface is series paratext.

## Volume synthesis

Carroll’s NICOT *Amos* is the most thorough evangelical treatment of the book currently ingested by this wiki, and its enduring contribution is **method plus ethic together**: a defense of final-form reading that still does the historical and critical homework, joined to a social-ethical reading that refuses both thin “ethical monotheism” clichés and uncritical liberation-theology capture.

First, **structure and rhetoric**. Four primary sections (Preface; OAN; Words 3–6; Visions 7–9) replace a flat three-part outline; nested chiasms in 5:1–17, 5:18–27, and 6:1–14 give load-bearing centers (“Yahweh is his name,” 5:8d; **human justice** at 5:24; death at 6:9–10). Against pure “rhetoric of entrapment,” the completed book already names Israel in 1:1–2 — the OAN explain why the roar comes, because Israel is “morally embroiled,” not merely tricked by poetry.

Second, **theology of reversal and world-authorization**. Every cherished tradition (exodus, election, Divine Warrior from Zion, Day of Yahweh, cult) is turned into indictment. Cult critique is not generic anti-ritualism: via Geertz, sanctuaries authorize the unjust social imaginary, so judgment begins at Bethel’s altar. *Mišpāṭ* and *ṣĕdāqâ* are relational-covenantal and creation-grounded, not abstract ideals; 5:24 is a jussive demand for human justice, not primarily a prediction of divine flood.

Third, **cruxes that correct earlier wiki summary**. *ʾănāk* is **tin** (soft fortresses), not a plumb line; 9:11–15 is **eighth-century authentic** for Carroll (against Wellhausen’s roses-and-lavender appendix); the sieve (9:9) is corporate exile with targeted sword-judgment on boastful sinners (9:10); Cush in 9:7 means distant peoples — racist “despised black slave” readings are named and condemned. Amos is a person of means (*nōqēd* / *bôqēr* / *bôlēs šiqmîm*), not a romantic poor shepherd.

Fourth, **reception**. The same two texts Qumran midrashim favored (5:26–27; 9:11) are the NT’s only Amos quotations (Acts 7; Acts 15). James’s use of 9:11–12 underwrites Gentile inclusion under exalted Davidic rule without classic dispensational sequencing as the primary sense. Modern reception runs from Savonarola through MLK’s 5:24 to Latin American and “concerned evangelical” streams — Carroll’s own trajectory from SETECA through *Contexts for Amos*.

Tensions with already-ingested sources are generative rather than fatal. Dearman’s Hosea (NICOT) notes that **unlike Amos, Hosea never presents YHWH as creator** — Carroll’s three doxologies (4:13; 5:8–9; 9:5–6) and creator/Hosts theology supply the Amos side of that contrast. Nogalski’s Twelve-redaction interest is registered but demoted: discrete superscriptions make intertextual dialogue a **second-level** exercise. The volume thus deepens [[amos]], [[day-of-the-lord]], [[remnant]], and the Twelve cluster with attributed, locus-anchored claims the 2026-06-05 batch could not ground.
