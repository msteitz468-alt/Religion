# The Book of Joshua (NICOT) — Marten H. Woudstra

## What the source is

Marten H. Woudstra's *The Book of Joshua* (1981) in the **New International Commentary on the Old Testament** (NICOT; series editor R. K. Harrison; Eerdmans). Woudstra (Calvin Theological Seminary; NIV translation committee) offers a theocentric, redemptive-historical exposition with an original relatively literal Hebrew translation. Reformed evangelical; dialogue with critical scholarship without adopting Hexateuch or Noth DH as controlling frames.

## Hermeneutical frame

Woudstra reads Joshua as **prophetical historiography**: theocentric (not anthropocentric / moral-example) narrative of YHWH's redemptive acts; history en route to a goal ("already/not yet"); interpreter as humble listener to the narrator and ultimately the "great Someone Else." Literary-structural unity (frames, *maʿal* inclusio, prolepsis) argued against Hexateuch and Noth DH fragmentation. Restrained typology (Joshua–Jesus rest, Heb 4) vs Origen over-allegory. Hostile to liberation "example-method." Confessional: revelation/inspiration shape engagement with archaeology and critical methods.

**Load-bearing moves (verified in digests):** three-part structure Conquered / Distributed / Kept (C-A-45); holy land–holy people via Achan *maʿal* and eastern-altar frame (C-C-19; C-G-13); land as gift not seizure (C-B-05); *ḥerem* as judicial judgment with Gen 15:16 frame, not “sub-Christian” (C-A-40; C-C-14); Rahab’s confession of universal lordship and *ḥesed* (C-B-22, C-B-25); covenant occasion of the book (C-A-24); three graves as fulfilment symbol (C-G); early-date lean and list-as-programmatic (C-A-16–21); no modern territorial land claims from OT promise (C-A-36).

## Volume / work synthesis

Woudstra’s NICOT *Joshua* is a confessional Reformed commentary whose controlling claim is that the Former Prophets—and Joshua in particular—are **interpretative (prophetical) history** of God’s covenant dealings, not a handbook of moral examples. The preface and introduction insist that the interpreter’s task is humble listening: meaning is already in the work, and ultimately the speaker is “the great Someone Else.” Against the “Bible story” approach and against Liberation theology’s Exodus-as-paradigm of political change, Woudstra relocates ethics inside redemptive history: morality without the fabric of redemption “cannot save.” History is presented as en route to a goal, with an “already / not yet” pattern that he treats as structurally native to Joshua (complete gift and incomplete possession held together, not as rival sources).

On composition, he reports Hexateuch and Noth-style Deuteronomistic models carefully and rejects both as controlling frames. He prefers basic **unity of composition** discovered in literary design—hortatory frames (chs. 1 // 23–24), *maʿal* bracketing of Achan (ch. 7) and the eastern altar (ch. 22), Caleb and Joshua inheritance brackets around the allotment, Anakim fear-to-expulsion, and the three graves at the end as fulfilment symbols versus Abraham’s purchased tomb. Early-date indicators (e.g. absence of Tyre at 13:6; vivid narrative; Judg. 1 as post-Joshua, not an alternate Conquest) support composition near Joshua’s time without requiring full Joshua-autograph. Archaeology is useful but site-identification is fragile; silence does not overturn the prophetical historiographic claim.

Theologically the book’s keynote is **joyful fulfilment of the land promise** under YHWH’s gift-language, with covenant obedience as the condition of keeping what grace gives. Land is the Lord’s possession (Lev. 25:23); Woudstra explicitly refuses modern territorial claims from the OT promise and allows eschatological expansion (Ps. 37 / Matt. 5:5; Heb. 4 rest typology) under restrained rules. The *ḥerem* is not minimized: it is divine judicial destruction when Amorite iniquity is full (Gen. 15:16), with human hostility and divine hardening both named; the practice ceases to be normative as practice, but the revelatory record remains normative as Scripture—and “sub-Christian” labels are rejected. Rahab’s *ḥesed* and confession of the Lord of heaven and earth stand as the counter-example of mercy within judgment. Cultus (Gilgal circumcision/Passover; captain of the LORD’s army; Ebal; Shiloh lots; Eleazar) and all-Israel unity run through conquest, allotment, and the Shechem renewal that ends with “you cannot serve the LORD” yet Israel serving all Joshua’s days.

Relative to other ingested NICOT/OT voices, Woudstra is more polemically anti–example-method and anti-DH than [[barry-webb|Webb]] or [[james-bruckner|Bruckner]], less philologically driven than [[david-tsumura|Tsumura]], and continuous with the conservative NICOT stream of [[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]] / Craigie on revelation and early dating.
