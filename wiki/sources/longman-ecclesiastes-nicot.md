## Hermeneutical frame

Attributed to Longman (Preface + Intro + commentary body):

1. **Genre criticism** — Whole book: *framed wisdom autobiography*. Body (1:12–12:7) parallels Akkadian **fictional autobiography** (esp. Cuthaean Legend of Naram-Sin, Sin of Sargon, Adad-guppi): first-person ID + narrative quest + wisdom instruction. Formal, not material, parallel. Sub-genres: reflection, proverb, anecdote, instruction.
2. **Two-voice canonical reading** — Qohelet (1:12–12:7) is a **virtual quotation** and pedagogical **foil**; Frame Narrator (1:1–11; 12:8–14) is the finished book’s authorial/normative voice addressing “my son.” Analogy: Job’s body vs. Yahweh speech / final frame.
3. **Grammatico-historical + ANE** — Unusual Hebrew; language not a secure date barometer (Fredericks; Zimmermann Aramaic-original and Dahood Phoenician rejected). MT largely stable; Aquila-like LXX often secondary.
4. **Anti-orthodoxizing of Qohelet** — Rejects Targum/Gregory flattening of Qohelet into consistent orthodoxy; rejects Kaiser/Whybray “positive Qohelet” and redactional “pious gloss” patches. Crenshaw right on Qohelet-as-skeptic, wrong to equate Qohelet with the book’s message.
5. **Pastoral / psychological** — Allender dedication; Qohelet as honest diagnosis of cursed life “under the sun” without redemption; carpe diem as **resignation**, not celebration.
6. **NT canonical completion** — No NT quotation; Rom 8:20 *mataiotes* = LXX *hebel*; Christ redeems from vanity by entering it (Final Word; Gal 3:13; Matt 27:45–46).

## Volume synthesis

Longman’s Ecclesiastes is a **genre manifesto** for the most theologically unsettling book in the Wisdom corpus. The introduction’s long path (title, authorship, language, Akkadian parallels, canon disputes) clears the ground for one load-bearing claim: the book has **two speakers**, and the body’s first-person skepticism is not the book’s last word. Qohelet adopts a Solomonic persona only through the search (roughly chs. 1–3), then drops it; his speech maps onto Mesopotamian fictional autobiography; the Frame Narrator quotes him as a teaching example and corrects him in 12:13–14 with compressed wisdom + law + prophets (“Fear God… keep his commandments… God will bring every deed into judgment”).

Commentary body consistently reads *hebel* as **“meaningless”** (Longman’s preferred English), *carpe diem* as **resignation** (anodyne/anesthesia, not Kaiser-style affirmation), fear of God in Qohelet’s mouth as **fright** before distant *Elohim* (never *YHWH*), and the epilogue’s goads/nails (with Fox) as **sting/harm**, not wholesome prodding. The aging poem 12:1–7 uses three metaphor clusters (storm; house/body; smashed precious objects); death ends the story for Qohelet. Longman’s Christian Final Word does not erase Qohelet’s diagnosis of life under the curse; it names Christ as the one who enters and overcomes that *hebel* world (Rom 8; Gal 3).
