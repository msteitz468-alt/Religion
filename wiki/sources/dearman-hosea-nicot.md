## What the source is

J. Andrew Dearman's *The Book of Hosea* in the New International Commentary on the Old
Testament (NICOT) series (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2010). A full-scale evangelical
critical commentary on Hosea — the most detailed evangelical treatment of the book —
combining Hebrew philology, historical background, and a final-form literary-theological
reading. Dearman is an OT scholar at Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary also known
for epigraphical work (Mesha Inscription, Transjordan).

The volume has two parts: a substantial **Introduction** (Origins and Transmission;
Literary Features and Composition; Historical Background; Hosea's Theology; Bibliography)
and a verse-by-verse **Text and Commentary** on all fourteen chapters, each unit giving
Dearman's own translation, textual notes, and exposition.

## Hermeneutical frame

Dearman reads Hosea as a **final-form literary-theological** interpreter, not a source-critical
reconstruction: he makes "acceptance of the final form of the text as the proper focus of
attention" a decisive commitment while accepting limited editorial growth (superscription, several
Judah references, "David their king" in 3:5) and refusing, characteristically, to "draw lines in
the sand" between prophet and editors. Two instruments organize the whole commentary: **cognitive-
metaphor theory** (source-domain / target-domain analysis; "Hosea thinks metaphorically") and the
notion of **"base texts" read typologically** (Van Leeuwen) — the Exodus/wilderness narrative, the
Decalogue prologue and first two commandments, and Exod 34:6–7 recur as the traditions Hosea
"adapted and applied." His signature synthetic thesis is the **root metaphor "Israel is YHWH's
household" (*bayit* / *bêt ʾāb*)**, which subsumes the marriage metaphor and unifies YHWH's roles
(husband, father, king, shepherd, farmer). The volume is confessionally evangelical (NICOT frame)
but engages critical scholarship (Wolff, Macintosh, Andersen–Freedman, Yee, Sweeney, Odell) on its
own terms.

Register note: this is a commentary — all interpretive, philological, and theological claims are
Dearman's and are recorded in **attributed voice** on the wiki pages. The volume's own translation,
textual notes, excursuses, and appendices are its body; the general-editor's preface is series
paratext.

## Volume synthesis

Dearman's *Hosea* is the most detailed evangelical treatment of the book, and its enduring
contribution to the wiki is the **household root metaphor**: the claim that the *bayit* / *bêt ʾāb*
("house of the father") is the single conceptual domain holding together Hosea's otherwise disparate
images, with marriage as its most intimate constituent rather than the organizing frame. This gives
the wiki a stronger account of *why* husband, father, shepherd, farmer, and king cohere in one
prophet, and it drives Dearman's load-bearing exegetical decision that "house of YHWH" (8:1; 9:4;
9:15) denotes Israel-as-household, not a temple.

A second contribution is Dearman's **narrative-typological** reading. Hosea knows YHWH by rehearsing
"base texts" — the Exodus and wilderness, the Decalogue prologue, Exod 34:6–7, Baal-peor, Gibeah,
the Sodom/Admah judgment — and applies them typologically to the eighth-century crisis. Dearman
extends this into reception history: Matthew's use of Hos 11:1 (Matt 2:15) is not Hosea's original
sense but an extension of Hosea's *own* hermeneutic, and Paul's use of Hos 13:14 (1 Cor 15:54–55)
depends on the LXX's more positive rendering of what Dearman reads in the Hebrew as a threat.

The volume sharpens several tensions already latent in the wiki's coverage. Against readings that
make chapter 11 flat "unconditional grace," Dearman insists on **chastened hope**: restoration is
wholly YHWH-initiated, yet the historical judgment is not cancelled — "the book of Hosea is preserved
to explain the fall of Israel and Samaria... It is not, however, a death sentence." Against late-
Deuteronomic dating of covenant language, he defends *bĕrît* in 6:7 and 8:1 as authentically Hosean.
And he notes, distinctively, that **unlike Amos, Hosea never presents YHWH as creator**, even while
making YHWH Lord of history, covenant, and fertility. These positions complement the wiki's
[[divine-human-marriage|marriage-metaphor]], [[jeremiah|Jeremiah]] (as "Hosea's heir"), and
[[book-of-the-twelve|Book of the Twelve]] pages while adding attributed, locus-anchored depth the
prior batch ingest lacked.
