# Ephesians (Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New Testament)

## What the source is

Frank Thielman's *Ephesians* (2010) in the **Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New
Testament** (BECNT) series (eds. Robert W. Yarbrough, Robert H. Stein; Baker Academic).
Thielman (Beeson Divinity School, Samford University) writes a grammatico-historical
exegetical commentary on the Greek text of Ephesians, structured pericope-by-pericope with
BECNT's standard apparatus (translation, structural overview, verse-by-verse comment,
"Additional Notes"). The volume is marked by heavy engagement with patristic exegesis
(Origen, Chrysostom, Theodoret, Oecumenius), Septuagint and Second Temple background
(especially Isaiah and the *Letter of Aristeas*), and awareness of the Ephesian imperial
cult and magical milieu (Clinton Arnold).

## Hermeneutical frame

Thielman reads Ephesians as an authentic letter of **Paul** (writing "Paul" throughout as the
author) via **grammatico-historical exegesis** of the Greek text, with four recurring moves:

1. **Text-critical seriousness.** The "in Ephesus" omission (1:1) in 𝔓46, א, B and the τοῦ
   λοιποῦ / τὸ λοιπόν variant (6:10) receive sustained Additional-Notes treatment; he adopts
   readings on internal-and-external grounds rather than deferring to the Alexandrian weight
   alone.
2. **Septuagint and Second Temple background.** Isaiah is decisive (11:5 and 59:17 behind the
   armor; 28:16 and 56–66 behind the temple); the *Letter of Aristeas* 139 anchors the "middle
   wall"; 4Q434, Daniel 2, and the Watchers tradition frame the berakah, the *mystērion*, and
   the powers.
3. **Patristic reception as a grammatical control.** Native-Greek-speaking Fathers (Origen,
   Chrysostom, Theodoret, Oecumenius, Jerome) are consulted for their syntactic instincts, not
   only their theology.
4. **Greco-Roman civic setting.** The Artemis cult, Ephesian magic (via Clinton Arnold), and
   especially the **imperial cult** are read as the polemical background against which
   Ephesians' *kyrios* / *sōtēr* / *euangelion* / "under his feet" vocabulary functions as
   counter-claim.

Thielman's exegesis is separated here from BECNT's apparatus: the **Additional Notes** carry
his text-critical and lexical adjudication; the **Exegesis and Exposition** carries the running
argument. This source page attributes both to Thielman, not to "Ephesians."

## Volume synthesis

Thielman's *Ephesians* is a self-consciously **middle-of-the-road evangelical** commentary that
earns its conservative conclusions by taking the critical case at full strength. Its
distinctive contribution to this wiki is a **methodologically careful defense of Pauline
authorship** that does not rest on assertion: rather than treat the stylistic anomalies as
non-existent, Thielman concedes the density of long sentences and the heavy verbal overlap with
Colossians, then argues that (a) a *non-polemical* letter removes the usual motive for
pseudepigraphy, so a pseudonymous Ephesians would be an anomaly; (b) dictation under light
Roman custody without revision accounts for the concentration of digressions; and (c) the
"minute and inconsequential" adjustments a forger of 6:18–20 vis-à-vis Col 4:2–4 would have had
to make are psychologically implausible. This is the argument recorded on
[[ephesians-authorship]] as the strongest current form of the traditional position, set against
Lincoln's influential pseudonymity case (c. AD 80–90).

Exegetically, the volume's center of gravity is **2:11–22**: the "middle wall" as the **Mosaic
law** functioning as a national-separating fence (Aristeas 139), torn down so as to create in
Christ **one new human being** (*heis kainos anthrōpos*) — a corporate new Adam, a third entity
rather than an amalgam — built into an eschatological multiethnic **temple** (naos) with Christ
as *akrogōniaios*, the "topmost foundation stone." The letter's cosmic frame (the church
proclaiming God's *polypoikilos sophia* to the powers "in the heavenly places," 3:10; the
*kosmokratores* of 6:12, the term's first known occurrence) is read against Ephesian magic and
the imperial cult without collapsing into a programmatic anti-imperial hermeneutic.

Against already-ingested sources the volume sits in productive tension. Its **complementarian**
reading of the household code — 5:21 as genuine reciprocal service that nonetheless does not
equalize the code's asymmetry, *kephalē* as authority Christologically redefined toward
self-sacrifice — is the direct evangelical foil to [[scot-mcknight|McKnight]]'s egalitarian,
"more Aristotelian than Christian" reading of the Colossian parallel (see
[[ephesians-household-code]]). On the law, Thielman's real-abolition reading of *katargeō*
(2:15) coheres with, and sharpens, the Pauline-law material on [[justification]] and the
[[new-perspective-on-paul]] discussions. On the powers, his "spiritual beings in a dimension of
existence" reading contrasts with McKnight's "polluted structures" construal recorded on
[[principalities-powers]]. What the reingest adds beyond the 2010 first pass is not a change of
substance but the missing **source page, coverage ledger, and attribution discipline**, plus a
handful of grounded densifications (the 4:9 descent as a post-resurrection descent/ascent
merism; the 6:24 *en aphtharsia* crux resolved via *Psalms of Solomon* 6.6).
