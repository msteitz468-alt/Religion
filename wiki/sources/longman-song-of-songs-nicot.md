## What the source is

## Hermeneutical frame

Attributed to Longman (Preface + Intro):

1. **Genre criticism** — Song is lyric love poetry anthology (23 poems), not allegory, drama, or cultic text; Saadia’s “keys” = genre.
2. **Grammatico-historical + ANE** — Egyptian love poetry primary parallel; *wasf* form; Wetzstein wedding songs support literal reading.
3. **Wisdom-canonical** — Childs: reflection on love within marriage institution; whole-canon bounds; Gen 2–3 *tĕšûqâ* arc; marriage metaphor trajectory (Hosea → Eph 5 → Rev 19).
4. **Anti-allegory, pro-theological second dimension** — Suppress neither human eros nor legitimate divine–human marriage analogy; refuse arbitrary detail-pressing.
5. **Pastoral** — Against church taboo and cultural sex-idolatry; functional decanonization if unused after literal recognition.

## Volume synthesis

Longman’s Song of Songs is a genre manifesto for a book whose “problem” is over-clarity
rather than obscurity. The introduction’s long history of interpretation (allegory as
body-repression; drama as plot-imposition; cultic as unproven *hieros gamos*) clears the
ground for an **anthology of 23 poems** whose centripetal forces (refrains, character
consistency, garden/*wasf* imagery) never harden into a single narrative timeline.

The theological payoff is deliberately threefold and hierarchical: human love first
(mutual, exclusive, total, beautiful; thrice-warned “do not awaken”); then
creation–fall–redemption of intimacy via Gen 2–3 and Song’s only other *tĕšûqâ* (already–not
yet); then divine–human marriage illumination that recovers the allegorists’ instinct
without their two errors. Commentary climaxes at 8:6–7 (love strong as death; flame of Yah)
and closes with open yearning (8:13–14), matching the intro’s claim that no perfect marriage
exists this side of heaven.
