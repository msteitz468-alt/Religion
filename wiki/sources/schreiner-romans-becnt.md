# Romans (BECNT) — Thomas R. Schreiner

## What the source is

Thomas R. Schreiner's one-volume commentary on Paul's Letter to the Romans in the
**Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New Testament** (BECNT) series (Moisés Silva, editor),
published by Baker Academic in 1998. Schreiner (Southern Baptist Theological Seminary,
Louisville) writes as a Reformed Baptist / New Calvinist; the volume is dedicated to John
Piper. Method: grammatico-historical exegesis proceeding paragraph-by-paragraph, tracing the
"flow of thought," with close Greek grammatical analysis, sustained interaction with critical
scholarship (Cranfield, Dunn, Fitzmyer, Käsemann, Moo, Wilckens), and explicit theological
synthesis. Its signature macro-thesis: the glory of God is inductively the central,
letter-permeating theme of Romans.

The BECNT format supplies, for each exegetical unit, the author's own translation (with
half-brackets marking textual variants), a discussion of structure, verse-by-verse exposition
("Exegesis and Exposition"), a summary, and "Additional Notes" treating text-critical and
technical problems.

## Hermeneutical frame

Schreiner's reading is grammatico-historical in execution and Reformed-confessional in
synthesis. Body exposition is close-grammatical and dialogical (weighing named scholars);
the theological verdicts (forensic+transformative righteousness, unconditional individual
election, third use of the law, future salvation of ethnic Israel) are Schreiner's own,
attributed as such. The BECNT "Additional Notes" carry the text-critical register; the
author's translation with half-brackets is a third evidence class distinct from the exposition.

## Volume synthesis

Schreiner's BECNT *Romans* is a one-volume, confessionally Reformed (New Calvinist) grammatico-historical commentary whose two organizing convictions are (1) that *the glory of God* is the letter's inductively demonstrable central theme, penultimate to which stands even the salvation of Jew and Gentile, and (2) that Paul's soteriology is at once forensic and transformative, individual and unconditional in its election, and continuous with a "third use" of the moral law. Against the multi-volume commentaries of Cranfield and Dunn — his most constant interlocutors — he aims for Calvin's "lucid brevity," and against the New Perspective (Sanders, Dunn, Wright) he mounts one of the fullest evangelical rebuttals in a major commentary: *erga nomou* is the whole Mosaic law (with Qumran/4QMMT support), the "righteousness of God" *involves* but is not *defined by* covenant faithfulness, and Romans 4 carries a real anti-works polemic — while granting that Sanders rightly demolished the caricature of a uniformly merit-based Judaism.

For the wiki, the re-ingest's chief value is twofold. First, it supplies **verbatim-anchored, loci-specific grounding** for positions the 2026-06-05 ingest had asserted only in summary — the *hilastērion* propitiation argument (God propitiating himself; Dodd rebutted), the 9:5 ascription of *theos* to Christ, the double-predestination stance on 9:13/9:22, the end-time-generation reading of 11:26 keyed to "life from the dead," and the manuscript case for the sixteen-chapter text against the Marcionite fourteen-chapter recension. Second, it **corrects a genuine fidelity defect**: the earlier ingest had filed Schreiner flatly with the Reformed "regenerate believer" reading of Romans 7:14–25, whereas he in fact follows Seifrid in denying that the passage turns on the regenerate/unregenerate question at all — its subject is the law's inability to transform, and the "I" is paradigmatic Paul. That correction now stands on both the commentator page and the controversy page.

Tensions with already-ingested sources are mostly complementary rather than contradictory. Schreiner's *hilastērion*-as-propitiation and penal-substitution register sits alongside — and is explicitly contrasted with on [[atonement]] — Aquinas's *satisfactio* and Athanasius's incorruption-restored model, without displacing them. His unconditional-individual-election reading of Romans 9 provides the Pauline substratum that the [[predestination]] page's Aquinas/Calvin material presupposed but did not previously document from Paul. And his anti-NPP exegesis is registered as *one position* on the [[new-perspective-on-paul]] page against Sanders/Dunn/Wright, not promoted to wiki voice. No new controversy pages were required; the existing `righteousness-of-god`, `all-israel-saved`, and `romans-7-identity` controversies absorbed the deepened material.

This is a complete read of the volume's exposition, not a partial pass.
