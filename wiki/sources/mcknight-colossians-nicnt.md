# The Letter to the Colossians (Scot McKnight, NICNT)

## What the source is

Scot McKnight's *The Letter to the Colossians*, published 2018 by Eerdmans in the New
International Commentary on the New Testament (NICNT) series (general editor Joel B. Green).
A full-length exegetical commentary on the Greek text of Colossians, working from the NIV as
its display translation with the author's own renderings. Genre: mainline evangelical
critical commentary — grammatico-historical exegesis with heavy Second Temple Jewish
background, sustained engagement with the New Perspective on Paul conversation (Dunn, Wright,
Barclay, Campbell, Gorman), and a consistent ecclesial-theological focus. McKnight (b. 1953)
writes from Northern Seminary (Lombard, IL).

## Hermeneutical frame

McKnight reads Colossians by a **grammatico-historical method saturated with Second Temple
Jewish background** (Philo, 1 Enoch, DSS/1QS, 4 Ezra, 2 Baruch, Sirach, Wisdom, Testament of
the Twelve Patriarchs), in sustained conversation with the **New Perspective on Paul** and its
successors (Dunn — his Doktorvater — Wright, Sanders, Douglas Campbell's apocalyptic Paul, and
John Barclay's *Paul and the Gift*). Several load-bearing commitments organize the reading and
must be kept as **attributed positions**, not promoted to wiki voice:

- **Christology as port of entry.** "The gospel… is a claim about Jesus before it is a claim
  about salvation; in Christology we discover soteriology." Paul's theology is "first a
  Christology and then an eschatology, ecclesiology, and soteriology," narrated as
  theocracy → monarchy → **christocracy**.
- **Ecclesial-missional theology over rescue-soteriology.** McKnight resists letting Pauline
  theology enter "through soteriology" (his critique of Sanders/Dunn/Campbell/Barclay);
  Colossians "begins… with the christological mystery," and "the ethic of Colossians is an
  ethic for a church."
- **Christoformity, not cruciformity** (revising Gorman): formation into the *whole*
  Christ-event (cross + resurrection + ascension), not the cross alone — the master category
  unifying his ethics.
- **Halakic-mystics reconstruction** of the opponents (following Sumney's method and Ian
  Smith's synthesis): Torah-observant Jewish/Jewish-Christian insiders combining calendar/food
  observance with ascetic angel-like heavenly-ascent mysticism, not pagan syncretism; kin to
  the Galatian opponents.
- **Powers as polluted structures**: created-by-Christ structures polluted by *real* demonic
  beings (a both-and against Wink's "inner reality" and against a purely celestial hierarchy).
- **Ephesian imprisonment** (mid-50s, ~57) over the traditional Roman provenance.

Three evidence registers are kept distinct in this source page: McKnight's **body exegesis**,
his **cited scholars** (named where he reports them), and the **primary/Second-Temple sources**
he adduces as background. The commentary is confessionally evangelical and partisan on two
axes — for Pauline authenticity (his "known-to-unknown" critique) and against complementarian
household-code readings ("more Aristotelian than Christian"); both are recorded as positions.

## Volume synthesis

McKnight's *Colossians* is a deliberately contrarian NICNT volume whose most original
contribution is methodological rather than exegetical: the **"logic of the known to the
unknown"** critique of authorship debates. Because no Pauline letter is "pure Paul" — every
one is co-authored, secretary-penned (Romans by Tertius), and situationally shaped — there is
no stable "known" baseline against which Colossians can be measured "deviant." His slogan
("I do not think Paul wrote any of the letters because it is far more likely that Paul was
behind all of the letters"; Colossians is "Pauline as much as but not more than Galatians and
Romans") reframes the whole authenticity question as a category error. This is the wiki's
sharpest statement of the *dissolve-the-dichotomy* option on the [[colossians-authorship]]
page, distinct from both traditional-Pauline and deutero-Pauline positions.

Exegetically the volume's spine is a **Christology-first, ecclesial-missional** reading. The
Christ-poem (1:15–20) is handled as a "prose-hymn" grounding a **christological anthropology**
(Adam is modeled on Christ the prior Eikōn-template, not Christ on Adam) and, at 1:19, a
**perichoretic** reading of the Father's fullness indwelling the Son that McKnight pushes
explicitly beyond Dunn toward Nicene/Chalcedonian orthodoxy. The atonement is consistently
**Christus victor** with a real but non-central substitutionary dimension; 2:15 ("disarmed the
powers") is expounded in his signature **"powers as polluted structures"** excursus. His
reconstruction of the opponents as **halakic mystics** ties the *stoicheia* of 2:8, 20 to
diaspora Jewish halakah (the same referent as Galatians) rather than pagan cosmic elements —
his most contestable and load-bearing interpretive move.

The volume adds two things the wiki did not previously hold in attributed, source-grounded
form. First, a **thoroughgoing egalitarian reading of the household code** (3:18–4:1): the
husband is never told to lead, *hypotassō* is middle-voice self-ordering grounded in
Christoformity, "fitting in the Lord" dethrones rather than establishes patriarchy, and
complementarianism is judged "hierarchicalism and patriarchalism… more Aristotelian than
Christian." This is a direct evangelical-vs-evangelical counterweight to Thielman on the
[[ephesians-household-code]] page. Second, a **neglected Pauline pastoral theology** read out
of the "authorizing biographical disclosure" (1:24–2:5), where McKnight's "missional-
Christoformity" reading of "filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" keeps Christ's
atonement sufficient while casting Paul as en-fleshing the Isaianic-servant mission for the
church's benefit.

Tensions with already-ingested sources are honestly on the page. Against **Thielman** (via the
Colossians text page and the household-code controversy), McKnight favors **Ephesus over Rome**
for the imprisonment and reads the household code egalitarially where Thielman reads it more
conservatively. Against **Dunn**, whom he otherwise follows closely, he rejects the
known-to-unknown authorship logic, pushes the 1:19 fullness reading past Dunn's
"not-yet-incarnation," and declines Dunn's "healthy synagogue apologetic" account of the
opponents in favor of genuine "errorists." The volume's confessional-evangelical partisanship
(pro-authenticity, anti-complementarian, paedobaptist-friendly on 2:11–13) is flagged in
`reliability_notes` so those claims are read as positions, not settled results.
