## Hermeneutical frame

France reads Mark as a unified narrative — a Graeco-Roman βίος structured as a "Drama
in Three Acts" governed by the Galilee/Jerusalem polarity — and practices Greek-text
exegesis informed by, but often critical of, form- and redaction-critical scholarship.
Body commentary (France's exegesis) and endnotes (engagement with other scholars,
attributed positions) are distinct registers; both are France's authorship.

## Volume / work synthesis

France's commentary is a sustained argument that Mark is a deliberate literary and
theological whole — an oral-performance βίος with a "feeling of otherness" (Zuntz) — best
read through the geographical drama of Galilee and Jerusalem. France explicitly disclaims
that his "Drama in Three Acts" is Mark's own design; it is his heuristic for a narrative
whose single-visit Jerusalem schema is a dramatic reshaping (John's multiple visits being
historically more plausible). The christology "is in the story": the Prologue hands the
reader privileged knowledge, and the narrative then traces every human character's failure
to grasp it until 14:62 and the centurion's confession.

Two positions give the commentary its distinctive profile. First, the consistent
enthronement (non-parousia) reading of the Son-of-Man "comings": Dan 7:13 in both 13:26 and
14:62 is a vindication-and-enthronement oracle fulfilled within the judges' generation —
the discourse of Mark 13 concerns the destruction of the temple through v. 31, with the
parousia entering only at v. 32 (περὶ δέ), against Wright and Beasley-Murray on the one
side and the traditional parousia reading on the other. Second, his "unfashionably"
minority position that 16:8 was not the intended ending: the original conclusion (a Galilee
reunion on the lines of Mt. 28) was lost or never written, and the majority "open ending"
reading is an anachronistically modern literary judgment traceable to Lightfoot (1950).

Other load-bearing commitments: qualified confidence in the Petrine/Roman tradition
(following Hengel) with a pre-64 date held lightly; Marcan priority without a mechanical
two-source model ("I do not need a solution to the Synoptic Problem"); rejection of
Wrede-type secrecy theories in favour of a partial, historically-motivated secrecy tied to
the paradox of God's kingship; a minority Passover chronology (meal held deliberately a day
early, Nisan 14, harmonizing Mark with John); Isaiah 53 rather than Dan 7 as the source of
the passion predictions' "must"; and a refusal to soften the cry of dereliction.
