# Hamilton, *The Book of Genesis: Chapters 18–50* (NICOT, Eerdmans 1995)

## What the source is

Volume 2 of Victor P. Hamilton’s two-volume NICOT Genesis commentary. Continues the same evangelical grammatical-historical program as Volume 1: original translation, verse-by-verse philology, literary structure, ANE notes, and per-unit “New Testament Appropriation.” **No separate Introduction** — the whole-book introduction is in Volume 1. Completes Abraham Cycle Part II (18–25:18), the Isaac/Jacob Cycle (25:19–36:43), and the Joseph Story (37:1–50:26). Dedication to Heather, Paul, Dawn, David; wife Shirley typed the bulk of both volumes on an Apple IIe; series editor R. K. Harrison (deceased by completion); manuscript editor Gary Lee. Hamilton’s preface quotes Paul Rees: he likes to write but likes better “to have written.”

## Hermeneutical frame

Same canonical-evangelical program as Volume 1 (see [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot]] and [[victor-hamilton]]). Volume 2 distinctive emphases, attributed to Hamilton:

1. **Narrative ethics without moralizing** — Patriarchs’ failures (wife-sister reprises; Jacob’s *mirmâ*; Dinah episode) are narrated without divine rebuke of the patriarchs as persons in the Intro’s theological program; election remains gratuitous (Rom 9 reception of 25:23).
2. **Akedah as test, not etiology or Isaac-Christ center** — Primary frame is Abraham’s surrender of the promise-bearer; 22:16–18 integral conditionality; NT focuses Abraham’s faith (Heb 11, Jas 2); resistance to making Isaac-typology the interpretive center.
3. **Younger-son election + nemesis** — Oracle, birthright, blessing, Ephraim>Manasseh; deceiver becomes deceived; still not moral-reward election.
4. **Providence climax at 50:20** — Same verb *ḥāšaḇ* for brothers’ evil and God’s good; instrument not substitute; Joseph “God sent me” (45:5–8) prepares the double intention.
5. **Source-critical restraint in narrative detail** — e.g. Midianites/Ishmaelites as overlapping terms rather than J/E seam (ch. 37); thematic divine names over automatic source assignment.

## Volume synthesis

Volume 2 is the longer narrative half of Hamilton’s Genesis: Sodom to Joseph’s bones. Its throughline is **election and providence operating through compromised agents**. Abraham’s hospitality and intercession (18) contrast Sodom’s social and sexual violence (19); the second wife-sister episode (20) and Isaac’s birth/expulsion of Ishmael (21) close the promise’s particularization. The Akedah (22) is the cycle’s ethical and theological summit: a genuine test whose stake is the covenant carrier, with an oath that introduces rare conditionality without dissolving Gen 15’s unilateral grace. Machpelah (23) is the first legal foothold in the land; Rebekah (24) stages endogamy and *ḥeseḏ*; Abraham’s death gathers Ishmael and Isaac at the tomb.

The Isaac/Jacob cycle stages gratuitous younger-son election (25:23; Rom 9), birthright and blessing, Bethel’s unconditional promises without rebuke, Laban’s mirror-deception, Peniel’s name Israel (assertiveness, not guaranteed transformation), fragile Esau reconciliation, and the morally raw Dinah narrative (Jacob’s rebuke tactical, not ethical). Edom’s *tôlĕdôt* (36) bridges non-covenant older brother as blessed outside the line.

Joseph (37–50) is Hamilton’s clearest providence treatise: dreams, coat, sale (Midianite/Ishmaelite overlap against J/E split), Judah–Tamar’s measure-for-measure, “Yahweh with Joseph,” testing brothers, Judah’s surety speech, “God sent me,” Ephraim over Manasseh, Gen 49’s Judah/*šîlōh* options, and **50:20** as double intention. The book ends in Egypt with bones awaiting *pāqad* — land by grace, not possession.
