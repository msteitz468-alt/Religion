## Hermeneutical frame

Four load-bearing methods:

1. **Canonical shape** (deClaissé-Walford §IV, building on Wilson/McCann): five-book editorial arrangement narrates Israel’s history from David through exile to restoration; sequence is hermeneutically significant; “story of the shaping of survival.”
2. **Poetry-first** (Jacobson/Tanner): meaning *in, with, and under* poetic language; multi-level parallelism; no true meter.
3. **Form criticism as background** (Jacobson §III): genre labels preliminary; each psalm as unique prayer; more affinity with Westermann/Brueggemann theological stream than pure cult-functional reconstruction.
4. **Lament as necessary faith**: unrelieved complaint and imprecation retained as sacred speech (Tanner on 88; Nancy on 109/137).

**Three evidence classes kept separate:** (a) authors’ translation + verse commentary; (b) Introduction surveys of other scholars’ positions; (c) pastoral/confessional applications in closing “Reflection” sections.

**Translation signature:** *hesed* left untranslated as loanword (130/255 OT occurrences in the Psalter).

## Volume / work synthesis

This NICOT Psalms is the wiki’s densest single-volume treatment of the Psalter and the clearest case of **canonical-shape criticism** as primary method. deClaissé-Walford’s five-book narrative — David → Solomon → crisis → Moses/YHWH-reigns → return/praise — organizes both Introduction §IV and the book intros that open each commentary block. The claim is not that every psalm was written for that slot, but that the **editorial arrangement** teaches postexilic Israel who it is without a Davidic nation-state: a Torah people (Ps 1 / 119) under Yahweh’s kingship (Pss 2, 93–99, 145–150), still authorized to lament (including Ps 88 and imprecation).

Jacobson’s contributions ground the volume in text criticism (Yarchin MS diversity; DSS fluidity; MT-first canonical aim), superscription theory (practical anonymity; historical superscriptions as early interpretation), form-critical taxonomy, and poetry. Tanner carries the darkest theological freight — Book Three’s crisis, Ps 88’s godforsaken speech, Ps 89’s unanswered “Where is your hesed of old?” — and the Book Four reframe that answers without restoring the monarchy. Nancy’s Book Five completes the arc with restoration thanksgiving, festal clusters, and the Final Hallel’s absolute praise.

Relative to already-ingested sources: the Psalter page now sits beside [[hartley-job-nicot|Hartley on Job]] on theodicy (doxological path through lament vs lawsuit/Yahweh speeches), [[davidic-covenant]] material from Tsumura/Leithart (Ps 89 as the poetic crisis of the same promise), and [[hesed]] from Lau’s Ruth (Psalter supplies the term’s densest biblical concentration). Tensions to keep open: (1) Wilson’s editorial thesis is assumed and extended rather than re-proved here; (2) the five-book “history of Israel” reading is a powerful **construct** attributed to deClaissé-Walford, not bare wiki fact; (3) Christological receptions (Pss 2, 22, 110, 118) are recorded as the authors state them, not collapsed into OT meaning.
