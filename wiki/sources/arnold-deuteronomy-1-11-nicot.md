## What the source is

Bill T. Arnold's *The Book of Deuteronomy, Chapters 1–11* (2022) in the **New International Commentary on the Old Testament** (NICOT; Eerdmans). Arnold (Asbury Theological Seminary; NICOT series editor from 2020) combines traditional historical-critical scholarship with literary-rhetorical and text-critical analysis, an original Hebrew translation, and evangelical confessional framing. Volume scope is **chs. 1–11 only**.

## Hermeneutical frame

Arnold reads Deuteronomy as **Mosaic speech at Moab** presenting Torah as divine Will (grace), not merely “law,” shaped by ANE treaty forms without being reduced to them. Method: historical-critical engagement + literary unity (discourse structure: superscription → historical → sermonic → Torah paraenesis) + text-critical pluriformity (Leningrad base; LXX/SP/DSS weighed case-by-case). Composition: *traditum* of Mosaic authorization with *traditio* of scribal updating; *Urdeuteronomium* path through northern refugees / Hezekiah / Manasseh / Josiah; rejects Noth’s isolation of Deut 1–3 as only DtrH intro. Theology: jealous holy love; revised name theology; education/life of the mind; retributive justice; poverty ethics; warfare and *ḥerem* treated with ethical seriousness (including metaphorical readings where Arnold argues them).

**Load-bearing attributed moves:** narrative horizons (Moab/Horeb/future “this day”); Torah = Mosaic Moab speech (A-E-01); paraenesis 5–11 vs instructions 12–26 (A-E-02); Shema *ʾeḥad* as unified stable self vs ANE divine fluidity (A-E); covenant bilateral not mere treaty copy (A-A-50); *ḥerem* / election by love not merit (A-F); ch. 4 idolatry narrow+broad / exile / incomparability (A-D); circumcision of heart + love of stranger (A-G); Gerizim–Ebal frame to ch. 12 (A-H).

## Volume / work synthesis

Arnold’s NICOT *Deuteronomy 1–11* is a dense, late-evangelical commentary that treats the book as **theological rhetoric in discourse units**, not a flat law code. The introduction maps composition history with unusual candor for the series: Mosaic *authorization* (possibly even *ipsissima verba* of farewell speeches) is held open while formation steps, non-Mosaic narrator, *Urdeuteronomium* from the Book of the Covenant, and eighth–seventh-century scribal contexts (including EST/Neo-Hittite treaty transmission) are affirmed. Noth’s DtrH influence is acknowledged; Noth’s detachment of Deut 1–3 from the Torah presentation at 4:44–49 is rejected.

Distinctive emphases relative to [[craigie-deuteronomy-nicot|Craigie (1976)]]: (1) fuller text-critical and pluriform manuscript practice; (2) sharper literary framing of **paraenesis (5–11)** vs later detailed Torah (12–26, outside this volume); (3) Shema *ʾeḥad* read against ANE fluidity of divine selfhood (Sommer et al.); (4) love–fear antinomy as commandable cognitive-affective loyalty; (5) education/*lmd* and “life of the mind” as whole-book theme; (6) more explicit multi-stage compositional path while still speaking of Mosaic voice. Craigie is more invested in second-millennium Hittite form as dating proof-edge; Arnold prefers first-millennium Neo-Hittite transmission of treaty forms and subversive YHWH-suzerainty in imperial context, while still using treaty background for covenant theology.

The commentary body through 11:32 ends the volume’s paraenetic arc: historical prologue (rebellion vs obedience prototypes), sermonic monotheism and anti-idolatry (ch. 4), Decalogue and Shema, *ḥerem* and election by love, desert testing and pride, rebellious past and heart circumcision, blessing/curse toward Gerizim–Ebal—setting the stage for the cult-centralization instructions that begin at 12:1 (next volume).
