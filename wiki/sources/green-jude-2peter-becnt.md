# Green, *Jude and 2 Peter* (BECNT, 2008)

## What the source is

Gene L. Green's *Jude and 2 Peter* is a volume in the **Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New Testament** (BECNT), edited by Robert W. Yarbrough and Robert H. Stein (Baker Academic, 2008; ebook edition 2013). Green (PhD, University of Aberdeen) is professor of New Testament at Wheaton College (Illinois); he previously taught New Testament at the Seminario ESEPA in Costa Rica and has written Spanish-language commentaries on the Petrine and Thessalonian letters, the Pillar *Letters to the Thessalonians*, and (as coauthor) *The New Testament in Antiquity*. He is known as an advocate for Majority World / global theology.

**Name disambiguation:** Gene L. Green is **not** [[joel-green|Joel B. Green]] (NICNT Luke). They are distinct scholars who share a surname.

The BECNT format supplies, for each unit: literary structure, author's translation, "Exegesis and Exposition," "Summary," and "Additional Notes" on text-critical and grammatical questions. Green's stated program is to read both letters **within their cultural and historical contexts** — the web of Jewish apocalyptic / midrash-pesher and wider Greco-Roman philosophy and social life (Epicurean theodicy, *vituperatio*, honor-shame, *imitatio*, secretarial composition).

Register discipline: the **body** is Green's own exegesis and exposition; **Additional Notes** carry text-critical judgments; the **Introductions** state positions on authorship, date, opponents, genre, and theology; series/author prefaces state confessional and translation commitments. All interpretive claims are attributed to Green (or scholars he cites), never promoted to bare wiki voice on this source alone.

## Hermeneutical frame

Green reads Jude and 2 Peter as **genuine first-century letters** embedded in Jewish and Hellenistic culture. Method is grammatical-historical exegesis attentive to:

- **Jewish apocalyptic and midrash/pesher** (esp. Jude's Enochic and Mosaic traditions; Qumran-style text-and-comment chains)
- **Greco-Roman social rhetoric** (*vituperatio*, honor-shame, *imitatio* as honorable literary reuse, secretarial composition)
- **Philosophical dialogue partners** (Epicurean-flavored skepticism about providence/judgment; popularized immutability-of-the-world claims)
- **Epistolary and rhetorical structure** (engagement with Watson et al. without reducing letters to pure speeches)

Stance is evangelical-critical: he engages Bauckham, Fornberg, Neyrey, Käsemann, and others and defends traditional authorship where he finds the critical case inconclusive. Theology and history are not bifurcated.

## Volume / work synthesis

Green’s BECNT *Jude and 2 Peter* is a sustained **grammatical-historical and cultural-contextual** commentary whose distinctive evangelical contribution is a minority defense of **authentic authorship for both letters** paired with a sociology-of-honor reading of their literary relationship. Jude is read as a genuine composition by the Lord’s brother (likely with secretarial help), rooted in mid-first-century **Palestinian Jewish Christianity**, aimed at outside **antinomian** infiltrators who pervert grace into license — not second-century gnostics or Carpocratians. 2 Peter is read as a genuine Petrine letter (different secretary from 1 Peter) that *includes* a testamentary purpose-section (1:12–15) without *being* a “testament” genre piece in Bauckham’s sense; its insider false teachers deny the **parousia and final judgment** with Epicurean-flavored but eclectic skepticism.

The volume’s sharpest theological edges for the wiki are three. First, Jude’s quotation of **1 Enoch 1.9** (and the Assumption/Testament of Moses allusion at v. 9): Green refuses apologetic minimizations (opponents-only authority; Paul-style incidental truth; authentic pre-flood fragment) and holds that Jude treats Enoch as **predictive, authoritative revelation** alongside Torah and apostolic prophecy, while stressing judicious use tied to canonical bedrock and ending near Augustine’s humble both/and. Second, **2 Pet 1:4** “partakers of the divine nature” is moral transformation into God-like virtue, not ontological divinization and not principally immortality (against Bauckham). Third, **2 Pet 3:10–13**: difficult εὑρεθήσεται (“found”), cosmic judgment by fire within a **linear three-world** scheme (not Stoic ἐκπύρωσις), and hope of **renewal not annihilation** (Isa 65:17/66:22).

On the literary relationship Green joins the majority: **2 Peter used Jude** via ancient *imitatio* (honorable remoulding, not plagiarism), which implies Jude’s higher honor as a member of Jesus’ family — a load-bearing sociology claim. Adaptation notes densified in this re-ingest include dropping the Michael–Moses legend and the Enoch quotation, retaining “glories” slander with intensification, adding the flood generation from other tradition (including affinity with 1 Pet 3:19–20 handling), and expanding Balaam. Opponents of the two letters remain **similar yet distinct** (grace-perversion outsiders vs parousia-denying insiders).
