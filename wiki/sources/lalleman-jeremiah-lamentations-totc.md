## Hermeneutical frame

- **Series format:** Context → Comment → Meaning (Firth/Longman, raw 405–411).
- **Lalleman:** historical context where knowable; **priority to literary context** (raw 950–951); **MT base** (raw 922–923).
- **Load-bearing theses:** 604 BC scroll-burning as "point of no return" (raw 870–871, 976–977); *šûb* arc from human imperative to divine causative restoration (raw 997–1036); two metaphor clusters (build/plant; medical) as coherence signals (raw 1040–1124); chs. 23–29 as true/false-prophecy centre rather than a ch. 25 break (raw 1542–1543).
- **Compositional posture:** Baruch active scribe; growth during ministry; ch. 52 later; skeptical of proof-light heavy Deuteronomistic schools; final form "from doom through judgment to hope" (raw 1121–1123).
- **Lamentations:** non-Jeremianic eyewitness near 587; form-critical + Deuteronomic-curse theology without the word "covenant"; hope at 3:22–24 important but not a solution (raw 5857, 5965, 6178).
- **Pastoral frame:** Jeremiah as forerunner of Christ / Suffering Servant; Meaning sections apply to Christian discipleship — kept attributed.

## Volume synthesis

Lalleman's TOTC is a **final-form, word-study-driven, pastoral** reading of Jeremiah and Lamentations that gives the wiki a second full-series voice beside Goldingay's NICOT. Where Goldingay's distinctives are the Gospels-composition analogy, both-and theology, and Lamentations as theodicy/anti-theodicy house of sorrow, Lalleman's distinctives are the **604 BC hinge**, the **šûb arc**, the **two metaphor clusters**, and a **structural map that centres true vs false prophecy (23–29)** rather than a ch. 25 break.

On Jeremiah she builds a clear narrative of hope: early ministry urges human return; after Jehoiakim burns the scroll, judgment is inescapable and only God's initiative can restore; the Book of Comfort (30–33) and especially the **new covenant** (31:31–34; restated as everlasting covenant at 32:40) are the summit of that initiative. She keeps confessional Christian application (Last Supper inauguration; Jeremiah as forerunner of Christ) in Meaning sections without erasing historical and critical discussion (MT/LXX dual editions; Baruch; skepticism of heavy Deuteronomism).

On Lamentations she refuses traditional Jeremianic authorship while affirming deep kinship of grief language, reads the acrostics and voices as crafted art, and insists that **3:22–24 does not solve the book** — ch. 5 remains open prayer under a God who "reigns for ever." That pairs productively with Goldingay's house-for-sorrow without collapsing the two readings.

**Tensions with already-ingested sources:** (1) Structure — Lalleman 23–29 centre vs common ch. 25 hinge (Goldingay works nearer the latter). (2) Lamentations authorship — Lalleman unlikely Jeremiah vs tradition vs Goldingay's openness to female authorship. (3) Avertibility of judgment — Lalleman's hard 604 hinge vs Goldingay's both-and. All three are recorded as positions on the text pages; no adjudication.
