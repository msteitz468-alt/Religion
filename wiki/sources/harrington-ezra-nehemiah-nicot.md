# The Books of Ezra and Nehemiah (NICOT) — Hannah K. Harrington

## What the source is

Hannah K. Harrington, *The Books of Ezra and Nehemiah*, New International Commentary on the Old Testament (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2022). Full-volume evangelical exegetical commentary on the unified book Ezra–Nehemiah, with twenty-three thematic **Excursuses** on Second Temple topics (intermarriage, gentile impurity, midrash Torah, Samarians/Samaritans, synagogue roots, conversion, etc.). Harrington’s distinctive frame combines **Second Temple comparative** reading (DSS, Jubilees, Josephus, Mishnah, Elephantine, Aramaic Levi) with **literary** architecture (Eskenazi, Throntveit, Talmon) and **cautious social-scientific** engagement (Douglas group/grid and Turner social drama as used by Janzen and Moffat). Confessional NICOT posture: she accepts “discrepancies” reflecting human transmission of sacred tradition while treating the text as carrying divinely inspired traditions for the community of faith.

## Hermeneutical frame

Harrington reads Ezra–Nehemiah as **the earliest Jewish literature of the Second Temple era** and as a seedbed for later Judaisms and, through them, Christianity. Distinctive moves:

1. **Unitary book, separate from Chronicles.** One original scroll; church fathers split the books. Separate authorship from Chronicles (Japhet/Williamson “disparity of vision”); she finds Steinmann’s orthographic argument (2 Chr 36 ← Ezra 1) persuasive. Leaves **Neh 8 in place** (against moving it into the Ezra Memoir).

2. **Chronology.** Ezra and Nehemiah both under **Artaxerxes I** (Ezra 458; Nehemiah 445–~432); late-Ezra (398) rejected. Early combination of material ~430–424 BCE; final form not before “Darius the Persian” (most likely Darius III, 335–331).

3. **Literary architecture.** Three parallel returns (Throntveit): temple / community / walls. Concentricity, resumptive repetition (e.g. Ezra 4:5b // 4:24b frames Artaxerxes material thematically, not as chronological error). Eskenazi: holiness expands from house of God → holy seed → holy city. Full-text focus: rebuilding community by **Torah**, not the wall alone.

4. **Social science (cautious).** Surveys Douglas group/grid (Janzen: high group / weak grid + witch-hunt; Moffat: mid group / strong grid; women as true outsiders) and Turner social drama (Moffat on Ezra 9–10). In the intermarriage Excursus itself she **prioritizes priestly terminology** (*zeraʿ qōdeš*, *maʿal*) over pure socioeconomic models while granting those models value.

5. **Second Temple comparative.** Nearly every major innovation is traced into DSS (4QMMT, CD, 1QS, Temple Scroll), Jubilees, Aramaic Levi, Josephus, and early rabbinic law. Gentile impurity at Neh 13:9; matrilineal root; synagogue cluster at Neh 8; midrash embryo at Ezra 7:10.

6. **Christian application.** Three-level model: Word-centered community; worship (temple → city → people); separation for identity — with NT reversals of ethnic impurity carefully marked (Acts 10; Gal 3:28; 2 Cor 6:14 as religious, not ethnic, concern).

## Volume / work synthesis

Harrington’s NICOT is the wiki’s densest single treatment of **post-exilic identity formation**. Where Breneman (NAC 1993) offers a cleaner evangelical theological synthesis of the same books, Harrington situates almost every pericope in a trajectory that runs from Persian Yehud through Qumran and into rabbinic and NT reception. The result is less a verse-by-verse “application” commentary than a **Second Temple origin story** for practices the later canon assumes: public Torah reading, penitential prayer as institution, city-wide holiness, genealogical priesthood, and the intermarriage ban.

Methodologically she is pluralist but not unprincipled. She rejects subjective diachronic rearrangements that smooth the narrative into modern chronology; she uses literary structure (resumptive repetition, chiasm, three returns) to explain alleged anachronisms; she reports social-anthropological models without equating them with the text’s own priestly rhetoric. On the intermarriage crisis her own analytic center of gravity is *maʿal* and *zeraʿ qōdeš* — Israel as sanctum desecrated by mixture — and she is explicit that Ezra offers **no conversion path** and treats children of mixed marriages as invalid members.

Two fidelity corrections relative to the 2026-06-05 pages matter for the wiki’s integrity. First, Harrington does **not** use the tagline “drastic but understandable”; she says the measures are **understandable** as minority ethno-religious survival and elsewhere “drastic action.” Second, Douglas/Turner are **interlocutors she surveys** (via Janzen and Moffat), not her exclusive preferred lens for Ezra 9–10. The older pages conflated those layers; this re-ingest separates them.

Tensions with already-ingested sources remain productive rather than contradictory in wiki voice: Breneman is more apologetically traditional on intermarriage justification; Leithart’s Chronicles (BTC) treats the Chronicler’s open stance toward the North as a different theological project from Ezra–Nehemiah’s separatism — which Harrington’s separate-authorship position supports. Ruth remains a canonical counter-testimony to ethnic exclusivism that Harrington herself notes some scholars read as anti-Ezran; she records that Ezra does not treat Ruth-type cases as applicable.
