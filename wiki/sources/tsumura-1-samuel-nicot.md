# Tsumura, *The First Book of Samuel* (NICOT, Eerdmans 2006)

## What the source is

Evangelical critical commentary on 1 Samuel in the New International Commentary
on the Old Testament (NICOT) series. Comparatively literal translation of the MT,
verse-by-verse philological and discourse-grammatical commentary, and sustained ANE
comparative notes (Ugaritic, Akkadian, Mari, Assyrian). Tsumura trained in Semitic
philology under C. H. Gordon; primary dialogue partners P. K. McCarter (AB) and
R. P. Gordon (often disagreed with). Series editor R. L. Hubbard. Dedicated with
2 Cor 12:9 Greek; institutional home Japan Bible Seminary / Hamadayama Church.

## Hermeneutical frame

Per Preface and Introduction (R0–R1), attributed to Tsumura:

1. **MT-first text criticism** — Stick to MT without easy LXX/4QSam emendation; many “errors” are phonetic spellings, brachylogy, or misunderstood grammar; majority of proposed emendations needless (p. 10).
2. **Discourse grammar** — Supra-sentential analysis: *wayqtl* mainline vs *weqtl* predictive; SETTING–EVENT–TERMINUS; background/foreground; Parunak-style transitional links (A/aB).
3. **AXB and vertical grammar** — Literary insertion patterns and poetic prose; aural composition.
4. **Semitic philology** — Ugaritic/Akkadian/Old Aramaic cognates; archaic measures (*ḥămôr leḥem* = Akk. *imēru*; *ṣemed* = *ṣimdu*).
5. **ANE contextual / maximalist** — Hallo: use every scrap; “absence of evidence is not evidence of absence”; Tel Dan *bytdwd*.
6. **Final-form literary** — Alter/Fokkelman/Sternberg useful; reader-response/deconstruction inadequate without ANE competence.
7. **DH skepticism** — Circular “Deuteronomistic” language arguments; final edit of Samuel no later than late 10th c. (1 Sam 27:6).
8. **Evangelical theological frame** — Purpose: king subject to prophet; messianic trajectory restrained (full Davidic promise in vol. 2).

## Volume synthesis

Tsumura’s 1 Samuel is the NICOT series’ most philologically specialized Former Prophets
volume. Its load-bearing contribution is not a new outline of the plot (four literary
blocks — Story of Samuel with embedded Ark; transitional ch. 8; Story of Saul; Story of
Saul and David — are stated clearly and used throughout) but a **method package**: MT
preservation via phonetic spellings, brachylogy, and AXB; discourse grammar that
resolves “chronological” tensions without source surgery; and ANE comparative work that
illuminates rather than relativizes.

Theologically, Tsumura states the book’s purpose as the twin establishment of monarchy
(chs. 8–12) and preparation of David (chs. 16–31), under the principle that **the king in
Israel is subject to the prophet** through whom God conveys his word. Saul is the
conditional experiment (*nāgîd* over YHWH’s *naḥălāh* who fails two obedience tests);
David is the king God chooses for himself. Hannah’s Song (2:1–10) is the thematic overture:
sovereignty, reversal of fortune, and the first *māšîaḥ* reference. The monarchy-request
tension is held deliberately — sin of motive (“like all the nations”) and providential
grant — without collapsing into either pure anti- or pro-monarchic source criticism.

On the two rejections (chs. 13 and 15), Tsumura’s sharpest claims are that Saul’s offense
is failure to heed the prophetic word more than priestly usurpation; that partial *ḥerem*
is not *ḥerem*; that Samuel’s killing of Agag completes the ban rather than offering
sacrifice (contra McCarter); and that *nḥm* in 15:11/35 is emotive “regret” while 15:29
is relational non-reversal of a judicial decision. On Endor (ch. 28), he rejects both
“God uses pagan channels as normal” and pure hallucination: something extraordinary even
to the medium occurs by God’s power, yielding a death oracle and transfer of kingdom to
David. Gilboa closes with an honorable tragic end and an explicit forward look to David’s
elegy and the messianic “Lord’s anointed.”

**Tensions with other ingested sources (not adjudicated):** more MT-conservative than
McCarter/Tov eclectic reconstruction; more DH-skeptical than many NICOT peers (Webb’s
modified acceptance; Bruckner’s critical comfort); maximalist on United Monarchy
historicity against minimalist denial of David (Tel Dan used as counter-evidence).
Alter’s literary David Story is held alongside as a complementary synchronic reading
(already on the text page).

volume remains a separate prior ingest.
