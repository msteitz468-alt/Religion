# Moo, *The Letter of James* (PNTC, 2000)

## What the source is

Douglas J. Moo's *The Letter of James* is a volume in the **Pillar New Testament Commentary** (PNTC), series editor D. A. Carson (Eerdmans, 2000). It is a substantial revision and near-doubling of Moo's earlier Tyndale (TNTC) commentary (1985). Carson (series preface) praises its "deceptive simplicity" and "gentle tone of thoughtful application." Base translation is the **NIV** (with constant Greek engagement). Moo (PhD, University of St. Andrews) writes as a grammatico-historical evangelical commentator whose best-known work is on Paul (BECNT Romans/Galatians); this volume extends that method into the General Epistles.

Register discipline for this ingest: the **body text** is Moo's own exegesis and exposition; the **Introduction** states his positions on reception, genre, authorship, occasion/date, theology, and structure; the **Series/Author Prefaces** state series aims and acknowledgments. All interpretive, theological, and historical-reconstruction claims are attributed to Moo (or to scholars he cites), never promoted to bare wiki voice on the strength of this single source.

## Hermeneutical frame

Moo reads James as a **grammatico-historical** commentary within the PNTC evangelical series frame: NIV base text, sustained Greek and OT/Jewish background, verse-by-verse exposition, and theological synthesis for pastors. Standing interlocutors regularly cited: Dibelius, Davids, Johnson, Martin, Laws, Ropes, Mayor, Mussner, Calvin, Luther.

**Genre:** formal letter (literary/circular rather than personal; closest NT parallel 1 John); resists Dibelius's pure *paraenesis* and pure "wisdom book" labels; preferred specific label = **sermon/homily put into letter form**.

**Organizing concern:** **spiritual wholeness** (*teleios*) against **double-mindedness** (*dipsychos*, 1:8; 4:8) and friendship with the world (4:4). The emotional climax is 4:4–10 ("you adulterous people").

**Authorship/date (positions):** James the Lord's brother; middle 40s; Jewish-Christian diaspora readers; "misunderstood Paulinism" on justification as dating argument; counter-objection that known pseudepigraphal letters were not accepted as authentic.

**James/Paul:** different senses of *dikaioō* (Paul: initial forensic entry by faith; James: final vindication that takes Spirit-produced works into account); James's "faith alone" = bogus claimed faith, not Pauline *pistis*; monergistic integration via implanted word (1:21).

**Distinctive crux decisions:** divine jealousy in 4:5 (context over split lexical data); anointing as consecration-symbol (not medicinal, sacramental extreme unction, or apostolic-age-only); physical illness in 5:14; voluntary-oath reading of 5:12; "perfect/royal law of liberty" = OT law as reinterpreted by Jesus, internalized under new-covenant promise.

Body vs apparatus: exposition is Moo's interpretive register; endnotes are bibliographic/technical; series/author prefaces state confessional and pastoral aims.

## Volume / work synthesis

Moo's PNTC *James* is a sustained **grammatico-historical and theologically integrative** commentary whose distinctive evangelical contribution is to let James speak with his own voice while seeking canonical coherence with Paul. The letter is read as an intensely practical **sermon-homily in letter form**, organized not by a single thesis sentence but by the call to **spiritual wholeness** — undivided loyalty to God — against the recurring diagnosis of *dipsychia* and world-friendship. Genre labels (paraenesis, wisdom) are engaged and then carefully limited; dependence on Jesus' teaching (especially Matthean forms) and Hellenistic Jewish moral tradition is front-loaded.

On history, Moo defends **James of Jerusalem** as author and a **middle-40s** date, answering the four standard critical objections and pressing the reception argument against transparent pseudepigraphy. The justification material in ch. 2 is both the letter's theological climax and a dating lever: James attacks a **misunderstood "faith alone" slogan**, not Paul's authentic doctrine — most intelligible before the Acts 15 consensus. At the level of soteriology Moo refuses the popular "demonstrate-only" escape for *dikaioō* and the non-eschatological soft reading of *sōzō*; he prefers **final-judgment vindication** for James and **initial forensic declaration** for Paul, integrating the apparent synergism monergistically through the **implanted word** (1:21) and Spirit-produced works.

Exegetical set pieces densified under this re-ingest include the **perfect/royal law of liberty**, the **2:14–26 ring structure** with v. 24 at the center, the **tongue/wisdom/quarrels** community block (3:1–4:12), the **4:5 divine-jealousy crux**, the **worldview clash** of 4:13–5:11, and the concluding triad of **oaths / anointing-healing / restoring the wanderer**.
