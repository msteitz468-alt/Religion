# The Second Letter to the Corinthians (PNTC) — Mark A. Seifrid

## What the source is

Mark A. Seifrid's *The Second Letter to the Corinthians* (2014) in the **Pillar New Testament Commentary** (PNTC) series (general editor D. A. Carson; Eerdmans / Apollos). Seifrid (Southern Baptist Theological Seminary; also taught at Concordia Theological Seminary, St. Louis) is known for Pauline justification studies (*Justification by Faith*, 1992; *Christ, Our Righteousness*, 2000). Base translation is the **ESV**. Motto of the work: *Verbum crucis, virtus Dei* ("The word of the cross is the power of God"; cf. 1 Cor 1:18). Carson (editor’s preface) presents Seifrid as a sure-footed guide through difficult Greek and creative partition reconstructions, making the letter “come alive again.”

## Hermeneutical frame

Seifrid combines **grammatico-historical** exegesis with a load-bearing **theologia crucis** (Lutheran *sub contrario*) as the organizing key to the whole letter. He **defends letter unity** against partition theories and practices **minimalist mirror-reading** of opponents (no reconstructed Gnostic/divine-man system; not Judaizers). Body vs apparatus: exposition is Seifrid’s interpretive register; footnotes carry secondary debate.

**Load-bearing moves (verified in re-read):**
- Single purpose = apostolic legitimation; opponents as intruders into apostle–church bond
- Apostle as **“Christian existence written large”** (exact phrase)
- Opponents: practical *theologia gloriae* of visible success
- 2 Cor 3 addressed to Corinthians; veil as universal blindness; *katargeō* = abrogate not fade
- Salvation passive / *extra nos* / communicative; sufficiency from God (3:5)
- Great exchange 5:21: irreducibly ontological exclusive+inclusive place-taking
- New creation personal-and-cosmic, localized *in Christ*
- *Haplotēs* = **simplicity** (not English “generosity”)
- 12:9 grace sufficient; **13:4** crucified *ex astheneias* (“out of” not “in” weakness)

## Volume / work synthesis

Seifrid’s PNTC *2 Corinthians* is a sustained **theologia crucis** exposition whose distinctive contribution is to hold the entire letter under one question: does the risen Lord speak in Paul, the weakling? Carson’s preface already flags the letter’s emotional range, difficult Greek, and the damage done by creative partition reconstructions; Seifrid answers with a patient unity thesis and a Lutheran-evangelical account of power hidden under weakness.

On opponents, the re-read confirms the minimalist practical-theology thesis and sharpens it: ch. 3 is not a second Judaizing front; the veil is universal; *katargeō* abrogates rather than fades. On soteriology, 5:17–21 densifies new creation as localized intersection of the eschaton with the fallen world and 5:21 as irreducibly ontological place-taking — forensic and extrinsic *in Christ*, but not bare “money in the bank” imputation. On the collection, *haplotēs* as **simplicity** stitches 1:12, 8–9, and 11:3 into one letter-theme. On the climax, the thorn remains unidentified by design, and 13:4’s *ex astheneias* (“out of weakness”) becomes a load-bearing claim about Christ and the apostle: power operates within weakness without removing it.

**What this adds to the wiki:** a complete source page and coverage ledger for a volume that shaped the 2 Corinthians cluster in a pre-ledger 2026-06 pass; verbatim-anchored densification of cross, new creation, reconciliation, new covenant, and opponents; several **fidelity corrections** (phrase “written large”; *haplotēs*; *ex astheneias*; Asia not primarily illness; offender ≠ 1 Cor 5 man; 5:21 ontology vs bare imputation summaries).

**Tensions with already-ingested sources** remain positions: Seifrid and Schreiner share anti-NPP forensic instincts but Seifrid’s *theologia crucis* and ontological-exchange language are denser here; Ciampa–Rosner on 1 Cor provide the social-values and OT-template backdrop to the same church; partition theorists (Furnish, Martin, Georgi) stay on the controversy page without adjudication.
