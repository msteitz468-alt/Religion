# Ciampa & Rosner, *The First Letter to the Corinthians* (PNTC, 2010)

## What the source is

Roy E. Ciampa and Brian S. Rosner's *The First Letter to the Corinthians* is a volume in the **Pillar New Testament Commentary** (PNTC), series editor D. A. Carson (Eerdmans / Apollos, 2010). It is one of the few major NT commentaries bearing two names as co-equal authors. Carson (editor's preface) flags the letter's pastoral range and praises the work as careful, sometimes innovative, and attentive both to Corinthian historical context and to biblical theology.

Base translation is the **TNIV**, with constant Greek engagement. Register discipline for this ingest:

1. **Ciampa–Rosner's exposition** — the main commentary voice (their interpretive and theological claims).
2. **Their translation / cited base text** of 1 Corinthians (pericope by pericope).
3. **Positions they report** from secondary literature (Fee, Thiselton, Hays, Mitchell, Winter, Wright, Garland, etc.) — attributed to the scholar named, not to Ciampa–Rosner.

## Hermeneutical frame

Ciampa and Rosner read 1 Corinthians as a coherent biblical-theological letter whose structure follows a Jewish moral template for Gentile converts (flee sexual immorality; flee idolatry; glorify God), under the social diagnosis that Roman Corinthian values have infiltrated the church. Methodologically they prioritize OT intertexture (Deuteronomy, Isaiah, Malachi) over Greco-Roman deliberative rhetoric as the primary frame, while still using social history (status, sophistry as heuristic, *macellum*, house-church dining) for context. Evidence classes kept separate: C&R exposition; base-text translation; secondary positions they report.

## Volume / work synthesis

Ciampa and Rosner's PNTC is the wiki's primary full-letter commentary on 1 Corinthians. Its distinctive contribution is not a novel historical reconstruction of a single Corinthian "party" but a **consistently applied social-values thesis** married to a **biblical/Jewish structural reading**: the letter is neither random pastoral scrap nor primarily a deliberative speech for unity, but a salvation-historical appeal that the cross ends the world's wisdom, that Gentile converts must flee *porneia* and idolatry, and that the Spirit-indwelt temple-community must glorify God until God is all in all.

Against the rhetorical school (Mitchell, Witherington), C&R keep OT templates (Deuteronomy as new-exodus ethics; Isaiah as two-wisdom and herald identity; Malachi 1:11 as worldwide worship) in the load-bearing position. Against overrealized-eschatology or Gnostic master keys, they treat cultural conformity as sufficient explanation for factionalism, lawsuits, sexual license, idol tables, charismatic status-seeking, and resurrection skepticism — without denying local theological distortions where the text requires them.

No inversion-of-source defects were found on the main theses already present (social values; panel structure; temple; OT templates) — those earlier summaries were largely accurate but under-sourced.
