# Moo, *Galatians* (BECNT, 2013)

## What the source is

Douglas J. Moo's *Galatians* is a volume in the **Baker Exegetical Commentary on the New Testament** (BECNT), edited by Robert W. Yarbrough and Robert H. Stein (Baker Academic, 2013; ebook 2013). Moo (PhD, University of St. Andrews) is Kenneth T. Wessner Professor of New Testament at Wheaton College Graduate School; he previously taught more than twenty years at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School and chaired the Committee on Bible Translation for the NIV 2011 revision (work that delayed this commentary). The BECNT format supplies, for each unit: literary structure discussion, author's translation, verse-by-verse exegesis and exposition, additional notes (text-critical and grammatical), and sustained interaction with contemporary scholarship.

Register discipline for this ingest: the **body text** is Moo's own exegesis and exposition; the **Additional Notes** and collected **Notes** carry text-critical and bibliographic detail; the **Introduction** states his positions on authorship, destination/date, agitators, structure, and major theological themes. All interpretive, theological, and historical-reconstruction claims are attributed to Moo (or to the scholars he cites), never promoted to bare wiki voice on the strength of this single source.

## Hermeneutical frame

Moo reads Galatians as a **grammatico-historical** commentary within the BECNT evangelical series frame: author's form-reflecting Greek translation, unit structure, verse-by-verse exposition, Additional Notes for technical problems. Standing interlocutors regularly cited: Lightfoot, Burton, Betz, Bruce, Mussner, R. Longenecker, Dunn, Martyn, de Boer (L214).

**Salvation-historical + apocalyptic (not either/or):** Christ is climax of Abrahamic promise *and* world-rupturing event; Mosaic covenant is temporary subordinate phase (3:15–25); new creation (6:15) and rescue from the present evil age (1:4) supply the eschatological frame.

**Dialogical NPP engagement:** Moo grants salvation-historical and ethnic-inclusion insights (agitators as "nomists," not pure Pelagian legalists) while insisting on a third **anthropological** level — human inability under Torah (esp. 3:10 syllogism; Westerholm: "What is wrong with humanity that Judaism cannot remedy?").

**Theological synthesis:** forensic justification (with future/ultimate vindication prominent in Galatians); objective genitive on *pistis Christou*; union with Christ as more fundamental than justification alone; "law of Christ" as new-covenant ethical demand distinct from Mosaic law.

Body vs apparatus: exposition is Moo's interpretive register; Additional Notes / collected Notes are technical and bibliographic; series/author prefaces state confessional and translation commitments.

## Volume / work synthesis

Moo's BECNT *Galatians* is a sustained **grammatico-historical and salvation-historical** commentary whose distinctive evangelical contribution is a **three-level** account of Paul's anti-Torah argument: (1) the Torah era ends with Christ (salvation history); (2) Torah observance excluded Gentiles (ethnic/social); (3) sinful humanity cannot do the law (anthropology, crystallized in the 3:10 curse syllogism). He grants major NPP insights (covenantal nomism's challenge to crude "legalism" caricatures; agitators as faith-plus-law "nomists") while refusing to let boundary-marker readings erase the Reformers' broader "works" category — *erga nomou* denotes "doing what the law requires," and may function as synecdoche for works generally.

On the letter's historical frame Moo is carefully tentative: slight South Galatian preference without locking early date; weak preference for identifying Gal 2:1–10 with Acts 11's private famine-relief consultation rather than Acts 15; possible ~AD 48 as earliest Pauline letter if the package holds. The autobiographical narrative (1:11–2:14) and the Antioch incident remain the backbone for reconstructing early Jewish–Gentile conflict, with "the truth of the gospel" (2:5, 14) as the criterion that even Cephas can violate.

The theological spine runs from 2:15–21's transitional thesis (justified by faith in Christ, not works of the law; union with the crucified Christ) through Abraham and the temporary *paidagōgos* (3:7–4:7), the Hagar–Sarah appeal (4:21–31), the 5:1–12 climax (circumcision severs from Christ), and the Spirit paraenesis (5:13–6:10), to the concentric closing where cross, **new creation**, and **Israel of God** reframe identity (6:11–18). Load-bearing exegetical decisions densified in this re-ingest: objective genitive *pistis Christou*; *stoicheia* as material elements with associated spirits (associate, do not equate, with law); *paidagōgos* as temporal supervisor not schoolmaster-to-Christ; "law of Christ" as new-covenant gospel ethics via Jesus/apostles + Spirit + love; Israel of God as epexegetic of all who follow the new-creation rule.
