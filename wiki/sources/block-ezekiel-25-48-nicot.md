# Block, *The Book of Ezekiel: Chapters 25–48* (NICOT, Eerdmans 1998)

## What the source is

Volume 2 of Daniel I. Block's two-volume NICOT commentary on Ezekiel, covering Part 2 of
his outline: "Messages of Hope and Restoration for Judah/Israel (25:1–48:35)." Same format
as Vol. 1 ([[block-ezekiel-1-24-nicot|Chapters 1–24]]): fresh translation, verse-by-verse
philological and rhetorical commentary, "Theological Implications" per unit. The publisher's
note states the commentary was written as a single work; the Introduction (in Vol. 1) covers
both volumes. This is a **fidelity re-ingest**: first ingested 2026-06-05 under
pre–Scope-and-Fidelity standards with no source page; Vol. 1 was re-ingested earlier today.

## Hermeneutical frame

Same frame as Vol. 1: evangelical grammatical-historical commentary with heavy ANE philology,
rhetorical criticism, and per-unit "Theological Implications" that shift into an explicitly
Christian homiletical register (the volume ends: "In Jesus the Messiah, the glory of Yahweh,
has descended and dwells among us… He is Immanuel"). Vol. 2 distinctives: proof-saying
(*Erweisworte*) analysis of the OAN; prophetic satire / "intensified reversal" / "literary
cartoon" as genre controls against overliteral reading; text-critical conservatism (MT
defended against emendation, P967, and accretion criticism); the contingency view of
unfulfilled prophecy (29:17–21); the **ideational** hermeneutic for chs. 40–48 ("Where God is,
there is Zion") against both literalist-millenarian and spiritualizing readings; Ezekiel as
second Moses. Three evidence classes kept separate: Block's translation, his commentary, and
the scholars/reception he cites (Zimmerli, Greenberg, Gese, Levenson, Lust, Wellhausen school,
Odell, Milgrom, Stevenson, targumim, b. Sanh. 92b, Theodoret, Revelation).

## Volume / work synthesis

Block reads chs. 25–48 as a single arc: "messages of hope and restoration." The oracles
against the nations (25–32) are "negative messages of hope" — proof sayings announcing
Yahweh's interventions as self-disclosure, structured in two 97-verse halves around the hinge
28:24–26 and saturated with sevens; Babylon is absent because the prophet regards it as "the
sword in Yahweh's hand." The two great foreign blocks end in Sheol (26:19–21; 28:1–19;
31:14–18; 32:17–32), giving Block occasion for the fullest OT netherworld theology he knows:
a demythologized communal cemetery, compartmentalized by nation, class, and honor, with no
eschatological judgment yet — "his Sheol is not to be confused with Gehenna or hell."

Ch. 33 closes the old era (Block insists it belongs logically with chs. 1–24; the OAN are an
editorial buffer), and the salvation oracles then resurrect, pillar by pillar, the four
demolished securities: Yahweh's kingship over his flock with "my servant David" as *nāśîʾ*
(34), the land (35:1–36:15), the name — "the theology of the book reaches its zenith" in
36:16–38's not-for-your-sake vindication and heart/Spirit transplant — the people (37:1–14),
the eternal covenant (37:15–28), and the guarantee against any repetition of 586 (Gog,
38–39). The final vision (40–48) is for Block neither blueprint nor millennial program but an
ideational Torah of divine presence: graded, concentric sanctity around the returned *kābôd*,
a cultic (not Davidic) prince, a cartoon river healing the land, and the closing name-theology
of *yhwh šāmmâ* replacing polluted "Jerusalem."

For the wiki, this ingest adds: the completed Sheol/afterlife dossier that pairs with (and,
via Block's "native Israelite soil" account of resurrection in 37:1–14, independently
converges with) Levenson's anti-Persian-derivation argument on
[[resurrection-of-the-dead]]; a documented four-position controversy on the king of Tyre; the
Gog reception chain that feeds [[two-messiahs]] (Messiah ben Joseph in the targumim and 3
Enoch) and [[millennialism]] (Rev 19–20); the P967 text-critical dispute (Lust vs.
Block/Greenberg); and the 40–48 hermeneutical controversy in which Block's ideational reading
sits against the dispensational-literalist tradition the wiki's evangelical sources elsewhere
represent. Tension with existing content is minimal; where Vol. 1's pages already carried the
four-pillars and kābôd frames, Vol. 2 material completes them (43:1–7 return; Hag 2:3–9
nonreturn as reception datum).
