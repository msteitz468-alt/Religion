## What the source is

Peter H. W. Lau, *Ruth*, New International Commentary on the Old Testament (Grand Rapids:
Eerdmans, 2023) — the replacement volume for Robert L. Hubbard Jr.'s 1988 NICOT Ruth, written
at Hubbard's own invitation. Lau (Sydney Missionary and Bible College; PhD Sydney University on
social identity and ethics in Ruth; former seminary teacher in Southeast Asia; part-time
physician) is an ethnically Chinese Australian scholar. The commentary is notable in the wiki
as the first Megilloth text ingested, the first sustained honor-shame / social-scientific
biblical commentary, and the first authored by a scholar from an Asian background.

Structure: Series Editor's Preface, Author's Preface (with an unusually explicit disclosure of
the author's social locations), List of Abbreviations, Bibliography, **Introduction**, and
**Text and Commentary** in four Acts following Lau's own translation, with philological and
social-scientific notes and footnotes.

## Hermeneutical frame

Lau explicitly braids four methods not previously found together in the ingested commentaries:
(1) narrative-literary analysis (chiasm, concentric framing, point of view, prolepsis,
deliberate ambiguity, U-shaped reversal plot); (2) social-scientific criticism, specifically
collectivist ANE **honor-shame** anthropology; (3) **missional** theology (Ruth as witness to,
instrument of, and equipment for God's mission); and (4) academic **social-identity theory**
(assimilation without erasure). Confessional NICOT convictions frame the whole. Body
(translation + exegesis) is separated from apparatus (footnotes, bibliography) and from the
self-disclosing prefaces.

## Volume / work synthesis

Lau's *Ruth* is the replacement NICOT volume for Robert Hubbard's 1988 commentary, and it reads
Ruth through a fourfold method — narrative-literary, honor-shame social-scientific, missional,
and social-identity — held together by a confessional-evangelical frame. Its governing thesis
is that Ruth narrates the **providential preservation of the family that produced David** and,
inseparably, models a **lifestyle of *ḥesed*** ("God's unceasing providence and kindness
encourage his people to follow a lifestyle of kindness"). Lau's structural signature is the
four-act chiasm (one act per chapter, three scenes each) with a *double pivot* at its center
(Naomi's recognition of the kinsman-redeemer, 2:20; her threshing-floor scheme, 3:1–5) and a
U-shaped honor→shame→higher-honor plot tracked through Naomi. He distinguishes Naomi (the
*central* character, whose emptiness→fullness is the crisis) from Ruth (the *main* character,
who holds the audience's interest) — his most-repeated original move.

Theologically the commentary foregrounds a *hidden* providence: God is named as agent only
twice (breaking the famine, 1:6; granting conception, 4:13 — both acts of fertility, for land
and for womb), the rest of his work signaled through narrative grammar (*wayyiqer miqrehā* "by
chance," 2:3; *hinnēh*; *yippōl dābār*). *ḥesed* (only three occurrences: 1:8; 2:20; 3:10)
governs the whole as a "virtuous feedback loop" running from YHWH through human agents back to
YHWH; the deliberately ambiguous antecedent at 2:20 (Boaz? YHWH? both) is, for Lau, the book's
central theological claim that divine and human kindness are inseparable. On law, Lau argues
Ruth models an *expansive*, spirit-over-letter application: Boaz exceeds the gleaning, redemption,
and (alluded) levirate customs, driven by *ḥesed*; and the Deut 23 (Moabite exclusion) and
Deut 7 (intermarriage) prohibitions do not bar Ruth because their underlying *principles*
(hospitality; exclusive devotion to YHWH) are met. On identity, he applies social-identity
theory: Ruth achieves *assimilation without erasure* — "Ruth the Moabite" ×7, still "the
Moabite" on Boaz's own lips at 4:5, 10.

What the source adds to the wiki: the first Megilloth text; the first sustained honor-shame and
social-identity-theory commentary; a methodologically self-aware, postcolonially and missionally
attuned evangelical reading. It coheres with, and sharpens, the wiki's existing *ḥesed*,
kinsman-redeemer, and Boaz-as-*gōʾēl*-type material. It stands in a productive tension with the
wiki's **[[hecker-zohar-pritzker-vol11|Zoharic *Midrash ha-Neʿlam* on Ruth]]** (Pritzker XI):
where Lau reads Ruth as narrative-historical *ḥesed* and honor-shame reversal, the Zoharic
register reads the same book as concealed soul-architecture and afterlife midrash — the two
registers are kept attributed and separable on [[book-of-ruth|the text page]], not collapsed.
On dating Lau lands, cautiously, on a *marginally monarchic* date against the current majority
postexilic consensus, and he rejects the majority "anti-Ezra/Nehemiah polemic" purpose — both
recorded as positions, not adjudicated in wiki voice.
