# The Epistles of John — I. Howard Marshall (NICNT, 1978)

## What the source is

I. Howard Marshall, *The Epistles of John*, New International Commentary on the New Testament
(NICNT), Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1978. A single-author evangelical verse-by-verse commentary on
1, 2, and 3 John by the Aberdeen New Testament scholar, written at the invitation of series
editor F.F. Bruce. English-text base with sustained engagement of Greek in the footnotes and
critical dialogue with Continental scholarship (Schnackenburg, Bultmann, Haenchen, Klein).

## Hermeneutical frame

Marshall reads the Epistles with **evangelical historical-grammatical exegesis** joined to a
consistent **pastoral-theological** orientation, mediating Continental (largely German)
scholarship — Schnackenburg above all, plus Bultmann, Haenchen, Klein, Conzelmann, Weiss — for
English readers without being captured by any one school. His method is to reconstruct the
concrete historical situation behind each letter, canvass the range of scholarly positions,
adjudicate cautiously (frequently conceding possibilities he does not finally adopt), and close
each unit with a homiletical application. He treats what the text *says* as distinct from what it
*presupposes* (e.g. the resurrection, unmentioned but assumed).

Distinctive load-bearing judgments recorded on the content pages: (1) he **leaves the
apostle-vs-elder authorship question genuinely open** while holding common authorship of all
three Epistles as "overwhelmingly probable"; (2) he reads the sinlessness paradox (3:6, 9)
**eschatologically** ("'Become what you are'"), *rejecting* the habitual/present-continuous
reading popular among British commentators; (3) he defends *hilasmos* as **propitiation-plus-
expiation** (Morris/Hill vs. Dodd), with God himself providing the propitiation; (4) he reads
the opponents' error via the **present-continuous participle** of 2 John 7 as a *separationist*
christology (the heavenly Christ withdrawing before the cross), closest to Cerinthus/Basilides
but not exactly identifiable; and (5) he judges the **Johannine Comma** (5:7–8) a scribal gloss
"beyond any shadow of doubt."

The introductions, apparatus, and body are treated as one authorial voice (single-author
commentary), distinct from the RSV base text and from the scholars Marshall quotes.

## Volume synthesis

Marshall's *Epistles of John* is a compact, judicious, single-author evangelical commentary whose
enduring value lies less in novelty than in the reliability of its adjudications. Written for the
NICNT at F.F. Bruce's invitation, it takes the three letters as the product of "the elder" — an
authoritative figure whose precise identity (the apostle John, or a follower known by the same
title) Marshall refuses to settle — writing from Asia Minor between the 60s and 90s to a community
fractured by a secession. The organizing exegetical problem throughout is the **secessionist
crisis**: opponents who had left the community (2:19), denied that Jesus is the Christ come and
remaining in the flesh (2:22; 4:2; 2 John 7), denied the saving necessity of his death ("water
*and* blood," 5:6), claimed sinlessness and a superior Spirit-anointing, and failed in brotherly
love. Marshall reads this as an incipient, pre-systematic Docetism closest to Cerinthus but not
exactly identifiable — a position the wiki carries on [[johannine-secessionists]].

Against the wiki's already-dense Johannine cluster, the fidelity re-read's most important payoff
was a **correction**: the original 2026-06-05 batch had attributed to Marshall the
habitual/present-continuous resolution of the sinlessness paradox (3:6, 9). The sequential read
shows Marshall explicitly *rejects* that reading as a "grammatical subtlety" and adopts instead an
**eschatological** construal — the sinlessness of the Age to Come, an indicative that is
"logically an imperative" ("'Become what you are'"). This now stands corrected on both the
commentator and text pages, with the habitual reading reassigned to Kruse (a genuine
Marshall–Kruse divergence, not a shared view). Two further exegetical signatures were folded in:
Marshall's **propitiation** reading of *hilasmos* (with the Denney line that love and propitiation
are inseparable, and the insistence that God himself provides the propitiation), which now links
the Johannine and Pauline atonement material on [[atonement]]; and his flat verdict against the
**Johannine Comma**, now recorded on [[testimony-martyria]].

Relative to [[kruse-letters-of-john|Kruse (PNTC, 2020)]] — the wiki's other ingested commentary on
these letters — Marshall is the earlier, leaner, more Continental-facing work; Kruse cites him
extensively, adds two decades of scholarship, 24 excursuses, and a rhetorical (epideictic)
analysis Marshall lacks. The two agree on apostolic-friendly authorship, the literal eyewitness
reading of 1:1–4, propitiation over expiation, and the ecclesiastical (non-heretical) reading of
Diotrephes; they diverge on the sinlessness paradox and on the precise referent of "water" in 5:6.
The ingest also filled two genuine gaps — figure pages for **Diotrephes** and **Gaius**, the
corporate actors of 3 John — while leaving **Cerinthus** as a flagged gap (a heresiarch mentioned
across ~36 pages who warrants a dedicated figure/heresy page built from patristic sources beyond
Marshall).
