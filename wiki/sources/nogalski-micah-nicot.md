## Hermeneutical frame

Nogalski reads Micah as a **multi-phase scribal-prophetic collection** shaped for the Book of the Twelve:

1. **Redaction criticism** — three developmental phases (early core 1–3; exilic 6–7 + ch. 1 updates; postexilic 4–5 anthology), with 6–7 entering before 4–5.
2. **Book-of-the-Four / Twelve intertextuality** — Hosea–Amos–Micah–Zephaniah relative order; Mic as Jonah–Nahum linchpin on Assyria; Exod 34:6–7 web; catchword and curse chains.
3. **Final-form literary analysis** — formula groups in 4–5; lawsuit drama in 6; liturgical speaker map in 7:8–20; judgment–hope vacillation as structural theology.
4. **Philology and text criticism** — own translation; selective apparatus; MT stability thesis for Micah.
5. **Social ethics** — elite land seizure, paid prophecy/priesthood, Omride cipher; 6:8 as lawsuit indictment, not free-floating slogan.

Register note: all interpretive, philological, redactional, and theological claims on wiki pages drawn from this volume are **Nogalski’s** (or scholars he reports) and stay in **attributed voice**.

## Volume synthesis

Nogalski’s Micah volume completes his NICOT treatment of the middle of the Twelve (with the 2023 Joel/Obadiah/Jonah book). The argument is that **Micah is both an eighth-century social-justice witness and a multi-century theodicy-and-hope machine** for Judah after 587 and into the Persian period. The early core (chs. 1–3) still delivers the sharpest Southern-prophet land-and-leadership critique in the corpus, climaxing at 3:12 — which the Masoretes, whether by design or discovery, marked as the exact middle of the Twelve scroll. From that midpoint the book (and the collection) bends toward eschatological peace (4:1–5 // Isa 2), remnant gathering, Davidic hope from Bethlehem, and a lawsuit-liturgy sequence that ends by asking the prophet’s own name as a theological question: who is a God like YHWH, forgiving the remnant?

What the volume adds to the wiki beyond the 2026-06-05 sketch is **method**: (1) a documented three-phase growth model with 6–7 before 4–5; (2) a disciplined refusal to settle Mic // Isa priority while insisting on functional difference; (3) 6:8 relocated from poster slogan to lawsuit rejoinder and Book-of-the-Four intertext; (4) 7:18–20 wired into the Exod 34:6–7 and Jonah–Nahum architecture of the Twelve; (5) manuscript stability claims grounded in Qumran and 8Ḥev 1. Tensions with already-ingested sources are mostly complementary: [[carroll-amos-nicot|Carroll on Amos]] supplies the Northern social-justice counterpart with a more synchronic final-form bias; Nogalski’s redactional Twelve frame is the explicit counterpart to his own Joel-as-literary-anchor thesis on the 2023 volume. The collection’s bias remains strong on critical–evangelical NICOT Twelve scholarship and thin on classical Jewish medieval Micah commentary and full tafsir-style reception.
