## What the source is

Bruce K. Waltke's *The Book of Proverbs: Chapters 1–15* (New International Commentary on the
Old Testament; Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2004) is the first of two volumes — among the most
technically ambitious commentaries in the NICOT series, representing over 25 years of research.
Volume 1 contains the full Introduction (title, text and versions, structure, ANE wisdom,
authorship, forms/poetics, theology, bibliography) and the Text and Commentary through
Collection IIA (10:1–15:29). Waltke supplies his own translation with detailed text-critical
notes, then comment proceeding through poetic units.

This is a **re-ingest** consolidating and deepening the 2026-06 first pass (which filed Waltke
only on the commentator and text pages without a source page or coverage ledger). This page
supplies the missing source-page apparatus and records the fidelity re-read of Vol. 1.

## Hermeneutical frame

Waltke's method (as stated in his Introduction and enacted in the commentary):
1. **Grammatico-historical exegesis** grounded in Hebrew philology and poetics — each proverb
   interpreted "on its own two feet," then in its literary unit, then in canonical context.
2. **Poetics prior to hermeneutics** — the grammar of literature (terseness, parallelism,
   concentric/chiastic structure) precedes the grammar of meaning.
3. **ANE comparative contextualization** — Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Ugaritic parallels used to
   illuminate form and setting, but theologically controlled ("pagan wisdom has no anchor in
   the covenant-God").
4. **Reformed canonical/biblical theology** — Proverbs read within a unified revelation; the
   prologue (Collection I) is the non-negotiable hermeneutical key.
5. **Typological Christology** — Woman Wisdom as a *type* of Christ (not a direct
   identification, not a hypostasis).

Register discipline for this ingest: Waltke's interpretive and theological claims are
attributed to him (or to the scholars he engages — von Rad, Koch, Kitchen, McKane, Delitzsch,
Fox); text-critical and structural facts he reports are recorded as his findings.

## Volume synthesis

Waltke's first volume is, in the wiki's holdings, the most technically ambitious single
commentary on any wisdom text and the fullest worked example of grammatico-historical method
disciplined by canonical-theological conviction. Its governing move is to make **poetics prior
to hermeneutics** — one must know *how* a proverb means (terseness, parallelism, and editorial
patterning up a hierarchy from sounds to collections) before asking *what* it means — and then
to read the whole through the prologue (Collection I) as a "hermeneutical key" that forbids
reading the later aphorisms as free-standing pragmatism. The Introduction's most consequential
argument is negative: against von Rad, Koch, Gese, and Crenshaw (and, he confesses, his own
earlier self), Waltke denies that Proverbs teaches a self-revealing cosmic order (*ma'at*)
discoverable by natural theology. The book's epistemology rests instead on [[fear-of-the-lord|the
fear of the LORD]] — special revelation through inspired spokespersons — and its personified
[[woman-wisdom|Woman Wisdom]] is a personification of Solomon's *revealed* wisdom, not of order
in creation.

For the wiki this volume most sharpens three things. First, it supplies a robustly *personal*
account of the [[doctrine-of-retribution|deed-consequence nexus]] — "personal divine retribution,"
not an impersonal mechanism — that stands as a constructive complement to Hartley's
interrogation of retribution in Job: the two NICOT volumes press the same problem (why the moral
order does not visibly balance) toward different but compatible resolutions, Hartley by
correcting misapplication from the side of the suffering righteous, Waltke by deferring
vindication into a future that "may lie beyond clinical death." Second, it commits Proverbs to
an implied **immortality of the righteous** (the *'al-māwet* reading of 12:28, plus 14:32; 15:24)
while insisting that Sheol/Mot/*repā'îm* language is "broken mythology" on which no doctrine of
the intermediate state can be built — a careful register-discipline worth propagating to other
Sheol-bearing texts. Third, its treatment of the unchaste wife as an **apostate covenant member**
(not a foreign goddess or cult prostitute) whose infidelity paradigmatically figures apostasy
against YHWH gives the wiki its most developed reading of the Woman Wisdom / Woman Folly
antithesis.

A standing tension with other ingested wisdom sources should be noted rather than smoothed: the
[[wisdom-literature|Wisdom Literature]] overview's characterization of the genre as proceeding
"by observation... rather than by appeals to revelation" is precisely what Waltke's inspiration
thesis contests for Proverbs. Both are held as positions. Waltke's confessional and text-critical
commitments (Solomonic authorship defended on Kitchen's structural grounds; MT priority; a
typological Christology reading Woman Wisdom as a type of Christ) are recorded throughout in his
attributed voice, not promoted to wiki voice.
