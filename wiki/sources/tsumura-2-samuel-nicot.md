# Tsumura, *The Second Book of Samuel* (NICOT, Eerdmans 2019)

## What the source is

Second and concluding volume of David Toshio Tsumura’s NICOT commentary on 1–2 Samuel.
Evangelical critical commentary: comparatively literal MT-based translation, verse-by-verse
philological and discourse-grammatical notes, ANE comparative material. Series editor
R. L. Hubbard; originally commissioned by R. K. Harrison for Semitic philology on a difficult
Hebrew text. Continues and defends the 2006 1 Samuel method (MT-first, discourse grammar,
DH skepticism, early Divided Monarchy audience).

## Hermeneutical frame

Attributed to Tsumura (Preface + Intro):

1. **MT-first** — Many “corrupt” readings are phonetic spellings; DSS/LXX/MT relations are not one-sided; *lectio difficilior*.
2. **Synchronic priority** before diachronic reconstruction; critique of uncontrolled intertextuality (Lyke/Bakhtin on Tekoa).
3. **Discourse grammar** — *wayqtl* initial vs sequential; SETTING/EVENT/TERMINUS; *weqtl* procedural (esp. 2 Sam 7).
4. **Genre** — Intermediate between epic (Judges) and annalistic (Kings); not David’s biography.
5. **Dating** — Early Divided Monarchy / Rehoboam (with 1 Sam 27:6); eternal dynasty not postexilic.
6. **Theology** — Davidic covenant central; “human desires and divine plans”; providence through monarchy despite sin; messianic trajectory.

## Volume synthesis

Tsumura’s 2 Samuel volume is the theological culmination of his Samuel project. Where
1 Samuel establishes the principle that the king is subject to the prophet, 2 Samuel shows
how God keeps an **unconditional** dynastic promise through a king who fails spectacularly.
The stated purpose is not a political manual of monarchy but the narrative of how God led
David’s life “despite his grave sins” in order to provide heirs — finally *the* heir — and
an eternal throne.

Methodologically the volume doubles down after criticism of the 1 Samuel book: MT can often
be read against easy LXX/4QSam emendation; synchronic linguistic work must precede redaction
hypotheses; genre uniqueness of Samuel (epic/annalistic intermediate) undercuts DH-driven
homogenization of Joshua–Kings. The most distinctive technical contribution remains the
**discourse partition of Nathan’s oracle** (2 Sam 7:8b–16) into past procedural and future
predictive speech, resolving the “rest” tension without multi-layer surgery.

Literarily, Tsumura’s AXB reading of Bathsheba inside the Ammon war and the concentric
**ABXX′B′A′** Epilogues (Song of David and Last Words at the center) give the book a clear
architecture: covenant at the middle of David’s reign, poetic *ḥesed*-to-the-anointed at the
close, temple site purchased through the census disaster. The Absalom cycle enacts “sword
from your house” without cancelling *ḥesed*.

**Tensions (not adjudicated):** MT-preservation vs McCarter/Cross eclectic reconstruction;
early composition vs late Dtr eternal-dynasty theories; Tsumura’s restraint on literary
“depth” vs Chapman/Alter-style narrative criticism (Tsumura invites literary work *after*
text fixation). Alter’s David Story remains a complementary synchronic reading already on
the text page.
