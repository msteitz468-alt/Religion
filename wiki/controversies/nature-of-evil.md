# The Nature of Evil: Privation vs. Real-but-Derivative Power

A standing dispute over the *ontological status* of evil, sharpened by the contrast between
the Aristotelian-rationalist tradition and the Kabbalistic-Hasidic stream that
[[abraham-joshua-heschel|Heschel]] draws on.

## Position 1 — Evil as Privation

In the [[maimonides|Maimonidean]]/Aristotelian (and parallel Christian Thomist) account,
evil has **no positive ontological status**: it is lack, absence, the privation of a due
good, rooted in matter. See [[evil-as-privation]]. On this view evil is not "created" and is
in principle reducible to non-being.

## Position 2 — Evil as Real, Active, and Parasitic on the Holy

In [[god-in-search-of-man|*God in Search of Man*]] (Part III, ch. 36), Heschel rejects the
privation account: evil is "real, mighty and tempting," and — drawing on the Zohar — is
*sustained by a holy spark within it*: "there is a holy spark of God even in the dark
recesses of evil. If not for that spark, evil would lose its power and reality." Good and
evil are intermingled at the root of creation ("from good issues evil"), figured by the
Ezekiel *nogah* (the brightness surrounding the darkness). Yet evil is "an instrument rather
than an iron wall; a temptation … rather than an ultimate power" — not an independent dualist
principle either. Heschel's further moves:

- The deeper problem is evil's **disguise within the good**: "the most horrible manifestation
  of evil is when it acts in the guise of good."
- The biblical answer to evil "is not the good but the holy"; the antidote to the *yetser
  hara* is the Torah and the [[mitzvah]].
- The conquest of evil's dominion is **eschatological**: "at the end of days, evil will be
  conquered by the One; in historic times, evils must be conquered one by one."

## Note

The two positions are not merely degrees of the same view: privation makes evil
*ontologically nothing*, while Heschel's Zoharic account makes it *something* whose very
power depends on a residue of holiness. Recorded without adjudication per the wiki's
contradiction protocol; the dispute is tradition-internal to Judaism (rationalist vs.
mystical streams) and runs parallel to Christian debates over privation.
