# The Crucifixion of Jesus — Qur'an 4:157 vs. the Gospels

The single sharpest factual contradiction between [[islam|Islam]] and [[christianity|Christianity]],
and the flagship cross-tradition controversy the *Needed Sources.md* plan anticipated. It bears
directly on the central Christian claim, since for Christianity the crucified-and-risen Christ is the
heart of the gospel ([[theology-of-the-cross|1 Cor 15:3–4]]).

## The Texts

- **[[quran|Qur'an]] 4:157–158** (Pickthall): "And because of their saying: We slew the Messiah, Jesus
  son of Mary, Allah's messenger — they slew him not nor crucified him, but it appeared so unto them…
  they slew him not for certain, but Allah took him up unto Himself."
- **The Gospels** narrate the crucifixion under Pontius Pilate as a public historical event
  ([[mark|Mark 15]]; [[gospel-of-john|John 19]]), the basis of the resurrection proclamation
  ([[jesus|Jesus]]; [[lamb-of-god|the Lamb]]).

## Positions

- **Substitutionist (mainstream Sunni tafsir)** — God rescued [[jesus|Isa]] and another was made to
  *appear* as him and was crucified in his place (variously identified as Judas, Simon of Cyrene, or a
  volunteer disciple). The Jews and Romans were deceived; Isa was raised bodily to heaven and will
  return.
- **Non-death / "it only appeared"** — Q 4:157's *shubbiha lahum* ("it was made to appear to them")
  is read as denying the *death*, not necessarily positing a substitute; Isa did not die on the cross.
- **Ahmadi survival theory** — the Ahmadiyya hold that Jesus survived the cross (a swoon) and later
  died a natural death; a minority, non-mainstream reading.
- **Christian historical crucifixion** — the crucifixion is among the best-attested events of
  antiquity (multiple Gospels, Paul, Tacitus, Josephus); its denial is theologically driven. For
  Christianity the cross is not incidental but the saving act.
- **Docetic resonance** — the substitutionist reading structurally echoes 2nd-century
  [[gnosticism|Gnostic]]/Docetic claims that Christ only *seemed* to suffer (Basilides reportedly had
  Simon of Cyrene crucified in Jesus' place), a parallel noted without claiming dependence.

## Why It Matters

This is not a difference of emphasis but a contradiction of fact and of soteriology: Islam honors
Jesus as a sinless prophet and Messiah yet denies the crucifixion that Christianity makes the center
of salvation. It connects to the deeper divergence over Jesus' divinity ([[tawhid|tawhid]] vs.
[[logos-christology|Logos Christology]]) and over the corruption (*tahrif*) of the [[gospel-of-john|
Injil]]. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
