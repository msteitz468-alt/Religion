# Tao-chia / Tao-chiao: The False Philosophical/Religious Dichotomy

## The Issue

For most of the 20th century, both Chinese and Western scholars of Taoism operated with a
fundamental distinction:

- ***Tao-chia*** ("Taoist school of thought" / "philosophical Taoism"): the authentic, profound
  tradition of the *Tao te ching* and *Chuang-tzu*; its sages were proto-naturalists, mystics,
  or proto-anarchists who anticipated Enlightenment values; this is the "real" Taoism
- ***Tao-chiao*** ("Taoist religion" / "religious Taoism"): the organized religion from the
  2nd century CE onward; its priests, ritual, alchemy, and immortality-seeking were a degraded,
  "superstitious" departure from the pure classical wisdom; disreputable and not worth serious study

This dichotomy was so deeply entrenched that most introductions to Taoism (including the
wiki's initial pages, drawing on older sources) treated it as self-evident.

## Position 1: The Traditional Dichotomy

The traditional position — held by scholars such as Fung Yu-lan, H.G. Creel, Holmes Welch, and
many others — holds that:
- The *Tao te ching* and *Chuang-tzu* represent a genuine classical philosophical tradition
- The organized religion that arose under the "Heavenly Masters" from the 2nd century was a
  different phenomenon that merely borrowed the name "Taoist"
- The classical tradition's "true heirs" were Ch'an/Zen Buddhism, not the Taoist priests
- "Serious" Confucian and Buddhist thinkers knew better than to be misled by Taoist superstition

**Sources for this position**: Fung Yu-lan's *History of Chinese Philosophy*; Creel's essay
(1956) attacking Maspero; Welch's *Taoism: The Parting of the Way*

**Historical basis**: This view was itself largely a product of Confucian ideology: Confucian
scholars from the 2nd millennium CE systematically denigrated Taoism as "gross" and "grotesque"
while acknowledging only the classical texts as marginally philosophically respectable. Western
Enlightenment values (naturalism, anti-ritualism, hostility to "superstition") amplified these
Confucian prejudices when Western sinologists encountered the material.

## Position 2: Kirkland's Revisionism

[[russell-kirkland|Russell Kirkland]] (*Taoism: The Enduring Tradition*, Routledge 2004) argues
that the entire tao-chia / tao-chiao dichotomy is a **modern scholarly fabrication** with no
basis in the actual history of Chinese religion:

1. **No one ever called themselves a practitioner of *tao-chia***: The term *tao-chia* was
   a Han bibliographic category invented to classify ancient texts, not a label any group
   of people used to identify themselves as a religious community.

2. **The classical texts do NOT represent a coherent "school"**: The *Tao te ching* and
   *Chuang-tzu* were not produced by a single community with a common self-identity. The
   *Tao te ching* grew through multiple stages of redaction; the *Chuang-tzu* was assembled
   by Kuo Hsiang (4th c. CE) from a larger and more diverse collection. To call these texts
   the product of "the philosophical Taoists" is to project backwards a coherence that never existed.

3. **The continuous thread is biospiritual cultivation, not "wu-wei philosophy"**: What
   actually persists across all periods and communities of self-identified Taoists is the
   practical tradition of [[biospiritual-cultivation|biospiritual cultivation]] — *ch'i*,
   *ching*, *shen* — which runs from the [[nei-yeh|Nei-yeh]] through T'ien-shih, Shang-ch'ing,
   Ch'üan-chen, and Lung-men. The wu-wei / naturalism emphasis Western scholars fetishized is
   a minority strand of one text.

4. **The "philosophical" reading was also soteriological**: Wang Bi's philosophical commentary
   was itself a form of "religious" reading — the commentary was transformative practice
   (cf. CLAUDE.md hermeneutical framework), not neutral metaphysical analysis. There is no
   clean break between "philosophical" and "religious" reading.

5. **The organized religion was NOT degenerate**: T'ang "literati Taoism" produced
   sophisticated texts and practices, attracted the political and intellectual elite, and
   was patronized by emperors as an expression of the highest Chinese values. Calling it
   degraded is simply applying Confucian ideology.

## Implications for the Wiki

The philosophical-daoism and religious-daoism pages in `wiki/traditions/daoism/sects/` were
created using the traditional dichotomy before Kirkland's challenge was ingested. These pages
need to be understood as **representations of a contested framework** rather than accepted
categories. The actual sect pages created after Kirkland (heavenly-masters, shang-ching,
ling-pao, quanzhen, cheng-i) represent historically attested Taoist communities.

## Current Status

Kirkland's position, while provocative, reflects a scholarly consensus that has been building
since the 1980s through the work of scholars like Anna Seidel, Michel Strickmann, and Stephen
Bokenkamp. The extreme form of the traditional dichotomy (Creel's "immortality cult stole
the noble title of 'Taoism'") is now largely discredited. The question of what, if anything,
distinguishes a "philosophical" from a "religious" Taoist reading remains actively debated.
