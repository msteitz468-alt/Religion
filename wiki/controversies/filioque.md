# The Filioque (Procession of the Holy Spirit)

The **Filioque** ("and the Son") is the Latin addition to the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed
asserting that the Holy Spirit proceeds "from the Father *and the Son*." It is one of the principal
doctrinal disputes between Western ([[catholicism|Catholic]]) and Eastern
([[eastern-orthodoxy|Orthodox]]) Christianity and a contributing factor in the East-West schism of
1054.

## Aquinas's defense (Summa Theologiae I, Q36)

[[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]] argues that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father **and the Son** on
three grounds (Q36, A2):
1. **Personal distinction.** The persons are distinguished only by relations of opposition. Apart
   from procession from the Son, the Son and the Holy Spirit would have no opposed relation and so
   could not be distinct persons.
2. **Order of intellect and will.** Since love proceeds from a word (the will follows the
   intellect), the Spirit (Love) must proceed from the Son (the Word) as well as the Father — see
   [[trinity]].
3. **Order of nature.** Where several proceed from one principle, they must be ordered to one
   another; the Spirit's order to the Son is that it proceeds from Him.

## Engagement with the Eastern position

Aquinas engages the Greek view directly (Q36, A2, ad 3; A3). He acknowledges that the Greeks concede
the Spirit is "the Spirit of the Son" and proceeds "**through** the Son" (*per Filium*), but resist
saying "proceeds **from** the Son" (*ex Filio*). He treats the two formulas as ultimately
reconcilable — "through the Son" implies "from the Son" — and attributes Damascene's denial of
procession from the Son to Nestorian influence, holding it "not to be held." On the propriety of the
**creedal addition** itself, Aquinas defends the Latin insertion as legitimate, made by the
authority of the pope to make explicit what was already implicit, against the charge that it violated
the conciliar prohibition on altering the Creed.

## The Eastern Orthodox position

The Orthodox objection is twofold and recorded here without adjudication: (1) **doctrinal** — the
Father is the sole *arche* (source/cause) within the Godhead; the Spirit proceeds from the Father
alone (*ek monou tou Patros*), though "through the Son" may be admitted as describing the temporal
manifestation or the eternal "shining forth," not the eternal *procession of being*; (2)
**canonical** — the unilateral Western insertion of *Filioque* into a creed promulgated by an
ecumenical council, without conciliar authority, is itself illegitimate regardless of the doctrine's
merits. (Full Orthodox sources — Photius, Palamas — to be ingested; this section is provisional.)
