Two linked textual problems attach to the Philistine champion of [[1-samuel|1 Samuel 17]]: **who
killed him**, and **how tall he was**. Both arise from divergences *within* the canon and between
its textual witnesses, which is why the dispute is classified here as `source-reliability` rather
than doctrinal. This page records the positions; it does not adjudicate them.

## The slayer

The problem is internal to the Hebrew Bible and does not depend on any modern critical premise.

- **1 Sam 17** narrates at length that [[david|David]], the youngest son of Jesse, kills
  [[goliath|Goliath]] of Gath with a sling and stone and beheads him with the champion's own sword.
- **2 Sam 21:19 (MT)** states, in a list of Philistine giants killed by David's warriors, that
  *[[elhanan|Elhanan]] son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite killed Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose
  spear was like a weaver's beam*.
- **1 Chr 20:5** reports the same episode but says Elhanan killed *Lahmi the brother of Goliath*.

**Positions recorded:**

| Position | Held by / basis |
|---|---|
| Chronicles preserves the original; 2 Sam 21:19 is a scribal corruption in which "Lahmi the brother of" dropped out | Common in confessional and many text-critical treatments; treats MT Samuel as damaged here |
| 2 Sam 21:19 preserves the older tradition; the Goliath duel was later attached to David | Common in historical-critical reconstruction; treats Chronicles as the harmonization |
| Elhanan is a personal name of David (throne-name/birth-name pairing) | A traditional harmonization; noted in rabbinic and later Christian exegesis |
| Two distinct Philistines named Goliath | Minority harmonizing option |

The wiki records all four. Note that the first two positions are *mirror images*: each treats the
other's preferred witness as the secondary text. Which one is "the corruption" is precisely what
is in dispute, and the manuscript evidence alone does not settle it.

## The height

- **MT 1 Sam 17:4** gives Goliath's height as **six cubits and a span** (~9 ft 9 in / ~2.9 m).
- **LXX (Codex Vaticanus), 4QSam-a from the Qumran finds, and Josephus** give
  **four cubits and a span** (~6 ft 9 in / ~2.0 m).

The shorter reading is attested by the oldest Hebrew manuscript evidence available for the verse
and by the earliest translation; the longer reading is the received Masoretic text. Positions
divide over whether the MT reflects an inflating tradition or the versions reflect a rationalizing
one. A four-cubit Goliath is still an extraordinary height for the period, so the narrative's
force does not depend on resolving this.

## Why it is not adjudicated here

Per the [[index|wiki's]] Contradiction Protocol, positions are preserved in their strongest form
with attribution rather than resolved. The collection currently holds
[[tsumura-1-samuel-nicot|Tsumura's NICOT 1 Samuel]] and
[[tsumura-2-samuel-nicot|NICOT 2 Samuel]], which favor a text-critically conservative,
MT-first approach; no ingested source in this collection argues the historical-critical
reconstruction at length. That is a **coverage gap, not a verdict** — the imbalance in the
wiki's sources should not be mistaken for a scholarly consensus.
