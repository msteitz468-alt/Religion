# Scientology — Religion, Commercial Enterprise, or Cult?

The central controversy about [[scientology|Scientology]] is **what it is** — and the answer has differed by
country, court, and observer. Because Scientology charges fixed "**fixed donations**" for [[auditing|
auditing]] and training along the **Bridge**, and tightly controls its members and its "tech," its status as
a *religion* is genuinely contested in a way few traditions are.

## Positions

- **Bona fide religion.** Scientology presents itself as a religion with scripture ("Source"), clergy (the
  Sea Org), and a soteriology (freeing the [[thetan|thetan]]). The **US IRS granted it tax-exempt religious
  status in 1993** after a long battle, and several countries recognize it as a religion.
- **Commercial enterprise / "therapy for profit."** Critics (and some courts) argue it is essentially a
  **business** selling self-improvement services, using religious classification for tax and legal
  advantage. Several European states (notably **Germany** and **France**) have refused religious recognition;
  French courts convicted Church entities of **organized fraud** (2009/2013).
- **Harmful / coercive cult.** Ex-members and journalists describe **coercive control** — the **disconnection**
  policy (cutting off critical family/friends), **"Fair Game"** (harassment of "enemies"), heavy litigation,
  Sea Org labor conditions, and the 1995 death of **Lisa McPherson** — arguing the organization is abusive
  regardless of its doctrinal claims.
- **New religious movement (scholarly-neutral).** Religious-studies scholars generally treat Scientology as
  a **new religious movement** worth studying on its own terms, bracketing the value judgment — the stance
  this wiki adopts (Contradiction Protocol: record the positions, do not adjudicate).

## Why It Matters

Scientology is a live test case for the **definition of "religion"** itself — exposing how much that
category does (legal protection, tax status) and how contested its boundaries are. It also illustrates the
wiki's broader pattern of disputed identity (cf. "are the [[latter-day-saints|Latter-day Saints]]
Christian?"), here pushed to the question of whether a movement is a *religion at all*.
