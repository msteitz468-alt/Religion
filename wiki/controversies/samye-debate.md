# The Samyé Debate

The **Samyé (bSam yas) debate** — traditionally dated to the late 8th century at Samyé monastery
under King Trisong Detsen — is the paradigmatic Tibetan controversy over how awakening is attained:
by gradual cultivation of analytical wisdom and method, or by a sudden, thought-free realization. It
pits the Indian gradualist **[[kamalasila|Kamalaśīla]]** against the Chinese Chan master
**[[heshang-moheyan|Hva-shang Moheyan]]**. The Tibetan tradition records a victory for Kamalaśīla and
the gradual–analytical path; the "Hva-shang position" thereafter became the standard foil for
quietism.

## The Two Positions

- **Hva-shang Moheyan (subitist/quietist):** since *all* conceptuality — virtuous or not —
  reifies and binds, the path is to stop all mental activity and rest in thought-free,
  non-conceptual absorption; analysis and the graded practices are for the dull. (As reported by his
  opponents.)
- **Kamalaśīla (gradualist/analytical):** genuine **[[samatha-vipassana|insight]]** is produced by
  *correct analytical discrimination*; "the root of the sublime wisdom that knows reality is correct
  analytical discrimination." To dispense with analysis is to "entirely abandon the Mahāyāna," which
  requires the union of **method and wisdom**. His three *Stages of Meditation* (*Bhāvanākrama*) were
  composed in this connection.

## Tsong-kha-pa's Use of the Debate

Six centuries later, [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] makes the refutation of "the system of the Chinese
abbot Ha-shang" a structural thread of the [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]] insight section (Vol.
III), explicitly siding with Kamalaśīla. He invokes it at three levels:

- in the **serenity** section, to deny that any non-discursive, blissful, or clear state is by itself
  meditation on emptiness;
- in the **over-negation** discussion, equating the refutation of *all* conceptuality with Ha-shang's
  rejected quietism;
- in chapter 25 ("Insight Requires Analysis"), his fullest anti-quietist argument: one must
  distinguish **not thinking of** true existence (mere cessation of mental activity) from **knowing
  the absence of** true existence (positive ascertainment by reasoning) — only the latter is the
  antidote to ignorance. The famous **fire-from-two-sticks** image (via Kamalaśīla and the *Kāśyapa
  Chapter Sūtra*): analytical discrimination kindles the nonconceptual wisdom that then consumes the
  analysis itself.

## Status

**Tradition-specific.** The historicity, format, and outcome of the 8th-century event are debated by
modern scholarship; what is firm is the doctrinal use the later Tibetan tradition — and Tsong-kha-pa
in particular — made of the contrast. Per the [Contradiction Protocol], the positions are recorded,
not adjudicated.
