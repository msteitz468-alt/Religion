# The Burial of Jesus: Joseph of Arimathea and the Empty Tomb

Whether [[jesus-of-nazareth|Jesus]] received a known, decent **burial** (by
[[joseph-of-arimathea|Joseph of Arimathea]], in an identifiable tomb later found empty) is
disputed between confessional and historical-critical readings. [[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]],
who reports having "used to think" the empty tomb "one of the most secure historical data,"
argues in [[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]] that it cannot be
established — distinguishing this question sharply from the
[[resurrection-appearances-visions|nature of the resurrection appearances]].

## Position A — Traditional / Apologetic

Jesus was buried by Joseph of Arimathea in a rock-hewn tomb (Mark 15:43–46); the empty tomb
was discovered by women. Apologists argue: women as witnesses would not be invented (their
testimony was legally weak); the disciples' willingness to die confirms they saw something;
opponents could have produced the body. Affirmed across Christian confessional traditions.

## Position B — Ehrman (historical-critical)

We cannot establish a decent, known burial, for three reasons: (1) Roman crucifixion
practice normally **left bodies on the cross** for scavengers (Ehrman marshals Horace,
Juvenal, Artemidorus, Petronius, the historian Martin Hengel); (2) criminals were commonly
denied burial / thrown into common graves (Diodorus, Dio Chrysostom, Suetonius, Tacitus);
(3) [[pontius-pilate|Pilate]]'s documented brutality (Philo, Josephus) makes a courtesy
burial of a crucified insurgent unlikely. The Joseph tradition is **absent from Paul** (the
pre-Pauline creed in 1 Cor 15:4 names no one for the burial, though it names Cephas for the
appearance) and from the Acts 13:29 speech (where unnamed "they" bury Jesus); it develops
from anonymous council → named Joseph (Mark) → Joseph + Nicodemus (John), the standard
embellishment of "naming the nameless" (Metzger) and "finding good guys among bad guys."
The empty-tomb and appearance traditions probably arose independently.

## Position C — Crossan

Jesus's body was left unburied and eaten by scavenging dogs. Ehrman declines to go this far:
"we do not know, and cannot know, what actually happened to Jesus's body."

## Status

**Open / tradition-specific.** Confessional traditions affirm the burial and empty tomb;
critical scholarship ranges from acceptance to Ehrman's skepticism. Not adjudicated here.
