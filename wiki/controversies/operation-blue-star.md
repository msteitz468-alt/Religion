# Operation Blue Star (1984)

The Indian Army's assault on the [[amritsar|Golden Temple complex]] in June 1984 — ordered by
[[indira-gandhi|Indira Gandhi]] to remove [[jarnail-singh-bhindranwale|Bhindranwale]] and his armed
followers from the fortified Akal Takht. Khushwant Singh (*A History of the Sikhs* Vol. 2, "Fatal
Miscalculation" and "Assassination and After") condemns it as a "grievous error of judgement." Casualty
figures are sharply disputed; per the Contradiction Protocol this page records all figures without
adjudicating.

## Chronology (per Singh)

- **1 June**: paramilitary (CRPF/BSF) probing fire; men killed inside the temple.
- **2 June**: Indira Gandhi's televised appeal to "shed hatred" — broadcast, Singh notes, after the army
  was already authorized.
- **4 June**: mountain guns used to destroy Babbar Khalsa sniper nests on the towers.
- **Night of 5–6 June**: full assault — "they threw in all they had: their commandos, frogmen,
  helicopters, armoured vehicles and tanks." Tank shells "brought down most of the edifice" of the Akal
  Takht. Akali leaders (Longowal, Tohra) surrendered after midnight.
- **6 June, morning**: [[jarnail-singh-bhindranwale|Bhindranwale]], Amrik Singh and Gen. Shahbeg Singh
  found dead in the basement.
- **8 June**: President Zail Singh visited as "bodies of women and children still floated in the sacred
  pool"; a sniper wounded his guard.

## Contested Casualty Figures (recorded, not adjudicated)

- **Official White Paper**: 554 "civilian-terrorists" killed, 92 army dead.
- **Rajiv Gandhi (Nagpur)**: army dead "over 700" (later retracted) — implying ~3,878 civilian dead by
  his own arithmetic.
- **Eyewitness accounts**: "between 1500 and 5000, mostly innocent pilgrims, including women and
  children."
- **Press**: *New York Times* 1,200; *Chicago Tribune* 2,000; Kuldip Nayar "nearly 100 devotees."

Singh's own assessment: the government figures "had many factual inaccuracies," never published the names
of the dead, and never refuted "damning evidence that quite a large number of those captured were
executed in cold blood." The crowd was swollen because the assault coincided with the martyrdom
anniversary of Guru Arjan — "~10,000 worshippers present on 3 June."

## Damage, Looting, and Aftermath

The army denied damage to the Harimandir, but journalists "counted hundreds of fresh bullet marks in the
gold-leaf and marble" and a Granth pierced by a bullet. The **Sikh Reference Library** — "hundreds of
rare handwritten copies of the Granth and hukumnamahs bearing the signatures of the Gurus" — was, per its
keeper D. S. Duggal, set alight by troops *after* the fighting stopped. The Nizam of Hyderabad's
gem-studded canopy (a gift of [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]) was among irreplaceable losses. Prisoners
"were denied drinking water; many were made to crawl on their bellies on hot tarmac; soldiers went about
the sacred premises with their shoes on, and drank rum and smoked cigarettes." The rural sweep
**Operation Woodrose** followed; Singh argues both operations "engendered feelings of alienation and
induced hundreds of young Sikh men and women to turn into terrorists."

## Singh's Critique and the Alternative

Singh calls it "a blunder," argues commandos or a siege ("cordoned off, deprived of drinking water…")
could have taken the small defending force, and cites the contrasting **Operation Black Thunder** (1988)
— in which police marksmen under K. P. S. Gill forced militants out of the temple *without entering it*,
with only three deaths — as proof: "a thundering success." His verdict: "Sikhs could be forgiven if they
came to the conclusion that Mrs Gandhi's government meant to give their community a bloody punch on the
nose." Many Sikhs of distinction (the historian Ganda Singh among them) returned state honours; the
author [[indira-gandhi|himself returned his Padma Bhushan]]. Over 4,000 Sikh soldiers deserted in protest.
The army action, Singh writes, "gave the movement for [[khalistan|Khalistan]] its first martyr."
