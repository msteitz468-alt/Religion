# Jñāna-karma-samuccaya: Knowledge Alone, or Knowledge with Action?

The central exegetical dispute of the **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]** (and of Vedanta generally) is
whether **liberating knowledge (jñāna) suffices by itself**, or whether liberation requires the
**combination (*samuccaya*) of knowledge with action/ritual (karma)**. The Sanskrit slogan for the
combination view is ***jñāna-karma-samuccaya-vāda***. Because the Gita praises both renunciatory
knowledge *and* engaged, devoted action, every commentator must say how the two relate — and they
disagree irreconcilably.

## The Positions

**1. Knowledge alone liberates — [[shankara|Śaṅkara]] / [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]].** Mokṣa is the
removal of **ignorance (avidyā)** by knowledge of the actionless Self; action belongs to the realm of
ignorance and cannot produce a result (liberation) that is *eternally already the case*. Action
([[karma-yoga|karma-yoga]]) only **purifies the mind** and qualifies one for knowledge, after which it
is renounced (*sannyāsa*). In his introduction to the *Gītā-bhāṣya* Śaṅkara names and refutes an
opponent who holds the combination view. *Recorded position, not adjudicated.*

**2. Knowledge combined with Vedic rites — the Vṛttikāra / Mīmāṃsā-leaning combiners.** Śaṅkara's
opponent (whom the tradition identifies with the *Vṛttikāra*, possibly **Bodhāyana**) holds that "the
well-ascertained conclusion of the whole of the Gita is that liberation is attained through Knowledge
*associated with* rites and duties like the Agnihotra prescribed in the Vedas and Smṛtis." Here the
ritual obligations of [[hinduism|Vedic religion]] are never simply dropped. This is the Mīmāṃsā-tilted
reading Advaita defines itself against.

**3. Knowledge and action are non-different — Ramakantha and [[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta]]
([[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Shaivism]]).** The Śaiva commentators also affirm a *samuccaya*, but reground
it: the Lord, pure consciousness, expresses himself as both **knowing (dṛk)** and **acting (kriyā)** —
these are his two inseparable powers, not two stages or two rival means. "Action and knowledge are not
two distinct entities, but combined create a whole" (Abhinavagupta on 5.14). Doership belongs to the
Lord shining as all things, so the opposition Śaṅkara draws between the actionless Self and the world of
action **dissolves** rather than being ranked. The earlier Kashmiri commentator **Ramakantha** had
already emphasized *jñāna-karma-samuccaya-vāda*.

**4. Action as a complete path in itself — modern karma-yoga.** Tilak's *Gītā-Rahasya*, Gandhi, and
Vivekananda recover selfless action as an **independent, sufficient spiritual discipline**, against
Śaṅkara's subordination of it to renunciation — making the Gita a charter for engaged, this-worldly
ethics.

## Why It Cannot Be Settled Textually

The Gita itself moves between exalting renunciation (5.13: the embodied one "rests, doing nothing") and
commanding action (3.8: "perform your obligatory duty"). Krishna even says Arjuna's confusion arises
because the teaching *seems* to praise both (3.1–2). Each school resolves the tension by its own
**metaphysics of the Self and of action**, not by a decisive verse — which is why the dispute is
*tradition-specific* rather than open or historically closed.
