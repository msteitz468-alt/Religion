# The Qira'at (Variant Qur'anic Readings)

The standard Islamic claim is that the **[[quran|Qur'an]]** is textually perfect and uniquely
preserved. The *qira'at* — the multiple licensed **recitations** of that text — complicate the claim
in a way exactly parallel to (but theologically weighted differently from) the manuscript variation
behind the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament. This is the wiki's first Islamic text-critical
controversy page, created per the schema flag in *Needed Sources.md*.

## The Issue

The consonantal skeleton (*rasm*) fixed under **Uthman** (c. 650) was written without short vowels or
most diacritics; the same skeleton can be vocalized in different ways, yielding different words and
occasionally different meanings. The recitation traditions transmitting these vocalizations were
systematized by **Ibn Mujahid** (d. 936) into the **seven canonical readings** (*al-qira'at al-sab*),
later extended to **ten** and fourteen. Each reading is transmitted through named *qurra'* (reciters)
via two *rawis* (transmitters) — e.g., the **Hafs an Asim** reading dominant today vs. the **Warsh an
Nafi** reading common in North/West Africa.

## Positions

- **Traditional (canonical readings)** — the variant readings are themselves divinely sanctioned,
  grounded in the hadith that the Qur'an "was revealed in seven *ahruf*" (modes/dialects). Variation is
  a feature of the revelation, not a corruption; all canonical readings are equally the word of God.
- **Single text, oral variants** — the *written* Uthmanic text is one and uncorrupted; the *qira'at*
  are oral-recitational, mostly minor (vowels, pausal forms), and do not touch the *rasm*. The
  preservation claim is about the *mushaf*, which holds.
- **Revisionist / historical-critical** — points to early non-Uthmanic codices (the **Sanaa
  palimpsest**, with a scriptio inferior diverging from the standard text), the maturation of Arabic
  orthography, and the late systematization of the readings to argue for a more gradual canonization
  than tradition allows. The lineage runs from Nöldeke–Schwally's *Geschichte des Qorāns* through
  Arthur Jeffery's work on the pre-Uthmanic codices to **[[bell-watt-introduction-to-the-quran|Bell &
  Watt]]** (who treat the Abu Bakr "collection" as a back-projection but judge the Uthmanic text
  *authentic* to Muhammad) and, at the radical end, John Wansbrough (a much later, gradual
  canonization). Recorded here without adjudication.
- **Traditionalist apologetic** — **[[al-azami-history-of-the-quranic-text|M. M. Al-Azami]]** answers
  the critical lineage directly: the Qur'an was completely written and memorized in Muhammad's own
  lifetime, the Uthmanic step was mere standardization of an already-fixed text, the variant-codex
  reports are exaggerated or fabricated, and the orientalist method (Goldziher, Jeffery, Bell,
  Wansbrough) is methodologically biased — contrasting the Qur'an's transmission favorably with the
  textual history of the Bible. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).

## Comparative Note

The structure mirrors the wiki's existing text-critical controversies — the
[[mark-ending|ending of Mark]], the [[documentary-hypothesis|Documentary Hypothesis]], the

historical evidence of variation. The *seven ahruf* / *qira'at* framework is the Islamic theological
mechanism for absorbing variation into orthodoxy, with no exact parallel in the other traditions.

## Evidence from Sahih al-Bukhari (Book 61)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 6, Book 61 (Virtues of the Qur'an), Nos. 509–582. M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

Bukhari's hadith corpus provides the primary Sunni evidence for both the seven ahruf doctrine
and the Uthman compilation. Key hadiths:

### The Umar–Hisham Incident (No. 513)
Umar heard Hisham reciting Surah al-Furqan differently from how the Prophet had taught him.
He brought Hisham to the Prophet. The Prophet affirmed *both* versions: "Thus has it been
revealed." Conclusion: "This Qur'an has been revealed to be recited in seven different ways
(*ahruf*)." This is the foundational Sunni hadith for the seven ahruf doctrine.

### Gabriel's Seven Modes (No. 514)
Narrated by Ubai bin Ka'b: Gabriel taught the Prophet in one mode; the Prophet requested
additional modes "for the ease of my community"; the modes increased progressively until seven.

### Ibn Masud and Al-Mu'awwidhatain (No. 560)
**The most striking canonical dispute in Bukhari**: Zirr bin Hubaish reports that Ibn Masud
(Abdullah bin Masud) "struck the (two suras of Refuge, i.e. Surah al-Falaq and Surah An-Nas
[113–114]) out of his Mushaf, saying, 'These two are not from the Book of Allah.'"
Ubai bin Ka'b explicitly contradicted him, stating the Prophet himself had taught him these
two suras. Neither hadith is suppressed; both are preserved. The Uthmanic text includes them;
Ibn Masud's position is that they are protection formulas, not Quran.

### Abu Bakr's Collection (No. 509)
Zaid bin Thabit's account of the first collection after the Battle of Yamama. The last two
verses of Surah Al-Tauba (9:128–129) were found only with Khuzaima bin Thabit and included
on that basis.

### Uthman's Standardization (No. 510)
The Quraishi dialect criterion: when Zaid and the editorial team differed, "write it in the
dialect of Quraish as the Qur'an was revealed in their tongue." All other variant codices were
then burned. This is the Sunni canonical account of standardization — itself evidence that
variation existed at the time.
