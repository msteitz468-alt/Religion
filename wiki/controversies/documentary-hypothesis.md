The Documentary Hypothesis (DH) is the dominant critical theory of Pentateuchal composition, holding that the Pentateuch (Genesis–Deuteronomy) is not a unified work from a single author (traditionally Moses) but the product of several distinct literary sources, combined by later editors.

## Classical Formulation (Wellhausen)

Julius Wellhausen (1844–1918) gave the theory its definitive formulation in *Prolegomena to the History of Israel* (1878). He identified four sources:

| Source | Symbol | Date (Wellhausen) | Location | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yahwist | J | c. 850 BCE | Judah | Uses *YHWH*; anthropomorphic; narrative |
| Elohist | E | c. 850 BCE | Northern Israel | Uses *Elohim*; dreams and angels as intermediaries |
| Deuteronomist | D | c. 620 BCE | Northern/Jerusalem | Confined to Deuteronomy; hortatory style |
| Priestly | P | c. 550–450 BCE | Post-exilic | Genealogies; chronological precision; ritual concerns |

Key criteria for source identification: (1) the distribution of divine names (*YHWH* vs. *Elohim*); (2) duplicate narratives (two creation accounts, three wife-sister stories, two flood accounts); (3) anachronisms; (4) distinctive vocabulary and theology.

Wellhausen further argued that none of the sources could provide authentic historical information about the periods they describe — they reveal only the literary periods in which they were composed. In Hamilton’s summary of that stance: “none of these traditions could give any authentic information about the patriarchs or Moses. They were only reliable for the period in which they were composed (viz., the middle of the 9th century B.C. and later).”

## Subsequent Developments

**Form criticism** (Gunkel, 1901): Argued that the literary sources themselves had an oral prehistory of short narrative units (*Sagen*, legends) with original *Sitze im Leben* (life settings). The sources are deposits of earlier oral traditions.

**Tradition history** (Noth, 1948): Identified five major themes unifying the Pentateuch, each originally independent and later combined. The historical patriarchs existed but the narratives about them are largely theological constructions.

**Rendtorff's block hypothesis** (1977): Rejected the existence of continuous J and E sources; instead proposed large independent blocks of tradition (primeval history, patriarchal stories, Exodus, etc.) each with their own literary history before being combined by a Deuteronomistic redactor. Hamilton reports Rendtorff’s verdict: “The positing of ‘sources’ in the sense of the documentary hypothesis can no longer make any contribution to understanding the development of the Pentateuch” — then counters that Rendtorff largely renames multilayered Genesis and that stripping promise-language makes many narratives (esp. barren-heir tension) pointless.

**Van Seters' late J / late Abraham** (1975+): Argued J was a single author working in the exilic period (6th century BCE); later confined Abraham traditions to Neo-Babylonian times as kerygmatic rather than historical. Hamilton: “Van Seters has not succeeded in replacing the Wellhausen consensus with a Van Seters challenge.”

## Hamilton’s Position (densified)

[[victor-hamilton|Hamilton]]’s full engagement is in the introduction and running commentary of [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Genesis 1–17]] and continues through [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|Genesis 18–50]]. He does **not** simply reassert Mosaic authorship as a confessional necessity.

### Core stance: composite ≠ errant; evangelical ≠ Mosaic-required

> “We do not believe that an evangelical view of Scripture is necessarily wedded to the Mosaic authorship of Genesis, as if one cannot have the first without having the second. ‘Composite’ is not a synonym for ‘errant.’ … Still, it is not without significance that recent studies have tended to support the essential unity of Genesis.” (Hamilton, Introduction)

He notes nearly eighteen centuries of Jewish and Christian assumption of Mosaic unity (Maimonides, Augustine, Calvin), then surveys Astruc through Wellhausen, Gunkel, Noth, Cross, Van Seters, Rendtorff, Kikawada/Quinn, Rendsburg, Tengström, and Radday/Shore. Cassuto’s case against JEDP is judged “the most convincing” among traditional unity defenders (often ignored as apologetic); Kitchen’s ANE-anomaly argument and Harrison’s tablet model are registered. On anonymity and lost originals: “it is most likely that a project to determine Genesis’ authorship and mode of composition is doomed from the start.”

### Divine names: thematic participants, not only J/P

Against using *YHWH* / *ʾĕlōhîm* alternation in Gen 6–9 as proof of two Flood sources, Hamilton (Cassuto-type reasoning) reads **thematic** distribution: general *ʾĕlōhîm* when the world is inundated; *YHWH* where deity is the thematic participant in a paragraph; nonthematic sections use *ʾĕlōhîm* even inside a “Yahweh” unit (e.g. 7:9).

> “Those who perceive a doublet in Gen. 6-9 appeal to this phenomenon to support their case. … This interpretation of the two names for deity is open to serious challenge. … normally Gen. 6-9 uses the more general ’ĕlōhîm (since it is the world that is inundated), and that YHWH occurs only where there is a special reason for it. … it is also evident that in those places where deity is thematic in a given paragraph YHWH is used.” (Hamilton; Flood narrative)

The same logic underwrites his reading of *Yahweh ʾĕlōhîm* in Gen 2–3 as theologically motivated compound (majestic cosmic *ʾĕlōhîm* + personal covenant *Yahweh*), not amalgam of sources — see [[genesis-creation-accounts|two creation accounts]].

### Alleged doublets re-read as literary design

Examples Hamilton develops against mechanical source dissection:

| Locus | Critical claim | Hamilton’s alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Gen 1 // 2 | Irreconcilable P and J creations | Complementary authority / under-authority frames; 2:4a **superscript** not P subscript |
| Gen 6:5–8 // 6:9–11 | Two Flood introductions (J / P) | 6:5–8 = preview (Yahweh as major actor); 6:9–11 shifts to Noah and earth |
| Clean animals one pair // seven pairs | P vs J contradiction | 6:19–20 general; 7:2–3 specifies seven pairs clean + one pair unclean |
| Gen 7:6–9 // 7:11–16 | P doublet | Epic resumption and chiastic symmetry; “dissecting the whole will prove… unproductive” |
| 8:13 *ḥāraḇ* // 8:14 *yāḇaš* | Needless J/P repetition | Two verbs for resurfacing creation: Noah’s observation vs objective report |
| 8:20–22 // 9:1–17 | Two Flood conclusions (J / P) | Incipient covenant announcement then amplification |
| Gen 10 Table of Nations | J/P composite disjointedness | Chiastic whole (Shem–Ham–Japheth intro reversed in body); inclusio 10:1 // 10:32 |
| Gen 15 // 17 | J and P Abrahamic covenants | One covenant reconfirmed; *ʿôlām* stress preserves unilaterality despite stipulations |
| Gen 37 Midianites // Ishmaelites | Classic J (Judah/Ishmaelites) vs E (Reuben/Midianites) split | Overlapping terms (Judg 8:22–28); “Ishmaelite” generic Bedouin, “Midianite” more ethnic; brothers as subject of “sold”; literary unity (Rudolph; Coats; Greenstein; Berlin; White) |
| Gen 45 Joseph/Pharaoh dual invitation | J/E conflict | Royal confirmation of Joseph’s initiative, not source conflict |

On Gen 37 specifically (vol. 2):

> “These traders are identified as Midianites and Ishmaelite. Judg. 8:22-28 state clearly that Midianite and Ishmaelite are overlapping, identical terms. … Thus ‘Ishmaelite’ is the more generic term (Bedouin nomad), while ‘Midianite’ is the more specific and ethnic term. … Enough has been said to indicate that recourse to a J-E hypothesis, or to an original story with later redactional expansions, is not the only option in order to make sense of Gen. 37.” (Hamilton; Gen 37)

### Unity arguments Hamilton endorses (without closing authorship)

- **Kikawada and Quinn**: Gen 1–11 five-part structure // Atrahasis; “the case for homogeneity is much greater than the case for heterogeneity.”
- **Rendsburg**: purposeful structure across cycles; DH “untenable” and to be “discarded” (Hamilton reports without making that his sole verdict).
- **Radday/Shore** computer study: main difference narrative vs speech, not J vs E; “massive evidence” of unity.
- **Tigay** on Gilgamesh/SP: multiedited Gilgamesh is empirically verifiable; “A multiedited Genesis remains, at best, a hypothesis.”

### [[peter-craigie|Craigie]] and Cassuto (adjacent evangelical/literary challenges)

- Both Hamilton and Craigie acknowledge divine-name and duplicate phenomena as real data requiring explanation; both reject Wellhausen’s evolutionary religious-history framework.
- Cassuto: divine names theologically motivated; “duplicates” as artistry; circular criteria.
- Craigie uses the [[suzerain-vassal-treaty|Hittite treaty form]] as dating evidence that bypasses source-critical arguments.

## Current Status of the Debate

The classical JEDP formulation has largely been abandoned by critical scholars but no consensus replacement has emerged. Current approaches include:
- **Neo-Documentary** (Friedman, Baden): A refined version of JEDP, defending the existence of continuous sources
- **Supplement hypothesis**: P is a redactional layer, not an independent source
- **Fragmentary hypothesis**: Multiple small units without continuous sources
- **Literary/canonical approaches** (Childs, Rendtorff): Focus on the final form's theological coherence

## Centrist "Believing Criticism" Position (Bruckner)

[[james-bruckner|Bruckner]]'s *Exodus* represents a mediating position between conservative evangelical resistance and full critical acceptance:

- Accepts that Exodus' final form "was likely redacted from diverse sources in the crisis of the sixth century, in view of deliverance from Babylon"
- Quotes Brueggemann approvingly: "current scholarship finds the hypothesis in its classical form less and less useful"
- Does not argue for Mosaic authorship as a theological necessity; note that Hamilton also refuses to *require* Mosaic authorship for an evangelical doctrine of Scripture, while still arguing literary unity more strongly than Bruckner
- Works from the final canonical form (following Childs) rather than reconstructing sources
- Uses Fretheim's creational theology as the interpretive framework, which bypasses source-critical debates entirely

This position distinguishes Bruckner from both the more unity-committed evangelicals (Hamilton, Craigie) and those who use source criticism to fragment the text's meaning. The "believing criticism" approach accepts compositional complexity while reading the final form theologically.

The DH remains the necessary conversation partner for any serious academic engagement with the Pentateuch.

### Ashley (NICOT Numbers) — mediating final-form position

[[timothy-ashley|Timothy R. Ashley]] (*The Book of Numbers*, NICOT, 1993) surveys traditional Mosaic authorship claims, classical Wellhausen JEDP, form/tradition criticism (von Rad, Noth), Rendtorff’s block-editing, and the Kaufmann/Milgrom/Weinfeld/Hurvitz stream that dates P preexilic. His own mediating stance (attributed):

- Moses has a key role in the origin of **some** material (only writing notice in Numbers: 33:2 itinerary framework); full traditional Mosaic authorship of the whole is not affirmed.
- Long transmission is undeniable; materials from ch. 22 on especially point later than the conquest.
- Final form likely **preexilic** (possibly united monarchy)—not necessarily postexilic P.
- Redactional histories (as in Budd) are useful summaries but **not knowable** as recoverable histories; primary job is final-form explanation for the Church.
- Inspiration not limited to the earliest stage; Church and Synagogue confess the whole OT text.
- When the text presents literary difficulty, seek a literary/functional solution from within the final form rather than positing an editor indifferent to logic and style.

This places Ashley near Hamilton/Craigie on final-form reading and near Kaufmann/Milgrom on P dating, while remaining more open than Cole to long transmission.
