# Is Suffering a Universal Mark of Existence?

A doctrinal dispute over the contents of the canonical list of universal characteristics —
the **three marks of existence** (*tilakkhaṇa*) or **Three Dharma Seals**. The disagreement
is not over the consonance of the teaching but over whether **suffering (*dukkha*)** belongs
on the list of *universal* marks alongside impermanence and nonself.

## Position 1 — The Standard Three Marks (*anicca, dukkha, anatta*)

The dominant formulation, and the one carried on the
[[three-marks-of-existence|three marks]] page from [[walpola-rahula|Rahula]] and the wider
[[theravada|Theravada]] tradition. All conditioned phenomena bear three marks:

1. **Anicca** — impermanence;
2. **[[dukkha|Dukkha]]** — unsatisfactoriness;
3. **[[anatta|Anatta]]** — non-self.

The marks form a chain the suttas repeat: *"what is impermanent is dukkha; what is dukkha is
non-self."* On this reading, suffering is a pervasive feature of all clinging to the
impermanent [[five-aggregates|aggregates]].

## Position 2 — Thich Nhat Hanh: *anicca, anatta, nirvana*

**[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]]** (*[[thich-nhat-hanh-heart-of-the-buddhas-teaching|The
Heart of the Buddha's Teaching]]*) argues that putting suffering on the same level as
impermanence and nonself is an **error**:

- Impermanence and nonself are genuinely **universal** — a "mark" of *all* things (a table
  is impermanent and has no separate self). **Suffering is not**: it is "strange to talk
  about a table or a flower as being filled with suffering." A table makes us suffer only if
  we wrongly attribute permanence or separateness to it — the suffering is in the
  *attachment*, not the table.
- The argument "impermanent, therefore suffering, therefore nonself" is therefore
  **illogical** as a statement of universal marks.
- The authentic third Seal is **[[nirvana|nirvana]]** — "the joy of completely extinguishing
  our ideas and concepts." He cites the Northern transmission's *Saṃyukta Āgama* (which
  states this four times) and **[[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]**, who lists nirvana as one of the
  Three Dharma Seals. The correct list: **impermanence, nonself, nirvana**.

## Why It Stays Open (Tradition-Specific)

The dispute reflects a real divergence in the textual record: the **Pali** sources favor the
*anicca/dukkha/anatta* triad, while a strand of the **Northern (Sanskrit/Āgama) and
Mahayana** tradition transmits *anicca/anatta/nirvana* (sometimes adding suffering as a
fourth). It is therefore **tradition-specific**, not a simple error on either side:
[[theravada|Theravada]] retains suffering as a contemplative universal; a
[[mahayana|Mahayana]]/[[zen|Zen]] strand (Thich Nhat Hanh) restores nirvana to keep the
Seals "universal" in the strict sense and to resist a pessimistic over-reading of the First
[[four-noble-truths|Noble Truth]]. Recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
