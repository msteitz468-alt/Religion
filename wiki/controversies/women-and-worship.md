# Women and Worship (1 Corinthians 11 / 14)

Two passages in 1 Corinthians regulate women's speech and demeanor in corporate worship: head covering while praying or prophesying (11:2–16) and the injunction that women "remain silent in the churches" (14:34–35). The tension between permission to prophesy (ch. 11) and a silence command (ch. 14) is among the most contested issues in NT ethics and ecclesiology. This page records major positions without adjudicating them; densification from [[ciampa-rosner-1-corinthians-pntc|Ciampa & Rosner (PNTC, 2010)]] is attributed.

## 11:2–16 — Head Coverings, Glory, and *Kephalē*

### What the text does (descriptive)

Paul praises the Corinthians for holding traditions (11:2), then states a hierarchical chain: the head of every man is Christ, the head of the woman is man, the head of Christ is God (11:3). Men praying/prophesying with covered heads dishonor their head; women doing so uncovered dishonor theirs (11:4–5). Creation arguments (woman from man; woman created for man) and an appeal to "nature," custom, and the practice of the churches follow. Women praying and prophesying in the assembly are **assumed**, not forbidden.

### Ciampa & Rosner's reading (attributed)

[[roy-ciampa-brian-rosner|Ciampa and Rosner]] treat 11:2–16 as the first subunit of the positive "glorify God in worship" panel (11:2–14:40), after the negative treatment of idolatry (8:1–11:1). Load-bearing moves they make:

1. **Glory/shame chain.** Behavior that shames one's "head" reflects up the chain to Christ and God; worship must accrue honor to God, not disgrace.
2. ***Kephalē* as authority (not pure "source").** They prefer an authority reading of "head," while noting mutuality "in the Lord" (11:11–12) and that the order of pairs in v. 3 is driven by pastoral focus on man and woman in worship, not a simple descending org chart.
3. **Creation, not merely culture.** Gen 1–2 underwrites the argument; image of God is not denied to women; "glory" becomes the key term balancing "shame."
4. **Angels (11:10).** Multiple options reported (Gen 6 watchers; cosmic auditors; zeal for glory; DSS nakedness traditions; Ps 8); C&R refuse a single forced solution while keeping the verse's demand for *exousia* (authority/appropriate sign) on the woman's head.
5. **"New Roman woman" background (reported carefully).** They allow that elite Corinthian women influenced by freer imperial fashion may have abandoned matronal head covering; class dynamics may link this unit to the Supper abuses (11:17–34).

## 14:34–35 — "Women Should Remain Silent"

### Text-critical question

Some Western witnesses place vv. 34–35 after v. 40. **Fee, Hays, Schrage** and others take the verses as a non-Pauline interpolation (marginal gloss later inserted). **Ellis** suggests Paul himself wrote a margin note before sending.

**Ciampa & Rosner** reject inauthenticity: thirty-six words would be an "extraordinarily long 'marginal note'"; **no manuscript omits the text**; Western transposition more likely seeks a "more appropriate location" for a difficult directive than proves non-Pauline origin.

### Interpretive options (positions)

| Position | Claim | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximal silence** | Women may not speak at all in church | Hard to reconcile with 11:5 praying/prophesying |
| **Cancels ch. 11** | 14:34–35 is Paul's "true" restriction | C&R: "found little support" |
| **Corinthian quotation** | 34–35 = Corinthian slogan answered in v. 36 | C&R: formal criteria for quotation not met; v. 36 addresses mixed church (masculine plural) |
| **Weighing prophecies** | Women may not sift/evaluate prophecy (v. 29b) | Reported as growing conservative evangelical consensus (Thiselton, Grudem); C&R grant social awkwardness of wives publicly criticizing husbands but distance from Grudem's "deceptive innocent questions" scenario |
| **Situational silence — disruptive questions (C&R preferred)** | *Sigaō* already means situational restraint in 14:28 (uninterpreted tongues) and 14:30 (prophet yields floor); women/wives asking questions in assembly in ways disgraceful by Greco-Roman norms should ask their own husbands at home | Does **not** cancel 11:2–16; heading in C&R: aside on another disorder interfering with fitting worship |

### C&R preferred sense (detail)

- Continuity of *sigaō* across three silence situations (tongues, prophecy turns, women) under the theme "done in a fitting and orderly way" (14:40).
- "Be in submission" and "ask their own husbands at home" point primarily to **wifely respect** and household instruction, not a blanket ban on female prophecy or prayer.
- First-century "disgraceful" public mixed interrogation does not automatically map onto modern Western assemblies where mixed public speech is not shameful; **principles** (do not act disgracefully; respect spouse's dignity; preserve order) travel, **concrete forms** may differ.
- Garland (as reported): instructions conditioned by social realities and fear of resemblance to orgiastic cults; apply with awareness of local decorum.

## Relation of the Two Passages

Any reading that makes 14:34–35 a total ban on female speech must explain 11:5. C&R resolve the tension by **particularizing** the silence (type of speech / situation), not by canceling chapter 11. Both units sit inside the larger concern for edifying, God-glorifying worship after the idolatry panel (chs. 8–10) and before the resurrection climax (ch. 15).

## Resolution Status

**Open** across traditions and within evangelical scholarship. Historic catholic and orthodox practice, Protestant complementarian and egalitarian schools, and critical reconstructions remain live. This page records positions; it does not settle church order.
