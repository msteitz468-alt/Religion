# The Millennium (Revelation 20)

Revelation 20:1–10 — Satan bound for "a thousand years," the martyrs/saints reigning with Christ
("the first resurrection"), then Satan released for a final rebellion (Gog and Magog) and cast into
the lake of fire — is, in [[grant-osborne|Osborne]]'s words, "easily the best-known portion of the
book as well as one of the most divisive passages in the Bible." How one relates the thousand-year
reign to the second coming of Christ defines the three classic millennial positions and has divided
churches for centuries. This page records the positions; Osborne's own (premillennial, but
explicitly irenic) is presented as one among them.

## The Text

- **20:1–3** — an angel binds the dragon (Satan) and seals him in the abyss for a thousand years "so
  that he might no longer deceive the nations," after which "he must be released for a short time."
- **20:4–6** — thrones; the souls of the beheaded martyrs and those who refused the beast "came to
  life and reigned with Christ a thousand years." "This is the first resurrection… they will be
  priests of God and of Christ and will reign with him."
- **20:7–10** — Satan released, deceives the nations (Gog and Magog), surrounds "the camp of the
  saints"; fire from heaven consumes them; the devil is thrown into the lake of fire.

## The Three Positions

**1. Premillennialism.** Christ returns *before* ("pre-") the millennium: at the parousia (19:11–21)
he defeats the evil forces, then reigns on earth with the resurrected saints for the thousand years,
which ends with Satan's release, the final rebellion, the general resurrection, and the great white
throne judgment. Two forms:
- **Historic (classical) premillennialism** — one return of Christ *after* the tribulation; the whole
  church passes through it; held by some earliest fathers (Justin, Irenaeus, Hippolytus) and by Ladd,
  Beasley-Murray, Mounce, and **Osborne**.
- **Dispensational premillennialism** — adds a pretribulational rapture of the church, a seven-year
  tribulation, and a literal thousand-year reign centered on national Israel; reads the numbers
  literally (Walvoord, Thomas). See [[parousia-1-thess-4|Parousia and the rapture]].

**2. Amillennialism.** There is *no* literal future earthly reign ("a-"/no millennium). Revelation
20:1–10 is symbolic of the **church age between the two advents**: the "binding" of Satan is his
restraint from deceiving the nations since the Gentile mission began (cf. Mark 3:27), and the "first
resurrection" / reign of the saints is the present heavenly reign of the deceased faithful (or the
believer's spiritual regeneration). The millennium ends with Satan's brief release and the parousia.
Rooted in Augustine and Ticonius, dominant for ~1,200 years; held by Hendriksen, Hoekema, and (in a
"redemptive-historical modified idealism") G. K. Beale.

**3. Postmillennialism.** Christ returns *after* ("post-") a millennium understood as a future (or
progressively realized) **age of gospel triumph and peace** brought about within history before the
second coming. Associated with Whitby, the Puritans, and Jonathan Edwards; revived at times in
optimistic eras.

## The Key Exegetical Pivots

- **Is the "thousand years" literal or symbolic?** Osborne reads it **symbolically** (a real but
  indefinite, complete period), consistent with the book's other symbolic numbers (144,000; 42
  months) — but symbolic does *not*, for him, mean non-future. Dispensationalists read it literally.
- **What is "the first resurrection" (20:5–6)?** Premillennialists: a *bodily* resurrection of the
  saints preceding the millennium, distinct from the general resurrection of "the rest of the dead"
  after it (the natural reading of two resurrections separated by the thousand years). Amillennialists:
  a *spiritual* resurrection (regeneration, or the soul's entry into heaven at death).
- **Recapitulation vs. sequence.** Amillennialists typically read ch. 20 as *recapitulating* (going
  back over) the church age rather than following ch. 19 chronologically; premillennialists read
  19→20 as a sequence (parousia, then reign).
- **The "binding" of Satan.** A present restraint since Christ's first coming (amil), or a future,
  total incarceration (premil)? Rev. 20:3 ("no longer deceive the nations") is read very differently.

## Osborne's Position

Osborne adopts **premillennialism**: the saints not destroyed at Armageddon (19:17–21 pictures the
*army*, not all earth-dwellers) live on as the "nations" of 20:7–9; during the thousand years they
live under the reign of Christ and the risen saints with Satan *entirely* bound, yet the instant he
is released "they all flock after him in a millisecond." For Osborne this is the **climax of the
book's theodicy**: it proves the totality of human depravity (even "fourteen lifetimes" under Christ's
rule, with no deceiver, do not change them) and so the *necessity* of eternal judgment (the great
white throne, 20:11–15) against universalism.

Crucially, Osborne holds this **irenically**: "In this commentary, I take a premillennial approach
but recognize the viability of the other two positions. This issue will not be solved until the
events take place… we should not fight over these issues but be 'iron sharpening iron.'" He reads the
thousand years as symbolic, distinguishing his historic premillennialism sharply from dispensational
literalism.

## Why It Matters

The millennium debate organizes wider eschatological systems (rapture, tribulation, the role of
Israel, the nature of the kingdom) and has been a major fault line in Protestantism — especially the
20th-century rise of dispensational premillennialism in popular Anglophone Christianity. Osborne's
treatment models reading Revelation 20 as **apocalyptic** (symbolic numbers, theological purpose)
rather than as a literal timetable, and his insistence on charity across the positions is itself a
methodological stance. See [[apocalyptic-literature|Apocalyptic Literature]].
