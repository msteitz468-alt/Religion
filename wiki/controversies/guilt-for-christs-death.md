# Guilt for Christ's Death

Who bears moral responsibility for the crucifixion of [[jesus|Christ]], and to what degree, is a
question with a long and consequential history — bound up with the polemical charge of "deicide"
levelled against the Jewish people and its repudiation in modern Catholic teaching (Vatican II,
*Nostra Aetate* §4). This page records the positions without adjudicating among them
(**Contradiction Protocol**); the named scholastic locus is [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]],
[[summa-theologica|*ST*]] III Q47 A5–A6.

## The scholastic question (ST III Q47 A5–A6)

Aquinas asks whether Christ's persecutors **knew who He was**, and whether their sin was the gravest.
He distinguishes tiers:

- **The Jewish rulers/elders** "knew… that He was the Christ promised in the Law," but "did not know
  the mystery of His Godhead." Their ignorance was **"affected ignorance"** — a chosen refusal:
  "they perverted [the signs] out of hatred and envy of Christ." Affected ignorance does not excuse;
  hence "their sin was the most grievous, both on account of the kind of sin… and from the malice of
  their will." He cites Jerome: "the rulers of the Jews crucified the Son of God, not from ignorance,
  but out of envy," and Chrysostom: "they exceeded the measure of their fathers; for these latter
  slew men, but they crucified God."
- **The common Jewish people** "did not fully comprehend that He was the Christ… they were deceived
  afterwards by their rulers." Their crime was grievous in kind but "in one respect lessened by
  reason of their ignorance" — Aquinas invokes Luke 23:34 ("they know not what they do") via Bede.
- **The Gentile soldiers** who physically crucified Him were "much more excusable, since they had no
  knowledge of the Law."

He ranks the culpability: (1) the Jewish rulers (affected ignorance + envy); (2) Judas (greed);
(3) the chief priests (envy and hate); (4) Pilate (worldly fear of Caesar); (5) the soldiers
(following orders). Crucially he holds that **Christ's willing His Passion does not excuse the
injustice of His slayers**: "Christ's slayers are not excused of their injustice."

## The scriptural tension Aquinas is reconciling

His grading is an attempt to harmonize apparently conflicting New Testament data:

- **1 Cor. 2:8** — "If they had known it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory"
  (suggesting ignorance).
- **Acts 3:17** — Peter: "I know that you did it through ignorance."
- **Luke 23:34** — "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do."
- against **John 15:24** and the Johannine portrayal of culpable rejection.

Aquinas resolves it by the *affected ignorance* category: the rulers knew Christ as the promised
Messiah (so were culpable) but not explicitly as God (so 1 Cor. 2:8 stands).

## The wider controversy

- The **deicide charge** — holding the Jewish people collectively and perpetually guilty of killing
  God — drew on this exegetical tradition (and on Matt. 27:25, "His blood be on us and on our
  children") and fuelled centuries of Christian anti-Judaism. See the-jews-in-christian-polemic
  if developed.
- **Modern Catholic repudiation**: *Nostra Aetate* (1965) declared that Christ's death "cannot be
  charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today,"
  rejecting the collective and hereditary guilt the older reading had underwritten — a reversal of
  the polemical use to which Q47 had sometimes been put, though not of Aquinas's own graded analysis
  of the individuals involved.
- **Critical-historical** readings stress Roman responsibility (crucifixion being a Roman penalty
  under Pilate) and read the Gospel emphasis on Jewish initiative as shaped by the early Church's
  apologetic situation.
