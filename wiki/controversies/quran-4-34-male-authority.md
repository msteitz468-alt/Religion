# Q 4:34 — Male Authority and the *daraba* Clause

Sura al-Nisa' 4:34 is, in [[amina-wadud|Wadud]]'s words, "classically viewed as the single
most important verse with regard to the relationship between men and women." It contains three
disputed cruxes — **qiwama** (male authority/maintenance), **faddala** (preference), and the
final clause licensing, in the standard rendering, a husband to **scourge/strike (daraba)** a
wife from whom he fears **nushuz**. The verse is the central battleground of modern Qur'anic
gender debate.

> "Men are **qawwamuna 'ala** women, [on the basis] of what God has **preferred (faddala)**
> some of them over others, and of what they spend of their property... So good women are
> **qanitat**... As for those from whom you fear **nushuz**, admonish them, banish them to
> beds apart, and **strike them (wadribuhunna)**. Then if they obey you, seek not a way
> against them." (Q 4:34, composite)

## The Terms in Dispute

- **qiwama / qawwamuna 'ala** — "are maintainers/protectors/in authority over." Read as
  rank/guardianship, or as economic responsibility, or as conditional duty.
- **faddala** ("preferred") — read as an absolute, God-given male superiority, or as a
  relative, partial, *ba'd*-over-*ba'd* ("some over some") distinction.
- **[[qiwama|darajah]]** (Q 2:228, "men have a *degree* above them") — a universal hierarchy,
  or a context-bound advantage in divorce only.
- **nushuz** — wifely "disobedience/rebellion," or (since the same word describes *husbands* in
  Q 4:128) mutual "marital discord."
- **daraba** — "to strike/beat," or (per Lane / *Lisan al-'Arab*) a far weaker "to separate
  from / set forth," and in any case the non-intensive form opposed to *darraba* (to beat
  repeatedly).

## Positions (recorded, not adjudicated)

**1. Classical-traditional.** *Qiwama* establishes men's God-given authority and guardianship
over women (often beyond the family, as a social order); *faddala* and *darajah* express a real
male precedence; *qanitat* means wives "obedient" to husbands; *nushuz* is wifely disobedience;
and the third step permits a (light, non-injurious) physical chastisement after admonition and
separation fail. This is the dominant reading across the classical *tafasir*.

**2. Sayyid Qutb (*Fi Zilal al-Qur'an*).** Restricts *qiwama* largely to the **husband–wife**
relationship and grounds it in the male's duty of material maintenance; reads *qanitat* as a
willing devotional disposition (not external "obedience," *ta'a*) and *nushuz* as a **state of
disorder** between the couple rather than wifely insubordination. Wadud adopts Qutb on
*qanitat*/*nushuz* while rejecting his retention of family hierarchy.

**3. Reformist / feminist ([[amina-wadud|Wadud]], building on [[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur
Rahman]]).** On a holistic, [[wadud-quran-and-woman|"hermeneutics of tawhid"]] reading:
- *qiwama* is **conditioned** by the particle *bi-* on two contingent facts — *faddala* and
  material support — so "if either condition fails, the man is not *qawwam*"; it names a
  *responsibility* (especially toward the childbearing woman), extendable beyond the material,
  not a rank.
- *faddala* is *ba'd*-over-*ba'd*, never men-as-class over women-as-class; the only specified
  material preference is the **double inheritance share** (Q 4:7), itself reciprocal with the
  maintenance duty.
- *darajah* (2:228) is bound to its **divorce** context (the unilateral male repudiation),
  not a universal hierarchy.
- *nushuz* = mutual discord (cf. 4:128); the three steps are a graduated **de-escalation**
  ending in possible divorce; *daraba* does not necessarily mean violence and, against the
  background of pre-Islamic abuse, functions as a **restriction**, not a license — "not
  permission, but a severe restriction of existing practices."

**4. "No-harm" / symbolic-strike and re-vocalization readings.** A wider modern current
(Laleh Bakhtiar's "go away from them"; *adribu* re-readings) presses *daraba* away from
"strike" entirely — converging with (3) on outcome while differing on philology. (Recorded as a
family of reformist positions.)

## Why It Stays Open

The dispute is not over the consonantal text (undisputed) but over **lexical range** (*daraba*,
*qiwama*, *nushuz*), **scope** (marriage vs. society; this verse vs. universal rule), and
**hermeneutic** (atomistic literalism vs. principle-extracting historicism). It is therefore
a live, unresolved controversy — and a flashpoint in the legitimacy struggle over
[[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] and women's religious authority.
