# "All Israel Will Be Saved" (Romans 11:26)

Romans 11:25–27 contains Paul's most explicit statement about the future of ethnic Israel in relation to the gospel: "a partial hardening has come upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And in this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written, 'The Deliverer will come from Zion, he will banish ungodliness from Jacob; and this will be my covenant with them when I take away their sins.'" The phrase "all Israel" has been the subject of intense exegetical and theological debate, touching on questions of eschatology, the church-Israel relationship, and the nature of God's covenant faithfulness.

## The Context

Romans 9–11 is Paul's sustained answer to the question raised by Jewish unbelief: Has God's word (his covenant promise to Israel) failed? Paul's answer in three movements:
- **Chapter 9**: God never promised salvation to every ethnic Israelite; there has always been an elect remnant (Isaac not Ishmael; Jacob not Esau)
- **Chapter 10**: Israel's failure is not God's fault; the gospel was preached and Israel rejected it
- **Chapter 11**: Israel's hardening is temporary and part of God's larger plan; "all Israel will be saved" at the end

The immediate context of 11:25–27:
- A "mystery" is being disclosed (11:25) — something previously hidden, now revealed
- Israel's hardening is "partial" (*apo merous*) — not every Israelite, not permanent
- The hardening lasts "until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in" — a temporal limit
- "And in this way" (*kai houtōs*) introduces the resolution: "all Israel will be saved"
- OT backing: Isaiah 59:20–21; 27:9 (the Deliverer coming from Zion)

## The Positions

### 1. Future Mass Conversion of Ethnic Israel (Schreiner)

[[thomas-schreiner|Schreiner]] (BECNT Romans, 1998): "all Israel" = the mass of ethnic Israel, saved in a future event near the second coming. The hardening of Israel is temporary; when the Gentile mission reaches its appointed completion ("the fullness of the Gentiles"), God will turn Israel from unbelief to faith on a large scale.

Key arguments:
- "Israel" throughout Romans 9–11 consistently refers to ethnic Israel (9:4; 9:6, 27, 31; 10:1; 10:19, 21; 11:1–2, 7, 25). A sudden shift to "the church" at 11:26 is exegetically unnatural.
- The context presupposes an ethnic entity being *added to* after previously being mostly excluded: Gentiles are grafted in (11:17–24); natural branches are broken off but not amputated permanently.
- Paul's anguish over unbelieving Israel in 9:1–5 and 10:1 concerns people who are currently unsaved; the hope of 11:26 resolves that anguish with a future salvation event.
- *Kai houtōs* ("and in this way") marks the *manner* of Israel's salvation, not merely the *timing* — through the provocation to jealousy mechanism described in 11:11–24.
- The OT citations (Isa 59:20–21; 27:9) speak of a coming Deliverer and a covenant of forgiveness — language pointing toward a decisive eschatological event.

Schreiner: "God will turn ungodliness from Jacob and promises that at the end of history, probably near the second coming, 'all Israel will be saved.'" This does not require every single Israelite to be saved, but a large-scale, climactic turning of the Jewish people to Christ — specifically the **end-time generation** of ethnic Israel alive at the parousia.

Three refinements from the BECNT re-reading sharpen his position:
- **Timing tied to resurrection.** "Their acceptance" is "life from the dead" (11:15), which Schreiner reads as **physical resurrection / the climax of this age** — Israel's salvation *follows* the fullness of the Gentiles (11:25) and immediately precedes the general resurrection.
- **No hope for those who die in unbelief.** Crucially, "no hope… is held out to Israelites who die without acknowledging Jesus as their Messiah." Those hardened who "died in their hardening will face judgment"; the promise concerns the future generation, not a retroactive salvation of all Israelites throughout history.
- **The olive tree is *not* ethnic Israel.** In 11:17–24 the cultivated olive tree = the **people of God (Jews and Gentiles together)**, not ethnic Israel — since unbelieving Jewish branches are broken off and believing Gentile branches grafted in. The root/firstfruits (11:16) = the **patriarchs**. This keeps Schreiner's future-ethnic-Israel reading distinct from any "Israel = the tree" identification.

He explicitly rejects the two-covenant / *Sonderweg* implication: Israel's future salvation is by the **same gospel of Christ** (11:26's Deliverer removing ungodliness from Jacob), not a separate covenant path.

### 2. The Elect Remnant Throughout Church History (Some Reformed Amillennialists)

Some Reformed amillennial interpreters (Hodge, Berkhof, and others) argue that "all Israel" = the full number of elect ethnic Jews who will be saved cumulatively across church history — not a dramatic future event but the ongoing ingathering of Jewish believers generation by generation. "All Israel" = the complete Israel of election, not every Israelite or even a majority.

**Arguments**: Fits with the remnant theology of Romans 9 and 11:1–6; avoids positing a special end-time exception to normal gospel proclamation; "in this way" (*kai houtōs*) may describe the manner of the whole argument (through the Gentile mission provoking jealousy), not a single event.

**Difficulty**: "All Israel" most naturally suggests more than a remnant; the contrast between "part" (*apo merous*) and "all" implies a larger future group than is currently being saved; the "mystery" language (11:25) suggests something new and surprising, not the continuation of current patterns.

### 3. The Church — Spiritual Israel (Amillennialism; Barth)

Some amillennial interpreters identify "all Israel" with the whole people of God — the church comprising both Jewish and Gentile believers. "Israel" in Paul can be used of the spiritual seed of Abraham (Gal 6:16 — "the Israel of God"). On this reading, "all Israel" = all the elect, saved through the interplay of Jewish and Gentile missions described in 11:11–32.

**Arguments**: Avoids a two-track soteriology; aligns with Galatians 3 and Ephesians 2 (one people of God); Galatians 6:16 uses "Israel" of the church.

**Difficulties**: Romans 9–11 consistently uses "Israel" for ethnic Israel; the "mystery" of 11:25 is precisely that *ethnic* Israel, which is currently hardened, will be saved — making the referent the church would evacuate the mystery; the contrast throughout chs. 9–11 between Israel and the Gentile nations requires ethnic distinction.

### 4. National Israel Restored (Dispensational Premillennialism)

Dispensational interpreters read "all Israel" as the national restoration of the Jewish people at the end of the age, often connected with the Great Tribulation, the millennium, and Israel's national repentance (Zechariah 12:10–14). This may involve a literal land restoration and a distinct Jewish role in the millennium.

**Distinctive feature**: The emphasis on national corporate identity and a distinctive future for Israel as a nation-state, separate from the church.

**Difficulty for Schreiner's reading**: Schreiner does not embrace dispensationalism's sharp Israel-church distinction or its "two-covenant" implications; the future salvation of Israel is by the same gospel of Christ, not through a separate covenant path.

## Exegetical Cruxes

**"All Israel"** (*pas Israēl*): Does "all" mean every individual, the elect number, or the majority? Most interpreters grant it means "Israel as a whole" rather than mathematically every Israelite.

***Kai houtōs*** ("And in this way" / "And so"): Does this refer to temporal sequence ("after this, all Israel will be saved") or manner ("in this manner all Israel is saved throughout history")? Most modern interpreters: manner, describing the mechanism of 11:11–32 (Gentile fullness → Jewish jealousy → Jewish salvation). But Schreiner: the "fullness of the Gentiles" as the precondition still implies a temporal sequence.

**The OT citations (Isa 59:20–21; 27:9)**: Is the "Deliverer" Jesus at his first coming (atonement), or Jesus at his second coming (parousia)? Schreiner: the context points toward the parousia, when Christ comes to bring Israel's salvation to completion.

## Resolution Status

**Open / tradition-specific.** The ethnic-Israel future-salvation reading (Schreiner) commands majority support among evangelical NT scholars. The church-as-Israel reading is a minority position even within amillennialism. The elect-remnant reading is defended by serious exegetes. Dispensationalism holds the national restoration reading with different presuppositions about Israel and church.
