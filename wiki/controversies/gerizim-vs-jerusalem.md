# Gerizim vs. Jerusalem — the Chosen Place of Worship

## The Issue in Dispute

Deuteronomy commands that sacrificial worship occur only at **"the place that the LORD your
God will choose"** (Deut. 12), but — pointedly — **never names it**. Two communities claiming
descent from ancient Israel give opposite answers: the **[[samaritans|Samaritans]]** hold
that the chosen place is **[[mount-gerizim|Mount Gerizim]]** above [[shechem|Shechem]], while
**Jews** hold that it is **[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] / Mount Zion** and its Temple. The dispute
is not a marginal disagreement but the **structural fault line of the Samaritan–Jewish
schism** (Leith, Coogan Ch. 8).

## The Positions

**Samaritan**: Gerizim is *the* place God ordained. The claim is grounded in the
**Samaritan Pentateuch**, which at **Deuteronomy 27.4** reads **"Mount Gerizim"** (where the
altar of unhewn stones is to be built) rather than the Masoretic **"Mount Ebal,"** and which
includes a **Gerizim "tenth commandment"** making worship there a Torah obligation. The
Samaritans appeal to Gerizim as the **mountain of blessing** (Deut. 11.29; 27.12), to
patriarchal altars at Shechem, and to the antiquity of the central highlands' cult. They
maintain that the Jerusalem Temple "never could be" the chosen place (Coogan Ch. 8 quotes the
Samaritan view that Jerusalem "was not and never could be that sacred place").

**Jewish**: Jerusalem/Zion is the chosen place — the city of [[david|David]], site of
Solomon's Temple, "the place where the LORD caused his name to dwell." The Masoretic
Deuteronomy 27.4 reads **Ebal**, and the whole Deuteronomistic History narrates the
**centralization of legitimate worship in Jerusalem** and the delegitimizing of rival
sanctuaries (including [[bethel|Bethel]] and [[dan|Dan]]). Returning exiles **rebuffed
Samaritan offers** to help rebuild the Temple, and Nehemiah recoiled at intermarriage with
the Samarian leadership (Neh. 13.28–29).

**Christian**: **John 4.20–24** stages the dispute directly — the Samaritan woman: "Our
ancestors worshiped on **this mountain**, but you say… in **Jerusalem**." Jesus' answer
**relativizes both**: "the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this
mountain nor in Jerusalem… true worshipers will worship in spirit and truth." Early
Christianity thus reframes, rather than adjudicates, the sacred-place question.

## Historical Trajectory

A Samaritan temple stood on Gerizim by the **Persian / early Hellenistic period** —
Josephus links it to the governor **Sanballat** and a marriage with the Jerusalem priesthood
and dates it to the late 4th century, but excavation at **Tell er-Ras / the summit** points
to a substantial sanctuary already in the **5th century** (Coogan Ch. 8). The Hasmonean
**John Hyrcanus destroyed the Gerizim temple in 128 BCE** and later razed Shechem and
Samaria — hardening the breach. The Samaritans never relocated their cult; their community at
**Kiryat Luza** still offers the **Passover sacrifice** on Gerizim today.

## Status

**Open / tradition-specific.** There is no shared adjudication: each community reads the
**same Deuteronomic texts** to opposite conclusions, and the textual crux (Deut. 27.4
Gerizim vs. Ebal) is itself contested, with some scholars judging the **Samaritan "Gerizim"
reading to be the older**. The dispute remains live in a way most ancient controversies are
not, because the Samaritan community and its Gerizim worship survive.
