# The Third Cycle of Job — Textual Disorder and Reconstruction

The dialogue of [[book-of-job|Job]] runs in three cycles of speeches. The first two are regular:
each of the three comforters speaks and Job answers. The third breaks down. This page records the
positions on what happened and what, if anything, should be done about it. It does not adjudicate.

## The symptoms

These are descriptive facts about the received Masoretic text and are not in dispute:

1. [[eliphaz-the-temanite|Eliphaz]]'s third speech (22:1–30) is normal — [[john-hartley|Hartley]]
   counts 38 lines — and Job's answer that follows is 99 lines.
2. [[bildad-the-shuhite|Bildad]]'s third speech (25:1–6) is only 10 lines, with neither
   introduction nor conclusion. For comparison, Hartley's second-cycle counts give Eliphaz 68
   lines and the other friends 47 and 56.
3. [[zophar-the-naamathite|Zophar]] has no third speech at all.
4. Job's closing response (chs. 26–28) is unusually long and contains material that reads like the
   friends' own position — above all 27:13–23, which asserts the certain doom of the wicked, and
   the [[wisdom-literature|Hymn to Wisdom]] of ch. 28.
5. Job 24:18–24 is textually obscure, has attracted many proposed emendations, and appears to
   affirm the [[doctrine-of-retribution|doctrine of retribution]] that the surrounding complaint
   (24:1–17) is attacking.

A further datum constrains any reconstruction: the Qumran Targum of Job (11QtgJob) **supports the
MT order of chs. 24–27**. Whatever disorder exists is therefore early enough to precede that
witness, and cannot be treated as a late scribal accident.

## Positions

### Position 1: Full redistribution (Gordis)

Robert Gordis reassigns the material across three speakers to reconstruct a complete cycle:
Bildad receives 25:1–6 and 26:5–14; Job retains 26:1–4 and 27:1–12; **Zophar receives 27:13–23**
as a third speech.

*Recorded as Hartley reproduces it; the wiki has not ingested Gordis directly.*

### Position 2: Full redistribution, alternative arrangement (Hölscher, similarly Duhm)

Bildad receives 25:1; 26:2–4; 25:2–6; 26:5–14 — i.e. the two chapters are interleaved — with Job
retaining 27:1–12 and Zophar again receiving 27:13–23.

### Position 3: Minimal reconstruction (Hartley's own)

Hartley moves **exactly one block and creates nothing**:

| Passage | MT assignment | Hartley's assignment | His stated confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24:18–24 | Job | **Job** (as imprecation, not doctrine) | "Cautiously accepting" |
| 24:25 | Job | Job | "clearly belongs to Job" |
| 25:1–6 | Bildad | Bildad, but damaged and incomplete | certain it is damaged |
| 26:1–14 | Job | Job | confident |
| 27:1 (superscription) | — | secondary editorial insertion | "seems to be secondary" |
| 27:2–12 | Job | Job | certain |
| **27:13–23** | Job | **Bildad** | "if it is correctly assigned to him" |
| ch. 28 | Job | outside the dialogue entirely | reported as the received view |
| Zophar's third | — | never existed | asserted with a positive reason |

His four arguments for moving 27:13–23 to Bildad, in his own order: 25:1–6 "lack[s] both an
introduction and a conclusion" and is "far too short"; the wording of 27:13–23 "is closer to the
comforters' type of discussion than to Job's"; the block "offers a direct rebuttal to the ideas Job
was struggling with in ch. 24"; and joining them yields a fuller speech. Because the resulting join
is rough, he adds that "it may be assumed that other lines have been lost after 25:6 and both
before and after 27:13–23."

Hartley states his governing restraint against the fuller reconstructions: "Each reconstruction is
still faced with very rough transitions between sections and lacks the full character of the
speeches of the other two cycles. The wide variety of the multiple reconstructions cautions further
against major reapportioning of the verses in chs. 24–31 in order to achieve a full third cycle."
He concedes his own result is not clean either — "The result is not a full-orbed speech for either
person."

### Position 4: Relocate 24:18–24 to a friend

Hartley reports that "many contemporary interpreters" (unnamed in the commentary body) relocate
24:18–24 into either Bildad's or Zophar's third speech, on the ground that it supports retribution
and so must be friend-material displaced by the cycle's breakup.

### Position 5: Retain 24:18–24 for Job as imprecation (Andersen, followed by Hartley)

Francis Andersen reads v. 18's style as imprecatory: these are curses Job *utters* against the
lawless, not doctrine Job *affirms*. Hartley adopts this "cautiously," on three grounds — the style
"accords more with ch. 24" than with the alternatives; questioning God's method of governing is not
the same as abandoning belief in retribution ("While seriously considering the exceptions to the
rule, he does not throw out everything he has believed about justice"); and Job functionally *wants*
the wicked punished, as proof that God will likewise act justly in his own case. Hartley translates
the verses accordingly in the optative — "let their portion be accursed in the land." Andersen's
summary, which Hartley quotes: "His position is more balanced, but more baffled."

## Zophar's missing third speech — two distinct explanations

Hartley runs **two non-identical tracks**, and they should not be conflated:

- **Transmission damage.** The cycle "has suffered severely early in its transmission"; 27:13–23 is
  physically dislocated and lines are lost around the joins.
- **Literary design.** Zophar's silence is *meaningful*. In the Aim to Zophar's second speech
  Hartley argues: "If this is true, Zophar has closed his thinking in reference to Job's guilt. That
  is why he would have no reason to deliver a third speech. Having made up his mind that Job's fate
  is sealed, he would have nothing more to say." He supports this with speech-length data — Eliphaz's
  three speeches shorten progressively (98, 68, 58 lines) and Bildad's likewise (43, 41), but
  Zophar's second is *longer* than his first (56 vs 40), which Hartley reads as the author signalling
  that Zophar will not speak again.

Track two, not track one, is Hartley's answer to the missing speech. He never posits a lost Zophar
speech.

## Ch. 28 at the margin of the dispute

Hartley removes the Hymn to Wisdom from the dialogue rather than reassigning it: "Since its abstract,
reflective tone does not match well any of the speakers, it is taken to be a piece that stands
outside the dialogue. It functions as a bridge between the dialogue and the groups of speeches that
are coming." He nonetheless defends it as authorial and integral, rejecting Gordis's scribal-insertion
account and the relocations proposed by Buttenweiser (to just before the Yahweh speeches) and
Szczygiel (to after Job's final response) — "these suggestions do not provide the hymn a better
setting or improve the book's structure."

## Unresolved tensions in Hartley's own account

Recorded rather than smoothed:

- **A verse-range inconsistency.** His reconstruction gives Bildad **27:13**–23 throughout, but when
  rejecting the relocation of 24:18–24 he refers to "a reconstructed speech of Zophar from
  **27:14**–23." The second may be his description of someone else's hypothesis rather than his own
  assignment; the printed text does not say.
- **Job's eighth response** is given as 26:1–14; 27:2–12 in his discussion of the reconstruction, but
  as 26:1–27:12 in the Introduction's outline.
- 21:16b and 22:18b are a verbatim doublet assigned to two different speakers, which Hartley never
  discusses as a transmission problem.
