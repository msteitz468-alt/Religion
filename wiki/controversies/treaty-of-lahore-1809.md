# Treaty of Amritsar / Lahore (25 April 1809)

The agreement, negotiated by Charles Metcalfe, that fixed the **Sutlej** as the eastern boundary of
[[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]'s Punjab and placed the cis-Sutlej (Malwa) Sikh chiefs under British
protection. This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1, Chs. 12–13**.

## Account (Khushwant Singh)

When a Franco-Russian threat prompted the East India Company to court the Punjab, **Charles Metcalfe** (then
23) was sent to Ranjit Singh's camp (first meeting near Kasur, September 1808). As Ochterlony's troops moved
up to the Sutlej, the British issued a proclamation (9 February 1809) securing the cis-Sutlej chiefs from
"the authority and influence of Maharajah Ranjit Singh." Ranjit's general Mohkam Chand urged war; Fakir
Azizuddin counselled submission, and Ranjit "swallowed his pride and agreed to recognize the Sutlej as the
Punjab's eastern boundary" (Singh, ch. 13).

Singh's verdict is sharp: "The treaty of Lahore was a grievous blow to the Punjabi dream of a unified
Punjab. Malwa was forever cut off from the rest of the country." Within two or three years, he notes, the
British converted their position "from protecting to a sovereign power" over the Malwa chiefs (proclamation
of 22 August 1811) — a step a Secret Committee in London itself acknowledged departed from the principle
fixing the Jumna as Britain's western limit. The cis-Sutlej chiefs had foreseen the squeeze, one of them
saying British protection "will be like consumption, which takes a long time to kill," whereas Ranjit's
advent "will be like a stroke of paralysis" (ch. 13).
