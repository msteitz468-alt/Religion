# Wang Bi vs. Heshang Gong

The defining hermeneutical divide of [[daoism|Daoism]] and the wiki's exhibit of the **"commentary-as-
transformation"** framework: the two most influential commentaries on the **[[daodejing|Daodejing]]** read
the *same text* toward two utterly different ends — and each commentary is itself a different kind of
*practice*.

## The Two Readings

- **[[wang-bi|Wang Bi]] (226–249) — philosophical / metaphysical (*xuanxue*).** The [[dao|Dao]] is **wu**
  (non-being), the ontological root of all determinate being; the commentary cultivates **metaphysical
  insight**. The "valley spirit" and "mysterious female" are images of the generative ground of being.
  This is the [[philosophical-daoism|philosophical-Daoist]] classic, the literati's "received" text.
- **[[heshang-gong|Heshang Gong]] — religious / longevity-cultivation.** The same images are decoded as
  the **adept's inner body** — organs, breath, vital essences; the commentary is a **manual for nourishing
  life (*yangsheng*) and attaining immortality**. This is the [[religious-daoism|religious-Daoist]]
  classic, foundational for internal alchemy (*neidan*).

## Why It Matters

The divergence is not over *what the words say* at the literal level but over **what the text is for** —
metaphysics vs. soteriological body-practice. This is a third distinct pattern of scriptural division in
the wiki, alongside:

- the **multi-canon** split of Buddhism ([[heart-sutra|Mahayana sutras]] `rejected_by: [theravada]`), and
- the **one-canon-opposite-metaphysics** split of Hindu Vedanta
  ([[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|Advaita/Vishishtadvaita/Dvaita]]).

Here the canon is shared (`canon_scope: canonical_for [daoism]`) and even the metaphysics is not strictly
opposed; what differs is the **mode and purpose of reading** — the commentary as transformation. Recorded
without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
