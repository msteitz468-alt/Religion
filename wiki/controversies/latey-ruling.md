# Latey Ruling: *Re B & G Wards* (High Court, London, July 23, 1984)

## Background

In **July 1984**, the High Court of London heard a child custody case (*Re B & G Wards*) in which the question of the children's exposure to Scientology was central. Justice **William Latey** investigated Scientology in the course of the proceedings.

## Finding

Justice Latey issued a ruling on **July 23, 1984** — just one month after the American [[armstrong-case|Armstrong Case]] ruling — that included sweeping characterizations of Scientology and its founder:

- Scientology: **"corrupt, sinister, and dangerous"**
- [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]: **"a fraud and a liar"**
- The organization uses "techniques of thought manipulation and control... to make its adherents utterly dependent"

The ruling found that it was not in the children's best interests to be raised in the Scientology environment.

## Significance

1. **Contemporaneous with Armstrong**: the Latey and Breckenridge rulings (both 1984) form the first cluster of significant judicial findings against Scientology; their proximity amplified their combined impact.
2. **Independent assessment**: Justice Latey arrived at his conclusions independently of the American proceedings, based on evidence presented in the English case — including [[gerry-armstrong|Gerry Armstrong]]'s testimony (Armstrong appeared in both proceedings).
3. **Long-term use**: the ruling is cited extensively in subsequent scholarship (including Atack) and in legal proceedings as evidence that courts in multiple jurisdictions have made adverse findings.

## Contradiction Protocol

- **Church position**: the ruling was a custody determination, not a theological assessment; Latey's characterizations exceeded the scope of the proceedings; the ruling has no authority beyond its immediate context.
- **Critics' position**: the ruling is a considered judicial finding from a senior English judge who examined evidence carefully and independently.

Recorded without adjudication.
