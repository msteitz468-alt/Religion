# Hubbard Military Records Forgery

A central controversy in the scholarly evaluation of [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]'s biography is the status of his **World War II military record** — specifically the Church's claim that Hubbard was a decorated combat veteran who was "blinded with injured optic nerves" and "lame from wounds" and cured himself through early Dianetics techniques.

## The Official Record vs. the Church's Narrative

The **official Navy record** (National Personnel Records Center, St. Louis, Missouri) shows:
- Hubbard was found "not qualified for command" after a 1942 incident involving unauthorized live-fire exercises in Mexican territorial waters
- He was assessed as "not temperamentally fitted for independent duty assignment"
- He graduated at the **bottom of his class** in his sub-chasing training course
- His famous "depth charge" battle off the Oregon coast (allegedly 68 hours against a Japanese submarine) was investigated and found to have no Japanese submarine present; the Navy concluded no contact had occurred
- No combat wounds; no Purple Heart; no "Marine Medal" — a decoration that, per independent researchers, **did not exist as described**

## The Forged Document

[[tommy-davis|Tommy Davis]] presented Wright's New Yorker fact-checkers with a **"Notice of Separation"** purporting to show Hubbard's honorable separation and military decorations.

Two archivists at the **St. Louis National Archives** — Charles Pellegrini and Erik Voelz — independently examined the document and pronounced it a **forgery**:
- The signatory named "Howard D. Thompson" does not appear in any Navy records
- **Font inconsistencies**: typography inconsistent with wartime military documents
- **Medals that did not exist** until after Hubbard left the service were listed
- **Ditto marks** (shorthand notation style) inconsistent with the period

Wright's New Yorker fact-checker Yvette Siegert traveled to St. Louis specifically to obtain the official records. The article's official documents and the Church's submitted document can be compared online (per Wright's notes; New Yorker DocumentCloud posting).

## Significance

The military record controversy is not merely biographical trivia. Hubbard's entire public mythology — his self-description as a "nuclear physicist," "commander," "war hero," and miracle self-healer — is the **foundation of his authority** as the founder of Scientology. The religion's hermeneutical claim is that Hubbard discovered universal truths through his own extraordinary research and experience. If the biographical claims are fabricated, this does not necessarily falsify the doctrines, but it does undermine the *charismatic authority* on which the institution rests.

Wright draws the comparison: Joseph Smith's fraudulent translation of the Egyptian papyri (confirmed after 1966 discovery of the originals) did not destroy Mormonism. Similarly, Hubbard's documented biographical fabrications have not destroyed Scientology — but they are part of the public record and must be.

## The Church's Response

The Church maintains that its "Notice of Separation" document is authentic; it has produced what it describes as further corroborating records. The Church points to the 1973 fire at the National Personnel Records Center that destroyed many Army and Air Force records (Wright notes the fire affected only Army/Air Force records, not Navy records) as an explanation for gaps.

Tommy Davis, when confronted with the forgers' findings, eventually stopped representing the Church. He claimed the homophobic passages in Hubbard's published works were inserted by "a bigot" — another claim Wright characterizes as implausible and undocumented.
