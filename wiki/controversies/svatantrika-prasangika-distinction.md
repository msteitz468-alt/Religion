# The Svātantrika/Prāsaṅgika Distinction

A foundational dispute within [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] over the correct *method* for establishing
[[sunyata|emptiness]] — and, beneath it, over what emptiness denies. The Tibetan labels *Rang rgyud
pa* (Svātantrika) and *Thal 'gyur ba* (Prāsaṅgika) are later, but, per
[[candrakirti|Candrakīrti]]'s *Prasannapadā*, the substance is Indian.

## Origin of the Dispute

[[buddhapalita|Buddhapālita]], commenting on [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s refutation of the four kinds
of production ("from self, from other, from both, without cause"), negated them by drawing out the
absurd consequences (*prasaṅga*) of the opponents' own positions. **[[bhavaviveka|Bhāvaviveka]]**
attacked him, holding that a Mādhyamika must state **autonomous** (*svatantra*) syllogisms — with a
subject and reason established by a valid cognition shared by both parties. **Candrakīrti** then
defended Buddhapālita and argued that Mādhyamikas should use *only* consequence arguments, not
autonomous ones — and in doing so founded what the Tibetans call the Prāsaṅgika position.

## The Positions

- **Svātantrika** (Bhāvaviveka; and, as Yogācāra-Svātantrika, **Śāntarakṣita** and
  [[kamalasila|Kamalaśīla]]): accepts that things exist **"by way of their own character"
  (*svalakṣaṇa*) conventionally**, even while denying intrinsic existence ultimately. Because a
  non-mistaken valid cognition can therefore establish a subject "appearing in common" to both
  parties, autonomous syllogisms are legitimate.
- **Prāsaṅgika** (Buddhapālita, Candrakīrti): denies intrinsic character **even conventionally**.
  Since no valid cognition establishes anything as existing by its own character, there is no
  commonly-appearing subject, so autonomous syllogisms collapse; the Mādhyamika argues only from
  premises the *opponent* accepts (consequences that, once drawn, generate inference in the opponent's
  mind).

## Tsong-kha-pa's Reading (Lamrim Chenmo Vol. III)

[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] devotes chapters 18–21 of the insight section to the question, and his
reading became the [[gelug|Gelug]] norm. Its claims:

1. **The criterion is conventional intrinsic character, not the "ultimately" qualifier.** He rejects
   as wrong the common Tibetan view that Svātantrikas are simply those who add "ultimately" to the
   object of negation — definitive sūtras add it too. The real divide is whether things have
   intrinsic *character* even conventionally. The explicit criterion (paraphrasing *Prasannapadā*):
   when the reason is established for *both* parties by a valid cognition apprehending intrinsic
   character, the argument is autonomous; when it is established only for the *opponent*, it is
   Prāsaṅgika.
2. **The Prāsaṅgika does have a system and assertions.** Against several Tibetan readings (which he
   catalogues and refutes) that the Prāsaṅgika "has nothing to assert even conventionally," he insists
   that the "no thesis" passages of Nāgārjuna, Āryadeva, and Candrakīrti deny only theses/cognitions
   framed "by way of intrinsic character," never conventional assertion as such. The Prāsaṅgika
   affirms dependent-arising, the two truths, and four theses established by reasoning.
3. **The critique is self-consistent.** Refuting autonomous syllogisms does not disable the
   Prāsaṅgika's own arguments, because a consequence using what the opponent accepts (e.g. the
   eye-that-does-not-see-itself) still refutes the opponent and converts to an inference — without
   requiring a commonly-established intrinsic-character subject.

He declares his own allegiance plainly: "we are followers of the Prāsaṅgika system… we refute
essential or intrinsic nature even conventionally; yet all that has been taught about cyclic
existence and nirvana must be fully compatible with that refutation."

## Status

**Tradition-specific.** Within Indo-Tibetan Madhyamaka the dispute is live and the terms are
contested; the Gelug elevation of Prāsaṅgika as the single definitive view is itself disputed by
other Tibetan schools. Per the wiki's [Contradiction Protocol], the positions are recorded, not
adjudicated.
