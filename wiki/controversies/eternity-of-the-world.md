# Eternity of the World vs. Temporal Creation

## The Dispute

The question of whether the world is **pre-eternal** (*qadīm*) — having no temporal beginning, co-eternal with God — or **temporally originated** (*ḥādith*) — brought into being at a specific moment by a free divine act — is the first and most fundamental controversy between the Islamic philosophical tradition (*[[falsafa]]*) and the *[[kalam]]* theologians in the medieval Islamic world.

Al-Ghazālī's *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (late 11th c.) is the primary surviving record of how the Ash'arite position confronted and attempted to demolish the philosophers' position on this question. Al-Ghazālī explicitly presents both sides before rebutting the philosophers. His stated purpose is to "muddy their doctrine and throw dust in the face of their proofs with that which would reveal their incoherence," not to defend a specific positive doctrine in this work (First Discussion, §134).

---

## Position 1: The Philosophers (*Falsafa*) — World Is Pre-Eternal

**Holders**: The falsafa tradition as represented by [[al-farabi]] and [[avicenna]] (and tracing back to Aristotle and Plotinus, not in the text).

**Core claim**: The world has "never ceased to exist with God ... to be an effect of His, to exist along with Him, not being posterior to Him in time, in the way the effect coexists along with the cause and light along with the sun" (§1). God's priority over the world is "a priority in essence and rank, not in time" (§1).

**Principal arguments** (as reconstructed and reported by al-Ghazālī):

1. **The preponderance argument (Proof 1)**: A temporal existent cannot proceed from an Eternal without a change in the Eternal — a new "giver of preponderance." Any such change is itself a temporal event requiring explanation, regressing infinitely. Hence, if the Eternal is unchanging, the world must be co-eternal (§6–10).

2. **The impossibility of a temporal first event via eternal will (elaboration of Proof 1)**: Before the world, the wilier existed, the will existed, and its relation to the willed existed. Nothing came anew. How, then, did the object of will come into existence anew when "the state of renewal did not differ from the previous state in anything whatsoever" (§16)? An eternal necessitating cause with all conditions fulfilled cannot be followed by delay in its effect.

3. **The pre-eternity of time (Proof 2)**: If God is prior to the world in time, there was a time before the world — but that prior time is itself real temporal extension, which must itself have preceded the world's existence, entailing a pre-eternal time. Since time is co-extensive with motion, motion (and the heavens) must also be pre-eternal (§78–80).

4. **Eternal temporal possibilities (secondary proof)**: God could have created the world earlier by any magnitude; such hypothetical possibilities have measurable quantity, which is time; hence time pre-existed the world (§96–98).

5. **Eternal possibility (Proof 3)**: The world's existence is eternally possible; possibility has no beginning; therefore the possible existent itself is eternal (§108–109).

6. **Pre-eternity of matter (Proof 4)**: Possibility is relational and requires a material receptacle; primary matter therefore cannot have been temporally originated (§112–114).

7. **Eternal circular motion as mediating principle**: The eternal circular motion of the celestial spheres, necessitated by the eternal celestial souls, provides the bridge between Eternal God and temporal sub-lunar events. "It is inconceivable for a temporal event to proceed from an eternal except through the mediation of an eternal circular movement" (§74).

**Implication for divine will**: The philosophers allow that God's will cannot "specifically relate to one of the two [equally] possible [existents] through [sheer] coincidence" (§39). A will that could select between perfectly similar alternatives without a differentiating reason would be incomprehensible. Hence God does not freely choose between alternatives; He necessarily produces what is best or what is necessitated by His nature.

## Position 2: Ash'arite / Kalam — World Is Temporally Originated

**Holder**: [[al-ghazali]] (representing the [[asharism|Ash'arite]] school of *kalam*).

**Core claim**: The world was "temporally created by an eternal will that decreed its existence at the time in which it came to be; that [the preceding] nonexistence continued to the point at which [the world] began; that existence prior to this was not willed and for this reason did not occur" (§13).

**Key commitments**:

- **Time is created**: "Duration and time are both created" (§36). There was no pre-creation temporal interval. The question "why did God not create sooner?" is malformed: "before" the world there is no "before" in any real, existent sense, just as "beyond" the world's surface there is no void or filled space. Both are errors of the estimative faculty (*wahm*) (§82, §86–87, §92–94).

- **The estimative faculty vs. reason**: The estimative faculty cannot conceive an event without a temporal "before," just as it cannot conceive a finite body without an "outside." But clear reason can accept both: "clear reason does not disallow a first beginning that is preceded by nothing; and if the estimation falls short of grasping this, one must not heed it" (§94).

- **Divine will as irreducible specifier**: "Will stands as an expression for an attribute whose function — nay, its essence — is to differentiate a thing from its similar" (§41). The demand for a further reason why God's will selected *this* moment is a category error — it misunderstands what will *is*. Even in human experience, a person facing two perfectly equal options will inevitably select one through an act of will that is not determined by any external differentiator; to deny this is to say he would remain perpetually undecided, "which ... is impossible, its falsity known by necessity" (§46).

- **The philosophers' own parallel difficulties**: The philosophers cannot explain why the celestial poles are located where they are among the infinite, similar points of the celestial sphere (§52–57), or why the celestial spheres move in the directions they do rather than the reverse (§58–64). These selections among similars are unavoidable on their principles too.

- **Possibility as a mental judgment**: "Possibility, necessity, and impossibility" refer back to "rational judgments," not to mind-independent material receptacles (§115–127). Universals likewise exist in the mind, not in the concrete — a position the philosophers themselves hold.

- **Ghazālī's methodological disclaimer**: This work does not defend creation-in-time positively. It only "muddies" the philosophers' proofs. The positive Ash'arite case was to be made in the planned *Principles of Belief* (§134).

## Position 3: Averroes — Neither Side Demonstrates

**Holder**: [[averroes|Averroes]] (Ibn Rushd), in the *[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahāfut
al-Tahāfut]]*, c. 1180 — a generation after Ghazālī.

Averroes defends the philosophers' pre-eternity but **rejects the framing** of both prior positions.
His verdict is that "neither the arguments of the theologians for the temporal creation of the
world… nor the arguments of the philosophers… suffice to reach absolute evidence or afford stringent
proof": the four proofs are "in the highest degree dialectical," and Ghazālī's objections are
sophistical. His own reconstruction:

- The world is in **"everlasting becoming"**; an eternal agent's act has no beginning ("a limited
  act can only be imagined of a limited agent"). God's priority is a **third kind** — "the priority
  of the unchanging timeless existence to the changing existence which is in time" — neither temporal
  nor the causal simultaneity of two temporal things.
- "**Will**" said of an eternal agent is **equivocal** with human will; the dispute is partly verbal.
- He distinguishes the impossible **actual/essential infinite** from the admissible
  **accidental/potential infinite**, which is "a necessary consequence of the existence of an eternal
  first principle."
- He suspects Ghazālī did not sincerely hold the Ashʿarite position, "recurring to the philosophers"
  in *The Niche for Lights*.

See [[eternity-of-the-world]] (concept) and [[harmony-of-religion-and-philosophy]].

## Points of Direct Conflict

| Issue | Philosophers | Ash'arite / Ghazālī |
|---|---|---|
| World's temporal status | Pre-eternal (*qadīm*) | Temporally originated (*ḥādith*) |
| Time itself | Co-eternal with motion / world | Created; no pre-creation time |
| Divine causation | Eternal necessitation (effect co-eternal with cause) | Free eternal will specifying a temporal moment |
| Divine will | Cannot select between similars without determinant | Defined precisely as the capacity to select between similars |
| Role of estimative faculty (*wahm*) | Trusted guide to temporal intuitions | Systematically misleading about limits of space and time |
| Matter | Pre-eternal (unoriginated) | Temporally created along with form |

## Resolution Status

**Tradition-specific**. Within the falsafa tradition as continued by [[averroes]], who wrote the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* in direct reply to al-Ghazālī, the philosophers' position on the world's pre-eternity was defended. Within the Ash'arite *kalam* tradition, al-Ghazālī's refutation of the philosophers was broadly accepted as successful. Neither side convinced the other. The question of pre-eternity was one of the three positions for which al-Ghazālī declared the philosophers guilty of unbelief (*[[takfir]]*) — a charge directly connected to the incompatibility of their view with Quranic cosmology — though the controversy over whether *takfir* is warranted is itself a further dispute within the Islamic tradition.
