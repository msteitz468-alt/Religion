# Armstrong Case: *Church of Scientology of California v. Gerald Armstrong*

**Case citation**: Church of Scientology of California v. Gerald Armstrong, Superior Court, Los Angeles County, Case No. C 420153.

## Background

[[gerry-armstrong|Gerry Armstrong]] was a [[sea-org|Sea Organization]] member assigned to gather Hubbard's personal papers for an authorized biography to be written by Omar Garrison. Working with approximately 20 boxes of documents he rescued from destruction at Gilman Hot Springs (Gold Base), Armstrong discovered that Hubbard's official biography was systematically fabricated — military records, academic credentials, and personal conduct all contradicted by the primary documents.

Armstrong left the Church in **November 1981** and took document copies with him. The Church filed suit in **August 1982** seeking return of the materials and damages.

## Judge Breckenridge's Ruling (June 22, 1984)

Judge **Paul Breckenridge** issued a ruling on June 22, 1984, that became the most significant judicial assessment of Hubbard and Scientology to that point. Key findings:

- [[l-ron-hubbard|Hubbard]] is **"schizophrenic and paranoid"**
- Hubbard is a **"pathological liar"**
- The Church is **"corrupt"** and a combination of "illegal and immoral acts"
- The documents Armstrong preserved were **genuine** and significant
- Armstrong's conduct was **justified** given the circumstances
- The Church's treatment of Armstrong after his departure constituted **Fair Game** in practice

The ruling also quoted from the documents themselves — including material confirming the gap between Hubbard's claimed and actual military record — and found the Church's witnesses less credible than Armstrong's.

## Settlement

In **December 1986**, Armstrong settled for **$800,000** under a confidentiality agreement. He subsequently challenged the enforceability of the confidentiality provisions on First Amendment grounds, and the Church has pursued additional litigation against him for alleged violations of the settlement terms.

## Significance

1. **Evidentiary**: The Armstrong case is the primary reason many of Hubbard's personal documents entered the public record; subsequent researchers (including Atack and Wright) drew on the trial record.
2. **Judicial assessment**: Breckenridge's findings are the most-cited judicial characterization of Hubbard; the Church consistently challenges their weight as the findings of a single trial court.
3. **Fair Game documentation**: The case demonstrates the Church's operational response to perceived threats: surveillance, multiple lawsuits, and public defamation of Armstrong as a "bigot" and thief.
4. **Ongoing litigation**: Armstrong became one of the most persistently litigated ex-members in the Church's history — embodying the dynamic described by Atack and others as the use of legal process as harassment.

## Contradiction Protocol

- **Church position**: Breckenridge's findings were biased; Armstrong is a thief and violator of fiduciary duty; the documents were obtained improperly and do not establish what Armstrong claims.
- **Armstrong / scholarly position**: the documents are genuine; the ruling is a factual finding based on evidence; the Church's response to the case is itself evidence of Fair Game.

Recorded without adjudication.
