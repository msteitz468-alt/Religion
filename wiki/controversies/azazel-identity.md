# Identity of Azazel (Leviticus 16)

On the Day of Atonement, Aaron casts lots over two goats: “one lot for the Lord and the other lot for Azazel” (Lev 16:8). The goat for YHWH is sacrificed; its blood is taken into the holy of holies. The goat for Azazel has the high priest confess Israel’s sins over it and is sent into the wilderness. What—or who—is **Azazel**?

This page records positions without adjudicating. Primary documentation in this wiki: [[gordon-wenham|Wenham]], *Leviticus* (NICOT) — [[wenham-leviticus-nicot]] (slice F, Day of Atonement).

## Position 1 — Demon / supernatural being of the wilderness

**Claim:** Azazel is a demonic or angelic figure associated with the desert; the goat returns sins to their author or removes them to the sphere of the chaotic Other.

**Support noted by Wenham:** majority modern scholarly leaning; Second Temple and later traditions that personify Azazel.

**Objection Wenham records:** even advocates usually deny that the goat is a *sacrifice or gift* to Azazel (sins merely sent back to their author). Still, the rite is easily misread as a demonic gift. Hertz’s objection: Lev 17:7 (next chapter) condemns offerings to satyrs—so homage to a wilderness demon can hardly be built into the holiest Temple rite.

**Related reception (not Wenham’s etymology):** Zoharic and broader Jewish mystical readings treat the goat for Azazel as regulated portion for the Other Side / Accuser (see [[leviticus]] Zohar section; [[holiness]]).

## Position 2 — “Complete destruction” (abstract noun)

**Claim (Hoffmann; Hertz, as reported by Wenham):** Azazel is a rare Hebrew noun meaning **complete destruction**. The goat is devoted to annihilation of sin, not to a personal being.

## Position 3 — “Rocky precipice”

**Claim (Rashi; refreshed by G. R. Driver, as reported by Wenham):** Azazel names the **precipitous, flinty rock** / land of cutting-off where the goat is destroyed. Rashi: “It was a precipitous and flinty rock.”

**Wenham’s linking observation:** if Lev 16:22 expands 16:10, “land of cutting off” may interpret Azazel toward “rocky/craggy”; if the phrase means a place that *cuts off*, it may favor “total destruction” instead.

## Position 4 — Wenham’s functional conclusion (etymology open)

Wenham does **not** force a single philology. He concludes:

> “Whether Azazel means, the mountain where the goat is destroyed, the sin which is given to destruction, or the evil angel who is given a bribe so that he does not become an accuser, it all comes back to the same basic idea: that **sin is exterminated from Israel**.”

**Ritual sense he emphasizes regardless of name:**
- Blood rites purge the sanctuary (unseen by the people)
- Scapegoat is the **public visual aid** that sin is removed
- Self-affliction (16:29–31) prevents cheap grace from watching the goat alone
- Washings after the rite prevent recontamination of camp and sanctuary

## Christian typological afterlife (separate from etymology)

Wenham notes the **NT itself makes nothing of the scapegoat** led into the wilderness; from the *Epistle of Barnabas* (~200 CE) Christians have often typed the sin-bearing goat as Christ crucified outside Jerusalem. Hebrews 9–10 centers the **blood-into-the-sanctuary** half of the day, not the wilderness goat. Do not collapse Barnabas typology into “what Leviticus means” or into “what Hebrews teaches.”
