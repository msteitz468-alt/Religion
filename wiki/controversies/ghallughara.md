# The Ghallugharas

In Sikh memory, two eighteenth-century massacres are named *ghallūghārā* (holocaust): the **Choṭā
Ghallūghārā** (lesser, June 1746) and the **Vaḍā Ghallūghārā** (great, 5 February 1762). They are the
defining traumas of the persecution era and a standing crux over casualty figures. This page draws on
**Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1, Chs. 7–8**.

## The Choṭā Ghallūghārā (June 1746)

After Lakhpat Rai's general massacre of the Sikhs of Lahore (10 March 1746), a Sikh concentration on the
Ravi north of Lahore was pursued and trapped against the hills: "nearly seven thousand were killed and three
thousand prisoners were taken to Lahore and executed at Shahidganj" (Singh, ch. 7). Singh records that this
"holocaust of June 1746 is known as the *choṭā ghallūghārā*, to distinguish it from the greater disaster,
the *vaḍā ghallūghārā* of 1762" (fn.20).

## The Vaḍā Ghallūghārā (5 February 1762)

During [[ahmad-shah-abdali|Abdali]]'s sixth invasion, the Afghan king covered 150 miles in two days to catch
a Sikh column near the village of Kup as it moved toward Barnala. "Most of the thirty thousand Sikhs were
non-combatants: old men, women, and children" (Singh, ch. 8); the Sikh soldiers formed "a living fortress"
around them. Abdali returned to Lahore with "fifty cartloads of heads," erected pyramids of skulls, and blew
up the [[amritsar|Harimandir]] again. Yet "by May 1762 they were up in arms again."

## The Controversy over Numbers

Singh records the spread of figures explicitly: "between twelve to thirty thousand mentioned by Muslim and
English historians to between five and seven thousand by Rajwade" (ch. 8, fn.36). His own emphasis is that
whatever the total, "most were non-combatants." The dispute is therefore twofold: the raw casualty count
(high Muslim/English figures vs. Rajwade's lower estimate), and the **interpretive** weight — Sikh tradition
reads the Ghallugharas as proof of the community's indestructibility, while the figures themselves remain
unsettled. Per the Contradiction Protocol, these positions are recorded without adjudication.
