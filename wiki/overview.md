# Wiki Overview

High-level synthesis of the wiki's current scope and contents.

---

## Scope

As of 2026-06-07, this wiki covers:

**Jewish tradition (rabbinic)**: The Babylonian Talmud and its rabbinic context — structure, hermeneutics (Halacha/Aggadah, Thirteen Middot), codification history, and key controversies (Oral Torah authority, Pharisee/Sadducee/Karaite divisions). Covered primarily through a traditionalist apologetic secondary source (Unterman 1952).

**Jewish tradition (mystical / Kabbalah)** — *added 2026-06-07*: [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] and its foundational text, the [[zohar|Zohar]] (Pritzker Edition Vols. I–XII, complete). Vols. I–IX (Matt) provide the main running Torah commentary (full Genesis–Deuteronomy) at the Sod/theosophic/theurgic level. Vol. X (Wolski) adds the earliest *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* stratum (Genesis-focused concealed midrash on soul, justice, patriarchs). Vols. XI–XII (Hecker and Wolski) add further *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* on the Megillot + the independent Zoharic Compositions (Idra Zuta, Heikhalot, Raza de-Razin physiognomy, Sitrei Otiyyot on letters/YHVH, Qav ha-Middah from Ein Sof, Tosefta, festival esoterica). Core theosophy: [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] and the ten [[sefirot|sefirot]]; the divine feminine ([[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]) and the holy union (*zivvuga qaddisha*); the demonic [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]; theurgy (prayer as adornment for union, protection, letter mysticism, assembly); and [[pardes|PaRDeS]] hermeneutics (Sod in running commentary, concealed midrash, and independent composition modes). This remains the wiki's **first Jewish-mysticism source** and a first-order scope expansion. Authorship is itself a major [[zohar-authorship|controversy]] (Shim'on bar Yoḥai, traditional, vs. Moses de León, critical). The full Pritzker ingest now tracks the complete range of the Zoharic literary corpus.

**Christian OT/NT scholarship**: Evangelical and broadly evangelical commentary on all five Torah books, four Former Prophets (including Kings), three Megillot (Esther, Song of Songs, Lamentations), two post-exilic Historical Books, 1–2 Chronicles, four Wisdom books (Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes), four Latter Prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel), Daniel (Writings/Prophets), seven Minor Prophets from the Twelve (Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Zechariah), and Acts. Commentary series: NICOT (Hamilton on Genesis, Woudstra on Joshua, Webb on Judges, Lau on Ruth, Tsumura on 1–2 Samuel, Harrington on Ezra-Nehemiah, Craigie on Deuteronomy, Wenham on Leviticus), UBCS (Bruckner on Exodus), NAC (Cole on Numbers), BECNT (Bock on Acts). Woudstra and Hamilton/Craigie represent conservative Reformed/evangelical hermeneutics; Bruckner represents the centrist "believing criticism" approach; Webb combines literary-canonical criticism; Lau brings honor-shame social-scientific analysis, missions theology, and ethnic minority perspective; Tsumura brings Japanese evangelical Semitic philology and MT-first text criticism; Harrington (NICOT Ezra-Nehemiah 2022) brings Second Temple specialist expertise, ritual purity analysis, and Mary Douglas / Victor Turner social-scientific framing.

**Primary texts in scope**: Babylonian Talmud, Mishna, **The Zohar**, Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 Samuel, 2 Samuel, 1–2 Kings, 1–2 Chronicles, Ezra-Nehemiah, Esther, Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel, Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Zechariah, Matthew, Mark, Luke, Gospel of John, Acts, **Romans**, **1 Corinthians**, **2 Corinthians**, **Galatians**, 1–2–3 John, Hebrews, **James**, **1 Peter**, **2 Peter**, **Jude**, **Revelation**.

## Notable Cross-Tradition Patterns

**Covenant** runs as the unifying theological category across all ingested sources: Talmudic covenant (circumcision, Torah obligation), Deuteronomic covenant (suzerain-vassal form), Abrahamic covenant in Genesis (Gen 15 + 17), Levitical holiness as covenant embodiment, the new covenant in Acts.

**Oral vs. written tradition**: The Jewish Oral Torah debate (Pharisees vs. Sadducees vs. Karaites) has a structural analogy in the NT debate about speeches in Acts (Bock vs. Haenchen/Dibelius) and in the Documentary Hypothesis controversy (oral prehistory of sources, Gunkel).

**Apologetic posture across traditions**: Both Unterman (defending the Talmud) and the evangelical commentators (defending biblical historicity) operate in apologetic modes — their scholarship is simultaneously exegetical and defensive. This shared posture is a methodological similarity worth tracking as the wiki expands.

**The Documentary Hypothesis** is the single most discussed hermeneutical debate across the ingested Christian sources. All six evangelical commentators engage it; all critique it to varying degrees without fully rejecting its literary observations.

**Intra-evangelical tension on liberation hermeneutics**: Bruckner engages liberation hermeneutics as a legitimate reading strategy; Woudstra is explicitly hostile; Webb occupies a middle position — he engages feminist scholarship seriously (the most of any ingested commentator) but is less interested in political applications than in canonical literary meaning.

**Feminist scholarship enters the wiki**: Webb's Judges is the first ingested source to engage feminist biblical criticism (Bal, Exum, Trible, Ackerman) as a genuine scholarly dialogue partner. The question of women in Judges is now a live topic requiring future dedicated commentary sources.

**New literary criticism as theological method**: Webb combines Robert Alter's narrative criticism with Childs's canonical criticism into a unified approach. This is methodologically distinct from any of the other ingested commentators and represents the most sophisticated literary exegesis in the wiki to date.

**Honor-shame analysis enters the wiki**: Lau's Ruth is the first ingested source to apply honor-shame social dynamics (from collectivist ANE anthropology) systematically to a biblical text. This represents the most methodologically diverse single commentary in the wiki, combining narrative analysis, social identity theory, missions theology, and self-disclosed contextual reading.

**The Former Prophets + Megilloth interface**: Ruth sits between Judges and 1 Samuel in the LXX canon, functioning as a theological counterweight to Judges' downward spiral. The wiki now covers this transition: Webb (Judges ending in chaos) → Lau (Ruth embodying *ḥesed* amid that chaos) → Tsumura (1 Samuel resolving the kingship question Judges raised) — a canonical sequence now fully readable.

**Methodological diversity within NICOT**: The NICOT series now provides a striking internal contrast: Webb (literary-canonical, Alter + Childs), Lau (honor-shame social science + missions theology), and Tsumura (Semitic philology + discourse grammar) represent three entirely different methodological traditions applied to consecutive books in the Hebrew Bible. This makes NICOT an unusually rich site for tracking how method shapes interpretation.

**Intra-evangelical tension on the DH**: Tsumura is the most skeptical of the DH among ingested commentators — more so than Woudstra, who does not develop detailed text-critical arguments. Tsumura's philological case against late redaction (archaic vocabulary preserved that a postexilic editor would have modernized) adds a new category of anti-DH argument to the wiki.

**The Davidic Covenant as OT theological center**: With 2 Samuel ingested, the wiki now contains the OT's most consequential covenant promise — 2 Sam 7's unconditional commitment to David's eternal dynasty. This is the hinge between the Abrahamic and Mosaic covenants and NT Christology. The Former Prophets sequence is now complete from conquest (Joshua) through the David arc (1-2 Samuel).

**Second Temple Judaism enters the wiki**: Harrington's Ezra-Nehemiah commentary introduces a new period — the Persian period and the origins of Second Temple Judaism — not covered by any prior ingest. For the first time the wiki contains a text that is simultaneously a historical source for postexilic Yehud, a foundation document of rabbinic Judaism, and a site of sharp controversy about ethnic identity and religious exclusivism. The midrash concept, flagged in the lint report, is now filed.

**The intermarriage controversy** is the most politically charged topic yet ingested — more so than the *ḥerem* controversy, because it involves questions of ethnic identity, matrilineal descent, and the rights of women and children that remain live debates in both Jewish and Christian communities. The wiki now has a dedicated controversy page and connects this to Ruth (canonical counter-testimony) and the NT reversals (Acts 10; Gal 3:28).

**Methodological first**: Harrington is the first ingested commentator to use social-scientific method (Douglas, Turner) as a *primary* analytical lens rather than background illumination. Breneman (NAC 1993) provides a useful traditional evangelical contrast to Harrington — same texts, radically different methods, 29 years apart.

**Esther — the hidden God**: The wiki now contains the OT's only book in which God is never named.

**A new commentary series enters: the Brazos Theological Commentary (BTC)**: Leithart's Chronicles is the wiki's first post-critical doctrinal commentary — written by a theologian, not a biblical scholar, operating under the explicit premise that doctrine clarifies rather than obscures scripture. This represents the methodological opposite of Tsumura (maximum philological rigor, minimum theological imposition) and of Harrington (social-scientific method, maximal critical apparatus). The BTC approach is exploratory and deliberately unsettled on the question of how doctrine and historical-critical method relate.

**Chronicles completes the Chronicler's history**: The wiki now covers the Chronicler's two-part work: [[1-2-chronicles|Chronicles]] (Leithart, BTC) and [[ezra-nehemiah|Ezra-Nehemiah]] (Harrington + Breneman). The two works are now documented with their theological relationship (separate authors; Chronicles ending with Cyrus's commission as a Jubilee decree; Chronicles narrating the transition to the Gentile world-order that Ezra-Nehemiah begins to populate).

**Cyrus as the surprise Davidic figure**: The Chronicler's typological "punch line" — Cyrus inherits the Davidic commission — is a genuinely striking claim with strong OT support (Isaiah 44-45) and significant implications for understanding the Davidic covenant's trajectory. The wiki's Davidic Covenant concept page has been updated accordingly. Esther's canonical controversy (Qumran exclusion, Luther's hostility, LXX supplements) and its theology of hidden providence introduce a new mode of theological writing in the Hebrew Bible, distinct from the explicit providential language of Ezra-Nehemiah's "hand of God." This differs from Lau's honor-shame approach (which is also social-scientific but focuses on ANE cultural categories rather than anthropological theory).

**Wisdom literature enters the wiki**: Job (Hartley, NICOT 1988) is the wiki's first Wisdom text — a radically different literary and theological world from the narrative books. Job operates with no covenant history, no Israel-specific setting (the characters are Edomite), and no redemptive-historical framework. Its governing question — *is disinterested fear of God possible?* — is the most theologically penetrating question in the OT. Two new concept pages: Theodicy and Wisdom Literature. One new controversy page: Job 42:6 (recantation or surrender — 5 positions). The ANE parallel literature section (the "Babylonian Job," Babylonian Ecclesiastes, Egyptian lament texts) extends the wiki's coverage of the genesis-ancient-near-east comparison theme into the Wisdom tradition. Hartley's claim that Isaiah's Suffering Servant was influenced *by* Job (rather than vice versa) is a distinctive and significant claim for Christology.

**Methodological note**: Hartley (Job) occupies a middle position in the NICOT methodological spectrum — more message-focused than Tsumura (philological), less typologically driven than Webb (narrative-canonical). His sustained defense of single authorship for Job is the most detailed literary-unity argument in the wiki to date.

**The Psalter enters the wiki**: deClaissé-Walford, Jacobson, and Tanner's NICOT Psalms (2014) is the wiki's only co-authored commentary and brings the most radical canonical-criticism yet. The central claim (building on Gerald Wilson): the Psalter's five-book structure is a deliberate editorial narrative of Israel's history from the Davidic monarchy through exile to restoration — five books that parallel the same arc recorded in Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, and Ezra-Nehemiah. Psalm 89 (the broken Davidic covenant) is the Psalter's theological hinge; Book Four's response (Moses/"YHWH reigns") is its answer. The wiki's first commentary on the most-cited OT book in the NT. The Psalms page now cross-links to Chronicles (the organizational foundation for Levitical music), the Davidic Covenant (Ps 89's crisis), *ḥesed* (the governing divine attribute), and Ezra-Nehemiah (the restoration narrative). The lament-to-praise arc provides a large-scale narrative structure complementary to all existing narrative books.

**Proverbs completes the Wisdom triad and introduces the wiki's most developed Christology**: Waltke's NICOT Proverbs (2 vols., 2004/2005) is the most technically ambitious commentary in the wiki (25+ years in the making; two large volumes; Reformed evangelical). Proverbs is the "confident" Wisdom text — it affirms the retribution doctrine that Job tests and qualifies. Its central epistemological claim, "the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom," is a *compound expression* with both rational (revealed law) and non-rational (love, trust, reverence) dimensions — not natural theology, not humanist ethics, not deistic world-order. Woman Wisdom (Prov 8) is the wiki's first dedicated Christological concept page: Waltke rejects both the Arian use of Prov 8:22 and the direct patristic identification of Wisdom with Christ, arguing instead for a *typological* relationship (similarities + Christ's superiority at every point). This positions the wiki to trace the full trajectory of Wisdom Christology from Proverbs through the NT. The Amenemope/Proverbs direct verbal parallel is the strongest ANE literary connection in the wiki. The seven-collection structure (with Collection I as the hermeneutical key) provides the most developed poetics treatment in the wiki. Two new concept pages: Woman Wisdom and an update to Wisdom Literature.

**Ecclesiastes completes the Wisdom quartet and introduces the wiki's sharpest intra-Wisdom tension**: Longman's NICOT Ecclesiastes (1998) is the most theologically challenging commentary in the wiki — not because Longman is heterodox, but because his subject is. The central hermeneutical move: Ecclesiastes has *two voices*. Qohelet (1:12-12:7) is a foil — a skeptical wisdom teacher who accurately describes life "under the sun" (under the curse, without redemption) but is not the book's normative voice. The Frame Narrator (1:1-11; 12:8-14) frames Qohelet's speech as a teaching example and corrects it with the normative conclusion (12:13-14): "Fear God and keep his commandments" — three compressed phrases covering wisdom, law, and prophets. This two-voice structure parallels Job: both books contain problematic human speeches corrected by a normative voice at the end. Longman's genre contribution (Akkadian fictional autobiography, from his Yale dissertation) explains the Solomonic persona as a recognized literary convention. The *hebel* concept page documents the governing term and its NT echo in Romans 8 (*mataiotes*). New sharpest intra-wiki tension: Waltke's Proverbs (confident, "fear of the LORD" as positive covenantal foundation) vs. Longman's Ecclesiastes (Qohelet's "fear God" means dread before a dangerous being; retribution is unobservable). This is not a contradiction to resolve but a polyphony to hold.

**Song of Songs enters the wiki and completes the canonical arc of human sexuality**: Longman's second NICOT volume (Song of Songs, 2001) is the most interpretation-saturated commentary in the wiki — the text requires a history of interpretation section before exegesis can even begin, because 1,800 years of allegorization have made it harder to read the obvious. The genre identification (anthology of love poetry, not allegory or drama) is the key. The three-dimensional canonical theology is the most structurally elegant in the wiki: (1) human love poetry (primary — must not be suppressed); (2) sexuality redeemed — the *tĕšûqâ* arc from Gen 2 (naked, no shame) through Gen 3:16 (desire as domination) to Song 7:11 (desire as mutual, egalitarian love), reversing the curse; (3) illuminating the divine-human marriage metaphor that runs from Hosea through Revelation. The divine-human marriage concept page documents that trajectory. Longman is now the wiki's most-represented individual commentator alongside Waltke (two NICOT volumes each). The allegorical interpretation history (Targum, Origen, Bernard of Clairvaux, Luther, Westminster Assembly) is the most comprehensive reception history in the wiki.

**Isaiah enters the wiki as the first prophetic book and the most Christologically dense OT text**: Oswalt's two-volume NICOT Isaiah (1986/1998) is the largest commentary in the wiki (two volumes covering the most NT-quoted OT book; 21 direct NT quotations plus numerous allusions). Oswalt brings the Wesleyan-Arminian holiness tradition — the first Wesleyan voice since Hartley and the first non-Reformed systematic hermeneutic for a major book. The central debate is single authorship, defended on the grounds that the multiple-authorship hypothesis creates a self-defeating theology: "Second Isaiah" cannot use God's ability to predict the future as the proof of divine superiority while himself writing after the fact. The governing theological concept ("Holy One of Israel," 29 occurrences vs. 7 in the rest of the OT) ties the book's unity together: holiness = ontological otherness + moral/ethical perfection. The book's unified structure centers on servanthood: how can sinful Israel become God's servant? Ch. 6 is the hermeneutical key; chs. 7-39 establish trust; chs. 40-48 provide motivation; chs. 49-55 provide the means (the Suffering Servant); chs. 56-66 work out the servant life. The Servant Songs concept page documents the four songs and the identity debate (collective vs. historical individual vs. messianic). Isaiah 53 is now the wiki's most explicitly Christological OT passage — the *ʾāšām* guilt offering, "by his wounds we are healed," the post-death vindication. New cross-wiki tension flagged: Hartley argued Isaiah was influenced *by* Job; Oswalt's treatment presents the Servant Songs as Isaiah's own revelation — the direction-of-influence controversy is now documented. Isaiah also provides the fullest prophetic development of the divine-human marriage metaphor (Isa 54:5 = "your Maker is your husband"), updating that concept page.

**Jeremiah and Lamentations enter the wiki with three commentaries, two series, and the New Covenant**: Three sources cover Jeremiah/Lamentations — Goldingay's NICOT Jeremiah (2021) and Lamentations (2022) are the wiki's most recent volumes; Lalleman-de Winkel's TOTC is the wiki's first Tyndale commentary. Goldingay (Anglican/Fuller) brings the most methodologically adventurous approach yet: the Gospels analogy (Jeremiah's composition ≈ Gospel composition; MT ≈ Luke; LXX ≈ Matthew; "curators and storytellers" instead of "Deuteronomists") and the "both-and" theology (the scroll refuses univocal answers; a prophet knows which time it is). His formulation "In Jeremiah, the word became flesh" is the most provocative in the wiki — Jeremiah's suffering embodies the divine message, proleptic of the Incarnation. Lalleman's TOTC contributes the *šûb* analysis (110+ occurrences; the entire book is structured around the shift from human imperative to divine promise, with the 604 BCE burning of the scroll as the turning point) and the two metaphor clusters (build/plant and medical). The New Covenant (31:31-34) is now the wiki's central OT soteriological concept page — the longest OT quotation in the NT (Heb 8 and 10), the text that names the "New Testament," and the basis for the Last Supper formula. Lamentations provides the wiki's most concentrated grief theology: five alphabetical poems, "the problem is the presence of God (not absence)," theodicy and anti-theodicy simultaneously, one verse of hope (3:21-24) surrounded by unrelieved grief. Canonical note: Lamentations sits between Jeremiah (the diagnosis) and Isaiah 40-55 (Yahweh's response to its unanswered prayers). The divine-human marriage concept page now has its fullest legal development documented: Jeremiah 3's deployment of Deut 24 — the legally impossible offer of God to take back his adulterous wife.

**Ezekiel enters the wiki with Block's two-volume NICOT and the *kābôd* departure/return as the theological spine**: Block (Baptist/Wheaton) provides the most structurally rigorous commentary yet — the "four pillars" framework maps every judgment oracle to a specific pillar demolished and every restoration oracle to the same pillar rebuilt, giving Ezekiel's apparently unwieldy 48 chapters a coherent theological argument. The divine *kābôd* concept page documents the most sustained OT theology of divine presence: from the tabernacle (Exod 40) through the temple (1 Kgs 8) through Ezekiel's departure (chs. 8-11) and return (ch. 43) to John's "we have seen his glory" (1:14) and Revelation's permanent illumination (21:23). The book ends with *YHWH šammâ* — "YHWH is there" — as the hermeneutical key to all of Ezekiel 40-48: the final vision is a theological constitution for divine presence, not an architectural blueprint. Two concept pages updated: the divine-human marriage page now includes Block's defense of Ezek 16 and 23 against misogyny charges; the new-covenant page now includes the Ezek 36:25-27 parallel with a comparison table. The dry bones (ch. 37), the Gog-Magog oracle (chs. 38-39 as "literary cartoon"), and the "prophet of the Spirit" theme (36:27 as Ezekielian counterpart to Jer 31:31-34) are all now documented.

**Daniel enters the wiki with the first NIVAC and the apocalyptic genre concept page**: Longman's NIVAC Daniel (1999) is his third appearance in the wiki (after Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs) and the wiki's first NIV Application Commentary. The NIVAC's three-part structure (Original Meaning → Bridging Contexts → Contemporary Significance) makes it the most application-focused commentary in the wiki. The central message — "In spite of present appearances, God is in control" — is both the summary of Daniel and a live word for any persecuted or marginalized community. The apocalyptic genre concept page is now the wiki's hermeneutical manual for reading Daniel, Revelation, Zechariah, and Ezekiel 38-39: images communicate truth not precision; numbers are symbolic; the purpose is assurance not calendar. The Son of Man passage (Dan 7:13-14) is now documented as the most important single OT Christological passage after Isaiah 53 — Jesus's self-designation, the charge at his trial, the framework of Revelation's throne-room scenes. Longman's irenic handling of the date controversy (6th century vs. 2nd century BCE) models how to engage a genuinely difficult scholarly question without making it a litmus test of orthodoxy. Longman is now the wiki's most-represented individual commentator (three books: Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Daniel). The Daniel/Jeremiah connection is documented: Daniel 9's prayer is triggered by reading Jeremiah 25/29 — one of the clearest instances of inner-biblical interpretation in the OT.

**Hosea, Joel, and Amos — the Minor Prophets enter the wiki with three NICOT volumes**: The Book of the Twelve begins. Dearman's Hosea (Austin Presbyterian/ca. 2010) documents the origin of the divine-human marriage metaphor: Hosea is the earliest writing prophet to develop it, using the root metaphor of YHWH as head of his household (*bayit*). The household-as-metaphor holds together husband, father, shepherd, and king as aspects of the single patrimonial role. The knowledge of God (*da'at Elohim*, 4:1; 6:6) is Hosea's governing ethical concept. Hosea is Jeremiah's "spiritual father and most devoted imitator" — now documented. Carroll's Amos (Denver Seminary, 2020) is the wiki's most sustained social justice commentary: the systematic reversal of every Israelite theological tradition (exodus, covenant, Day of Yahweh, cult) against Israel is Amos's fundamental rhetorical strategy. "Let justice roll on like a river" (5:24) — now documented in its literary and theological context. Carroll defends synchronic final-form reading against elaborate redaction theories. Nogalski's Joel/Obadiah/Jonah (Baylor, 2023) is the wiki's most recent commentary and the most methodologically distinctive: Joel as *scribal prophecy* (postexilic literary compilation); Joel as "literary anchor" of the Twelve (all twelve books' themes present in Joel); three Day of Yahweh scenarios (judgment on Judah; near-term deliverance; Spirit outpouring + judgment on nations). The Book of the Twelve concept page now documents the 12-prophet canonical collection and its intertextual logic. Joel 2:28-32 is now the wiki's most important NT founding-of-the-church passage — Peter's Pentecost citation. The divine-human marriage concept page is updated with Hosea as the identified source of the metaphor.

**Galatians enters the wiki — and the NPP debate reaches its fullest documentation**: Moo's BECNT Galatians (2013) is the most thoroughly documented instance of the New Perspective on Paul debate in the wiki. The distinctive contributions: (1) South Galatian theory slightly preferred, with Galatians possibly the earliest surviving Pauline letter (c. AD 48); (2) a three-level logic of Paul's anti-Torah argument — salvation-historical (Torah era ended with Christ) + ethnic/social (Torah boundary markers excluded Gentiles) + anthropological (human inability to do Torah: Gal. 3:10 syllogism, Westerholm's formulation); (3) future-oriented justification — the letter is not primarily about "how you get saved" but about "ultimate justification" at the final judgment; (4) the objective genitive reading of *pistis Christou* ("faith in Christ") against Hays, Campbell et al. — the most extensive treatment of this debate in the wiki; (5) "the law of Christ" (6:2) as the new covenant's distinctive ethical authority, contrasting with the Mosaic law.

The Galatians ingest also generates the wiki's most important single methodological controversy page: the New Perspective on Paul — now documented with positions from Sanders, Dunn, Wright, Barclay, Watson (NPP/Beyond NPP), and the evangelical response from Schreiner, Moo, Seifrid, and Westerholm. This debate runs beneath much of the Pauline material already ingested (Romans: Schreiner; 2 Cor: Seifrid). The *pistis Christou* controversy page is the wiki's first dedicated Greek syntax controversy page — the question of the genitive touches Pauline grammar, participatory Christology, the NPP, and Reformation soteriology simultaneously.

Cross-wiki tension newly sharpened: Galatians's "new creation" (6:15) and 2 Corinthians's "new creation" (5:17) are now both documented, and the relationship between them (identical phrase, different argumentative contexts) clarifies the concept. The eschatological-temple concept (from 1 Corinthians) relates directly to Galatians's "Israel of God" and "sons of Abraham" — the people-of-God question raised by all three Corinthian-Galatian letters is now visible as a single continuous theological problem in the wiki.

**James enters the wiki — Moo PNTC (2000), the General Epistles expand, and the wiki gains its first figure page**: Moo's *Letter of James* is his second source in the wiki (after BECNT Galatians), showing his range from the Pauline corpus into the General Epistles (now Hebrews + James). James is the great NT counter-pole to Paul on justification ("a person is justified by works and not by faith alone," 2:24), and the ingest documents Moo's signature **two-senses-of-*dikaioō* resolution**: Paul's "justify" = the initial forensic declaration at conversion; James's = the ultimate *vindication at the final judgment* that takes works into account. James's "faith alone" is the bogus, word-only, demonic faith (2:19) he attacks — not Pauline faith. The new [[james-paul-justification|James and Paul]] controversy page sits alongside the existing NPP material as the wiki's fullest treatment of the faith/works question; the [[covenantal-nomism|covenantal nomism]] concept (Sanders, qualified by Moo) now has its own page and links both debates. Other new material: the [[james-authorship|authorship/early-date]] controversy (Moo defends James of Jerusalem, mid-40s, possibly the earliest NT document, against the pseudepigraphal consensus); the [[double-mindedness|double-mindedness / spiritual wholeness]] concept (*dipsychos*/*teleios* as the letter's organizing concern); and the [[anointing-healing-james-5|anointing and healing]] controversy (Moo's consecration-symbol reading; critique of "enough faith" theology; the Catholic extreme-unction history).

**1 Peter enters the wiki — Jobes BECNT (2005), the first solo woman commentator, and the General Epistles broaden again**: Karen Jobes's *1 Peter* brings the wiki its first solo-authored woman commentator and a Septuagint specialist's method. Three distinctive contributions: (1) the **Roman colonization theory** — the recipients were converted elsewhere (probably Rome) and *displaced to Asia Minor* by Claudian colonization/expulsion, rather than evangelized in situ — a fresh historical reconstruction that grounds the letter's pervasive "foreigner/resident alien" language in a real event; (2) reading 1 Peter against its **Septuagint** matrix; and (3) **syntax criticism / bilingual interference**, arguing the Greek's syntax (as opposed to its rhetoric) points to a Semitic-language author, undercutting the standard "Greek too good for a fisherman" objection to Petrine authorship (which Jobes defends, with an early date). New controversy pages: [[1-peter-authorship|authorship/date/colonization]], [[spirits-in-prison-1-peter-3|the spirits in prison (3:18–22; 4:6)]] (Jobes reads it as Christ's *ascension* victory over fallen angels, not a descent into hell), and [[1-peter-christ-and-culture|Christ and culture]] (the Elliott-vs-Balch sectarian-resistance / acculturation debate, resolved by Jobes/Volf as differentiated engagement). New concept: [[christian-exile-diaspora|Christian exile / diaspora identity]] (*parepidēmoi* / *paroikoi*). The [[servant-songs|Servant Songs]] page is updated with Jobes's claim that **1 Peter is the source of the church's identification of Jesus with the Isaiah 53 Suffering Servant** (2:21–25) — Peter exegetes Jesus' death in light of Isa 53, not vice versa. With James and 1 Peter added to Hebrews and the Johannine corpus, the General Epistles are now substantially represented.

**Jude and 2 Peter enter the wiki — Gene L. Green BECNT (2008), and the General Epistles are nearly complete**: Gene L. Green's *Jude and 2 Peter* (not to be confused with Joel B. Green, the Luke commentator) brings the wiki the two most "marginal" NT letters and a method marked by intensive cultural-contextual and **sociology-of-honor** reconstruction. Green defends the **authenticity of both letters** — a minority position for 2 Peter — and his most important moves are: (1) dismantling Bauckham's **testament-genre** argument against 2 Peter's authenticity (2 Peter merely *includes* a testamentary section, 1:12–15; it is not a testament); (2) arguing that **Jude genuinely treats 1 Enoch and the Assumption of Moses as authoritative revelation** (against the apologetic minimizings), while using them judiciously, tied to canonical bedrock — the [[jude-1-enoch-canon|canon controversy]] sits exactly where a high doctrine of Scripture meets historical-critical data; and (3) framing **2 Peter's reuse of Jude** as honorable ancient *imitatio*, with the striking implication that Jude held the *higher* honor as a member of Jesus' family (the [[2-peter-jude-relationship|relationship controversy]]). New controversy pages: [[2-peter-authorship|2 Peter authorship/testament genre]], [[jude-1-enoch-canon|Jude and 1 Enoch]], [[2-peter-jude-relationship|the 2 Peter–Jude relationship]]. New concept: [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis / partakers of the divine nature]] (2 Pet 1:4) — the wiki's first treatment of *deification*, where Green argues for moral transformation over ontological divinization, opening a cross-tradition window onto Eastern Orthodox soteriology. The [[new-creation|New Creation]] concept is updated with 2 Peter 3:10–13 (new heavens and new earth as renewal, not annihilation). With James, 1 Peter, 2 Peter, Jude, Hebrews, and the Johannine corpus all now ingested, the **General (Catholic) Epistles are nearly complete** — only 2–3 John (covered within the Johannine corpus) remain outside a dedicated Petrine/Jacobean-style treatment.

**Revelation enters the wiki — Osborne BECNT (2002), and the entire NT now has text-page coverage**: Grant Osborne's *Revelation* is the wiki's first commentary on a full-scale apocalypse and completes dedicated text-page coverage of all 27 NT books. Osborne (TEDS; a doctoral student of [[i-howard-marshall|I. H. Marshall]]; author of *The Hermeneutical Spiral*; classical Arminian; historic premillennial) reads the book through its blended **genre** (apocalypse + prophecy + letter), insisting that the symbols be read via first-century background with a "hermeneutics of humility," and that its numbers are symbolic. His method is **eclectic with the futurist primary** (close to Beale's idealist-primary eclectic) — generating the wiki's fullest treatment of the [[revelation-interpretive-schools|four interpretive schools]] (historicist/preterist/idealist/futurist). On the most divisive passage he is **historic premillennial** but notably irenic — see the new [[revelation-millennium|millennium]] controversy (premil/amil/postmil). He defends **apostolic Johannine authorship** and a **Domitianic date** (the new [[revelation-date-authorship|authorship/date]] controversy). New concept: [[lamb-of-god|the Lamb of God]] — the slain Lamb who is also the conquering Ram, whose decisive victory was won at the cross, and whose "oneness" with God establishes Christ's deity. Four concept pages were updated to reach their consummation in Revelation: [[apocalyptic-literature|apocalyptic literature]] (Revelation as the NT apocalypse), [[antichrist|antichrist]] (the beast and 666), [[new-creation|new creation]] (Rev 21–22), and [[eschatological-temple|the eschatological temple]] (the New Jerusalem as the Holy of Holies, with no temple but God and the Lamb).

**The fourth figure page completes the three Jerusalem "pillars"**: [[john-apostle|John the Apostle]] joins Peter, James, and Jude under Figures. Because every Johannine book's authorship is contested, John's page synthesizes the authorship debates spanning the wiki's ingested commentators — [[j-ramsey-michaels|Michaels]] (Gospel), [[i-howard-marshall|Marshall]]/[[colin-kruse|Kruse]] (Epistles), and [[grant-osborne|Osborne]] (Revelation) — and ties together the Johannine corpus (Gospel, Epistles, Apocalypse) now fully ingested. The wiki now documents all three pillars of Gal 2:9 (Peter, James, John) as figures.

**The family of Jesus as a documented force in the early church**: with [[jude-brother-of-jesus|Jude]] joining [[james-the-just|James the Just]], the wiki now has figure pages for **two of the "brothers of the Lord"** and traces the *desposynoi* (the relatives of Jesus) — their non-dynastic, servant-framed authority, the Palestinian midrashic/apocalyptic hermeneutic of their letters, and Hegesippus's account of Domitian interrogating Jude's grandsons. The two Jacobean/Judan letters (James, Jude) and the Petrine pair are now legible as a non-Pauline, largely Palestinian stream of earliest Christianity.

**Methodological milestone — the wiki's first figure page**: [[james-the-just|James the Just]] (the Lord's brother) is the first entry under the long-empty Figures section. It models the figure-page format defined in the project brief: biographical overview (incl. the AD 62 martyrdom attested by Josephus), primary-source appearances, tradition-specific reception (Pauline, Jewish-Christian legend, Catholic/Orthodox), the kinship-to-Jesus question (Helvidian/Epiphanian/Hieronymian), and historicity controversies — cross-linked to the text and controversy pages. This opens a new structural register for the wiki: major biblical figures as dedicated, multi-tradition pages. (Abraham and Rahab, both prominent in James 2, are now flagged as figure-page candidates for future ingests.) The 1 Peter ingest adds the **second** figure page, [[peter-apostle|Simon Peter]] — the wiki now documents two of the three Jerusalem "pillars" (Peter and James; John remains a candidate). The Jude/2 Peter ingest adds the **third**, [[jude-brother-of-jesus|Jude, the brother of Jesus]], extending the family-of-Jesus coverage. The Revelation ingest adds the **fourth**, [[john-apostle|John the Apostle]] — completing figure pages for all three Jerusalem "pillars" (Peter, James, John).

## Transliteration Standards

- **Hebrew**: SBL Academic style (no pointing in running text unless exegetically relevant)
- **Greek**: Standard academic transliteration; retain Greek script where meaning depends on it
- **Arabic**: Simplified transliteration without diacritics for running text; full diacritics in technical pages
- **Sanskrit/Pali**: IAST for Sanskrit; standard Pali transliteration

**Obadiah, Jonah, and Micah complete the first six Minor Prophets with Nogalski's two NICOT volumes**: Nogalski is now the wiki's second 3-source commentator (alongside Longman), with Joel/Obadiah/Jonah (2023) and Micah (2024) — the wiki's two most recent NICOT volumes. Obadiah (21 verses, shortest in the Bible) is the kinship betrayal book: Edom/Esau betrayed Judah/Jacob, and poetic justice demands equivalent punishment (v. 15b). Its intertextual architecture — adapting Jer 49:7-22 while mirroring Amos 9's structure — is among the most intricate in the Twelve. Jonah is the Bible's funniest book (satire; the pagan sailors and all of Nineveh outperform the prophet at every turn) and its most universalist: Jonah 4:2 cites Joel's citation of Exod 34:6 to argue that Yahweh's compassion extends even to Israel's worst enemy. The canonical Jonah-Nahum dialogue in the Twelve gives both halves of Jer 18:7-10: mercy for repentance (Jonah) + justice for resumed evil (Nahum). Micah gives the three-century compositional history of a Southern prophet: 8th-century core (social justice; Samaria → Jerusalem) + exilic update (chs. 6-7; why Jerusalem fell) + postexilic reframing (chs. 4-5; Zion reconstituted). Micah 3:12 is the Masoretic middle of the entire Twelve by verses, immediately followed by Micah 4:1's vision of universal peace — the most dramatic structural contrast in any prophetic collection. Micah 4:1-4 = Isaiah 2:2-4 (verbatim) bridges the first and sixth books of the four Latter Prophets. Micah 6:8 ("do justice, love *ḥesed*, walk humbly") is the OT's most compact ethical summary.

**Kings and Zechariah complete the Former Prophets and bring the wiki's first NIBC volume**: Provan's NIBC Kings (1995, Hendrickson/Baker) is the wiki's first New International Biblical Commentary — a "believing criticism" series. Provan defends reading Kings as a coherent unified narrative (against atomizing redaction-critical approaches), and his four-theme theology (YHWH is God; exclusive worship; divine judgment; promise) organizes the whole arc from Solomon through the exile. The Deuteronomistic History is now fully represented (Deuteronomy through Kings). The slender thread of Davidic hope — Jehoiachin at the table of the king of Babylon (25:27-30) — is now documented as the book's canonical hinge between despair and eschatological possibility. Boda's NICOT Zechariah (2016, Eerdmans) is the most technically dense commentary in the wiki (864 pages). The key contributions: the three-level compositional history of chs. 1-8; the inner-biblical allusion mapping (Zechariah is among the most allusion-dense OT books); the leadership theology (three streams affirmed then critiqued); and — most significant for NT readers — the Zechariah-Passion matrix (9:9; 11:12-13; 12:10; 13:7; 14:8 = the density of Zechariah quotations in Matthew's Passion exceeds almost any other OT source). Zechariah 4:6 ("Not by might nor by power, but by my Spirit") and 14:9 ("YHWH will be king over the whole earth") are now documented as the theological pillars of the night visions and eschatological oracles respectively.

**Luke enters the wiki with Green's NICNT (1997) — the sharpest methodological departure in the series**: Joel Green (Asbury/Wesleyan) reads Luke as Greco-Roman historiography using "discourse analysis" (narratology + social-scientific method) with no interest in redaction criticism, Q-source tracking, or synoptic comparisons. This is a radical turn: where France's Matthew is organized around OT fulfillment and France's Mark around narrative christology, Green's Luke explicitly refuses to compare Luke to its Synoptic siblings and insists on reading it as a self-contained narrative continuous with Acts. The key contributions: (1) Luke-Acts as one story — the gospel is incomplete without Acts; (2) theocentric narrative — God's purpose (δεῖ) and the Holy Spirit drive the plot, not Jesus as biographical subject; (3) the Nazareth sermon (4:16–30) as the programmatic text for the whole gospel — "good news to the poor," release, Jubilee, eschatological "today"; (4) holistic salvation — no dichotomy between social and spiritual, present and future, individual and communal; (5) the Travel Narrative (9:51–19:48) as Luke's most distinctive literary creation, containing the Good Samaritan, Prodigal Son, Rich Man and Lazarus, and the discipleship teaching unique to the third gospel; (6) women as systematic models of faithful response, not peripheral characters; (7) Jerusalem as the goal and center — contrast to Mark's Galilee return. New concept page: Jubilee (the Year of the Lord's Favor) — cross-referenced from Leviticus 25, Isaiah 61, 11QMelchizedek, and Luke 4:18–19. The Wesleyan tradition is now the strongest non-Reformed strand in the wiki (Green on Luke, Oswalt on Isaiah, Hartley on Job). The NICNT now has three Synoptic Gospel commentaries (France on Matthew and Mark, Green on Luke) with strikingly different methods — making it the richest single series for intra-evangelical methodological comparison.

**Mark enters the wiki with the first NIGTC volume — France's second commentary and a "narrative christology" counterpart to his NICNT Matthew**: France's NIGTC Mark (2002) is methodologically distinct from his NICNT Matthew: where Matthew is organized around the explicit fulfillment theme and OT formula-quotations, Mark requires a fundamentally narrative-christological approach — "the christology is in the story." The commentary introduces three major categories new to the wiki: (1) **"Drama in Three Acts"** as structural analysis; (2) the **Messianic Secret** as an interpretive crux (with a new dedicated concept page engaging Wrede's 1901 monograph and France's reinterpretation); (3) the **ending of Mark** (16:8 and the Longer Ending — a new controversy page). France's treatment of the Galilee/Jerusalem polarity deepens what was already documented in the Matthew page: here it is the organizing dramatic principle rather than one feature among many. The wiki's NT coverage now spans the two synoptic gospels with the deepest OT engagement (Matthew) and the foundational narrative gospel (Mark) through the same commentator — allowing intra-France comparison of method across two different series (NICNT vs. NIGTC) and two different audiences (English commentary vs. Greek text commentary). France's "cross-fertilization" model of Synoptic relationships is now documented twice, consistently across both commentaries, providing a sustained alternative to the standard two-source theory. The wiki's first NIGTC volume also introduces a new methodological register: commentary on the Greek text at the grammatical/syntactical level, distinct from the English-text commentary format that has dominated all prior ingests.

**The Johannine Epistles enter the wiki — and the NT Christological crisis arrives**: Marshall (NICNT, 1978) and Kruse (PNTC, 2nd ed. 2020) are the two ingested commentaries on 1, 2, 3 John. The wiki now covers, for the first time, a NT letter-corpus dealing with Christological heresy from within a Christian community. The secessionist crisis is the earliest documented internal Christian schism in the wiki: a group that had been members of the Johannine community denied the incarnation and the saving significance of Jesus's death (proto-Docetism / Cerinthian influence) and organized a counter-mission. The author's response — insisting on right Christology, brotherly love, and assurance — is the defining theological contribution of the corpus. New items: the *antichrist* concept page (unique Johannine terminology); the Johannine secessionist controversy (5 positions, open); two commentator pages; the text page for all three letters. The *hilasmos* (propitiation/expiation) debate is now documented within the letters-of-john text page as an interpretive crux — a full concept page is deferred pending more detailed coverage of the excursus in Kruse's commentary.

**Methodological note**: The Johannine ingest introduces "mirror reading" (reconstructing the opponents' views from the author's rebuttals) as a named hermeneutical method — and its pitfalls. Both Marshall and Kruse are careful to note that mirror reading is susceptible to circularity and overdetermination; the secessionists' exact teaching remains genuinely uncertain.

**The PNTC series enters the wiki** alongside the NICNT, NICOT, NIGTC, BECNT, NAC, UBCS, NIBC, and BTC. The wiki now has representation from all major evangelical NT commentary series in current production.

**The NT epistolary genre enters the wiki** for the first time (Acts is narrative; Gospels are Gospels). The three Johannine letters — ranging from the 5-chapter circular homily of 1 John to the two shortest NT books (2 and 3 John) — represent three distinct epistolary subgenres: circular pastoral address, community letter, and private individual letter.

**The Gospel of John enters the wiki — Michaels NICNT (2010), and the Johannine canon is now complete**: The wiki now contains all four canonical Gospels (Matthew and Mark via France, Luke via Green, John via Michaels) and the three Johannine Epistles (Marshall + Kruse). Michaels's NICNT John is the replacement volume for Leon Morris's 1995 edition and represents a half-century of Johannine scholarship in retirement. The most methodologically distinctive feature: Michaels is the only ingested NT commentator to combine *evangelical confessional commitment* with explicit engagement of Bultmann as a close-reading partner (not just a source-critical adversary). His most characteristic claims: (1) the "preamble" is 1:1–5, not the "prologue" of 1:1–18 — a structural reorientation that repositions John the Baptizer as the first narrative voice; (2) "light" (*phōs*) is the Gospel's dominant image, not "Logos/Word" — the Logos title appears only four times; (3) rebirth *precedes* faith in John's soteriology — the Father's drawing initiative is primary, not secondary to human decision; (4) the Beloved Disciple may be a brother of Jesus (based on 19:26–27), not John son of Zebedee. New concept pages: Logos Christology, I Am Sayings (Egō Eimi), The Paraclete/Advocate, Testimony/Witness (Martyria). New controversy pages: The Beloved Disciple identity, John and the Synoptic Gospels, Johannine Determinism, John 1:1 ("The Word Was God"). 

**Hermeneutical framework note**: Michaels uses synchronic close reading with evangelical confessional commitment, making him methodologically close to France but with a different series context (NICNT vs. NIGTC) and a substantially more expansive reading of Johannine soteriology. Where France's Gospels track fulfillment (Matthew) and narrative Christology (Mark), Michaels tracks testimony/revelation as John's governing framework.

**The Johannine witness concept page introduces a new NT hermeneutical structure**: The testimony (*martyria*) framework — a chain of interlocking testimonies from the Father through John the Baptizer, Jesus, the signs, the Scriptures, the Paraclete, and the Beloved Disciple to the written Gospel and its readers — is unique to John among the Gospels and represents a fundamentally different epistemic architecture from the Synoptics' "kingdom proclamation" mode. The Gospel is structured as an extended legal drama; the public ministry (chs. 2–12) is a *trial* before it is a trial.

**The Johannine canon as a whole**: The wiki now covers the Gospel of John (Michaels), Acts (Bock), and 1–2–3 John (Marshall + Kruse). The Gospel and Epistles share vocabulary, theology, and community but apply terms differently (most notably *paraklētos* = Holy Spirit in the Gospel, Jesus as heavenly advocate in 1 John 2:1). The secessionist crisis documented in 1 John is now more fully legible against the Gospel's Christology. The Beloved Disciple question connects the Gospel to the Epistles' "elder" and to the broader question of the Johannine community's historical identity.

**Romans enters the wiki — Schreiner BECNT (1998), and the Pauline corpus begins**: Romans is the first Pauline letter in the wiki, the longest NT epistle, and the most theologically contested text in Christian history. Schreiner (SBTS, Reformed Baptist, New Calvinist) brings a one-volume commentary aimed at pastors and scholars, dedicated to John Piper and explicitly committed to tracing the "glory of God" as the letter's central theme inductively. His position: the righteousness of God is both forensic (imputed status) and transformative (saving power); justification is both declaration and new creation in Christ. He is a leading evangelical critic of the New Perspective on Paul (Sanders/Dunn/Wright) — maintaining that "works of the law" denotes human merit broadly, not merely ethnic boundary markers. On Romans 9: unconditional individual election. On Romans 11:26: future mass conversion of ethnic Israel near the second coming. On Romans 7:14–25: regenerate believer's ongoing struggle. New concept pages: Justification; the New Perspective on Paul. New controversy pages: The Righteousness of God; "All Israel Will Be Saved"; Romans 7 — the Identity of the "I."

**First Pauline letter creates critical new cross-wiki tensions**:
- **Schreiner's justification (forensic + transformative)** stands in productive tension with **Michaels's Johannine soteriology** (eternal life as knowledge/revelation; no "repentance" language; rebirth precedes faith). John and Paul use different frameworks; both are now documented and can be compared.
- **Schreiner's unconditional election (Romans 9)** directly parallels and reinforces the Calvinist reading in the **Johannine Determinism** controversy (faith precedes rebirth vs. rebirth enables faith). Both now documented.
- **The New Perspective on Paul** critique of "works of law as boundary markers" intersects with the **intermarriage controversy in Ezra-Nehemiah** (ethnic/covenant boundary maintenance) and with the **Oral Torah / Halacha** pages documenting Jewish legal tradition.
- **Schreiner's "all Israel" (future ethnic Jewish conversion)** creates a cross-reference point with **Ezekiel 37 (dry bones)**, **Zechariah** (the nation looking on the one they have pierced, 12:10), and the **new covenant** concept page.

**The BECNT series now has two entries**: Acts (Bock) and Romans (Schreiner) — both Reformed-evangelical, one Dispensational Baptist (Bock) and one Reformed Baptist (Schreiner), creating a useful intra-series methodological comparison.

**1 Corinthians enters the wiki — Ciampa-Rosner PNTC (2010), the first co-authored NT commentary and the first with a biblical-theological OT-primary method**: The ingest of Ciampa and Rosner's Pillar NT Commentary on 1 Corinthians introduces a commentator pair whose distinctive contribution is reading the letter as a document saturated in OT categories rather than Greco-Roman ones. Their signature claim — the social values thesis — reframes every Corinthian problem as cultural infiltration (Roman status-seeking, sexual license, idol participation) rather than theological deviance. The letter's structure follows a Jewish moral template for Gentile converts: flee sexual immorality, flee idolatry, glorify God. The same four-element pattern (cross/wisdom → sexual purity → anti-idolatry/proper worship → resurrection hope) appears across the Pauline corpus (Romans 1:21–28, 1 Thessalonians 1:9–10, Romans 15:5–16, Ephesians, Colossians, Titus), suggesting Paul has an instinctive shape to his Gentile instruction.

**Two OT books as primary templates**: Deuteronomy provides the canonical analogue for the letter's ethical framework — Paul as new Moses explaining the new-exodus ethics to the newly redeemed. Isaiah provides the key to chapters 1–4: the two-wisdom contrast, Paul's eschatological herald self-understanding (the suffering servant sent to the nations), and the eschatological vision of worldwide Gentile worship. Malachi 1:11 ("in every place") is cited in 1:2 as the fulfillment of that vision.

**The eschatological temple concept is now documented**: The church as God's dwelling place (1 Cor 3:16; 6:19; Eph 2:21; 1 Pet 2:4–5) is the wiki's first dedicated ecclesiological concept page. It grounds Ciampa-Rosner's ethics and cross-links to holiness, divine glory, tabernacle, new covenant, and passover pages.

**New cross-wiki tensions introduced**:
- **Ciampa-Rosner's glory-of-God teleology** (God "all in all," 15:28) directly parallels **Schreiner's** argument that glory of God is the ultimate purpose of Romans. Both now documented; the Pauline cross-wiki concept of divine glory is a recurring theme worth a dedicated concept page.
- **Ciampa-Rosner's bodily resurrection** (1 Cor 15 as the consummation of creation, not a spiritualized escape) stands in tension with whatever position may emerge from future Gnostic or mystical sources — a controversy page now anticipates this.
- **The "flee sexual immorality / flee idolatry" structure** has direct OT antecedents now in the wiki: the intermarriage controversy (Ezra-Nehemiah), Hosea's sexual immorality metaphor, the Deuteronomy page's purity laws, and the golden calf apostasy in Exodus — all now linkable to 1 Corinthians.
- **The social values thesis** suggests the Corinthian church mirrors the pressure modern churches face from surrounding culture — a connection Ciampa-Rosner explicitly develop in their postmodernity section, noting Paul's relevance to a culture that elevates peer-group local narratives over universal truth.

**2 Corinthians enters the wiki — Seifrid PNTC (2014), and the Corinthian correspondence is now complete**: Seifrid's commentary is the most emotionally intense source in the wiki to date and the first to deploy the *theologia crucis* as an explicit hermeneutical key. Its central claim — that God's saving work operates *sub contrario*, hidden under its opposite — gives 2 Corinthians a different theological register than 1 Corinthians (Ciampa-Rosner: social values and OT moral template) or Romans (Schreiner: forensic righteousness and glory of God). All three are complementary approaches to Pauline theology; their differences are methodological as much as substantive.

**The Corinthian correspondence as a complete dossier**: The wiki now covers all four letters of the Corinthian correspondence: the lost "Previous Letter" (referenced), 1 Corinthians (Ciampa-Rosner), the lost "Tearful Letter" (referenced), and 2 Corinthians (Seifrid). The full narrative arc of Paul's most troubled pastoral relationship is now readable: founding mission → factionalism → painful confrontation → tearful letter → fragile reconciliation → apostolic defense. This canonical sequence is the closest thing in the NT to a sustained pastoral crisis with a documented history.

**The theologia crucis as a new hermeneutical mode in the wiki**: Seifrid's *sub contrario* reading is methodologically distinct from everything ingested so far. Earlier commentators (Schreiner, Ciampa-Rosner) use grammatico-historical + theological synthesis; Seifrid uses this plus an explicitly Lutheran theological grammar (power in weakness, alien righteousness, the hidden God). This represents a new data point in the wiki's map of evangelical hermeneutical diversity: Reformed-Baptist (Schreiner), biblical-theological/OT-primary (Ciampa-Rosner), and Lutheran-evangelical (Seifrid) are now all documented for the Pauline letters.

**New cross-wiki tensions and connections**:
- **Seifrid's "apostle as Christian existence in large-screen display"** connects to **Michaels's Johannine soteriology** (eternal life as knowledge/relationship) and to **Schreiner's "glory of God as goal"** — three different frameworks for the same fundamental claim that Christian existence is participation in Christ's dying and rising.
- **The new creation (5:17)** creates a major new concept page that cross-links to **Isaiah** (the primary OT source), **Romans 8** (the cosmic development), **1 Corinthians 15** (the bodily resurrection as new creation), and the **imago Dei** (the eschatological restoration of the image). The already/not yet structure of new creation is the most fully developed eschatological category in the wiki so far.
- **Seifrid's minimalist opponents hypothesis** is a methodological statement about mirror-reading that applies beyond 2 Corinthians — it speaks directly to the **Johannine secessionist controversy** (how much can we reconstruct opponents' theology from the author's rebuttals?) and to **the NPP debate** (are we mirror-reading Paul correctly when we say he opposes self-righteousness?).

## Sociology of Early Christian Growth: Stark Ingest (added 2026-06-06)

Ingest of Rodney Stark, *The Rise of Christianity: A Sociologist Reconsiders History* (Princeton University Press, 1996). Stark (University of Washington; later Baylor) applies formal social-scientific tools to early Christian history: rational choice theory, network-conversion theory (the Lofland-Stark model from 1965 fieldwork), religious economies framework (Iannaccone), quantitative demographic modeling, and 22-city urban analysis.

**Scope contribution**: The first sociological source in the wiki. Adds a distinct explanatory register to the existing theological and historical-critical accounts. Covers the full 40–300 CE arc of early Christian growth and provides detailed treatment of Antioch (now a full location page), the Hellenized diaspora Jewish mission field (now a full group page), and two new concept pages (religious economy, network conversion). Complements Hurtado (what was distinctive) and Kruger (how the church developed internally) with a third analytical layer: *how the sociological growth mechanisms operated*.

**Nine principal arguments**:
1. **40%/decade growth model** — no miraculous growth rate required; comparable to modern Mormon growth
2. **Lofland-Stark network conversion** — social ties, not preaching, drive conversion; open networks essential
3. **Jewish mission succeeded** — Hellenized diaspora Jews as primary pool; Marcion affair as evidence
4. **Epidemic nursing and differential mortality** — Antonine and Cyprian plagues; Christian nursing → ~20% higher survival; network restructuring
5. **Sex ratio and fertility** — Christian prohibition of infanticide/abortion → female surplus in church; Guttentag-Secord: higher women's status; exogamous marriage → secondary converts; superior birthrate
6. **Quantitative urban analysis** — 22 cities; synagogue presence + Romanization explain 67% of variance in Christianization timing; Gnosticism as Christian heresy (regression). Rudolph *Gnosis* (2026-06-07) adds phenomenological synthesis (Pleroma/Sophia/Demiurge/gnosis myth as coherent late-antique religion), Nag Hammadi primary sources, Manichaeism/Mandaeans, and new pages (scholarship summary, 4 concepts, nag-hammadi text); see expanded [[gnostics|Gnostics]] and [[valentinus|Valentinus]].
7. **Antioch as urban chaos case study** — 195 persons/acre; 41 catastrophes in 600 years; Christianity as revitalization movement
8. **Martyrdom as rational choice** — Iannaccone's sacrifice-and-stigma model; free-rider problem; Ignatius as rational actor in regal-imperial journey; 60s martyrdoms solving failed-prophecy crisis
9. **Religious economy** — paganism declining before Constantine; exclusive vs. nonexclusive firms; Isis spread correlated with Christian spread (+0.67)

## Early Christianity in the Roman World: Hurtado Ingest (added 2026-06-06)

Ingest of Larry W. Hurtado, *Destroyer of the Gods: Early Christian Distinctiveness in the Roman World* (Baylor University Press, 2016). Hurtado (1943–2019; University of Edinburgh) was the leading exponent of a social-historical approach to earliest Christology and Christian distinctiveness. This book is a popular-level synthesis of his major academic work (*One God, One Lord* [1988]; *Lord Jesus Christ* [2003]); it argues that early Christianity was genuinely, recognizably distinctive in the Roman-world environment across five axes, and that its most striking feature — the dyadic veneration of God and Jesus — was a mutation from within Jewish monotheism, not a product of pagan syncretism.

**Scope contribution**: The first historical-critical / social-historical source in the wiki. Expands coverage to include the first three centuries of Christianity as a religious-sociological phenomenon, early Christian manuscript culture (*nomina sacra*, codex preference), and the intellectual history of Christological origins (Bousset debate).

**Five axes of distinctiveness** documented in Hurtado:
1. **Exclusivist monotheism in the Roman religious market** — Christians and Jews refused to honor the gods; only Christians actively evangelized against them and drew persecution specifically for this refusal.
2. **Bookishness** — The codex (~75–100% of early Christian scriptural manuscripts) vs. the Roman bookroll (95%); *nomina sacra* as visual scribal piety; the earliest recoverable Christian visual culture.
3. **Trans-ethnic identity** — "Neither Jew nor Gentile" as a new *tertium quid*; not ethnic religion, not philosophy, not voluntary association, but a new corporate identity demanding primary loyalty.
4. **Sexual/moral rigorism** — Requiring of all members the level of sexual fidelity only expected of Jewish proselytes; radical departure from Roman sexual ethics, especially for men.
5. **Dyadic devotion** — The joint corporate veneration of God the Father and Jesus as co-recipients of worship, expressed through baptism, invocation, hymns, Lord's Supper, prayer, and confession. This is Hurtado's most distinctive analytical contribution. Argues it arose within Aramaic-speaking Jewish Christianity within the first months/years of the movement — before any substantial Gentile influence was possible. The primary literary evidence is Paul's letters (c. AD 50-55 but reflecting practices originating c. AD 32-34): 1 Cor 8:4-6 (modified *Shema*), Phil 2:9-11 (Isa 45:23 applied to Jesus), Rom 10:9-13 (calling on the name of the Lord = Jesus = salvation). Refutation of Bousset's *Kyrios Christos* (1913) thesis: the *Maranatha* (Aramaic: "come, Lord") in 1 Cor 16:22 is decisive — Aramaic, not Greek; Jewish, not Gentile.

## Patristic Philosophy: Wolfson Ingest (added 2026-06-07)

Ingest of Harry Austryn Wolfson, *The Philosophy of the Church Fathers, Volume I: Faith, Trinity, Incarnation* (Harvard University Press, 1956; 678-page PDF scan). This is the classic, exhaustive reconstruction of the "philosophy of the Church Fathers" as a coherent Christian version of Greek philosophy (parallel to Wolfson’s earlier *Philo*). It builds directly on Philo as prolegomenon and uses Wolfson’s hypothetico-deductive method (general hypothesis + representative texts pieced into unified system, tracing "latent processes of reasoning" behind scattered terms/formulas/analogies; often concluding with Augustine or John of Damascus).

**Key additions and contributions** (complementary to the recent Stead *Philosophy in Christian Antiquity* ingest):
- **Faith and Reason (Part One)**: Detailed attitudes (Paul vs. "wisdom of the world"; inevitable introduction of philosophy); allegory as philosophical method (Philo’s terms + NT predictive/moral/preexistential senses; classifications in Clement [corporeal/psychical/spiritual], Origen [with wider "mysteries"], development in Jerome/Augustine/Cassian; Antiochians deviate); scriptural presuppositions (six of Philo’s eight embodied in Christian Rule of Faith: God’s existence/unity, creation, providence, Law/revelation incl. Oral Law, ideas); philosophy as "handmaiden of Scripture"; single faith theories (Tertullian: self-sufficiency + use of philosophy; Origen: rationalized faith superior yet simple faith meritorious) vs. double faith theory (Clement: faith as assent to Scripture [immediate or demonstrated = gnosis/scientific faith]; equality of simple and demonstrated; Augustine: philosophy permissible, faith as assent, equality of both).
- **Trinity (Part Two)**: NT tripartite confessions (baptismal, 2 Cor salutation, Paul’s hortatory) via Paul’s Spirit = preexistent Christ identification; John’s Logos (Philo + Wisdom of Solomon); early identification of preexistent Christ as both Logos and Spirit (no clear pre-birth Trinity in Apostolic Fathers/Apologists); differentiation and twofold stage theory (Logos becomes distinct at a point); single stage in Irenaeus/Origen (debates on Clement; "eternal generation" as continuous process + from eternity; by will [Philo/Apologists/Origen/Athanasius] or nature); lingering identifications (Spirit with preexistent Christ: "God," wisdom, functions applied to both); "mystery of the generation" (begetter analogies, physical images like sun beam, mystery remains); "mystery of the Trinity" (three-and-one: relative unity of rule or substratum/genus/species—ousia/hypostasis, homoousios; detailed in Origen [hypostases for individuals, ousia for common, homoousios + rule], Tertullian [personae/substantia; contrasting pairs], Basil fixing vocabulary amid Nicene vagueness/heresies [ousia/hypostasis for common substratum; Creed "begotten of the ousia of the Father," homoousios; expression open to individual/generic/substratum interpretations generating suspicions like homoiousios]).
- **Logos and Platonic Ideas (Ch. XIII)**: Fathers’ views on intelligible world/ideas; which Plato interpretation followed (Aristotelian, Albinian, Philonic transcendent in Logos/mind of God often influential); analysis of Justin, Tertullian, Clement, Origen, Augustine.
- **Incarnation (Part Three)**: "Two-and-one" via physical union analogies (Aristotelian composition/mixture/"predominance" [soul-body/form-matter, loosely mixture/composition]; Stoic variants; five kinds); orthodox "predominance" (Tertullian electrum [popular], Origen, Gregories, Augustine, Cyril, Leontius, John Damascene, Fifth Council); perichoresis ("total penetration," Stoic mixture term) for interchange of properties (Gregories, pseudo-Cyril, Maximus, John Damascene); mystery remains (differences from human union); unorthodox (Apollinaris/Monophysites apply differently to deny full human nature; Nestorius "composition"/synapheia for two persons); connected wills/operations (Monenergist/Monothelete controversies; Sophronius, Sergius, Honorius, Maximus vs. Pyrrhus; prohibitions).
- **The Anathematized (Part Four)**: Gnosticism as "verbal Christianizing of paganism" (syncretisms into tiered supramundane systems with prolation; Cerinthus, Simon Magus, others classified by number of beings/Gods/natures, Christology as Ebionitic/Docetic/Sidereal Monophysitism or Polyphysitism; not true philosophy—Fathers trace elements to Plato etc. but distinguish); heresies as logical from catholic difficulties (Modalism [Sabellius: one thing of many persons] and Creationalism [Arius: three generically different ousiai/hypostases] for Trinity; neo-Ebionism [Theodotus, Nestorius] and neo-Docetism [Apollinaris, Eutychians] for Christology; mixed cases; statutory Catholicism emerges).

## Early Christian Primary Texts: Holmes Apostolic Fathers Ingest (added 2026-06-06)

Ingest of Michael W. Holmes (ed./trans.), *The Apostolic Fathers: Greek Texts and English Translations*, 3rd ed. (Baker Academic, 2007). This is the standard modern critical edition of the ten documents (and Papias fragment corpus) traditionally grouped as the "Apostolic Fathers" — the earliest non-canonical Christian writings after the NT. The collection spans ca. 96–225 CE and covers the generation immediately after the apostles. Holmes provides the critical Greek text, fresh English translations, and historical-critical introductions for each document.

**Documents now in the wiki** (all new text pages):
1. **1 Clement** (ca. 96 CE) — Roman church to Corinth; collegial presbyterate; succession argument; Peter/Paul martyrdom witness (ch. 5); nearly canonical (Codex Alexandrinus, Codex Hierosolymitanus)
2. **2 Clement** (ca. 140 CE) — oldest complete Christian sermon outside NT; anonymous Corinthian presbyter; first NT text cited as *graphē* (2.4); early functional Christological equation (1.1); non-canonical sayings
3. **Didache** (late 1st c.) — oldest eucharistic prayers; Lord's Prayer with doxology; baptism rubric; itinerant/resident governance transition; sole complete Greek text in Codex Hierosolymitanus (discovered 1873)
4. **Epistle of Barnabas** (ca. 70–135 CE) — radical allegorism; thoroughgoing supersessionism; Two Ways section; in Codex Sinaiticus (nearly canonical)
5. **Shepherd of Hermas** (ca. 90–155 CE) — Roman; postbaptismal repentance; elm-and-vine parable; minimal Christology; more early copies than many canonical writings; in Codex Sinaiticus
6. **Epistle to Diognetus** (ca. 150–225 CE) — Greek apology; "third race" identity passage (sec. 5); soul-body analogy (sec. 6); sole manuscript destroyed 1870; includes Fragment of Quadratus (earliest Christian apologist, Hadrian's reign)
7. **Fragments of Papias** (ca. 100–125 CE) — 28 fragments; "living voice" over books; Mark as Peter's interpreter; Matthew's "Hebrew logia"; literal millennialism; Two Johns problem

*Letters of Ignatius, Letter of Polycarp, and Martyrdom of Polycarp are documented in the existing figure pages for [[ignatius-of-antioch|Ignatius]] and [[polycarp|Polycarp]] rather than as separate text pages.*

**Scope expansion**: The wiki now covers primary early Christian texts in addition to OT, NT, rabbinic, and patristic-philosophical secondary scholarship. This creates a new register: documents that are themselves the object of scholarly study (not scholarly tools for studying the Bible). The canon-boundary question is now addressed from multiple angles — Kruger (theological-historical), Holmes (textual-critical), and the primary texts themselves (who cites what as scripture, which manuscripts include which books).

**Key hermeneutical frameworks in the Apostolic Fathers**:
- **Radical allegory** (Barnabas) — the most extreme example of allegorical exegesis in early Christianity; predates and influences the Alexandrian tradition
- **Jewish-Christian Two Ways ethics** (Didache, Barnabas) — shared antecedent source; moral dualism from Qumran tradition
- **Oral tradition primacy** (Papias) — "the living voice" over written texts; oral chain of custody from eyewitnesses
- **Greek apologetic rhetoric** (Diognetus) — Platonizing; soul-body categories; anti-idol polemic from Jewish tradition
- **Deliberative rhetoric** (1 Clement) — appeal to cosmic *taxis*; OT exempla; nearly classical in form
- **Apocalyptic-prophetic** (Hermas) — Jewish-Christian apocalypse form; 4 Ezra / 1 Enoch parallels
- **Anti-Docetic polemic** (Ignatius, Polycarp) — real flesh, real suffering, real resurrection; eucharistic realism as the test

**Cross-wiki connections newly established**:
- The Apostolic Fathers texts are the primary evidential base for [[monepiscopate|monepiscopate]] development, [[nt-canon-formation|NT canon formation]], [[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]], and [[bauer-thesis|Bauer thesis refutation]] — all previously documented from Kruger's secondary account; now anchored in primary sources

## The Biblical World (Bronze Age–Persian Period): Coogan Ingest (added 2026-06-07)

Ingest of **Michael D. Coogan (ed.), *The Oxford History of the Biblical World*** (Oxford,
1998) — a multi-author historical-critical and archaeological survey. This ingest was
directed specifically at **enriching the `groups/` and `locations/` page types**, which
had previously covered only early-Christian groups (Pharisees, Gnostics, etc.) and three
sites (Alexandria, Antioch, Nicaea). The wiki had **no Hebrew Bible / ANE peoples or
places** at all. This ingest fills that gap from the Bronze Age through the Persian period.

**Major scope expansion**: the wiki now covers the human and geographical backdrop of the
Hebrew Bible — the peoples Israel emerged from, fought, and was exiled by, and the cities
and sites where its history unfolded — grounded in archaeology and ANE texts rather than
the biblical narrative alone.

## Jewish Mysticism — The Zohar / Kabbalah: Matt Ingest (added 2026-06-07)

Ingest of **Daniel C. Matt (trans.), *The Zohar: Pritzker Edition, Volume I*** (Stanford
University Press, 2004) — the standard scholarly English translation, from a reconstructed
critical Aramaic text, of the foundational work of [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]]. Volume I covers
the Haqdamah (Proem) and the Torah portions Be-Reshit (Gen 1:1–6:8) and Noaḥ (Gen
6:9–11:32), with a scholarly introduction by Arthur Green.

**First-order scope expansion**: this is the wiki's **first source on Jewish mysticism**.
Until now "Jewish tradition" in the wiki meant rabbinic-halakhic Judaism (Talmud, Mishna,
Unterman). The Zohar introduces a wholly different register — a theosophical, mythic,
erotic, theurgical conception of God that the classical rabbis would scarcely recognize,
yet which became, within two centuries, the third canonical pillar of Judaism alongside
Bible and Talmud. The ingest establishes a new tradition page ([[kabbalah]]), a new text
page ([[zohar]]), and a cluster of new concept pages.

**Core theology recorded**:
- **Two-tier Godhead**: the unknowable [[ein-sof|Ein Sof]] (Infinite) vs. the ten
  [[sefirot|sefirot]] (Keter…Malkhut), the dynamic, gendered emanations that constitute the
  knowable inner life of God. The biblical "blessed Holy One" addressed in prayer is
  Tif'eret (6th sefirah), not Ein Sof.
- **The divine feminine**: [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]/Malkhut as a full divine persona — Bride,
  Moon, Assembly of Israel — whose union (*zivvuga qaddisha*) with Tif'eret is the central
  theurgical event and whose exile is Israel's exile.
- **Theodicy of the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]**: evil as the parasitic shadow of Gevurah's
  untempered excess, originating *within* the divine rather than as a second principle.
- **Theurgy**: commandments performed with *kavvanah* maintain the sefirotic union — human
  action completes something incomplete in God.
- **Sod-level hermeneutics ([[pardes|PaRDeS]])**: theogonic reading (Scripture as the inner
  biography of God), sefirotic correspondence, reverse parsing (Gen 1:1 as "With Beginning,
  [Ein Sof] created Elohim"), letter mysticism.

**Hermeneutical framework**: **Sod** — the deepest of the four PaRDeS levels — read against
the wiki's existing **grammatical-historical** (Hamilton on Genesis) and **historical-
critical** (Coogan) voices. The Zohar neither confirms nor contests the plain sense; it
claims to read *beneath* it. Daniel Matt himself is a **historical-critical** scholar
(de León authorship) translating with deep sympathy for the text's vision.

**Contradictions flagged (not flattened)**: the Zoharic/rabbinic ambivalence toward
[[noah]] (righteous only "in his generation") vs. the NT's unqualified "herald of
righteousness"; **emanation** vs. *creatio ex nihilo* on the [[genesis]] page; evil from
*within* the divine (Kabbalah) vs. evil as external adversary or moral trial
([[theodicy]], [[revelation]]).

**Authorship controversy**: attributed to the 2nd-c. Tanna [[shimon-bar-yochai|Shim'on bar
Yoḥai]] but composed by [[moses-de-leon|Moses de León]] and his Castilian circle c.
1280–1300 (Scholem's critical consensus). A paradigm critical-vs-confessional dispute,
recorded at [[zohar-authorship]].

**Cross-wiki connections**: the patriarchs are mapped to sefirot — [[abraham]] (Ḥesed),
[[isaac]] (Gevurah), [[moses]] (Tif'eret, "bridegroom of the Shekhinah"), with [[elijah]]
(Yesod/circumcision) and [[korah]] (left-vs-right conflict) re-read accordingly; [[adam]]
gains a Kabbalistic section (the first sin as cosmic divorce); comparisons run to
[[cappadocian-fathers|the Trinity]], [[philo-of-alexandria|Philo]], and Sufi *ta'wil*.

**Source/translator pages**: [[zohar]] (text), [[daniel-matt]] (translator),
[[moses-de-leon]] (author).
