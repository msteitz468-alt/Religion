# Wendy Doniger

**Wendy Doniger** (b. 1940) is an American **Sanskritist and historian of religions** (University of
Chicago), among the most prolific — and most publicly contested — modern Western interpreters of
Hinduism. The wiki knows her through *[[doniger-the-hindus|The Hindus: An Alternative History]]* (2009),
ingested at the scope of its method chapters (Chs. 1 and 25).

## Hermeneutical Method

Doniger reads Hindu texts as a **historian of religions with a psychoanalytic and feminist sensibility**,
attentive to sexuality, the body, and myth. Distinctive moves recorded from the ingested chapters:

- **Alternative history "from the margins."** She tells Hinduism's story chronologically while
  deliberately privileging **women, the lower castes (Dalits), and animals** over the Brahmin mainstream —
  recovering their "**perfumes**" (*vāsanās*) in texts authored by elite men, via a "hermeneutic of
  suspicion."
- **Anti-essentialism — the "Zen diagram."** Against any single defining belief, she models Hinduism as a
  **polythetic cluster** (after Wittgenstein's family resemblance): a set of overlapping circles with
  **no central ring** — "no central something to which the peripheral people were peripheral." Hindus are
  "**polydox**," more **orthoprax than orthodox** (caring about right practice over right belief).
- **A self-described "recovering Orientalist."** A textual scholar who nonetheless turns texts *against*
  the Orientalism (and the Brahmin-centrism) that produced them, to defend their richness as evidence for
  the marginalized.
- **Myth as the "smoke of history."** Myths cannot reconstruct events but reveal "the history of
  sentiments rather than events" — and, because "ideas are facts too," themselves drive history.
- **Against the "siren song of hindsight."** She refuses to read later Hindu meaning back into pre-textual
  remains — the discipline at work in her treatment of the [[indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley]]
  ("Is Indus religion a myth?", the proto-Śiva seal debate) — and frames Hinduism's formation as
  **fusion / bricolage** of Vedic and non-Vedic ("the non-Veda is the *fons et origo*"), neither claiming
  priority (her Viṣṇu-and-Brahmā "mutual creation" image).
- **Animals as a social cipher** — horse/cow/dog mapped to power/purity/pollution and to
  Kshatriya/Brahmin/Pariah.

Her readings — foregrounding sexuality (e.g. the *linga* as phallus), Freudian symbolism, and a
debunking stance toward pious self-image — are precisely what make her work a flashpoint; see
[[who-defines-hinduism|the controversy]].

## Significance for the Wiki

Doniger is the wiki's principal **modern-critical, anti-essentialist** voice on Hinduism — a deliberate
counterweight to the tradition's own self-description and to confessional readings. Her "Zen diagram"
account directly informs the [[hinduism|Hinduism]] tradition page's treatment of the
**definition problem**, and her work anchors the [[who-defines-hinduism|"who defines Hinduism?"]]
controversy (with the Hindu-nationalist objection and Rajiv Malhotra's response as the other pole).
