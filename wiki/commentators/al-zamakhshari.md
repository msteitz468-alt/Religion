# Al-Zamakhshari

**Abu al-Qasim Mahmud b. Umar al-Zamakhshari** (born Rajab 27, 467 / March 18, 1075 at
Zamakhshar in **Khwarazm**; died Dhu al-Hijja 8, 538 / June 12, 1144 in Jurjaniyya) was the
greatest **[[mutazilism|Mu'tazilite]]** Qur'an commentator and one of the towering Arabic
grammarians and lexicographers of the classical age. His Qur'an commentary, **[[quran|al-Kashshaf]]**,
is the paradigm case the wiki uses for **[[tafsir|*tafsir bi'l-ra'y*]]** (reason-based exegesis) and
a textbook example of the **Contradiction Protocol** in reception: a work condemned for its theology
yet so philologically indispensable that the orthodox preserved it by purging it.

## Biographical Context

A native of the Persianate region of Khwarazm — a stronghold where Mu'tazilism continued to thrive
long after it had been eclipsed in the central lands — al-Zamakhshari travelled widely for learning,
studying grammar and lexicography with the leading masters of his day. He visited Mecca twice and
resided there for roughly twelve years, earning the honorific **Jar Allah** ("Neighbor of God"). He
lost a foot (variously attributed to a fall through a roof or to frostbite on a Khwarazmian journey)
and walked with a wooden leg. He composed some fifty works across exegesis, hadith, jurisprudence,
literature, grammar, and lexicography — including the lexicon **Asas al-balagha**, the grammar
**al-Mufassal**, and a Mu'tazilite creed, **al-Minhaj fi usul al-din**, which lays bare the doctrine
that drives his commentary.

## Hermeneutical Method

Al-Zamakhshari's tafsir is the disciplined, **rationalist** pole of Qur'anic interpretation:

- **Rhetoric (*balagha*) and inimitability (*i'jaz*)**: His central exegetical conviction is that the
  Qur'an's miraculous character lies in its **rhetorical perfection**. *Al-Kashshaf* ("the
  unveiler") is built to unveil that eloquence — its metaphor (*majaz*), simile, syntax, and word
  order — making it as much a masterwork of Arabic literary criticism as of theology. This is why
  even his enemies could not do without him.
- **Grammar, lexicography, and variant readings (*qira'at*)**: He grounds meaning in rigorous
  philology and adduces the licensed [[qiraat-variant-readings|variant recitations]] as exegetical
  evidence.
- **Ta'wil of anthropomorphism**: Following Mu'tazilite *[[tawhid|tawhid]]*, he refuses to ascribe
  body, place, direction, face, hand, or eye to God; the anthropomorphic ("ambiguous", *mutashabih*)
  verses are read by **[[allegorical-reading|ta'wil]]** (metaphorical interpretation) — "the hand of
  God" as power, "God's face" as essence, the "sitting upon the throne" as dominion.
- **Doctrine through philology**: The five Mu'tazilite principles ([[mutazilism|*al-usul
  al-khamsa*]]) are not imposed but argued *out of* the grammar and rhetoric of the text — a method
  later students (notably **Fakhr al-Din al-Razi**, by contrast, the great Ash'arite-leaning
  rationalist) and refuters both had to engage on his own philological terms.

## Characteristic Positions

Through *al-Kashshaf* al-Zamakhshari defends the whole Mu'tazilite system: the **createdness of the
Qur'an**, divine **unity** stripped of all anthropomorphism, divine **justice** entailing genuine
human **free will** (against predestinarian readings of "sealing the hearts" and "leading astray"),
the **promise and the threat** (the unrepentant grave sinner is irrevocably punished), the
**intermediate position** of the grave sinner, and the duty of **enjoining right and forbidding
wrong**. The notoriety of his Mu'tazilism is captured in the report that *al-Kashshaf* originally
opened, "Praise be to God who **created** (*khalaqa*) the Qur'an" — later softened by copyists to "who
**sent down** (*anzala*) the Qur'an" to make the work palatable.

## Reception and Influence (the "Purged" Commentary)

*Al-Kashshaf* posed a problem the Sunni tradition solved by **expurgation rather than rejection**:

- **Refutation**: **Ibn al-Munayyir al-Iskandari** (d. 683/1284) wrote
  *al-Intisaf min al-Kashshaf*, the standard point-by-point Sunni rebuttal of its Mu'tazilite
  interpretations, routinely printed in the margins of *al-Kashshaf* itself.
- **Sanitized successor**: The Ash'arite **al-Baydawi** (d. ca. 685/1286) produced
  *Anwar al-tanzil* — essentially an abridged *al-Kashshaf* that **keeps the rhetorical-grammatical
  brilliance but strips out the Mu'tazilism**, becoming the standard Sunni *madrasa* commentary. The
  contrast (al-Zamakhshari subordinating revelation to reason where they conflict; al-Baydawi giving
  revelation priority) is the classic Mu'tazilite/Ash'arite hinge.
- **Glossators**: A dense tradition of *hashiyas* (al-Jurjani, **Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani**, and others)
  grew up around the text — the surest sign of a canon-shaping work.

That a confessedly heterodox tafsir became indispensable to the orthodoxy that condemned it is the
defining fact of al-Zamakhshari's afterlife, and is recorded here **without adjudication** of the
theology at stake (Contradiction Protocol).
