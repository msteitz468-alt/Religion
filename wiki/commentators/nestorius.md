# Nestorius

Nestorius (c. 386 – c. 450), Patriarch of Constantinople (428–431), was the Antiochene theologian condemned at the Council of Ephesus (431) for his rejection of the title **Theotokos** ("God-bearer," Mother of God) for the Virgin Mary, and the eponym of "Nestorianism" — the two-subject Christology later associated with the Church of the East. In Johannes Zachhuber's [[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]] he is a transitional figure in the history of the **[[double-homoousion|double homoousion]]**: he speaks repeatedly of Christ as "consubstantial with us," but in a pre-Cappadocian, *derivative* sense rather than the technical sense of [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|the classical theory]].

## Christological Position

Nestorius's central concern was to protect the full, distinct reality of Christ's humanity and the immutability of the divine Word. He objected to **Theotokos** on the principle that "it is a property of every mother to bear what is consubstantial with her": Mary, being human, bore the human nature, not the divine Word — so she is properly *Christotokos* ("Christ-bearer"), not *Theotokos*. This presupposes a strong distinction of the two natures, each with its own *prosōpon*, conjoined in the one Christ — which [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]] attacked as splitting Christ into "two Sons."

## Zachhuber's Analysis

- **The double homoousion in a derivative key.** Nestorius frequently calls Christ "consubstantial with us," but Zachhuber stresses three points: (1) it is not yet a fixed *formula* paired symmetrically with consubstantiality with the Father; (2) he does not use *homoousios* in the Cappadocian technical sense (he rarely speaks of distinctive properties of a nature, and lacks the Basil/Gregory jargon); (3) his usage is *derivative* — tied to the principle that a mother bears what is consubstantial with her (*propria est parentis homoousios nativitas*), closer to the older Athanasian/Apollinarian sense in which the Son's *origin* is central. He is thus a witness "on the way toward" the double homoousion, not a user of the established phrase.
- **Two natures → two hypostases.** The Antiochene logic Nestorius represents accepted (with [[theodoret-of-cyrus|Theodoret]]) that two natures imply two hypostases — a reading that the **Church of the East** later embraced explicitly, modifying the concept of hypostasis to account for the saviour's personal unity (the topic of a later scope of Zachhuber).

## Reception and Afterlife

Condemned at Ephesus (431), deposed, and exiled. The churches that maintained a dyophysite, two-hypostases Christology in his name — the **Church of the East** ("Nestorian") — flourished in the Sasanian Empire and along the Silk Road, becoming one of the three permanent post-Chalcedonian communions alongside the Chalcedonians and the [[oriental-orthodoxy|miaphysites]]. Modern scholarship (and the rediscovered *Bazaar of Heracleides*) has reassessed how far Nestorius's own views matched the "Nestorianism" condemned in his name.
