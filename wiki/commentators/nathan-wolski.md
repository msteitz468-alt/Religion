# Nathan Wolski

Contemporary scholar of Jewish mysticism and Kabbalah, best known in this context as the translator and annotator of **Volume 10** (*Midrash ha-Ne’lam*, Stanford University Press, 2016) and **Volume 12** (*Zoharic Compositions*, Stanford University Press, 2019) of the Pritzker Edition of the [[zohar|Zohar]]. Vol. X presents the earliest stratum of the Zoharic literary corpus — a distinct “concealed midrashic” layer focused on Genesis and the hidden dimensions of the soul, divine justice, and biblical narrative. Vol. XII completes the presentation of the independent “Zoharic Compositions,” the non-running-commentary esoteric treatises (Heikhalot, Raza de-Razin, Sitrei Otiyyot, Qav ha-Middah, Tosefta, and festival units) that form some of the most concentrated theosophic expressions in the corpus.

---

## Biographical and Scholarly Context

Nathan Wolski is a leading academic in the field of Jewish mystical literature. His work on the Pritzker Edition focuses on the *Midrash ha-Ne’lam*, widely recognized as the oldest component of the Zoharic corpus. Unlike the main running Torah commentary translated by Daniel C. Matt (Vols. 1–9), the Midrash ha-Ne’lam represents an earlier, more midrashically oriented voice within the emerging Zohar tradition — one that emphasizes concealed meanings, the inner life of the soul, the fate of the righteous and wicked, and allegorical reinterpretations of the patriarchal stories.

## The Midrash ha-Ne’lam in the Pritzker Edition (Vol. X)

Wolski’s translation and extensive annotation make this foundational but often under-emphasized layer accessible in English for the first time in a critical edition. **Standards reingest 2026-07-18** densified the body with coverage ledger and verbatim loci — source page [[wolski-zohar-pritzker-vol10|Wolski Pritzker Vol X]].

**Wolski’s own framing (preface, attributed):**
- **Earliest Zoharic stratum**: “recognized by all Zohar scholars as the earliest layer”; “first fruits of the Zoharic world”; “primal light” / “first foundation” reworked by the main Zohar.
- **Contrast with main body**: sefirotic symbolism “virtually absent… (except for parts of parashat Shemot)”; no Companions epic around Rashbi as default frame; diverse cast (Rabban Yoḥanan b. Zakkai, R. Eli’ezer, R. Akiva, Masters of Mishnah, etc.).
- **Dual mode + bilingualism**: philosophical allegory (Hebrew layer) — patriarchs as soul’s descent (Abraham=soul, Sarah=body, Lot=evil impulse, Melchizedek/Michael, King of Sodom/Hell, Isaac/Rebekah resurrection body, Laban refined impulse) — plus proto-kabbalistic midrash (Aramaic layer) on creation, angels, soul, Israel. Wolski calls MhN “one of the high points, if not the high point, of medieval Jewish philosophical allegory.”
- **Scope**: Be-reshit–Va-Yetse (first half of Genesis) + Shemot + brief Ki Tetse + three MS addenda; print split between *Zohar Ḥadash* (misnomer) and main Zohar Genesis/Shemot.
- **Authorship positions**: Scholem = younger Moses de León; some recent earlier-date arguments for parts; Maimonides + Naḥmanides influence → thirteenth-century provenance secure (Wolski).

**Body densification (reingest digests, not preface alone):** creation by letter *he* / 32 paths / 42; wisdom removed for the righteous; seven openings of ascent; Bookkeeper/Dumah–Ephron registers; Shemot proto-sefirot (Gevurah, crowns, Shekhinah garden); Ki Tetse reincarnation 2–3×; Shema cools Hell; dog attribute / path of life. See [[zohar|The Zohar]] Vol. X structure, [[zoharic-afterlife|Zoharic Afterlife]], [[allegorical-reading|Allegorical Reading]].

## The Zoharic Compositions in the Pritzker Edition (Vol. XII)

**Standards reingest 2026-07-18** densified Vol. XII body with coverage ledger and verbatim loci — source page [[wolski-hecker-zohar-pritzker-vol12|Wolski & Hecker Pritzker Vol XII]]. Wolski units: Heikhalot Be-Reshit / Pequdei (seven holy halls + impurity halls in Pequdei; “low comprised in high,” **2:244b**); Piqqudin (contiguous 47 commandments; general→particular knowledge of God).

Wolski’s second contribution to the Pritzker Edition (with Joel Hecker) presents the concluding block of the independent “Zoharic Compositions” (the non-running-commentary esoteric strata whose treatment began in Vol. XI with Hecker). The volume carries a joint Introduction by Wolski and Hecker. Wolski translated and annotated the Heikhalot Be-Reshit, Heikhalot Pequdei, and Piqqudin units; Hecker handled the remainder. These are self-contained theosophic treatises — often labeled Tosefta, Sitrei Otiyyot, etc., by later editors — focusing on emanation, theurgy, letter mysticism, and esoteric knowledge rather than parashah exegesis. The Introduction stresses that much of this material, though printed as *Zohar Ḥadash* (“New Zohar”), comprises some of the *earliest* Zoharic strata.

Key units translated/annotated by Wolski (as presented in the volume):

- **Heikhalot Be-Reshit and Heikhalot Pequdei (Halls)**: Two versions of the divine halls (the Pequdei version far grander and including halls of impurity). The halls are imagined as Malkhut/Shekhinah’s maidens or chambers, adorned through prayer and ritual for union with the upper sefirot and Binah (“seventh with seventh”). The worshiper “arranges and adorns them in preparation for their union with the sefirot, and ultimately for the union of the male and female grades of Divinity.” The seventh hall is hidden; a multi-colored pillar stands within it. Patriarchs and Israel in distress arouse Shekhinah for protection. Deepens the theurgy of adornment and the *zivvuga qaddisha*. (See also [[heikhalot|Heikhalot]].)

- **Piqqudin (Commandments)**: Theurgic and festival-oriented treatment of commandments (e.g., the Ten Commandments in Shavu’ot context; the supernal shofar symbolizing Binah sounded on Shabbat eve).

Additional units in the volume (Hecker): *Raza de-Razin* (physiognomy, metoposcopy, and chiromancy with emphasis on the mutability of features through sin or *teshuvah* — see [[raza-de-razin|Raza de-Razin]]); *Sitrei Otiyyot* (YHVH letters engraving and generating the sefirot as chariots); *Qav ha-Middah* (luminous Line of Measure from Ein Sof structuring the sefirot); Merkevet Yeḥezqel; Zohar al ha-Parashot; and passages (mis)labeled as Tosefta (apotropaic power of Torah, Moses’ staff, blending of Compassion and Judgment).

These compositions emphasize theurgy (prayer and ritual as preparation for divine union and protection), letter mysticism (YHVH generating sefirotic structure), Ein Sof as source of emanation and measure, and esoteric physiognomic knowledge as part of the sod tradition. Wolski’s (and Hecker’s) apparatus situates the material within the evolution of the Zohar as a multi-generic corpus and within 13th-century Castilian Kabbalah.

## Relation to the Broader Zohar and Wiki

Together, Wolski’s Vols. X and XII bracket the Matt volumes (I–IX) with the earliest concealed midrashic voice and the final independent theosophic compositions. This completes the Pritzker Edition’s coverage of the Zoharic “library” in the wiki: running Torah commentary (I–IX), concealed midrash (X–XI), and compositions (XI–XII). The Compositions (Vol. XII) enrich treatment of Shekhinah and the *zivvuga qaddisha* (halls and adornment), sefirot and Ein Sof (letters, measure, chariots), theurgy (prayer, protection, ritual), divine names/letters, and the soul (chariots, halls). They complement the dramatic assembly genre of the *Idra Zuta* (Vol. XI) and the main commentary’s systematic theosophy/theurgy.

Wolski’s work underscores that the Zohar is not a single uniform book but a corpus of distinct strata and voices, providing critical English access to some of its most recondite and influential units.

## Relation to the Broader Zohar and Wiki

The Midrash ha-Ne’lam offers a different entry point into Zoharic thought: more midrashic and soul-centered than the theosophic/theurgic running commentary on the parashot. It enriches the wiki’s treatment of Genesis (already enhanced with the main Zoharic reading from Vols. I–III) with an earlier, “concealed” layer. Themes of the soul, gilgul, divine justice, and hidden wisdom resonate with material from the Sava de-Mishpatim (Vol. V) and the visionary/eschatological sections in Vol. IX (Rav Metivta).

Together with Vol. XII, Wolski’s contributions complete the picture of the Zoharic corpus in the wiki: the running Torah commentary (I–IX), the concealed midrashic strata (X–XI), and the independent theosophic compositions (XI–XII). The Compositions deepen theurgy (prayer as adornment for union, apotropaic protection), letter mysticism (YHVH generating sefirot), Ein Sof emanation, Shekhinah (halls), and esoteric knowledge (physiognomy). They complement the *Idra Zuta* assembly genre (Vol. XI) and the systematic theosophy of the main commentary.

Wolski’s scholarly apparatus situates the text within the evolution of the Zohar, underscoring that “the Zohar” is not a single uniform work but a corpus with distinct strata and voices.
