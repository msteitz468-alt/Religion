# Larry W. Hurtado

## Biographical Context

Larry W. Hurtado (1943–2019) was Professor Emeritus of New Testament Language, Literature and Theology at the University of Edinburgh. An American scholar who spent the bulk of his career in Britain, he was among the most influential NT scholars of his generation, particularly in the study of early Christology — the origin and nature of devotion to Jesus in the earliest Christian decades.

His scholarly trajectory moved from text-critical work (his dissertation on Codex W in Mark) through the investigation of Jewish monotheism and early Christology (*One God, One Lord*, 1988) to his major technical work *Lord Jesus Christ: Devotion to Jesus in Earliest Christianity* (2003). *Destroyer of the Gods* (2016) represents an accessible, late-career synthesis aimed at a broad academic audience.

## Hermeneutical Method

Hurtado is a **historical-critical scholar** with strong commitment to the social and material dimensions of early Christianity:

- **Historical-critical method**: He uses the full range of critical tools — source criticism, form criticism, social-historical analysis — but within a broadly confessional Christian framework. He is not a polemical skeptic but neither a confessional apologist; he aims for descriptive historical analysis.
- **Social-historical approach**: Deeply interested in the early Christian movement as a sociological phenomenon — its growth, social composition, network dynamics, and relationship to the wider Roman world.
- **Material culture / papyrology**: Unusual for a NT scholar, Hurtado integrates papyrological and codicological evidence (manuscript bookforms, nomina sacra) as direct evidence for early Christian practice and self-understanding. This reflects his lifelong interest in *The Earliest Christian Artifacts* (2006).
- **Intra-Jewish mutation**: His key thesis in Christology is that the extraordinary devotion to Jesus evident in our earliest sources represents a *mutation* within Second Temple Judaism — not a pagan import (contra Bousset) but a genuine novelty arising within the Jewish matrix of the early Jesus-movement. This has major implications for the relationship between Jewish monotheism and early high Christology.
- **Critical engagement with the *religionsgeschichtliche Schule***: Hurtado systematically challenges the German history-of-religion school's claims of pagan parallel and influence on early Christianity (see [[kyrios-christology-origins|Kyrios-Christology Origins]]).

## Key Analytical Concepts

### Dyadic Devotion
Hurtado's most important contribution to scholarship: the observation that from the earliest recoverable evidence, Jesus was included alongside God as a *co-recipient* of the devotional practices of early Christian circles. This includes: baptism in Jesus' name, hymns to Jesus, invocation of Jesus (Maranatha), the Lord's Supper as a meal in Jesus' honor, and prayer to/through Jesus. No other Jewish "chief agent" figure (angel, patriarch, personified attribute) received this kind of corporate cultic veneration. Hurtado argues this dyadic pattern defines the Jesus-movement as a distinctive mutation within early Judaism. See [[dyadic-devotion|Dyadic Devotion]].

### "Bookishness"
His detailed codicological work demonstrating that early Christians were exceptionally text-oriented — not only in reading scriptures in worship, but in preferring the codex bookform for sacred texts (vs. the dominant bookroll), developing the nomina sacra scribal convention, and producing an extraordinary volume of literary output. See [[nomina-sacra|Nomina Sacra]].

## Major Works (referenced in *Destroyer of the Gods*)

- *One God, One Lord: Early Christian Devotion and Ancient Jewish Monotheism* (1988) — first statement of the "chief agent" and "mutation" thesis
- *Lord Jesus Christ: Devotion to Jesus in Earliest Christianity* (2003) — major technical monograph; comprehensive treatment of dyadic devotion in the first two centuries
- *The Earliest Christian Artifacts: Manuscripts and Christian Origins* (2006) — codicological analysis of early Christian manuscripts
- *Destroyer of the Gods: Early Christian Distinctiveness in the Roman World* (2016) — accessible synthesis; this source

## Characteristic Positions

1. **High Christology is early**: Devotion to Jesus as divine Lord is not a late development (pagan-influenced second century) but is present in our *very earliest* sources, including Paul's undisputed letters.
2. **Jewish matrix, not pagan**: The emergence of high Christology is explained by Second Temple Jewish conceptual resources (Messiah, exaltation, divine agency) transformed by the resurrection conviction — not by pagan hero cults or mystery religions.
3. **Mutation, not syncretism**: The Jesus-movement was a genuine novelty within Judaism, distinguishable even from the most exalted "chief agent" figures in Jewish apocalyptic (1 Enoch's "Chosen One," Wisdom, Michael).
4. **Early Christian distinctiveness**: Against a tendency in modern scholarship to flatten early Christianity into its cultural context, Hurtado insists on taking seriously the evidence that early Christianity was perceived as — and actually was — genuinely different.

## Controversies

The Bousset thesis: see [[kyrios-christology-origins|Kyrios-Christology Origins]]. Hurtado's position is the current scholarly mainstream response to Bousset: Jewish-Christian (not Gentile-pagan) circles as the locus of origin for high Christology.
