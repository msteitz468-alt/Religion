Timothy R. Ashley (b. 1947) is an evangelical Old Testament scholar whose principal contribution in this wiki is *The Book of Numbers* in the **New International Commentary on the Old Testament** (Eerdmans, 1993; MS submitted Aug 1990; preface Aug 1992). At the time of writing he taught at Acadia Divinity College / Acadia University, Wolfville, Nova Scotia. The commentary is dedicated to David H. Wallace and the memory of Robert B. Laurin (1927–77). Wife and colleague in ministry: Rev. Maxine F. Ashley.

## Hermeneutical Method

Ashley’s stated primary task is **explaining the final form of the text as a commentary for the Church**. He engages Hebrew philology (with George Buchanan Gray as the indispensable older interlocutor) and surveys redaction-critical proposals (especially via Philip Budd’s WBC) but deliberately de-emphasizes recoverable documentary histories: he is “quite sure that no one knows these things nor really can know them.” Literary difficulties are to be solved from within the text’s function, not by positing an editor indifferent to logic and style.

**Inspiration claim (attributed):** inspiration is not limited to the earliest compositional stage; Church and Synagogue confess the whole OT text as God’s Word.

**Text-critical stance:** MT generally preferable to Sam. Pent., LXX, Vulg., or Qumran; running translation based on MT as far as possible.

## Characteristic Positions (NICOT Numbers)

**Structure.** Prefers traditional **geographic** analysis (Sinai 1:1–10:10; Kadesh 13:1–19:22 with travel 10:11–12:16; Moab 22:2–36:13 with travel 20:1–22:1) over D. T. Olson’s two-census generation scheme as the commentary’s controlling outline, while registering Olson’s value. Working outline is five major parts. Thematic map: **Orientation → Disorientation → New Orientation** (travel transitional).

**Thesis.** *Exact obedience to God is crucial*—taught through disobedience (chs. 11–21) and through **new torah amid failure** (chs. 5–9, 15, 17–19, 27–30, 32–36).

**Authorship/composition.** Moses has a key role in origins of some material (only writing notice 33:2 for itinerary framework); long transmission undeniable; final form likely **preexilic** (possibly united monarchy), following Kaufmann/Milgrom/Weinfeld/Hurvitz against necessarily postexilic P; will not return to full traditional Mosaic authorship of the whole. See [[documentary-hypothesis|Documentary Hypothesis]].

**Census figures.** Surveys face-value, gematria, Barnouin, Petrie/Mendenhall/Gottwald/Wenham/Clark *ʾelep̄* solutions; finds subgroup theories weak because the final text totals *ʾelep̄* as thousands; prefers **R. K. Harrison**—numbers as symbols of relative power/triumph/importance.

**Meribah (Num 20).** Multi-factorial sin: inexact obedience, anger, self as miracle-worker, formally *lack of trust* to hold Yahweh holy publicly; parallel to people’s unbelief in ch. 14.

**Copper serpent (Num 21).** Obedience-looking, not autonomous magic; prefers *copper* (Timna); records John 3:14 as Christian reception.

**Korah (Num 16–19).** Two rebellions united by common cause (not artificial source-splicing that denies cogency); Aaronic *kipper* alone stops plague; ch. 18 redefines encroachment liability; ch. 19 red cow after mass death.

**Balaam (Num 22–24).** Relatively neutral portrait in the cycle; Deir ʿAllā engaged cautiously; star/scepter (24:17) has long messianic reception that Ashley records without equating it to the author’s full messianic intent; ch. 31 fills the narrative blank of 24:25.

**Phinehas (Num 25).** Zeal pattern (Samuel/Agag; Mattathias); *covenant of peace* = everlasting high-priestly line; amalgamation of two Peor stories without reconstructing independent forms.

**Midian war (Num 31).** Framed as unfinished business before Moses’ death; *ḥērem* content extracted without softening; booty purification and gold atonement; Balaam’s counsel and death.

**Cities of refuge (Num 35).** Bloodguilt, intentional vs unintentional, high-priest death as release horizon—see [[cities-of-refuge|Cities of Refuge]].

## Dialogue Partners

Gray (ICC philology), Budd (WBC redaction summaries), Milgrom (articles; JPST lightly at proof stage), Harrison (WEC; census solution), Allen (EBC), D. T. Olson (structure), Kaufmann/Weinfeld/Hurvitz/Milgrom stream on P dating, Kitchen (Egyptian parallels), Wenham (often interlocutor on ritual).
