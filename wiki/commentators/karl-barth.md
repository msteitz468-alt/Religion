# Karl Barth

## Biographical Context

Karl Barth (1886–1968) was a Swiss Reformed theologian widely regarded as the most important Protestant theologian of the twentieth century. Born in Basel, he studied under Wilhelm Herrmann and other Ritschlian liberals at Marburg and Berlin, but broke sharply with liberal theology in 1919 with his Romans commentary (*Der Römerbrief*), which applied a dialectical theological method and emphasized the radical otherness of God. He held chairs at Göttingen (1921–25), Münster (1925–30), and Bonn (1930–35), where he helped draft the Barmen Declaration (1934) against German Christian capitulation to Nazism. Expelled from Germany by the Nazi government, he returned to Basel in 1935, where he remained until his death.

## Hermeneutical Method

Barth operates within what he calls "scientific" or "church" dogmatics — a **Word of God theology** (also called dialectical theology or neo-orthodoxy). His governing hermeneutical commitments:

- **Christocentric focus**: All theological statements are to be normed by the revelation of God in Jesus Christ. Scripture is authoritative not as an inerrant text in itself but as a witness to the Word of God that *became* flesh in Christ.
- **Threefold form of the Word**: Barth distinguishes the revealed Word (Jesus Christ), the written Word (Scripture as witness), and the proclaimed Word (preaching). Scripture is not itself the primary form of the Word but points to it; inspiration is a dynamic event, not a static property.
- **Dialectical method**: Theology must hold together affirmation and negation — God is wholly other (*totaliter aliter*) and yet graciously self-disclosed. Human speech about God is always inadequate yet is commanded by grace.
- **Rejection of natural theology**: Barth famously rejected every attempt (including Brunner's) to ground theology in a natural knowledge of God apart from revelation. The *analogia entis* (analogy of being) that underlies natural theology he called "the invention of Antichrist."
- **Church as locus**: Dogmatics is not free speculation but the Church's self-critical examination of its proclamation against the norm of the Word of God. Theology is ecclesial, not merely academic.
- **Hermeneutical framework**: Broadly within the literal/historical tradition inherited from the Reformation, but radicalized: the grammatical-historical sense is not the final goal; the text points *through* its human authorship to a divine address that confronts the reader anew.

## Major Works

- *Der Römerbrief* (The Epistle to the Romans), 1st ed. 1919, 2nd ed. 1922 — the breakthrough work introducing dialectical theology
- *Church Dogmatics* (*Kirchliche Dogmatik*), 1932–1967 — 13 part-volumes; the magnum opus, covering the Word of God (I), the Doctrine of God (II), Creation (III), Reconciliation (IV); a projected vol. V on Redemption was never completed
- *Anselm: Fides Quaerens Intellectum* (1931) — his methodological turn toward analogy and the ontological argument
- *The Barmen Declaration* (1934, co-authored) — confessional rejection of the German Christian movement

## Characteristic Positions

- **Sola scriptura** radicalized: Scripture is norm (*norma normans*) but only as it points to Christ; the Church has no independent authority parallel to Scripture.
- **Universal election in Christ**: In *Church Dogmatics* II.2, Barth reconceives election not as God's predetermination of individual destinies but as God's self-election in Christ — a major revision of Calvinist double predestination.
- **Rejection of apologetics**: Theology begins from revelation and does not justify itself before philosophical reason.
- **Actualism**: God's being is in his acts (*Sein ist im Akt*); both God and humanity are understood dynamically rather than through static substance categories.
- **Speech-primacy**: The Word of God means, first and literally, that God *speaks*. Speech is not a symbol for something else (against Tillich) but the actual form in which God has communicated with his Church.
- **Rejection of anthropological starting points**: There is a way from Christology to anthropology but no way from anthropology to Christology — directed against Gogarten's "Church anthropology" as the starting point of theology (*CD* I.1 §5, lines 6037–6038).

## Known Controversies

- **The Brunner debate** (1934): Barth's *Nein!* response to Emil Brunner's argument for a natural "point of contact" (*Anknüpfungspunkt*) for revelation in fallen humanity. See [[natural-theology]].
- **Relationship to liberal theology**: Debate over whether Barth's break with his Ritschlian teachers was as total as he claimed.
- **Universalism charge**: Barth's doctrine of universal election in Christ raises the question of whether he implies universal salvation (*apokatastasis*) — he consistently denied it while refusing to deny God's freedom to save all.
- **Philosophical theology**: Critics (especially Eberhard Jüngel and later Bruce McCormack) debate the extent to which Barth employs or rejects philosophical categories.

## Influence

Barth's influence on twentieth-century Protestant theology is pervasive: the neo-orthodox movement, post-liberal theology (Lindbeck, Hauerwas), post-Barthian Reformed thought, and ecumenical theology all draw heavily on him. His break with liberal theology and his political resistance to Nazism gave neo-orthodoxy both a theological and an ethical shape.

## Positions Grounded in *Church Dogmatics* I.1 (§§1–12)

The following are directly attested positions from the ingested text, with line numbers.

### §§1–2: Task of Dogmatics; Task of Prolegomena

- **Theology's self-justification refused**: Barth rejects all of H. Scholz's six demands on science (consistency, coherence, verifiability, physical congruity, independence, axiomatisability). Theology "must absolutely subordinate and if necessary sacrifice all concern for what is called science elsewhere" (lines 370–371).
- **Dogmatics as *theologia crucis***: "our Theology is certain: It snatches us away from ourselves and places us outside ourselves" (Luther, lines 602–604). The certainty is outside the theologian, in the Word.
- **Schleiermacher as "great and mature classical exponent of Modernism"** (lines 1719–1720): his prolegomena reduce the Church to a fellowship defined by piety (*Christian Faith* §§133–135) and thus anthropologise theology's starting point.
- **The Brunner debate engaged in §2**: "revelation itself creates of itself the necessary point of contact in man" (lines 1409–1411) — the *Nein!* argument is already present here, before the 1934 exchange.
- **Prolegomena as part of dogmatics**: The *pro* means "first part," not "prior to." Prolegomena are "possible only as part of dogmatics itself" (lines 1986–1989).

### §§3–4: Church Proclamation; The Word of God in Threefold Form

- **Against Modernist proclamation**: Schleiermacher and Tillich reduce proclamation to man's self-dialogue — "man is finally conceived of as conversing only with himself" (line 2747).
- **Against Roman Catholic sacramentalism**: Grace operates *ex opere operato* rather than through Word-and-faith; preaching is rendered "Silentium altissimum" (line 2898).
- **Scripture as self-imposing Canon**: "The Bible constitutes itself the Canon. It is the Canon because it imposed itself upon the Church as such, and continually does so" in virtue of its content (lines 4884–4921).
- **Bible as witness, not revelation itself**: *Deus dixit* must be distinguished from *Paulus dixit* (line 5175). The Bible is the Word of God only dynamically — "to the extent that God causes it to be His Word" (lines 4988–4989).
- **Trinitarian analogy of the threefold Word**: "we can substitute for revelation, Scripture and proclamation the names of the divine persons Father, Son and Holy Spirit and vice versa" (lines 5537–5544).

### §5: Nature of the Word of God

- **Word as speech, act, mystery** — not three separate attributes but three exegetical levels; mystery qualifies act, which qualifies speech. The Word is the *Dei loquentis persona* (§8.2, line 494); act = contingent contemporaneity, power to rule, decision; mystery = secularity, one-sidedness, spirituality.
- **Actualism of the Word**: the Word is always real "*suo modo, sua libertate, sua misericordia*" (in its own way, freedom, mercy — line 7245), never a universally ascertainable fact.
- **Rejection of Gogarten**: There is no "Church anthropology" preceding the doctrine of the Word — the only direction runs from Christology to anthropology.

### §6: Knowability of the Word of God

- **Rejection of Cartesianism**: "We do not find the Word of God in the reality present to us. Rather — the Word of God finds us" (lines 8955–8959).
- ***Imago Dei* totally annihilated by sin**: "not real outside faith; it is real only in faith" (line 10968) — against Brunner's *Anknüpfungspunkt*.
- ***Analogia fidei* vs. *analogia entis***: The correspondence between human knowing and the Word is constituted by faith, not a permanent metaphysical likeness. See [[analogia-fidei]].
- **Faith as God's miracle**: "The Word of God becomes knowable by making itself known ... just as much as is the Word itself" (lines 11389, 11396).

### §7: Word of God, Dogma and Dogmatics

- **Dogma as eschatological**: "The Word of God is above dogma as the heavens are above the earth" (lines 12229–30); dogma is achieved only at the end of the *ecclesia militans*.
- **Three-level distinction**: dogma (the agreement itself) / dogmas (confessional propositions) / dogmatic propositions (science output) — none is to be identified with revealed truth.
- **Three marks of scientific dogmatics**: orientation to proclamation; critical correction; Scripture as decisive criterion — "dogmatic work stands or falls by whether the standard is the revelation attested in Holy Scripture" (lines 12954–55).

### §8: God in His Revelation

- **Trinity first**: Placed at the head of all dogmatics — one cannot say what makes Scripture holy without first knowing *who* the God is whose revelation makes it so (lines 307–317).
- **Threefold structure of revelation forces the Trinity**: Who is the revealer? What does He do? What does He effect? — three questions, one answer (lines 100–102). Formal expression: "God reveals Himself. He reveals Himself through Himself. He reveals Himself" (line 103).
- **Revelation is self-grounded**: "God's revelation has its reality and truth wholly and in every respect — both ontically and noetically — within itself" (lines 528–530).
- **Dogma is Church exegesis**: "Trinity," "essence," and "person" are not biblical terms; the doctrine is "indirectly, though not directly, identical" with the biblical witness (lines 677–680, 728–803).
- **Vestigia Trinitatis rejected**: Traces of the Trinity in creaturely structure (Augustine's soul-triad, etc.) risk making the world's capacity for revelation the premise of theology; the Augustinian argument leads logically to Feuerbach (line 2289). See [[vestigium-trinitatis]].

### §§9–10: Triunity of God; God the Father

- **"Modes of being" (*Seinsweisen*) over "persons"**: Three *Seinsweisen* — the literal translation of τρόπος ὑπάρξεως — expresses "relatively better and more simply" what the tradition meant by *persona* (lines 3113–16), without the modern connotations of individual self-consciousness. "We are not speaking of three divine I's, but thrice of the one divine I" (lines 2658–59). See [[modes-of-being]].
- **Antitrinitarianism fails as monotheism**: Arianism pays adoration to a creature; Modalism makes God's revelation a form that differs from His existence (lines 2731, 2754–56).
- **Relations of origin**: God is "pure Giver" (Father), "Receiver-and-Giver" (Son), "pure Receiver" (Spirit) (lines 3362–63). Relations of origin = modes of being themselves.
- ***Opera trinitatis ad extra indivisa sunt***: All external works are one act of the whole Trinity; distinctions are by appropriation only (lines 3909–24). See [[opera-trinitatis]].
- **Perichoresis as "final sum"**: mutual indwelling (John of Damascus) both confirms and relativises modal distinction (lines 3697–99). See [[perichoresis]].
- **God "antecedently Father in Himself"**: Eternal intradivine fatherhood presupposed in revealed fatherhood; the creaturely application is derivative (lines 4659–60, 4695–4713).
- **Against "theological favouritisms"**: "All theological favouritisms are thus forbidden: the one-sided belief in God the Father ... so-called Christocentrism ... and all the nonsense perpetrated with isolated veneration of the Spirit" (lines 4805–08).

### §§11–12: God the Son; God the Holy Spirit

- **Jesus Christ "antecedently in Himself"**: Christ does not *become* God's Son in the event of revelation — "the event of revelation has divine truth and reality because Jesus Christ reveals Himself as the One He already was before, apart from this event, in Himself too" (lines 5709–5716). Eternal Sonship is the condition of gracious revelation.
- **Reconciliation as Son's appropriated work**: "to the extent that God in the fact of His revelation treats His enemies as His friends ... revelation is itself reconciliation" (lines 5433–5436). See [[reconciliation]].
- **Against Ebionitism and Docetism**: Both flatten the "perpendicular" divine dimension. The NT witnesses did not move from historical figure to heavenly Son — deity was their beginning, not conclusion (lines 5102–5108).
- **The Spirit as subjective side of revelation**: The Son is the objective element; the Spirit is the act by which that disclosure reaches its hearers — "no less than the Father, and no less than Jesus, God Himself, altogether God" (lines 7979–7982). See [[holy-spirit]].
- **Eschatological reserve on the Spirit**: "The Spirit remains the Holy Spirit, wholly and utterly the Spirit of promise" (lines 8251–8252). "To have the Holy Spirit is to let God rather than our having God be our confidence" (lines 8132–8134).
- **The Eternal Spirit "antecedently in Himself"**: "What He is in revelation He is antecedently in Himself" (lines 8314–8318). Spirit = eternal act of communion of Father and Son (Augustine, *De Trinitate* VI.5.7).
- ***Filioque* affirmed**: Statements about the immanent Trinity cannot differ from revelation. In revelation the Spirit is the Spirit of Father and Son; therefore He proceeds from both antecedently. Denying *Filioque* severs the God-human relation in the Spirit from the Mediator (lines 9011–9013, 9077–9078). See [[filioque]].
