Joseph Caro (1488–1575 CE) is the author of the *Shulchan Aruch* ("Prepared Table"), the most authoritative practical code of Jewish law, which remains the standard halakhic reference for traditional Jews worldwide. His work represents the final major stage in the [[codification-debates|long history of Talmudic codification]].

## Biographical Context

Born in Spain, Caro fled with his family following the Expulsion of 1492 and eventually settled in Safed, Palestine, which under Ottoman rule became a major center of Jewish mysticism and legal scholarship in the 16th century. He was a contemporary of Moses Cordovero and Isaac Luria (*ha-Ari*), the kabbalistic masters of Safed, though his own contribution was primarily legal rather than mystical.

## Major Works

**Beth Joseph** ("House of Joseph," 1542–1559): A massive commentary on the *Arba'ah Turim* (Four Rows) of Jacob ben Asher (1280–1340). Caro spent twenty years on this project. He cited all relevant sources from the Talmud through the Geonim and medieval authorities for every legal question. By the end he remained dissatisfied — existing codifications could not function as handbooks for the ordinary Jew because they required the reader to navigate extensive source material.

**Shulchan Aruch** (1565): A condensation of *Beth Joseph* presenting halakhic conclusions without sources or citations. The *Shulchan Aruch* follows the four-part structure of Jacob ben Asher's *Turim*:

| Section | Hebrew | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| Way of Life | *Orach Chaim* | Prayer, Sabbath, holidays, daily practice |
| Guide of Knowledge | *Yoreh De'ah* | Kashrut, purity, mourning, interest |
| Stone of Help | *Even ha-Ezer* | Marriage and divorce |
| Breastplate of Judgment | *Choshen Mishpat* | Civil law |

The *Shulchan Aruch* was controversial on publication for the same reasons [[maimonides|Maimonides']] *Mishna Torah* had been: its presentation of conclusions without sources made it authoritative but potentially supplanting of Talmud study. Historians compare Caro's achievement to Maimonides' *Mishna Torah* in scope and influence.

## Hermeneutical Method

Caro follows the **practical school** of Talmudic interpretation — oriented toward deriving binding halakhic decisions for contemporary Jewish practice, not toward theoretical or philosophical exposition. In the *Beth Joseph*, his method for determining the ruling when authorities disagree is to follow a "majority of three": Alfasi, Maimonides, and Asher ben Jehiel (*Rosh*). The *Shulchan Aruch* presents the resulting conclusion without showing the work.

The *Shulchan Aruch* reflects Sephardic practice. Ashkenazic communities initially resisted its authority; the Ashkenazic gloss (*Mappah*) by Moses Isserles (Rema, 1530–1572) was added within decades and became inseparable from the standard text, giving the combined work authority across both communities.

## Place in the Codification Chain

Caro is the culmination of a chain: [[rabbi-judah-ha-nasi|Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi]] (Mishna) → Saboraim (sealed Talmud) → [[hai-gaon|Hai Gaon]] (Geonic responsa) → Isaac Alfasi (*Halakhot*, 1013–1103) → [[maimonides|Maimonides]] (*Mishna Torah*, c. 1180) → Jacob ben Asher (*Arba'ah Turim*, c. 1340) → Joseph Caro (*Shulchan Aruch*, 1565). After Caro, the center of halakhic creativity moved to Eastern Europe; the *Shulchan Aruch* itself, however, remained the accepted reference.
