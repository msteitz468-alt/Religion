# Theophilus of Antioch

Bishop of Antioch and Christian apologist, author of the three books *Ad Autolycum*. He is notable for two firsts in the vocabulary of doctrine and for a sharp formulation of [[creation-ex-nihilo|creation from nothing]]. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|Kelly]] and [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Pelikan]].

## Contributions

- **First to apply "Triad" (*trias*) to the Godhead.** Theophilus called the first three days of creation "types of the Triad, that is, of God and of His Word and of His Wisdom" — the earliest use of the term that became [[trinity|Trinity]]. He identified the Spirit with divine Wisdom and used the Stoic distinction of the [[logos-christology|immanent and expressed Word]] (*logos endiathetos / prophorikos*).
- **Creation *ex nihilo*.** "The power of God is manifested in this, that out of things that are not he makes whatever he pleases," and "nothing is coeternal with God" — a decisive step toward [[creation-ex-nihilo|creation from nothing]] against the eternity of matter.
- **The antiquity argument.** He extended the [[biblical-criticism-antiquity|claim of biblical priority]] to the Greek poets, who (he charged) "plagiarized from the Scriptures."
