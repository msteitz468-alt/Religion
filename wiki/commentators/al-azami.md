# M. M. Al-Azami

**Muhammad Mustafa Al-Azami** (c. 1932–2017) was one of the modern era's foremost scholars of
**hadith** and a leading traditionalist defender of the integrity of Islamic scripture. Indian-born
(Mau, Uttar Pradesh), he trained at Dar al-Ulum Deoband, took an M.A. at **al-Azhar** (Cairo, 1955)
and a Ph.D. at **Cambridge** (1966), and became Professor and chair of Islamic Studies at **King Saud
University** (Riyadh). He held visiting posts at Michigan, Princeton (King Faisal Visiting Professor),
Oxford (St Cross), and Colorado, and received the **King Faisal International Prize** for Islamic
Studies (1980).

## Major Works

- ***Studies in Early Hadith Literature*** and ***On Schacht's Origins of Muhammadan
  Jurisprudence*** — his influential rebuttals of the Western (Goldziher–Schacht) skepticism about
  the authenticity of hadith, arguing for the early and reliable written transmission of prophetic
  reports.
- ***The History of the Qur'anic Text from Revelation to Compilation*** (2003) — the work ingested
  here; see [[al-azami-history-of-the-quranic-text|the scholarship page]].
- Critical editions of major hadith and *sira* works (the *Muwatta'* of Malik, *Sahih Ibn
  Khuzayma*, *Sunan Ibn Maja*, the *Maghazi* of Urwa ibn al-Zubayr).

## Method and Characteristic Positions

Al-Azami works from the **isnad-critical methodology of the *muhaddithun*** — the classical science of
chains of transmission — and turns it against modern Western scholarship:

- The **"law of witness"**: a text has authority only when received in person, with eyewitness
  attestation, from teacher to pupil. He uses this to argue that the [[quran|Qur'an]] was fully
  recorded and memorized in [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s lifetime, and to reinterpret the collection-
  traditions (the Yamama deaths, Zayd ibn Thabit's procedure) that [[bell-watt-introduction-to-the-quran|Bell
  & Watt]] read as signs of a late, uncertain text.
- **Anti-orientalism**: a sustained charge that the field (Nöldeke, Goldziher, Schacht, Jeffery,
  and—most radically—Wansbrough) projected the Bible's textual problems onto the Qur'an out of bias
  and "transmuted Islam into foreign idioms."
- The **comparative argument**: the textual history of the Bible (the [[documentary-hypothesis|OT
  source theory]], the [[mark-ending|variant endings]] of the NT) is the real locus of "corruption,"
  not the Qur'an.

## Reception and Controversy

Celebrated in traditionalist and much mainstream Muslim scholarship as a rigorous defender of
scriptural integrity; criticized by historical-critical scholars as **apologetic** — assuming the
reliability he sets out to prove and reading dissent as malice. The wiki records the dispute with
**[[william-montgomery-watt|Watt]]** and the critical lineage without adjudication (Contradiction
Protocol); the two positions meet on the [[qiraat-variant-readings|qira'at controversy page]].
