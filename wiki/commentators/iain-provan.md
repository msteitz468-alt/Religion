Iain W. Provan is a Scottish OT scholar who taught at New College (University of Edinburgh) and later at Regent College, Vancouver. His NIBC commentary on 1 and 2 Kings (1995) is the wiki's first **New International Biblical Commentary** volume — distinct from NICOT. The NIBC series describes itself as practicing "believing criticism."

Provan is perhaps best known for his collaborative biblical theology (*A Biblical Theology of the Old and New Testaments*, 2003, with V. Philips Long and Tremper Longman III) and his work on OT historiography (*Seriously Dangerous Religion*, 2014).

## Key Contributions

**Reading Kings as a unified story**: Provan's most consistent methodological commitment — insisting that Kings rewards reading from beginning to end as a coherent narrative. He argues that the perceived "incoherence" scholars have found reflects problems with their reading methods (atomizing into sources and redactions) rather than problems with the text.

**Three-dimensional approach**: Provan frames Kings as simultaneously narrative literature (a story), historiographical literature (about the past), and didactic literature (designed to teach readers about God). All three dimensions require engagement; none can be reduced to the others.

**Typological canonical reading**: Explicit commitment to reading Kings typologically in the context of the full Christian canon, particularly the NT. This is not a evasion of OT meaning but an extension of it — the OT characters and events prepare for and anticipate their NT fulfillments.

**Defense of OT historicity**: Against the trend toward sharp separation of "biblical Israel" and "historical Israel," Provan argues for the reliability of Kings as historical witness while acknowledging its selective and theologically shaped character. His selective skepticism critique: scholars distrust biblical claims but often accept modern reconstructions uncritically.
