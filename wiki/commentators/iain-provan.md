Iain W. Provan is a Scottish OT scholar who taught at New College (University of Edinburgh) and later at Regent College, Vancouver. His NIBC commentary on 1 and 2 Kings (1995) is the wiki’s first **New International Biblical Commentary** volume—distinct from NICOT. The NIBC series (editors W. Ward Gasque, Robert L. Hubbard Jr., Robert K. Johnston) describes itself as practicing **“believing criticism.”**

Provan is also known for collaborative biblical theology (*A Biblical Theology of the Old and New Testaments*, 2003, with V. Philips Long and Tremper Longman III) and for work on OT historiography (*Seriously Dangerous Religion*, 2014; earlier *Hezekiah and the Books of Kings*, BZAW 1988).

## Hermeneutical method (from the Kings Introduction)

Attributed to Provan, NIBC *1 and 2 Kings* (1995), Introduction:

1. **One book as narrative literature** — Regnal formulae, plot, characters; final-form coherent story from David to Jehoiachin. Prefers plural “authors” who were not victims of sources or incompetence; cumulative reading beginning to end. “The story ‘works.’”
2. **Historiographical literature** — About Israel’s past; selective and theologically shaped; authoritative **portrait** (text) vs subject (history behind); referentiality cannot be dodged; resists pure “fictive world” insulation and biblical-vs-historical-Israel skepticism that discards the narrative as starting point.
3. **Didactic literature** — Teaches about God and God’s ways (Rom 15:4; 1 Cor 10:11). Themes: exclusive LORD; worship and ethics; sin, judgment, delayed mercy; **promise** (Davidic conditional/unconditional paradox; Abrahamic land/seed).
4. **Canonical / typological Christian reading** — OT speaks on its own terms *and* in the total Christian canon; patterning of characters/events; four dedicated excursuses; commentary designed to be read through, not raided.
5. **Believing criticism** (series frame) — Probing scholarship with full biblical authority; neither precritical paraphrase nor a “desert of criticism.”

He warns that “Deuteronomistic/Deuteronomic theology” as a scholarly construct can flatten final-form subtlety even among final-form readers.

## Characteristic positions on Kings (attributed)

- **Wisdom and law** structure Solomon’s reign; true wisdom is covenantal; 1 Kgs 8 prayer is the theological heart (Name theology; grace underpins law; exile/restoration).
- **Jeroboam’s sin** is political religion (inability to trust), not sincere alternative Yahwism; Exodus 32 echo.
- **Elijah/Elisha** as salvation within judgment; Carmel public test; Horeb whisper + long political war; Naaman and humble channels; Jehu incomplete (calves remain); Joash preserves David’s lamp.
- **2 Kgs 17** Decalogue-rooted indictment implicating Judah; resettlement cult as ironic non-worship.
- **Hezekiah / Manasseh / Josiah**: second David; worst king / irrevocable judgment; second Moses who comes too late.
- **Ending**: Jehoiachin table as Joash-like hope that **grace may triumph over law** (2 Sam 7:15–16).

## Influence and controversies

Provan’s NIBC Kings is a standard evangelical literary-canonical entry point to the book. It stands in constructive tension with classic DtrH reconstructions (Noth; Cross/Smend schools) and with historicist commentaries that, in his view, privilege “behind the text” over narrative coherence (he criticizes e.g. Wiseman-type approaches). His historiography stance engages minimalist “biblical Israel” constructions without reducing the text to pure fiction.
