# Tsongkhapa

**Tsong-kha-pa Blo-bzang-grags-pa** (1357–1419), founder of the **[[gelug|Gelug]]** ("virtuous")
school of [[tibetan-buddhism|Tibetan Buddhism]] and one of the most influential systematizers in the
history of Buddhist scholasticism. His **[[lamrim-chenmo|Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to
Enlightenment]]** (1402) organizes the entire Mahayana path into a graded curriculum inherited from
[[atisha|Atiśa]]'s *Lamp for the Path* and the **[[kadam|Kadam]]** tradition.

## Hermeneutical Method

As seen in the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]**, Tsong-kha-pa works as a **synthesizing
scholastic**. His characteristic move: state a thesis, corroborate it with a chain of citations from
the Indian authorities ("the two great trailblazers, Nāgārjuna and Asaṅga"), reinforce with reasoned
analysis, and illustrate with Kadam oral teaching. He insists vehicles are distinguished "not by
philosophical view but by deeds," and that the path requires **analytical** meditation (*vipaśyanā*
discernment), not stabilizing absorption alone — sharply against the "a time for study and a time for
practice" attitude and against the **Hva-shang** position that thoughtless absorption is the whole
path. He reads disputed sūtra passages contextually (an implicit *neyārtha*/*nitārtha* sorting),
holding that the valid cognition of the ultimate (emptiness) and conventional certainty about
dependent-arising "aid each other," and treats the **six perfections** as common to both the
perfection (sūtra) and mantra (tantra) vehicles.

## Major Works

- The **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]** (Stages of the Path) — the sūtra-path synthesis (the
  ingested source).
- His own *Basic Path to Awakening* (a commentary on the *Bodhisattvabhūmi* ethics chapter) is
  cross-referenced within the Lamrim for the bodhisattva-vow procedures.
- (His Madhyamaka and tantra works are not covered by the ingested source.)

## Characteristic Positions

- **Bodhicitta is the dividing line of the Mahāyāna** — not the knowledge of emptiness, which is
  shared with the lower vehicles.
- **Union of method and wisdom is indispensable**; he refutes the reduction of the path to
  emptiness-meditation alone (the Hva-shang polemic), siding with Kamalaśīla.
- **Both bodhicitta methods are valid and sequential** — the [[atisha|Atiśa]] seven-cause method and
  Śāntideva's exchange of self and other.
- Willingness to **adjudicate disputed scholastic questions himself** (e.g. the anger/virtue-
  destruction debate, where he refutes the "mere delay of fruition" position).
