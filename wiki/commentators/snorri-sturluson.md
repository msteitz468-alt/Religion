# Snorri Sturluson

**Snorri Sturluson** was the great **systematizer and recorder of [[norse-religion|Norse]] mythology** — an
Icelandic chieftain, twice lawspeaker of the Althing, poet, and historian, and the most important single
author behind what we know of Norse myth. He wrote in the **13th century, two centuries after Iceland's
conversion to Christianity**, which makes him both indispensable and a source to be read critically.

## Major Works

- The **[[prose-edda|Prose Edda]]** (c. 1220) — a handbook of poetics and mythology that **arranges the
  scattered myths into a connected narrative** (the *Gylfaginning*), the single clearest account of the Norse
  cosmos from creation to [[ragnarok|Ragnarök]].
- **Heimskringla** — a history of the kings of Norway, opening with the legendary, euhemerized origins of the
  gods.

## Method and the Interpretive Problem

Snorri is the archetypal case of the wiki's standing caution about Norse sources: a **Christian recording a
pagan religion he did not practice**. He treats the gods **euhemeristically** (in his Prologue, as deified
human migrants from Troy), frames the material as poetic learning rather than living faith, and may have
**ordered, harmonized, or colored** myths that were originally looser and oral. Yet his evident respect for
the tradition and his use of older skaldic verse make the Prose Edda an extraordinary preservation. Reading
Snorri **alongside** the older [[poetic-edda|Poetic Edda]] — not simply through him — is the basic
methodological rule of Norse mythology.
