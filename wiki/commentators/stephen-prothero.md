# Stephen Prothero

**Stephen Prothero** (b. 1960) is an American scholar of religion and professor of
religious studies at **Boston University**, best known as a public intellectual arguing
for **religious literacy** and against the popular notion that "all religions are one." In
the wiki's running debate over how the traditions relate, he is the chief modern voice of
**[[religious-pluralism-debate|particularism]]** — the direct counter-case to both
[[john-hick|John Hick]]'s pluralism and [[karen-armstrong|Karen Armstrong]]'s
mystical-experiential reading. His *God Is Not One* (2010) is the source ingested here.

## Biographical Context

Prothero trained in American religious history (Harvard PhD) and has taught at Boston
University since the late 1990s. He writes deliberately for a general audience as well as
the academy: his *Religious Literacy: What Every American Needs to Know — and Doesn't*
(2007) argued that Americans are intensely religious yet strikingly ignorant of the
content of religions — including their own — and that this ignorance is a civic liability.
*God Is Not One* (2010) is the constructive sequel: if we are going to be literate about
religion, we must start by seeing the religions as they actually are, in their
differences. Other works include *American Jesus* (2003), *The American Bible* (2012),
*Religion Matters* (2020), and *Why Liberals Win the Culture Wars* (2016).

His stance is that of the **secular religious-studies scholar**: he insists there are *two*
ways to talk about religion — the *religious* way (preaching one's creed) and the *secular*
way (observing and reporting "as objectively as possible") — and that his job is the second.
He charges that both the [[perennialism|perennial philosophers]] (Huston Smith, Aldous
Huxley, Joseph Campbell, and, he adds, Armstrong) *and* the New Atheists (Dawkins, Harris,
Hitchens, Dennett) fail precisely by smuggling **theology** into what they present as
neutral description.

## Hermeneutical Method

Prothero is not a textual commentator in the classical sense; his "hermeneutic" is a
**comparative-analytic framework** for reading whole traditions. Two devices define it.

### 1. The problem–solution–technique–exemplar model

Each great religion, on Prothero's scheme, articulates four things, and they differ
tradition to tradition:

| | **Problem** | **Solution / Goal** | **Technique** | **Exemplars** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Islam** | self-sufficiency / pride | submission, Paradise | the Five Pillars | Muhammad; the *ummah* |
| **Christianity** | sin | salvation | faith and/or good works | saints; people of faith |
| **Confucianism** | social chaos / disorder | social harmony | ritual propriety (*li*), education | the *junzi* / sage |
| **Hinduism** | *samsara* (bondage to rebirth) | *moksha* (release) | the yogas (devotion, knowledge, works) | gurus; the liberated |
| **Buddhism** | suffering (*dukkha*) | *nirvana* | the Noble Eightfold Path | arhat / bodhisattva / lama |
| **Yoruba religion** | disconnection | connection / flourishing | divination, sacrifice, possession | *babalawos*; the orishas |
| **Judaism** | exile / estrangement | return (*teshuvah*) | story and law (*halakha*) | the patriarchs; the people |
| **Daoism** | lifelessness / unnaturalness | flourishing, naturalness | *wu wei*, longevity arts | the sage; the immortal |

The point of the grid is **anti-reductive**: "Are Buddhists trying to achieve salvation? Of
course not — they don't even believe in sin." The traditions are climbing *different
mountains*, not different sides of one. The model is admittedly "simplistic — a starting
point," to be nuanced (Catholics and Protestants disagree on *how* salvation comes;
Theravada and Mahayana on what *nirvana* is). See [[four-part-model-of-religion]].

### 2. Ranking by contemporary impact

*God Is Not One* presents the eight in order of present-day "greatness" — by which Prothero
means **influence, not goodness** (the *Time* "Person of the Year" sense; Hitler and Stalin
once made that list). His ranking: **Islam, Christianity, Confucianism, Hinduism, Buddhism,
Yoruba religion, Judaism, Daoism**, with a coda on atheism. He is explicit that the ranking
reflects his own judgments and biases, and that *great is not good* — religion is both
"toxic and tonic," a force for the Inquisition *and* abolition.

He follows **Ninian Smart**'s "seven dimensions" of religion (ritual, narrative,
experiential, institutional, ethical, doctrinal, material) and **family-resemblance** logic
(Wittgenstein via the "no single essence" consensus): religions share overlapping
*tendencies*, not a common core. He pointedly refuses to export one tradition's vocabulary
onto another — no "sin" or "salvation" for Hindus and Buddhists, no "heaven" for *nirvana*.

## Characteristic Positions

1. **"God is not one."** The thesis title. The traditions converge somewhat on **ethics**
   (a Golden Rule appears widely) but **diverge sharply on doctrine, ritual, mythology,
   experience, and law** — and those divergences "matter," both to practitioners and in
   their real-world effects (marriage, politics, war).

2. **"Godthink" is the enemy.** His coinage for the ideological reflex that lumps all
   religions together — whether into one *treasure chest* (perennialism: Huston Smith's
   "same mountain," Gandhi, the Dalai Lama, Ramakrishna, and, he names her,
   [[karen-armstrong|Armstrong]]) or one *trash can* (the New Atheists). Both, he says, are
   doing **theology disguised as description**, and both start from desire (to praise, or to
   denounce) rather than from "how things are on the ground."

3. **Difference is not the enemy of tolerance — it is its precondition.** Following his BU
   colleague Adam Seligman: "there is no need to tolerate a religion that is essentially the
   same as your own." Real coexistence (his model: "Interfaith Dialogue 2.0," Eboo Patel's
   Interfaith Youth Core) starts by *naming* differences, not denying them.

4. **Yoruba religion is a great religion.** Polemically he elevates the **way of the
   orishas** — usually filed under "primal"/"primitive" religion — to the rank of a world
   religion (~100 million adherents across West Africa and the Atlantic diaspora). See
   [[yoruba-religion|Yoruba religion]]. He likewise *excludes* Sikhism, Jainism, Shinto,
   Zoroastrianism, Baha'i, Wicca, Scientology, and Rastafarianism — choices he flags as his
   own.

5. **Salvation is not a universal religious goal.** His sharpest jab at perennialism (and at
   inclusivists like Rahner and even Huston Smith): *salvation* is a *Christian* category —
   "Only Christians seek salvation." Asking which religion best delivers salvation is like
   asking which sport is best at scoring runs: it privileges baseball (Christianity) from
   the start. The real question is not which path reaches a shared summit but **which goal we
   should be seeking at all.**

## Relationship to Other Positions

Prothero is positioned in the wiki as the third leg of the modern pluralism trilogy, and
the **dissenting** one (Contradiction Protocol — recorded, not adjudicated):

- **vs. [[john-hick|Hick]] (pluralism).** Hick treats the traditions as culturally
  conditioned but comparably valid responses to one **[[the-real|Real]]**, unified by a
  shared *soteriological* structure (self- to Reality-centredness). Prothero rejects exactly
  this: the "shared soteriology" is an artifact of imposing one (Christian, or
  liberal-Protestant) template — the traditions do not share a single problem, a single
  goal, or a single "Real." He would read Hick's [[axial-age|axial-age]] "cosmic optimism"
  and self-to-Reality turn as more Godthink.

- **vs. [[karen-armstrong|Armstrong]] (developmental-experiential).** Prothero names
  Armstrong directly as a popularizer of the "all religions point to the same compassion /
  the same ineffable God" view, and files her with the perennialists. Where she finds a
  recurring **[[apophatic-theology|mystical core]]**, he sees a **convergence assumed rather
  than found**, flattening the doctrinal, ritual, and legal differences that constitute the
  traditions. This is the live disagreement on the [[religious-pluralism-debate|pluralism
  debate]] page, §5 vs. §6.

- **vs. perennialism (Huston Smith, Huxley, Campbell, Schuon).** His primary target. See
  [[perennialism|perennialism]]: he calls it "pretend pluralism," presumptuous in the same
  way Rahner's "anonymous Christians" is presumptuous — it conscripts outsiders into one's
  own scheme against their will (Hans Küng's critique, which Prothero turns on the
  perennialists themselves).

- **vs. the New Atheists.** He grants their core claim ("religion is one of the greatest
  forces for evil") but immediately balances it ("and one of the greatest forces for good")
  and accuses them of perennialism-in-reverse — "loading all religions into one boat,"
  seeing only the shared sins. His coda treats **atheism** itself as a worldview with its own
  internal diversity (friendly vs. angry/New Atheism).

- **Kinship.** His particularism runs parallel to **George Lindbeck**'s post-liberal
  *cultural-linguistic* model of doctrine and to much recent academic comparative religion;
  it is the descriptive-fidelity pole against the charity/parity pole.

## Criticisms

- The **"different mountains" picture can overstate incommensurability**, underplaying real
  convergences (the Golden Rule he himself concedes) and the long history of mutual
  influence and borrowing he also documents.
- His **ranking "by impact"** is admittedly subjective and invites the charge of
  sensationalism (Islam first "because it is in the news").
- Treating each tradition through a single **problem–goal** can itself be a soft
  reductionism — internal pluralism (the "many Buddhisms" he stresses) strains the
  one-problem-per-religion grid.
- Naming Armstrong and Huston Smith as "theologians in disguise" cuts both ways:
  particularism, critics note, is *also* a stance with its own (liberal, pluralist-civic)
  commitments — the very move he makes against the perennialists (cf. D'Costa's "pluralism
  is a covert theology" charge on the [[religious-pluralism-debate|debate page]]).

## Influence

*God Is Not One* became a widely assigned text in introductory religious-studies courses
and a touchstone of the **religious-literacy** movement. Together with *Religious Literacy*
it reframed public argument about religion in the US away from both the "coexist" bumper
sticker and the New Atheist polemic, toward a "know the differences first" pedagogy — the
spirit of Eboo Patel's interfaith-service model that Prothero endorses.
