# Abu Hamid al-Ghazali

Al-Ghazali (Latin **Algazel**, c. 1058–1111) was the preeminent [[sunni-islam|Sunni]]
theologian, jurist, philosopher, and later Sufi of the classical period — styled *Hujjat
al-Islam* ("the Proof of Islam"). For this wiki he is the author of the
**[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|*Tahafut al-Falasifa*]]** (1095), the decisive theological
assault on Islamic Aristotelian–Neoplatonist [[falsafa|philosophy]].

## Hermeneutical Method

Ghazali writes as an **[[asharism|Ash'arite]] dialectical theologian (*[[kalam|mutakallim]]*)**
but distinctively turns the philosophers' own **demonstrative logic (*burhan*)** against them,
arguing that logic is a neutral instrument and that the *[[falsafa|falasifa]]*' metaphysical
conclusions fail by their own standards. He insists on God's free **will** against necessitarian
emanation, and on **[[occasionalism|occasionalist]]** causation (Discussion 17) to preserve the
possibility of [[prophetic-miracle|miracle]] and the veracity of revelation.

## Characteristic Positions and the takfir

In the *Tahafut* Ghazali pronounces the philosophers **infidels** on three doctrines — the
[[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the world]], God's ignorance of particulars, and the denial
of [[bodily-resurrection|bodily resurrection]] — while treating their other errors as heretical
innovation. See *[[takfir]]*.

## Positions Established in the Tahafut (by Discussion)

- **The world is temporally originated (*hadith*)**; time itself is created, with no pre-creation
  interval (Disc. 1, §36). God's **will** is "an attribute whose function… is to differentiate a
  thing from its similar" (§41) — so God can specify a moment of creation without an external
  determinant. See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
- **The book is purely destructive** — "we have not undertaken to defend a specific doctrine"
  (Disc. 1, §134); a positive creed was reserved for his *Principles of Belief*.
- **Demonstration proves only the termination of causal regress, nothing more** (Disc. 6, §10) —
  the philosophers' further claims about God's simplicity, unity, and attributes are "arbitrary."
- **The philosophers' God is no true agent**: the world proceeds from Him as a shadow from a body,
  making "maker" and "handiwork" metaphorical (Disc. 3) — Ghazali urges them to "declare openly
  that God has no action." Against this he affirms God's real attributes vs. the
  [[mutazilism|Mu'tazila]]–[[falsafa|falasifa]] denial (Disc. 6). See [[divine-attributes-in-islam]].
- **Divine knowledge of particulars** follows from will ("that which is willed must necessarily be
  known to the willer," Disc. 11); he rejects the Avicennan "universal way" as uprooting the
  religious law (Disc. 13). See [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]].
- **[[occasionalism|Occasionalist]] causation** (Disc. 17): "the connection between what is
  habitually believed to be a cause and what is habitually believed to be an effect is not
  necessary" — fire does not burn; God creates the burning. Regularity is divine **habit (*'ada*)**,
  not necessity — securing [[prophetic-miracle|miracle]].
- **The soul's immateriality and immortality** he does not deny but holds the philosophers cannot
  *demonstrate* by reason alone "without need… for the religious law" (Disc. 18, §12). See
  [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy]].
- **Bodily resurrection** must be taken in its literal (*zahir*) sense — allegorizing it (as the
  philosophers do, Disc. 20) implies prophets deceived the masses. See [[bodily-resurrection]].

## Influence

His critique reshaped the relation of reason and revelation in Sunni Islam and provoked
**[[averroes|Ibn Rushd / Averroes]]**'s point-by-point reply, the
*[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahafut al-Tahafut]]*.

## Averroes's Counter-Reading

A generation later, **[[averroes|Averroes]]** answered the *Tahāfut* line by line in the
*[[incoherence-of-the-incoherence|Tahāfut al-Tahāfut]]* — and read Ghazālī against the grain. He
charged that Ghazālī's objections were largely **sophistical**, "opposing difficulty with
difficulty" to produce perplexity rather than refutation, and proposed renaming the book "the
Incoherence of Ghazālī." More provocatively, he doubted Ghazālī's **sincerity**: that he defended
Ashʿarism only "to flatter the masses of his times," while "in metaphysics he recurs to the
philosophers… most clearly shown… in his book called *The Niche for Lights*." Averroes also accused
him of confusing the demonstrative sciences and of contradicting himself across his works (e.g. on
whether spiritual resurrection is heresy — Ghazālī "erred in religious questions as he erred in
rational problems"). On the three *[[takfir]]* charges, Averroes answered each (see
[[eternity-of-the-world]], [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]], [[bodily-resurrection]]).
