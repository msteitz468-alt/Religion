# Avicenna (Ibn Sina)

Ibn Sina (Latin **Avicenna**, c. 980–1037) was the supreme systematizer of Islamic
**[[falsafa|falsafa]]** — an Aristotelian metaphysics reworked through Neoplatonic
**[[emanation]]**. In this wiki he appears chiefly as the principal target of
**[[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]]**'s *[[incoherence-of-the-philosophers|Tahafut al-Falasifa]]*: most
of the twenty refuted theses (the [[eternity-of-the-world|eternity of the world]], God as a
necessitating First Cause knowing only universals, the [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy|immaterial
immortal soul]], allegorized resurrection) are Avicennan positions.

## Characteristic Positions (as reported by al-Ghazali)

- **The world is pre-eternal**, a necessary effect of the Eternal cause (Disc. 1). On the
  separated human souls he holds the awkward view that they form a non-material *infinite* set
  after death — which Ghazali turns against the philosophers' own horror of actual infinites.
- **Souls are created in time** *with* their bodies (against pre-existence), yet immaterial
  (Disc. 1, §119; Disc. 19) — a tension Ghazali exploits: if the soul originates by a relation to
  this body, that relation may equally condition its persistence, so its survival is undemonstrated.
- **God knows particulars "in a universal way"** — "through a universal knowledge which does not
  enter time and… does not change" (Disc. 13). Ghazali's charge: God would then not know that
  Muhammad individually issued the prophetic challenge, "uprooting the religious laws in their
  entirety." See [[gods-knowledge-of-particulars]].
- **Necessary vs. contingent existence** (God as the Necessary Existent); the eternal
  [[emanation]] of intellects and spheres from the One ("from one only one proceeds"); the rational
  [[soul-in-islamic-philosophy|soul]] as an incorporeal substance surviving the body.
