# Heshang Gong

**Heshang Gong** ("the Old Man by the River") is the name attached to the most influential **religious**
commentary on the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] — the reading that made the classic a manual for **longevity and
self-cultivation**. The figure is legendary (a riverside immortal who instructed a Han emperor); the
commentary is the reality.

> **Note:** there is **no complete modern English translation** (an old partial German version by Erkes
> exists; Alan Chan's *Two Visions of the Way* is the scholarly study). See *Needed Sources.md* →
> Outstanding. This page is written from secondary knowledge.

## The Work

A chapter-by-chapter commentary that reads the Daodejing's cosmological and political imagery as
**physiological and spiritual instruction**: the "valley spirit" and "mysterious female" become the
adept's own inner organs, breath, and vital essences; "governing the state" doubles as "governing the
body." The goal is the nourishing of life (*yangsheng*) and ultimately immortality.

## Characteristic Position

Where [[wang-bi|Wang Bi]] finds an **ontology** (the Dao as non-being), Heshang Gong finds a **practice**:
the [[dao|Dao]] and its *qi* are to be cultivated *within the body*. This is the foundational text of the
[[religious-daoism|religious-Daoist]] reading.

## Hermeneutical Method — Commentary-as-Transformation

The clearest instance in the wiki of **commentary-as-transformation** in its strong sense: the commentary
is itself a soteriological technology, meant to *transform the adept's body and life*, not to explain a
doctrine. The opposite pole from Wang Bi — see [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|the controversy]].

## Influence

The Heshang Gong commentary was, for most of Chinese history, the **most widely circulated** Daodejing,
foundational for Daoist religion, internal alchemy (*neidan*), and traditional Chinese self-cultivation.
