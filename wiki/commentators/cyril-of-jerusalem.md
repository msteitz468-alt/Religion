# Cyril of Jerusalem

Bishop of Jerusalem and author of the *Catechetical Lectures* (and *Mystagogical Catecheses*), the single richest fourth-century source for the theology of Christian initiation. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. IX–X, XVI.

## Sacramental Theology

- **[[baptism]]** — a threefold schema: (1) total remission of pre-baptismal sins ("a cure which causes not only the patient's wounds but the very scars to disappear"); (2) positive sanctification, "the illumination and deification of the believer's soul, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit… adoption as God's son by grace"; (3) the "seal" (*sphragis*) imparting the Spirit at the moment of immersion. The water becomes effective through the epiclesis: after invocation it is "no longer mere water, but water united with the Holy Spirit."
- **[[confirmation]]** — the blessed oil becomes "the chrism of Christ, capable of producing the Holy Spirit"; chrismation confers the very status of "Christian."
- **[[eucharist]]** — uses the verb *metaballein* ("change/convert") for the consecration (analogy of Cana), while elsewhere retaining figural language ("the body is given to you in the figure of bread"). The rite is "the sacrifice of propitiation," propitiatory for both the living and the dead.

## Trinitarian Position

Conservative; he discouraged inquiry into the Spirit's Person but taught a doctrine approximating later orthodoxy — the Spirit "belongs to the Trinity," is *hypostatos* ("subsistent"), the "universal sanctifier"; the Son is "like the Father in all things," one with him in will and operation.

## Ayres on Cyril's Trinitarian Position (Nicaea and Its Legacy)

Lewis Ayres ([[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its Legacy*]], 2004) uses Cyril to show "the
problematic status even of the flexible categories" of the controversy: deposed (357),
reinstated at Seleucia (359), repeatedly exiled, and finally on the pro-Nicene side at
Constantinople (381), he was labelled "Homoiousian" by the historians, "though this probably
means little more than that he voted with the majority." Ayres reads him as broadly **Eusebian
with Alexandrian affinities** — but not Athanasian — who (unlike either Eusebius) freely
affirmed the Son's eternal, incomprehensible generation. In the *Catechetical Lectures* "the one
God" is consistently the Father; the Son is "begotten from all eternity by an inscrutable and
incomprehensible generation," confessed through the Jerusalem creed's X-from-X language ("Life of
Life, Light of Light… Wisdom of Wisdom"), which signals "a continuity of attributes between
Father and Son, not simply a derivation of lesser from greater." Cyril almost entirely avoids
technical *ousia* language and is wary of *hypostasis* as a non-scriptural term — steering
between Sabellian confusion (he likely has [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus]] in view) and
subordinationist over-distinction.
