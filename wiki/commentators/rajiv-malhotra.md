# Rajiv Malhotra

## Biographical Overview

American-Indian independent scholar, author, and public intellectual. Born c. 1950 in India; educated in physics and computer science in the United States; founded Infinity Foundation (Princeton, NJ), a philanthropy supporting research into Indian civilization, in the 1990s after an early career in information technology and business. Major works include *Being Different: An Indian Challenge to Western Universalism* (2011), *Breaking India* (with Aravindan Neelakandan, 2011), *Indra's Net* (2014), *The Battle for Sanskrit* (2016), and *Academic Hinduphobia* (2016). Announced but had not yet published *U-Turn Theory* as of *Being Different*.

## Hermeneutical Method

Malhotra does not work primarily as a textual commentator in the classical sense but as a **comparative civilizational analyst** deploying a systematic *purva paksha* methodology: he first reconstructs Western/Abrahamic frameworks in their own terms, then measures dharmic traditions against those frameworks — and then reverses the comparison by measuring Western civilization against dharmic standards.

His interpretive tools are drawn from multiple sources:
- **PaRDeS / darshana tradition**: Operates at the conceptual (*remez* / *vicara*) level rather than the peshat or sod levels
- **Comparative hermeneutics**: Parallel to (but distinct from) perennialism; he explicitly rejects the "sameness approach" (perennialism) while acknowledging that different traditions can learn from each other
- **Post-colonial discourse theory**: Uses Said's Orientalism and Fanon's frameworks as points of departure — then critiques their insufficiency for dharmic identity
- **Systems thinking**: Models dharma as an "open architecture" platform; sampradayas as "systems integrators"; uses computer-science and market-economics metaphors throughout

Key methodological commitments: (1) do not flatten differences; (2) insist on Sanskrit non-translatables — do not map dharmic concepts onto Western categories; (3) apply the same critical gaze to the West as Western scholars apply to India.

## Characteristic Positions

- **Against the sameness approach**: Argues that "all religions say the same thing" is either intellectual laziness or an unconscious surrender to a Western framework that absorbs dharmic traditions by making them "Hindu versions" of familiar Western concepts.
- **History-centrism as structural feature**: The Abrahamic religions' dependence on unrepeatable historical events (Sinai, Incarnation, Muhammad's prophethood) is a deep structural feature, not a peripheral one. It produces what he calls "history-centrism" — see [[history-centrism]].
- **Integral unity vs. synthetic unity**: The starting point of dharma is *purna* (the Whole), from which multiplicity emerges; the starting point of Western metaphysics is separate entities assembled by an external force. This distinction drives differences in logic, ethics, cosmology, and politics. See [[integral-unity]].
- **Cultural digestion**: Western civilization operates as the "tiger" that dismembers dharmic prey and incorporates selected elements into its own body, eliminating the source. See [[cultural-digestion]].
- **Sanskrit non-translatables**: 18 key categories resist accurate translation into Western terms. Insists on retaining Sanskrit originals rather than accepting Western near-equivalents.
- **Sapeksha-dharma**: Advocates "positive pluralism" — genuine mutual respect for differences — over *nirapeksha* (indifferentism/false neutrality). See [[sapeksha-dharma]].

## Major Works (Relevant to This Wiki)

- *Being Different* (2011): The primary ingested source. Comparative analysis across four structural differences between dharmic and Abrahamic/Western civilizations.
- *Breaking India* (with Neelakandan, 2011): On foreign-funded separatist projects in India — Dravidian nationalism, Dalit-separatism, evangelical conversion. Not yet ingested.
- *U-Turn Theory* (announced, not yet published at 2011): On Western appropriations of Indian spiritual traditions — yoga, meditation, Vedanta — that adopt practices while rejecting the metaphysical frameworks that give them meaning.

## Controversies

- Frequently in conflict with Western academic Indology, which he characterizes as structurally biased against dharmic traditions — a position shared by the *Invading the Sacred* project (Ramaswamy, Nicolas, Banerjee 2007), which he supported.
- His broad characterization of "Abrahamic traditions" as a unified bloc has been contested by scholars of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam who emphasize internal diversity.
- Plagiarism allegations surfaced in 2016 regarding his use of scholars' work (Andrew Nicholson) in *Indra's Net* — post-dates *Being Different*.
