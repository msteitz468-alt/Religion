# Harry Austryn Wolfson (1887–1974)

Harry Austryn Wolfson was a pioneering historian of philosophy who traced the development of religious and philosophic systems from Plato through late antiquity to Spinoza, with special attention to the Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions and their interactions with Greek philosophy. As Nathan Littauer Professor of Hebrew Literature and Philosophy at Harvard, he produced landmark studies that reconstructed coherent "philosophies" out of scattered texts using his distinctive hypothetico-deductive method.

## Biographical Context

Wolfson was a major 20th-century scholar of medieval and patristic philosophy, Jewish thought, and the history of ideas. His work on Philo (*Philo: Foundations of Religious Philosophy in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam*, 1947) established Philo as the key bridge figure who recast Jewish religion in philosophic form, producing a "Jewish version of Greek philosophy." The *Philosophy of the Church Fathers* (1956, Vol. I) continues this project, showing how the Church Fathers similarly created a "Christian version of Greek philosophy" by applying the same assumption (Greek philosophers discovered by reason what Scripture reveals) to the mysteries of faith, Trinity, and Incarnation. The planned multi-volume series was only partially realized in this form, but the method and framework influenced subsequent scholarship on the "philosophy of the Church Fathers."

## Hermeneutical Method

Wolfson’s signature "hypothetico-deductive method of text study" (described in his Spinoza work, Crescas, and Philo, and referenced in the Church Fathers preface): 
- Begins with a general analysis and forecast as a working hypothesis.
- Tests it against representative texts from the sources (not exhaustive lists, but illustrative: earliest appearances, texts introducing shades of difference or difficulties, or showing currency; often concluding with Augustine or John of Damascus).
- Pieces together scattered terms, formulas, and analogies into a unified, continuous system.
- Reconstructs the "latent processes of reasoning" behind "uttered words" (which "conceal as much as they reveal").
- Traces development, influence, and anticipation (e.g., from Philo through the Fathers to later philosophy).
- Explicitly serves as a "prolegomenon" to major problems of religious philosophy across seventeen centuries.

He treats the Fathers not as ad hoc theologians but as creators of a systematic philosophy, while remaining attentive to the controlling role of Scripture, tradition, and the "Rule of Faith." The method is historical-philological and reconstructive rather than purely analytic or confessional.

## Major Works and Characteristic Positions

- *Philo* (1947): Philo as the originator of the project of religious philosophy in the West; "What Is New in Philo?"; foundations for later Jewish, Christian, and Islamic thought.
- *The Philosophy of the Church Fathers, Volume I: Faith, Trinity, Incarnation* (1956): The core of the present ingest. Structures the "philosophy of the Church Fathers" around (1) Faith and Reason (attitudes toward philosophy, allegory as philosophical method building on Philo + NT, scriptural presuppositions from Philo embodied in the Rule of Faith, philosophy as handmaiden, single vs. double faith theories in Tertullian/Origen vs. Clement/Augustine); (2) the Trinity (NT tripartite formulas via Paul’s identification of Spirit with preexistent Christ; differentiation of Logos and Spirit; twofold vs. single stage generation; eternal generation by will or nature; unity as rule or substratum/ousia/hypostasis/homoousios; detailed in Origen, Tertullian, Basil, with Nicene Creed context and suspicions); (3) the Incarnation (generation as mystery with physical analogies; "two and one" solved via analogies of physical union, especially Aristotelian "predominance" (soul-body/form-matter) used by orthodox Fathers and councils, Stoic-influenced perichoresis for property interchange; mystery remains; unorthodox applications by Apollinaris, Nestorius, Monophysites/Monotheletes; wills and operations controversies); (4) The Anathematized (Gnosticism as pagan syncretism "Christianized" into tiered systems with prolation and various Christologies—not true philosophy; heresies like Modalism (Sabellius) and Creationalism (Arius) for Trinity, neo-Ebionism/neo-Docetism for Christology, arising logically from difficulties in catholic beliefs).
- Other works: Studies on Spinoza (as culmination of the medieval tradition), Crescas’ critique of Aristotle, etc.

Characteristic positions: The Fathers (like Philo) created a genuine philosophic system by harmonizing Scripture with Greek concepts (especially the problem of the one and the many for Trinity and Incarnation). Philo is the indispensable starting point and prolegomenon. Allegory is a central philosophical tool (with specific classifications traceable to Philo). Faith and reason are related in single- or double-faith frameworks with real debate among the Fathers. The orthodox solutions (Cappadocian vocabulary, predominance union) are philosophically sophisticated responses to inherent difficulties that also generated the major heresies. The "hypothetico-deductive" reconstruction reveals a more coherent "philosophy of the Church Fathers" than is often recognized.

## Influence and Controversies

Wolfson’s work is foundational for understanding patristic thought as philosophy (not merely theology borrowing tools). It has been praised for its depth, scope, and lucid reconstruction but also critiqued by some for over-systematizing or for the strength of the Philo-continuity thesis. His series framework (Plato to Spinoza) places the Fathers in a grand narrative of religious philosophy. In the wiki, he is the primary modern scholarly source for the systematic development of patristic philosophy of faith, Trinity, and Incarnation, complementing more concise treatments like Stead’s *Philosophy in Christian Antiquity*.
