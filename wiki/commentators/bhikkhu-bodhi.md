# Bhikkhu Bodhi

**Bhikkhu Bodhi** (born Jeffrey Block, Brooklyn, New York, 1944) is an American
Theravada monk and the foremost modern English translator of the Pali Nikāyas. He is the
principal anglophone bridge between the [[tipitaka|Pali Canon]] and the contemporary
reader, and his translations and introductions are this wiki's main lens onto the early
discourses.

## Biographical Context

Bodhi took a PhD in philosophy (Claremont, 1972) before ordaining as a Buddhist monk in
Sri Lanka under the German-born scholar-monk **Nyanaponika Thera**, whom he succeeded as
editor and then president of the **Buddhist Publication Society** in Kandy. He returned to
the United States in 2002, later teaching at Chuang Yen Monastery (a Chinese
[[mahayana|Mahayana]] center) in New York and founding **Buddhist Global Relief**, a
hunger-relief charity — an expression of socially engaged Buddhism adjacent to that of
[[thich-nhat-hanh|Thich Nhat Hanh]].

## Major Works

His standing rests on near-complete English translations of the four great **Nikāyas** of
the Sutta Piṭaka:

- the *Majjhima Nikāya* (*The Middle Length Discourses*, completing Ñāṇamoli's draft, 1995);
- the *Saṃyutta Nikāya* (*The Connected Discourses*, 2000);
- the *Aṅguttara Nikāya* (*The Numerical Discourses*, 2012);
- and the anthology **[[bodhi-in-the-buddhas-words|*In the Buddha's Words*]]** (2005), a
  thematic selection that is the entry point to the whole corpus.

## Hermeneutical Method

Bodhi reads the suttas in the **[[theravada|Theravada]]** mode — anchored in the Pali
commentarial tradition (the *aṭṭhakathā* of [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]]) but tempered by
modern philology and a philosopher's eye for systematic structure. His signature
contribution is *architectural*: he reorganizes scattered discourses into a graduated
sequence — from the human condition, through ethics and the [[karma|law of action]] and a
[[samsara|fortunate rebirth]], up to the [[noble-eightfold-path|path]] and the
[[nirvana|planes of realization]] — recovering the **gradual training** (*anupubbasikkhā*)
he argues is implicit in the canon. He is a careful but firm defender of the early texts'
doctrinal coherence against both romantic "the Buddha taught only meditation" reductions
and purely historicist dissolution.

## Influence

Bodhi's translations are the standard scholarly and practice references for the Nikāyas in
English and underwrite most contemporary Western [[theravada|Theravada]] and *vipassanā*
study. His insistence on **rebirth and kamma as integral** (not optional "cultural
baggage") has made him a prominent voice against secularized readings of the Dhamma.
