# W. Y. Evans-Wentz

**Walter Yeeling Evans-Wentz** (1878–1965) was the American editor whose 1927 volume, prepared
with the Sikkimese translator **Lama Kazi Dawa Samdup**, first brought the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan
Book of the Dead]] to the English-speaking world — and who **coined that English title**, retained
in all subsequent translations.

## Role and Method

Evans-Wentz was an editor and interpreter rather than a translator of Tibetan; Kazi Dawa Samdup
supplied the translation. The 1927 edition rendered only **three chapters** of the cycle (the
after-death bardos and the aspirational prayers) — Chapter 11 of the full fourteen-chapter work —
from an illuminated manuscript. His framing was strongly **Theosophical and comparativist**,
which shaped (and, on later scholarly assessment, sometimes distorted) the Western reception of
the text.

His edition carried a psychological commentary by **Carl Jung**, who read "The Great Liberation by
Hearing" *backwards* as a map of the psyche — a reading that decisively shaped the text's modern
Western afterlife. The later [[coleman-dorje-tibetan-book-of-the-dead|Coleman/Jinpa/Dorje
translation]] (the first *complete* one) supersedes Evans-Wentz on completeness and accuracy while
acknowledging his role in the work's fame.
