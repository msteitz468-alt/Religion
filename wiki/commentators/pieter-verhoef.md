# Pieter A. Verhoef

Pieter A. Verhoef was a South African Old Testament scholar in the Reformed
tradition (associated with Stellenbosch), author of the New International Commentary
on the Old Testament (NICOT) volume *The Books of [[haggai|Haggai]] and
[[malachi|Malachi]]* (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1987). The wiki's coverage of both
post-exilic books derives from this single volume.

## Hermeneutical Method

Verhoef writes as a **grammatical-historical, confessionally Reformed** exegete who
engages Continental critical scholarship closely while resisting its source-critical
conclusions. Characteristic features evident across the commentary:

- **Text-critical conservatism.** He repeatedly defends the Masoretic Text against
  the emendations of BHK and BHS, generally preferring the more difficult reading and
  concluding the MT is "preferable to the ancient versions." He brings fifteen years
  of Bible-translation experience to bear on the Hebrew.
- **Defense of literary unity.** Verhoef consistently argues against
  dismemberment — rejecting Rothstein's transposition of Haggai 2:15–19, Eissfeldt's
  and Steck's source-splitting of Haggai 1, and the critical consensus (Kuhl) that
  Malachi 2:11–12 and 3:22–24 are interpolations. Structural analysis is his chief
  tool, "an antidote to dismembering a literary unit."
- **Redemptive-historical / christological telescoping.** He reads prophecy as
  fulfilled "in stages" — "centrally in the first and finally in the second advent of
  Christ" — applying this to Haggai's temple-glory and Zerubbabel oracles and to
  Malachi's Day-of-the-Lord and Elijah passages. These christological readings are his
  own confessional voice and are recorded here as attributed.
- **Dialogue with Dutch Reformed exegesis.** His most-followed interlocutors are
  J. L. Koole and A. S. van der Woude; he engages Rudolph, Elliger, Beuken, Beyse,
  Baldwin, Calvin, and Wellhausen throughout, adjudicating positions rather than
  merely cataloguing them.

## Characteristic Positions (attributed)

- **Haggai** was *not* a priest and *not* a cultic prophet, "deliberately and
  emphatically called 'the prophet.'"
- **Haggai 2:7** — "the precious things of all nations" are treasures/spoils, not the
  Messiah; the older "desire of all nations" christological reading "is now generally
  abandoned."
- **Zerubbabel** (Haggai 2:20–23) is read eschatologically as the renewed election of
  the Davidic line, against purely historical readings (Junker); whether Haggai
  identified him with the Messiah "may be left open."
- **"Malachi"** is a proper name, not merely the title "my messenger."
- **Malachi's date** is "shortly after 433 BCE," between Nehemiah's two visits —
  against the more widely accepted pre-Ezra dating.
- **Malachi 1:2–3** ("Jacob I loved, Esau I hated") does *not* teach predestination to
  eternal life and damnation (against Calvin); its scope is the history of salvation.
- **Malachi 2:16** — God is the subject: "I hate, I am hating" divorce (a participle),
  against the LXX/Targum conditional "if one divorces out of hate."
- **Malachi 3:1** — "my messenger" is a human forerunner (identified with Elijah / John
  the Baptist), while "the messenger of the covenant" is identified with the Lord
  himself.
