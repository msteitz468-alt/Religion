# Gregory of Nyssa

Gregory of Nyssa (c. 335 – c. 394), younger brother of [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil of Caesarea]], was the principal developer of the **concrete account** of [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|Cappadocian philosophy]] and, in Johannes Zachhuber's reading ([[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]]), the thinker who turned Basil's terminological convention into "a properly Christian philosophy with enormous potential across practically all areas of the doctrine of faith." He is one of the three [[cappadocian-fathers|Cappadocian Fathers]] and is honored in the East as a master of mystical and apophatic theology.

## Biographical Context

Born c. 335 into the same wealthy, devout Cappadocian family as Basil and Macrina. Zachhuber describes him as "elusive": his vast corpus reveals little about his life or personality, and he habitually effaced himself behind his writings — many of his most original works present themselves as mere continuations or defenses of Basil. After Basil's death (378/379) and the accession of Theodosius I, Gregory emerged from his brother's shadow as a major intellectual and church leader, a favourite of the imperial court, an orthodox point of reference at and after the Council of Constantinople (381), and a rival (alongside [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory of Nazianzus]]) for the mantle of Basil's legacy — a rivalry from which, Zachhuber argues, he ultimately "emerged on top." His most creative philosophical work falls in the brief, intense years 379–385.

## Hermeneutical and Philosophical Method

Gregory's method is **ontological, cosmological, and synthetic** — building a realist metaphysics of universal nature and integrating Trinity, creation, anthropology, and salvation history into one system. Where Basil's account was abstract (logical/grammatical), Gregory added the **concrete** (physical/metaphysical) dimension.

### The Concrete Account

- **Creation as the simultaneous making of all being.** In the *Apology on the Hexaëmeron*, Gregory reads Genesis 1:1 as the instantaneous, non-extensional creation of the entire *ousia* of created being — a dynamic, seminal totality (a "whole," ὅλον, "fullness," πλήρωμα), compared to a geometrical point or instant, from which individuals (τόδε τι, Aristotle's "this something") emerge one by one in a divinely ordered sequence. This let him avoid identifying the "beginning" with the Logos (and so the subordinationist notion of Christ-as-mediator-of-creation) and resolve the problem of matter (*hyle* emerges from the mixture of intelligible properties).
- **Universal humanity.** In *On the Creation of Humanity* (ch. 16), the "man" of Genesis 1:26 is the *whole of humankind* — indefinite (*aoristos*) in meaning — contained "as if in one body" in the first creation by God's foreknowledge and power. All human beings are "one man." This made the unity of human nature across history available for theological use from fall to incarnation, redemption, and resurrection.
- **"Why not three Gods?"** In *To Ablabius*, Gregory answers the tritheism objection by doubling down: strictly, the universal "man" should *not* be used in the plural. Nature (*physis*) is "one, unified with itself, a precisely undivided monad, not increased by addition nor decreased by subtraction," even though seen in a multitude — illustrated by collectives (a people, an army, an assembly) and by the gold of which coins are made (coins are "golden," not "golds"). Unity of the divine is further grounded in the single divine activity/operation.

For Gregory, **continuity** between universal and particular is as important as their distinction, and ontological priority belongs to the **unity of being**. Zachhuber uses this to argue (against Lossky/Zizioulas) that Cappadocian philosophy is an ontology of being-as-one, continuous with Greek thought — *not* a "turn to the person."

### The Synthesis (Epistle 38)

The so-called **Epistle 38** (*On the Difference between Ousia and Hypostasis*), transmitted under Basil's name but most likely Gregory's (possibly written c. 379 on his late brother's behalf), fuses the abstract and concrete accounts. It adds names/objects as a third pole (the universal name's *indefinite/scattered* meaning), and offers a near-definition of *hypostasis* as "the conflux of properties around the particular" that gives the otherwise unstable common *ousia* its "stability" (στάσις) — shifting toward a binary of **being and existence** that anticipates the later essence/existence distinction. See [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|the classical theory]].

## Major Works

- **Apology on the Hexaëmeron** — cosmological continuation of Basil's homilies; the "concrete" ontology of creation.
- **On the Creation of Humanity** (*De opificio hominis*) — universal human nature; double creation; translated early into Latin and influential in the medieval West.
- **To Ablabius: On Not Three Gods** — the classic answer to the tritheism objection.
- **Against Eunomius** (12 books) — detailed refutation, taking up Basil's defense.
- **Epistle 38** (*On the Difference between Ousia and Hypostasis*) — the synthesis text (disputed authorship; most likely Gregory's).
- Mystical works (not the focus of Zachhuber): *The Life of Moses*, *On the Soul and the Resurrection* — Sinai ascent, apophaticism, *epektasis*.

## Characteristic Positions

- Realist universal: human nature (and the divine nature) is a genuine ontological unity, existing only and exclusively in its individual hypostases, with priority to the unity.
- The same logical/ontological apparatus underwrites Trinity, cosmology, anthropology, and the economy of salvation — the first systematic integration of Christian doctrine.
- Tensions he bequeathed: whether *hypostasis* = the concrete individual or only its particularizing properties; whether the individual is itself *ousia*. These "Achilles' heels" of the classical theory drove later (esp. Christological) development.

## Influence

Gregory's concrete theory was what made Cappadocian philosophy a comprehensive system; later Eastern writers received the *ousia*/*hypostasis* duality largely as he codified it (though they were more ambivalent about his strong realism). His unresolved tensions became the seedbed of sixth-century Chalcedonian and miaphysite philosophies (later scopes of [[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|Zachhuber]]). In the East he is the "father of fathers" for mystical and apophatic theology.
