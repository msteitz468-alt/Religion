# Surendranath Dasgupta

**Surendranath Dasgupta** (1887–1952) was a Bengali philosopher and Sanskritist, Professor of Sanskrit
at Government College Chittagong and later Principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta, and a lecturer at
Cambridge. He is the author of the monumental **[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|*A History of
Indian Philosophy*]]** (5 vols., Cambridge University Press, 1922–1955) — still the most comprehensive
single-author survey of the Indian philosophical traditions in English.

## Method and Significance

Dasgupta is a **modern-critical historian**, not a confessional commentator: he reconstructs each system
from its primary Sanskrit texts and their indigenous commentaries, working extensively from unpublished
manuscripts he collected on journeys through South India. His organizing frame is the classical Indian
map of the [[saddarshana|*ṣaḍ-darśana*]] (the six orthodox schools) plus the heterodox (*nāstika*)
systems — Buddhism, Jainism, and Cārvāka materialism — treated school by school rather than thematically.

His distinctive scholarly commitments:

- **The unity of Indian thought** lies in shared *problems* (karma, *mukti*, the soul, the pessimism→
  optimism arc), not in a single doctrine — see his General Observations in Vol. I.
- He gives the **theistic Vedānta schools of the South** their first connected English treatment
  ([[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]], [[madhva|Madhva]], Nimbārka, Vallabha, the Bhāgavata schools), drawing on rare
  manuscripts of their later dialecticians.
- For this wiki his most consequential contribution is **Vol. IV (*Indian Pluralism*, 1961)**, whose
  chapters on [[madhva|Madhva]] and his school — life, the thirty-seven works, the
  [[brahma-sutras|Brahma-Sūtra]] interpretation, the defence of [[panchabheda|pluralism]], and the
  dualist–monist controversy — let the wiki **ground the [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] pole on a real source**
  for the first time (Madhva's own *bhāṣya* having no complete free English translation).
