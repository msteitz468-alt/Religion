# Gordon J. Wenham

Gordon J. Wenham is a British evangelical Old Testament scholar, among the most important contributors to evangelical Pentateuchal commentary. His *Leviticus* in the NICOT series (Eerdmans / Routledge & Kegan Paul, c. 1979) is distinctive for integrating **Mary Douglas**’s social anthropology into evangelical exegesis while retaining a classic Protestant plain-sense and NT-application frame.

**Source page (fidelity re-ingest 2026-07-18):** [[wenham-leviticus-nicot]]

## Hermeneutical Method

Wenham names his approach the **"classic Protestant approach"** (Calvin’s commentaries on the last four books of Moses; he also praises Catholic R. North on Lev 25 as a fine modern example): equal seriousness for (1) plain original meaning—what the author meant and earliest readers understood—and (2) abiding theological value for the church (Rom 15:4).

**Tools he privileges alongside ordinary commentaries:**
1. **ANE comparative** studies of ritual and sacrifice (distinctives of biblical religion)
2. **Social anthropology** — symbolism of ritual on empirical footing (Douglas *Purity and Danger*; quotation permission acknowledged in the preface)
3. **New literary criticism** — structure, key words, repetition, summaries, triadic patterning

**What he deliberately de-emphasizes:** detailed source and historical criticism in the commentary body. Those debates are confined to Introduction sections on Sources and Authorship/Date, lest they turn Leviticus into “a desert more … than Sinai.”

**Running Christian frame:** each major unit ends with “Leviticus N and the NT,” tracing words, ideas, and rituals into the New Testament—Wenham reports surprise at how pervasive Levitical ideas are there.

## Characteristic Positions (from Leviticus NICOT)

**On authorship/date.** Three positions surveyed without dogmatic commitment: traditional Mosaic; Wellhausen postexilic P; Kaufmann-style preexilic non-Mosaic. He finds the **postexilic P date difficult to maintain** (Ezekiel’s use of Levitical material; P’s linguistic profile not late biblical Hebrew). Mosaic authorship is not treated as a theological necessity.

**On holiness.** Following Douglas, *qāḏôš* means more than separation to divine service: it means **wholeness and completeness**—physical perfection of offerings and persons; hybrids and confusions abominated; individuals must conform to their class; classes must not be mixed (cf. Lev 19:19; sexual morality as order vs confusion). Holy/common and clean/unclean form a grid; the unclean must not contact the holy.

**On the food laws.** Strong endorsement of Douglas’s structural analysis over hygienic or purely anti-pagan explanations. Clean/unclean distinctions mirror Israel’s election: as YHWH separated Israel from the nations, Israel separates clean from unclean animals. Daily diet teaches categorical holiness.

**On sacrifice (graded models).**
| Offering | Distinctive aim (Wenham) |
|---|---|
| Burnt | General atonement/propitiation; *kipper* as ransom; whole to God |
| Cereal (*minḥah*) | Covenant tribute/dedication; salt of the covenant |
| Peace | *Shalom*/well-being; communion meal |
| Purification (*ḥaṭṭāʾt*) | Sanctuary cleansing—not bare “sin offering” |
| Reparation (*ʾāšām*) | Trespass against holy things or God’s name; restitution + ram; Isa 53 uses *ʾāšām* |

Deliberate (“high-handed”) sin requires reparation and public confession as prerequisites; unintentional sin may be met by remorse and sacrifice. Efficacy is God’s grant of forgiveness, not automatic ritual.

**On Azazel (Lev 16).** Surveys demon / “complete destruction” / “rocky precipice” views; concludes the ceremony’s total meaning is that **sin is exterminated from Israel** even if etymology stays open. See [[azazel-identity]].

**On Nadab and Abihu.** Precise “strange fire” mechanics obscure; decisive is action **not commanded**. Same fire of glory (9:24) becomes judgment (10:2). Priestly essence = distinguish holy/common and unclean/clean (10:10).

**On ch. 17 / Holiness Code.** Prefers ch. 17 as **hinge** between cultic and practical halves rather than simply H’s opening. Chs. 17–26 culminate in covenant blessings/curses (ch. 26).

**On Jubilee and ch. 27.** Jubilee grounded in “the land is mine”; anti-ruin social theology; messianic *dĕrôr* trajectory. Ch. 27 after 26 is association-of-ideas plus **book-level recapitulation** of holiness (persons, animals, land), not a pointless appendix.

**On law and the Christian.** Ceremonial cult obsolete as ongoing cult yet indispensable as model for Christ’s atonement; moral principles continue; civil penalties require wise reapplication (church as minority culture // early church).

## Key Scholars Engaged

Mary Douglas (*Purity and Danger* — strongly adopted); Yehezkel Kaufmann (preexilic P — sympathetically engaged); Julius Wellhausen (postexilic P — critically engaged); Jacob Milgrom (Levitical vocabulary — frequently cited); Rolf Rendtorff (H redaction — engaged); Rashi / G. R. Driver / Hoffmann / Hertz (Azazel); D. J. Davies (sacrifice as relationship restoration; aggregation rites); R. K. Harrison (series editor); Calvin; R. North (Lev 25).
