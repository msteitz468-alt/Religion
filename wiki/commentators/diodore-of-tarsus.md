# Diodore of Tarsus

Bishop of Tarsus (from 378) and the chief theorist of the [[theoria|Antiochene exegetical school]], teacher of [[theodore-of-mopsuestia|Theodore of Mopsuestia]] and [[john-chrysostom|John Chrysostom]]. A staunch Nicene during the Arian struggle, he is remembered above all for codifying the Antiochene method of [[theoria]] as a disciplined alternative to [[allegorical-reading|Alexandrian allegory]]. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. III.

## Hermeneutical Method

Diodore framed the governing principle of Antiochene exegesis: the higher, contemplative reading (*theoria*) is permitted and indeed encouraged, but only on the *basis* of the historical narrative, never at its expense —

> "We do not forbid the higher interpretation and *theoria*, for the historical narrative does not exclude it, but is on the contrary the basis and substructure of loftier insights…. We must, however, be on our guard against letting the *theoria* do away with the historical basis, for the result would then be, not *theoria*, but allegory."

In practice he applied a restrained typology — Cain prefiguring the synagogue, Abel the Church, the spotless lamb Christ. Kelly notes that he, like his pupil Theodore, took an "extreme" version of the literal-historical position.

## Influence

Through his pupils Theodore of Mopsuestia and Chrysostom, Diodore's method became the hallmark of Antiochene biblical scholarship and, indirectly, of the [[arian-controversy|Antiochene Christology]] that would clash with Alexandria at Ephesus and Chalcedon.
