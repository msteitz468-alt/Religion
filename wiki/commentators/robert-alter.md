# Robert Alter

Robert Alter (b. 1935) is an American scholar of Hebrew and comparative literature at the
University of California, Berkeley, and the foremost exponent of the **literary reading of
the Hebrew Bible**. Trained as a comparatist (his early work was on the European novel and
on Stendhal), he turned that apparatus on the biblical text, arguing that the Bible's
ancient authors were conscious literary artists whose meaning is carried by the fine grain
of their prose. His *The Art of Biblical Narrative* (1981) and *The Art of Biblical Poetry*
(1985) reoriented a generation of biblical study; his decades-long project of translating
and annotating the Hebrew Bible culminated in the complete three-volume
*The Hebrew Bible: A Translation with Commentary* (2018). In this wiki he is read chiefly
through *[[alter-the-david-story|The David Story]]* (1999), his translation and commentary
on [[1-samuel|1]] and [[2-samuel|2 Samuel]].

## Hermeneutical Method

Alter practices the **[[literary-reading-of-the-bible|literary-critical]]** reading of
Scripture. Its working assumptions:

- **The text as a designed whole.** Against the source-critical and form-critical
  traditions (Wellhausen's [[documentary-hypothesis|Documentary Hypothesis]]; Leonhard
  Rost's [[succession-narrative|Succession Narrative]]; the
  [[deuteronomistic-history|Deuteronomistic]] redactional layers), Alter does not deny
  composite origins but insists that the received text is an artful unity whose effects are
  lost when it is dissected into hypothetical strata.
- **Close reading of the Hebrew.** Meaning rides on word-motifs (*Leitwörter*), repetition
  with variation, the deployment and withholding of epithets, syntactic pacing, and the
  contrastive treatment of speakers in dialogue.
- **Type-scenes and convention.** Recurrent narrative templates (the annunciation, the
  betrothal at the well, the hero's first encounter) are conventions the writer invokes and
  pointedly varies; the variation is the meaning.
- **Narrative reticence and the "gap."** Biblical narrative is "fraught with background";
  the writer characteristically withholds motive and inner life, compelling the reader to
  infer — as with the systematically blocked inner world of [[david|David]].
- **Literal, defamiliarizing translation.** Alter renders the Hebrew's parataxis, concrete
  idiom, and repetition rather than smoothing them, on the principle that the style *is* the
  meaning.

## Characteristic Positions

1. **The Bible as literature, not merely as document or doctrine.** Its authors were
   "imaginative writer[s]" comparable to Shakespeare, Stendhal, Tolstoy, and Proust, who
   transcend their own ideological starting points through "dialectic fullness of
   conception."
2. **The David story as the first realistic political narrative.** See
   *[[alter-the-david-story|The David Story]]*: David is "the first full-length portrait of
   a Machiavellian prince in Western literature," and the narrative an "imagining of
   history" analogous to Shakespeare's history plays — preserving the contours of real
   events while imagining their inner moral cost.
3. **A light-handed Deuteronomist.** The [[deuteronomistic-history|Deuteronomistic]] editor
   framed and lightly interpolated rather than authored the David material — most visibly in
   the seam between [[solomon|Solomon's]] pious deathbed instruction and the cold political
   testament around it (1 Kings 2).
4. **Against reductive source-ideology.** He rejects sorting Samuel into "Davidide,"
   "Saulide," and "prophetic" advocacy strands as "blind to the complexity of vision" of
   the author.

## Influence and Place in the Wiki

Alter is the wiki's anchor for the **literary turn** in modern biblical scholarship,
read alongside (and partly in dialogue with) Frank Kermode, J. P. Fokkelman, Shimon
Bar-Efrat, and — as a foil — Robert Polzin. His commentary grounds the David-narrative
text pages ([[1-samuel|1 Samuel]], [[2-samuel|2 Samuel]]) and the court-narrative figure
cluster ([[david|David]], [[saul|Saul]], [[samuel|Samuel]], [[absalom|Absalom]],
[[bathsheba|Bathsheba]], [[uriah|Uriah]], [[joab|Joab]], [[nathan|Nathan]],
[[abner|Abner]], [[amnon|Amnon]], [[michal|Michal]], [[jonathan|Jonathan]]).
