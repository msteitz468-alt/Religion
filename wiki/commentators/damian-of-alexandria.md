# Damian of Alexandria

Damian, miaphysite Patriarch of Alexandria (fl. c. 570–605), appears in Johannes Zachhuber ([[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]], §6.1) as a constructive anti-tritheist thinker whose theory survives almost entirely through the hostile *Contra Damianum* of [[peter-of-callinicus|Peter of Callinicus]]. Where [[severus-of-antioch|Severus]] and [[john-philoponus|Philoponus]] had pushed the [[oriental-orthodoxy|miaphysite]] tradition toward the particular, Damian "recognized the classical theory had to be reasserted" to counter the [[tritheist-controversy|tritheist controversy]] — and did so by retrieving the **abstract dimension** of Cappadocian thought.

## Hypostases Identical with Their Properties

Damian's central move was to **identify the hypostasis with its property (idioma)**: hypostases "are not merely characterized by their idiomata but are identical with them." His proof text was *Epistle 38* (read as defining hypostasis as the "bundle of properties"). He sought a middle course between tritheism (treating properties as substantial differentiae → three species) and Sabellianism (treating them as accidents in one subject → one hypostasis). But, Zachhuber notes, "the 'hypostatic property' is not a 'subsisting property' but a property causing an ousia to subsist" — the argument did not, by itself, yield Damian's claim. Peter retorted that Damian "might as well have admitted to either Arianism or Sabellianism."

## Ousia as Material Substrate

Damian preferred the **language of inherence** — hypostases are 'in' the substance — claiming continuity with Philoxenus and Severus. But where they meant individuals-in-the-collective-totality (the concrete universal), Damian's model resembled **Stoic qualities inhering in an undetermined universal substrate identified with matter**. He cited Basil, *Against Eunomius* II.4: "by 'substance' I mean the material substrate (τὸ ὑλικὸν ὑποκείμενον)" — a phrase [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]] had explicitly *negated*. Damian fused this with a quasi-Dionysian emphasis on the divine ousia as the "true 'one'," the super-substantial source in which the hypostases have their ontological foundation.

## Substantial Hypostasis

Because terms of the common divine substance are predicated of individual hypostases, Damian spoke of the **"substantial hypostasis"** (ὑπόστασις οὐσιώδης): the substance is "always indicated alongside" when we name a Person — "God the Father is both participant and participated." Zachhuber flags the cost: this implies Father, Son, and Spirit are not "truly and fully God" except insofar as they subsist 'in' the Trinitarian substance — a gradation the Cappadocian consensus had avoided. Damian, with his ps.-Dionysian stress on divine unity, may have seen this as "appropriately indicative of the ultimate priority of the single divine substance."

## Assessment

Damian "retrieved" two Cappadocian elements miaphysites had sidelined — the identification of hypostasis with property, and ousia as substrate — but his reading of Basil's abstract idiomata language is, for Zachhuber, "possible albeit ultimately implausible." His weakness: "no... good answer to the question of why the 'hypostatic properties' instead of individuating a substance are now themselves hypostases," producing the kind of *chorismos* the Peripatetics had criticized in Plato's forms. All three of his technical terms (differentia, natural property, hypostatic property) reappear in [[john-of-damascus|John of Damascus]]'s *Elementary Instruction*.
