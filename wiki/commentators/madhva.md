# Madhva

**Madhvacharya** (13th c.) is the founder of [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita Vedanta]] (dualism), the third of
the three classical Vedanta commentators alongside [[shankara|Shankara]] and [[ramanuja|Ramanuja]].

> **Note:** Madhva's [[brahma-sutras|Brahma-Sutra]] commentary has **no complete free English
> translation** (see *Needed Sources.md* → Outstanding); B.N.K. Sharma's scholarship is the practical
> substitute. This page is therefore written from secondary knowledge pending a source.

## Major Works

Commentaries (*bhashyas*) on the [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]], the [[bhagavad-gita|Gita]], the
principal [[upanishads|Upanishads]], and the *Anuvyakhyana* — together establishing Dvaita's realist
metaphysics.

## Characteristic Positions

The **fivefold eternal difference** (*pancha-bheda*): God–soul, God–matter, soul–soul, soul–matter,
matter–matter are all real and beginningless. **[[brahman|Brahman]] is Vishnu**, the sole independent
reality; souls and matter are eternally **dependent** and distinct. Souls form a **hierarchy**, and
Madhva uniquely affirms that some souls are **eternally bound** (a near-predestinarian view singular in
Indian thought). The path is **bhakti**; [[moksha|liberation]] is graded, blissful service — never
identity.

## Hermeneutical Method

Strict realism: the difference-texts are literal, the apparent identity-texts figurative — the exact
inverse of Shankara. Madhva is famous (and controversial) for bold grammatical re-parsings that turn
identity statements into statements of distinction.

## Controversies and Influence

The realist pole of [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the Advaita/Vishishtadvaita/Dvaita triad]]; accused
his opponents (especially Advaita) of disguised nihilism. His school became a sharp dialectical
tradition (Jayatirtha, Vyasatirtha) and inspired the Haridasa *bhakti* singers of Karnataka.
