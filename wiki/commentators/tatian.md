# Tatian

A Syrian-born [[justin-martyr|Justin Martyr]] disciple and Christian apologist, author of the *Oratio ad Graecos* (Address to the Greeks) and the **Diatessaron** — the influential single-narrative harmony of the four Gospels that remained the standard gospel text in the Syriac church for centuries. After Justin's death he founded the rigorist [Encratite] movement and was reckoned heterodox. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|Kelly]] and [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Pelikan]].

## Apologetic Positions

- **Immortality as gift, not nature.** Against the Greek doctrine of the soul's natural immortality, Tatian insisted: "the soul is not in itself immortal, O Greeks, but mortal. Yet it is possible for it not to die" — life beyond death being a gift through the resurrection, not a human possession. Pelikan makes this a key check on the Christian absorption of pagan immortality language (see [[bodily-resurrection]]).
- **The antiquity argument.** He sought to prove "our doctrines are older, not only than those of the Greeks, but than the invention of letters," by dating Moses (founder of "barbarian wisdom") before Homer — a staple of the [[biblical-criticism-antiquity|Christian claim to antiquity]].
- **The Diatessaron** harmonized the four Gospels into one continuous narrative; its later suppression in favour of the separated Gospels is a datum in [[nt-canon-formation|canon history]].
