# Thich Nhat Hanh

**Thich Nhat Hanh** (1926–2022) was a Vietnamese **[[zen|Thiền (Zen)]]** master, poet, and
peace activist — after the Dalai Lama the most globally recognized Buddhist teacher of his
era, and the popularizer of **"Engaged Buddhism"** and of **[[mindfulness|mindfulness]]** in
the West. He is the wiki's primary voice for a modern [[mahayana|Mahayana]]/Zen reading of
the early teaching.

## Biographical Context

Ordained a Zen monk in Vietnam at sixteen, "Thầy" (teacher) became a leading figure in
Buddhist responses to the Vietnam War, founding relief and social-work organizations and,
in 1966, the **Order of Interbeing** (*Tiếp Hiện*), a lay-and-monastic community with
Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings. Exiled from Vietnam for decades for his peace activism (Martin
Luther King Jr. nominated him for the Nobel Peace Prize in 1967), he established **Plum
Village** in France, which grew into the largest monastic community in the European
Buddhist world. He returned to Vietnam in his final years and died there in 2022.

## Hermeneutical Method

Thich Nhat Hanh reads the canon in the **Zen** mode — experiential, present-moment,
practice-first ("the Four Truths are not something to argue about... but to practice and
realize") — while drawing freely on both the [[tipitaka|Pali suttas]] and the
[[mahayana|Mahayana]] sutras and Āgamas. Three signatures:

1. **Interbeing.** He coined the English word **[[interbeing|interbeing]]** to render
   [[dependent-origination|dependent origination]] and [[sunyata|emptiness]]: nothing has a
   separate self; a sheet of paper "inter-is" with the cloud, the logger, the sun. This is
   his vernacular [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]].
2. **Engaged Buddhism.** Practice is inseparable from social and ecological action; "the
   teachings of the Buddha were not to escape from life." Mindfulness is to be
   *collective*, not merely private.
3. **Critical re-reading of received formulas.** He challenges the rote slogan "life is
   suffering," the claim that craving is the *sole* cause of [[dukkha|suffering]], and —
   most pointedly — the standard list of the [[three-marks-of-existence|three marks]],
   arguing the authentic **Three Dharma Seals** are impermanence, nonself, and
   **[[nirvana|nirvana]]**, not suffering (see
   [[three-dharma-seals-is-suffering-universal|the controversy]]).

## Influence

Through dozens of accessible books (*The Miracle of Mindfulness*, *Peace Is Every Step*,
*Old Path White Clouds*) and the global Plum Village network, Thich Nhat Hanh did more than
any other figure to bring **mindfulness** into secular Western life — including the clinical
mindfulness movement — and to model a socially and ecologically **engaged** Buddhism across
sectarian lines.
