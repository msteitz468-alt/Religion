# Robin Kirkpatrick

Modern translator and commentator of the [[divine-comedy|*Commedia*]]; Emeritus
Professor of Italian and English Literatures at Cambridge (Fellow of Robinson
College). Author of *Dante's Paradiso and the Limitations of Modern Criticism*
(1978), *Dante's Inferno: Difficulty and Dead Poetry* (1987), and *Dante: The
Divine Comedy* (Cambridge, 2004).

## Translation and Commentary Method

His verse translation (Penguin Classics Deluxe Edition) deliberately **abandons
end-rhyme**, arguing that Italian's vowel-endings and stressed–unstressed rhymes
give it an unfair advantage and that an obsession with rhyme distorts Dante's
precise meaning. Instead he reproduces the "aural density" of the original
through internal rhyme, assonance, and alliteration, while preserving Dante's
clearly-marked syntactic logic (one reason, he notes, Dante is "relatively easy
to translate" compared with Shakespeare). For similes he favours the formula
"Compare:…" to invite the reader into a "critical and collaborative venture."

## Interpretive Emphases

- Resists reducing the poem to **allegory** or to a "Gothic cathedral" *summa*;
  stresses Dante's individualism, productive doubt, and the primacy of the
  literal.
- Argues the [[paradiso|Paradiso]] is **collaborative and experimental**, not a
  series of magisterial expositions, and is still awaiting its due appreciation.
- Frames the poem ethically: written "to aid the world that lives all wrong,"
  engaging the reader's conscience and full faculties.
