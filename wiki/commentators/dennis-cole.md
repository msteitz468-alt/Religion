Dennis R. Cole is an American evangelical Old Testament scholar and archaeologist affiliated with New Orleans Baptist Theological Seminary. His commentary on [[numbers|Numbers]] in the New American Commentary (NAC) series (Broadman & Holman, 2000) is his principal contribution to the wiki's collection.

## Series Context

The NAC (New American Commentary) series is an explicitly evangelical, confessional commentary series committed to biblical inerrancy. Cole's volume is shaped by this framework: he defends Mosaic authorship and historicity throughout, engages critical scholarship respectfully but rejects its evolutionary dating assumptions.

## Hermeneutical Method

Cole employs **close literary reading combined with archaeological and ANE contextual analysis**. His primary methodological moves:

- **Structural analysis**: Cole proposes a seven-cycle (2-3-2) framework for Numbers rather than the traditional three-part geographical structure. This structural claim is the distinctive contribution of his commentary.
- **Archaeological grounding**: Cole uses archaeological evidence extensively to argue for the historical plausibility of Numbers' setting in the Late Bronze Age.
- **Typological reading**: Cole tracks typological connections between Numbers narratives and NT fulfillment, particularly the bronze serpent (Num 21 → John 3:14-15) and the Balaam oracle (Num 24:17 → Matt 2:2; Rev 22:16).
- **Rejection of source criticism**: Cole explicitly critiques the Documentary Hypothesis and Martin Noth's tradition-critical fragmentation of Numbers, arguing for the book's literary and theological unity.

## Characteristic Positions

**On Mosaic authorship**: Cole defends Mosaic origins following R. K. Harrison's conservative arguments. He cites Numbers 33:1-2 as explicit internal evidence of Moses' writing activity. He allows minor later scribal updating but rejects wholesale Priestly (P) redaction as a post-exilic imposition.

**On the census figures**: Cole defends the historicity of the large census figures (603,550 men) without adopting the *ʾelep*-as-"unit" emendation (Wenham). He connects the numbers to the divine promises to Abraham, reading them as evidence of covenant fulfillment.

**On the Balaam oracles**: Cole emphasizes the **reversal dynamic** — enemy divination turned to blessing — as evidence of YHWH's sovereignty over all prophetic speech, including that of pagan diviners. The narratives also introduce the messianic "star out of Jacob" oracle (24:17).

**On the seven-cycle structure**: Cole's structural proposal sets his commentary apart from predecessors. He argues the 2-3-2 pattern (two Sinai preparation cycles, three wilderness rebellion cycles, two conquest preparation cycles) reflects deliberate literary architecture, not haphazard editorial combination.

## Engagement with Jewish Interpretation

Cole's engagement with the Jewish interpretive tradition is minimal. He references *Bemidbar Rabbah*, Talmudic sources, and Targums at points, and cites Jacob Milgrom's JPS commentary as a dialogue partner — but medieval Jewish commentators are not systematically engaged. His primary interlocutors are modern critical scholars (Gray, Levine, Budd, Milgrom, Olson) and conservative evangelical scholars (Harrison, Allen).

## Key Scholars Engaged

Cole interacts most substantially with: Jacob Milgrom (comprehensive Jewish treatment; Cole's primary non-critical interlocutor), George B. Gray (source-critical ICC classic), Baruch Levine (contemporary source criticism), R. K. Harrison (conservative evangelical predecessor), Dennis Olson (census framework analysis Cole builds on), Mary Douglas (purity/anthropological approach; Cole critiques), Philip Budd (WBC source-critical analysis).
