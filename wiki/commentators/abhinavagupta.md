# Abhinavagupta

**Abhinavagupta** (c. 950–1016 CE) is the towering polymath of medieval Kashmir — the systematizer of
non-dual **[[kashmir-shaivism|Kashmir Śaivism]]** (Trika) and, in a separate sphere, the most important
theorist of **Sanskrit aesthetics**. He is one of very few thinkers whose work is foundational in two
unrelated fields at once.

## Biographical Context and Lineage

Born into a learned Kashmiri Brahmin family, Abhinavagupta studied under many teachers across
disciplines, insisting that **initiation and practice within a living mystical lineage** — not erudition
alone — are required to understand the tantric texts. His Śaiva descent runs from Vasugupta and the
*Śiva Sūtras* through Somānanda and **Utpaladeva** (founder of the Pratyabhijñā or "Recognition" school)
to his own teacher Lakṣmaṇagupta; for the Gita commentary he names his teacher **Bhūtirāja** and writes
at the request of disciples (especially **Lotaka**). His own foremost disciple, Kṣemarāja, carried the
tradition forward.

## Major Works

- ***Tantrāloka*** ("Light on the Tantras") and its digest ***Tantrasāra*** — the vast synthesis of
  Trika Śaiva theory and ritual.
- ***Īśvara-pratyabhijñā-vivṛti-vimarśinī*** and the *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa* — on the
  [[pratyabhijna|Pratyabhijñā]] ("Recognition") philosophy.
- ***Abhinavabhāratī*** (on Bharata's *Nāṭyaśāstra*) and the ***Locana*** (on Ānandavardhana's
  *Dhvanyāloka*) — the classics of **rasa** (aesthetic-emotion) theory, where aesthetic relish
  (*rasāsvāda*) is treated as a worldly taste of the bliss of consciousness.
- ***Gītārtha-saṃgraha*** — his commentary on the [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]; the wiki's ingested
  source (see [[marjanovic-abhinavagupta-gita|the source page]]).

## Characteristic Positions

Reality is **one consciousness, Śiva**, whose absolute freedom (*svātantrya*) manifests the entire
universe as his **real** self-expression — not as illusion. The self is already Śiva and need only
**recognize** (*[[pratyabhijna|pratyabhijñā]]*) this; bondage is self-forgetting (contraction), not a
real fall. Knowing (*dṛk*) and acting (*kriyā*) are the Lord's two inseparable powers — so for
Abhinavagupta knowledge and action are **non-different**, dissolving the Vedantic opposition between them
(see [[jnana-karma-samuccaya|jñāna-karma-samuccaya]]).

## Hermeneutical Method

Esoteric: Abhinavagupta comments **only on verses bearing a hidden meaning** (skipping the obvious),
calling his work an *artha-saṃgraha* ("collection of the [essential] meaning"), not a *bhāṣya*. He reads
the Gita's yoga and sacrifice language as veiled **tantric practice** — e.g. its breath-control verses
(4.29–30) as a guru–disciple initiation, its "sacrifice" as offering into the fire of one-pointedness
(citing the *Vijñāna Bhairava*). He claims the Gita's secret teaching is "**more esoteric than
Vedanta**" (*guhyāt vedāntād api guhyam*, 18.63). See [[allegorical-reading|esoteric/inner-meaning
reading]] across traditions.

## Influence

Abhinavagupta's synthesis is the high-water mark of Kashmir Śaivism; his aesthetics shaped all later
Indian literary theory; and his Gita commentary supplies the **fourth, Śaiva voice** beside the three
Vedanta readings (see [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the comparison]]).
