J. Andrew Dearman is an OT scholar at Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary, known particularly for his work on Hosea and on archaeological/epigraphical evidence related to the Mesha Inscription and Transjordanian history. His NICOT [[hosea|Hosea]] is the most detailed evangelical commentary on this difficult book.

## Hermeneutical Method

Dearman's approach is a **final-form literary-theological reading with historical sensitivity**.
He makes "acceptance of the final form of the text as the proper focus of attention" a decisive
methodological commitment, while insisting this "is not a rejection of historical analysis or
denial of literary growth in the Hosea tradition" — he accepts limited editorial updating
(Judah references, "David" in 3:5) but resists sweeping late-dating of restoration and covenant
passages. His stance toward redactors is **irenic**: he refuses to "draw lines in the sand"
affirming the prophet while denigrating the editors.

Two interpretive instruments are distinctive:
- **Cognitive-metaphor theory.** Dearman treats metaphor as "a way in which people think," not
  ornament, and consistently analyzes Hosea's images with **source-domain / target-domain**
  (vehicle/tenor) terminology. He argues Hosea "thinks metaphorically."
- **"Base texts" and narrative typology.** Following **Ray Van Leeuwen**, Dearman holds that
  Hosea reads Israel's present through inherited national traditions (the Exodus/wilderness, the
  Decalogue prologue and first two commandments, Exod 34:6–7, Baal-peor, Gibeah, Sodom/Admah) —
  "base texts" applied typologically. He frames Matthew's use of Hos 11:1 (Matt 2:15) as an
  *extension* of Hosea's own typological hermeneutic, not Hosea's original sense.

## Key Contributions

**Root metaphor of the household**: Dearman's central interpretive contribution — identifying YHWH as head of his household (*bayit*) as the single root metaphor organizing Hosea's diverse images. This holds together YHWH-as-husband, YHWH-as-father, Israel-as-wife, Israel-as-son, Israel-as-land/inheritance in a coherent conceptual structure.

**Literary-theological approach**: Final-form reading with historical sensitivity; acknowledges editorial history without being driven by source-critical reconstruction.

**Marriage metaphor's origins**: Documents Hosea as the earliest writing prophet to use the marriage metaphor for the covenant, making him the source of the tradition developed in Jeremiah 3, Ezekiel 16/23, Isaiah 54, and (indirectly) the Song of Songs.

**Hosea-Jeremiah relationship**: Thorough documentation of Hosea as Jeremiah's "spiritual father" — the most direct prophetic influence relationship in the OT. Dearman repeatedly calls **[[jeremiah|Jeremiah]] "Hosea's heir"/"an interpreter of Hosea"** (e.g. the geography-of-sacrifice charge, the wilderness-youth of 2:2, "drawn with *ḥesed*" in Jer 31:3, the defiled land of Jer 2:7, "return... I will heal your apostasies" in Jer 3:22).

## Characteristic Positions (attributed)

- **"House of YHWH" = Israel-as-household, not the temple** (8:1; 9:4; 9:15) — a load-bearing
  reading; he endorses and expands H. W. Wolff's "house of Yahweh" ≈ "house of Omri."
- **Institutional critique, not abolition.** Hosea attacks the corrupt *failures* of priesthood,
  monarchy, prophecy, and sacrifice — not the institutions as such (4:8; 6:6; 8:4). "I desire
  *ḥesed* and not sacrifice" (6:6) is "an absolute contrast... that presupposes nuanced
  commitments," a critique of overreliance on sacrifice, not its rejection.
- **Calf = baalized/syncretistic YHWH cult**, with aniconic Yahwism "older than and normative
  for" Hosea (8:5–6; 10:5; 13:2).
- **Covenant-marriage as a unique inner-Israelite development** — no ANE parallel; not borrowed
  from Canaanite fertility cult. Defends *bĕrît* in 6:7 and 8:1 as authentically Hosean against
  late-Deuteronomic redaction theories.
- **13:14 as threat, not promise** (rhetorical questions expecting "no"); **6:2 "third day"**
  allowing both healing and resurrection senses; **Jacob (ch. 12) as a dual mirror** for Israel.
- **Robust monolatry**: Hosea's prophecy is "a sustained application of the prologue and first
  two commandments of the Decalogue." Distinctive observation that, **unlike Amos, Hosea does not
  present YHWH as creator**.
