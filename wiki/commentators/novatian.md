# Novatian

A learned Roman presbyter and the first Roman theologian to write in Latin, author of *De Trinitate*. He became antipope in 251 as leader of the **rigorist** party that refused readmission to the *lapsi* (those who apostatized in the Decian persecution) — the origin of the [[novatianism|Novatianist]] schism, a doctrinally orthodox but disciplinarily severe movement. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. V, VIII.

## Trinitarian Theology

Novatian marks an advance on [[tertullian]]: he affirms the Son's distinct personal subsistence as **pre-temporal**, not merely economic. "Since the Father is always Father, He must always have had a Son"; the Son "received the beginning of His generation before all time." Yet his language remains strongly [[subordinationism|subordinationist]] — the Son is "subject to the Father, less than the Father," an inferiority springing from his derivative nature. On the Holy Spirit he recognised only the divine power active in the prophets and the Church, without clearly affirming the Spirit's personal subsistence. He read both [[adoptionism|adoptionism]] and [[modalism|modalism]] as misguided attempts to save the unity of God.

## The Novatianist Schism

On the readmission of the lapsed, Novatian led the severe party against Pope Cornelius. Cornelius criticised him for never having been "sealed" (confirmed) after his clinical baptism by affusion: "how could he have obtained the Holy Spirit?" — an early datum in the emergence of [[baptism|confirmation]] as a distinct rite.
