# Dante Alighieri (as poet-theologian)

This page treats Dante as a **theological and philosophical thinker** working
through poetry; for his biography and role as protagonist see the figure page
[[dante-alighieri|Dante Alighieri]].

## Hermeneutical Method

Dante engages the medieval [[allegorical-reading|fourfold sense]] (literal,
allegorical, moral/tropological, anagogical) but characteristically privileges
the **literal** level. In the *Convivio* he reads his own canzoni allegorically,
yet "tires of seeking a multiplicity of levels"; the disputed *Epistle to Can
Grande* applies fourfold exegesis to the opening of the [[paradiso|Paradiso]].
His method with all sources — classical, Scriptural, vernacular — is
**assimilation and experiment**: reinterpreting and combining texts to his own
ends, presenting himself as a syncretist who reconciles them as stages in the
progressive revelation of divine truth through history.

## Characteristic Positions

- **[[evil-as-privation|Evil as privation]]** of being (with [[augustine|Augustine]]
  and [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]): Satan as impotent anti-climax, parody of the
  [[trinity|Trinity]].
- **Aristotelian psychology**: the soul as the form of the body; the human as
  psychosomatic, not dualist. Aristotle is "the master of all those who think and
  know" (Inf. 4:131).
- **Rational diagnosis of [[sin|sin]]**: treachery, not lust or greed, as the
  gravest sin, because it destroys the rational and social bonds constitutive of
  humanity.
- **[[freedom|Free will]]** as the core of personality (Purg. 16–17).
- **Doubt as productive** of deeper understanding (Par. 4:124–32).
- **Providential Rome**: Rome as the divinely appointed agent of universal
  justice and authority (a political theology also in *De Monarchia*).

## Style

The vernacular "comic" style (median register, free to rise to rhetorical heights
or descend to vulgarism), the *terzina*, hendecasyllabic line, and a poetics
centred on **simile** ("the Descartes of simile," per Mandelstam) and on precise
observation ("a visual poet," per Eliot). Syntax is "the voice of intelligence";
Dante "relishes the word 'therefore.'"
