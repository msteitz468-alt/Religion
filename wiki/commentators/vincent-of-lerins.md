# Vincent of Lérins

A monk of the island monastery of Lérins (off the southern Gallic coast), author of the *Commonitorium* (c. 434), the most influential early codification of the criterion of catholic tradition. He is the source of the famous **Vincentian canon**: that catholic doctrine is "what has been believed everywhere, always and by all" (*quod ubique, quod semper, quod ab omnibus creditum est*). Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. II.

## The Vincentian Canon

Vincent presents a twofold bulwark of truth: "the authority of the divine law (i.e. the Bible) and the tradition of the Catholic Church." Scripture "is sufficient, and more than sufficient" in itself, but because it is susceptible of varied interpretation we must "have recourse to tradition" as the surest clue. Correct tradition is identified by three marks:

1. **universality** — confessed by the entire Church;
2. **antiquity** — shared by predecessors and Fathers;
3. **consent** — the agreed definitions of all or the majority of bishops and teachers.

Where a heretic invokes precedent, Vincent counsels preferring "the measured decision of a general council"; absent a council, one collates the views of representative Fathers who "have remained steadfast in the faith and communion of the Catholic Church" — i.e. the [[consensus-patrum|consensus of the Fathers]].

## Development of Doctrine

Vincent is not a pure conservative. He allows councils "to perfect and polish the traditional formulae," thereby declaring "not new doctrines, but old ones in new terms" (*non nova, sed nove*), and famously concedes an **organic development of doctrine** analogous to the growth of the human body from infancy to age — provided the development results in "not the least alteration to the original significance of the doctrine concerned." This anticipates the later (Newmanian) theme of doctrinal development.
