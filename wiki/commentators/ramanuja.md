# Ramanuja

**Ramanujacharya** (11th–12th c.) is the founding theologian of [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|
Vishishtadvaita Vedanta]] ("qualified non-dualism") and the most important figure of **Sri Vaishnavism**.
He fused the Sanskrit Vedanta tradition with the Tamil devotional (*bhakti*) movement of the **Alvars**,
producing a rigorous theistic alternative to [[advaita-vedanta|Shankara's]] non-dualism.

## Major Works

- The **Sri-Bhashya** — his great commentary on the [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]] (the wiki's
  ingested source); a sustained refutation of Advaita.
- The **Gita-Bhashya** (on the [[bhagavad-gita|Gita]]); the *Vedartha-Sangraha*; surrender hymns
  (*Saranagati Gadya*).

## Characteristic Positions

Reality is **one organic whole** in which souls (*chit*) and matter (*achit*) are the real **body** of
**[[brahman|Brahman]]** = the personal God **Vishnu/Narayana**, their inner controller (*antaryamin*).
Thus unity **and** real distinction: the self is inseparable from God but not identical to him.
**[[maya|Maya]] is God's real creative power, not illusion.** The path is **bhakti** and *prapatti*
(self-surrender); [[moksha|liberation]] is eternal loving communion, never absorption.

## Hermeneutical Method

Ramanuja's signature move is the **body-soul (*sarira-sariri*) relation**: it lets him read "that thou
art" as *inseparable dependence* rather than identity, harmonizing the Upanishads' identity-texts and
difference-texts without Advaita's two-truths device — which he attacks as making God and the world
unreal.

## Controversies and Influence

The classic rebuttal of Advaita's maya and *nirguna* Brahman; a middle term between Advaita and
[[dvaita-vedanta|Madhva's]] dualism (see [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the triad]]). His theology
shaped a vast devotional tradition and later Vaishnava thought.
