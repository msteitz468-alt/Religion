# Ramanuja

**Ramanujacharya** (11th–12th c.) is the founding theologian of [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|
Vishishtadvaita Vedanta]] ("qualified non-dualism") and the most important figure of **Sri Vaishnavism**.
He fused the Sanskrit Vedanta tradition with the Tamil devotional (*bhakti*) movement of the **Alvars**,
producing a rigorous theistic alternative to [[advaita-vedanta|Shankara's]] non-dualism.

## Major Works

- The **Śrī-Bhāṣya** — his great commentary on the [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]] (the wiki's
  ingested source, via [[george-thibaut|Thibaut]]); a sustained refutation of Advaita that opens with
  the *Mahāpūrvapakṣa* demolishing [[advaita-vedanta|Śaṅkara's]] preamble on [[adhyasa|superimposition]]
- The **Gita-Bhāṣya** (commentary on the [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gītā]])
- The **Vedārthasaṃgraha** (summary of the Vedāntic teaching from a Viśiṣṭādvaita perspective)
- Surrender hymns (*Śaraṇāgati Gadya*) and other devotional works

## Biographical Context

Ramanuja was born at Śrīperumbudūr (near Chennai), became a disciple of Yādavaprakāśa but broke with
him over Advaitic interpretations, and eventually became the head of the Śrī Vaiṣṇava community at
Śrīraṅgam. His dates span 120 years in tradition (disputed by scholars), reflecting his status as a
figure whose biography was hagiographically magnified. He organized the Sri Vaishnava community's
liturgical and theological life and is credited with composing the *Śrī-Bhāṣya* as a definitive
rebuttal of Advaita and grounding for theistic Vedānta.

## Hermeneutical Method

### The *Śarīra-Śarīrī* (*Body-Soul*) Relation

Ramanuja's signature hermeneutical move. He offers a formal definition:

> "A body is any substance which a conscious being is able to control and support for its own
> purposes, and which stands in an entirely subordinate relation to that being."

This allows him to:
1. Accept ALL Upanishadic texts at face value — both identity texts ("That thou art"; "the Self is
   Brahman") and difference texts (the soul as worshipper; individual souls' plurality)
2. Avoid Advaita's two-truths device entirely
3. Interpret "That thou art" (*tat tvam asi*) as *inseparable dependence*, not absolute identity

Under the body-soul relation: souls and matter are Brahman's real body — Brahman is their inner
Self and controller (*[[antaryami|antaryāmin]]*). The identity texts name the qualified unity (the
body is non-separable from its soul); the difference texts name the real distinction (controller and
controlled are genuinely distinct). See [[sarira-sariri|the body-soul relation]] (concept page).

### *Pariṇāmavāda* (Transformation Doctrine)

Ramanuja holds that Brahman genuinely transforms itself into the world (real causation), not merely
appears as it (Advaita's *vivartavāda*). The clay/pot analogy: clay really becomes a pot; the pot is
a real transformation, not an illusion. Brahman's creation is *pariṇāma* (modification) — a real
change of state while remaining the same substance. This makes the world genuinely real, not an
appearance or a lower truth.

### Sādhana and *Upāsanā* (*Dhyāna*)

Knowledge (*jñāna*) in Rāmānuja's system = **meditation (*upāsanā*/*dhyāna*)** = **repeated,
continued representation** of the object. The Sanskrit roots *vid*, *upas*, *dhyai* are used
interchangeably in the Upanishads — all mean the same meditative practice. "Knowing" Brahman is not
a one-time cognitive flash but a practice maintained **until death**. See [[upasana|Upāsanā]].

## Characteristic Positions

### The System: Reality is One Organic Whole

Reality has **three real categories**:
- **Brahman** (*cit* + *acit* as its body) = the personal God Vishnu/Nārāyaṇa; the inner Self of
  all; eternally free from all imperfections and possessing all blessed qualities
- **Souls (*cit*)** — many, distinct, atomic, conscious; each a real mode (*aṃśa/prakāra*) of Brahman
- **Matter (*acit*)** — non-sentient; the non-conscious dimension of Brahman's body; real, not illusory

Unity *and* real distinction: the system is called *vishishtādvaita* = non-dualism of the qualified
(whole) — one whole with internally real distinctions.

### Brahman is Personal and Saguna

[[brahman|Brahman]] = the personal God Vishnu/Nārāyaṇa:
- **Omniscient, omnipotent**, creator, sustainer, and dissolver of the universe
- **Saguna** (with qualities): possesses an infinite number of blessed attributes (*kalyāṇa-guṇas*)
- **Twofold character**: (1) freedom from all imperfection AND (2) possession of all blessed qualities;
  these are not in tension but are the two dimensions of the same perfect nature
- Ramanuja insists on the positive content of the *neti neti* ("not so, not so") formula — it denies
  not attributes themselves, but the *restriction* of Brahman to any finite set of attributes; positive
  descriptions follow directly in the text

### The *Antaryāmin* (*Inner Ruler*)

The *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* III,7 Antaryāmin-Brāhmaṇa is the key proof-text: Brahman dwells within every
entity — earth, water, fire, sky, sun, moon, and *within individual souls themselves* — ruling each
from within, yet remaining "the Immortal" untouched by what it inhabits. The repeated phrase
"that is thy Self, the inner Ruler, the Immortal" asserts real internal presence *and* real
transcendence simultaneously. See [[antaryami]].

### Souls: Knower, Agent, Atomic, Part

The individual soul is:
- **A real knower** (not merely apparent; Advaita's claim that the soul is pure consciousness is wrong —
  consciousness is the *mode* of the soul, not the whole of it)
- **A real agent** (actions are genuinely performed; the soul is not an inactive witness)
- **Atomic in size** (*aṇu*), residing in the heart, pervading the body through the **spread of
  consciousness** (as a lamp's light fills a room while the flame is in one spot)
- **Part of Brahman** (*aṃśa*) — a real mode/attribute, not a fragment broken off; the soul's relation
  to Brahman is as attribute to substance, inseparable yet real and distinct
- **Many, not one** — the plurality of souls is irreducible; liberation does not merge individual souls
  into a single Self

### *Māyā* as God's Real Creative Power

*Māyā* = Brahman's real creative power (*śakti*), not illusion. The world that *māyā* produces is
genuinely real, not an appearance superimposed on an undivided Brahman. This is the direct refutation
of Śaṅkara's central move.

### The Path: Bhakti, Upāsanā, Works

- **All three āśrama duties** remain obligatory; for householders, prescribed works (Agnihotra etc.)
  are prerequisites to knowledge — they please the Lord, who then grants the grace enabling *jñāna*
- **Knowledge has an independent fruit** (attaining Brahman, not merely assisting works) —
  against Jaimini's position
- **Bhakti** is the emotional colouring of all upāsanā; Ramanuja's path reaches its fruition in
  *prapatti* (self-surrender to the Lord's grace) as the ultimate form of devotion

### *Moksha*: Eternal Communion, Not Absorption

Liberation is **eternal loving communion with Brahman** — not absorption into a featureless One.
The released soul:
- Manifests its eternally existing true nature (obscured during bondage by *avidyā* and *karman*)
- Is conscious of itself as **non-divided** from Brahman ("I am Brahman, without any division")
- Is simultaneously a real distinct mode of Brahman (the body-soul relation persists; it cannot
  be otherwise, since the soul's mode-character is its essence, not a property of bondage)
- Creates by mere will; is self-ruler; can be with or without a body at pleasure
- Does NOT acquire Brahman's world-ruling power (creation, sustentation, dissolution) — this is
  the **exclusive** attribute of the Supreme Person
- Does NOT return to *saṃsāra* — Brahman's love for the devoted soul is the final guarantee

## Known Controversies

- **Against Advaita**: the *Mahāpūrvapakṣa* demolishes the coherence of *nirguṇa* Brahman and *avidyā*;
  a position recorded without adjudication. See [[brahman-atman-vedanta-schools|the controversy triad]]
  and the [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] page.
- **Against Sāṃkhya**: the *pradhāna* doctrine cannot account for directed cosmological production
- **Against Pāśupatas**: whose Brahman-as-purely-efficient-cause (not material cause) is refuted
- **For Pāñcarātra**: Ramanuja defends the *Pāñcarātra* Āgamas (including the *vyūha* doctrine) as
  authentic Vedic-grounded revelation — against critics who used them to discredit Vaiṣṇava orthodoxy

## Influence on Later Tradition

Through the *Śrī-Bhāṣya* and the Sri Vaishnava community (later split into Vaḍakalai/"Monkey" and
Tenkalai/"Cat" sub-schools over the role of effort in grace), Ramanuja established the dominant
framework for theistic Vedānta. His influence shaped all later Vaiṣṇava commentary, the theology of
bhakti movements across South and North India, and the explicit self-understanding of
[[iskcon|ISKCON]] and similar modern movements as heirs of the devotional tradition he systematized.
