# Plotinus

Founder of Neoplatonism (c. 204/5–270 CE) and teacher of [[porphyry|Porphyry]], who edited his
*Enneads*. In [[city-of-god|*City of God*]] **Books VIII–IX**, [[augustine|Augustine]] names him among
the greatest Platonists and treats him with marked respect — "Plotinus… enjoys the reputation of having
understood Plato better than any other of his disciples."

## Quoted by Augustine

- On providence and beauty: the divine care visible even in the splendor of flowers and leaves
  (*Ennead* III).
- On embodiment as mercy: "The Father in compassion made their bonds mortal" — which Augustine turns into
  an argument that the *mortal* body of man is a divine mercy, whereas the demons' *immortal* bodies seal
  an eternal misery.
- On the soul's true path: "Our way is, to become like God" — endorsed by Augustine as pointing to
  spiritual likeness, not physical ascent, as the soul's return, and used to rebuke the Platonists for
  then retreating to demonic mediation.

Augustine ranks Plotinus, with [[plato|Plato]], [[porphyry|Porphyry]], and [[apuleius|Apuleius]], among
the Platonists who glimpsed the immaterial God yet missed the one Mediator.
