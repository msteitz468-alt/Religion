# Hva-shang Moheyan

**Heshang Moheyan** (Chinese *Héshàng Móhēyǎn*; Tibetan **Hva-shang / Ha-shang Mahāyāna**) is the
Chinese **Chan** master who, in the Tibetan tradition, represents the **sudden, thought-free** path
defeated by [[kamalasila|Kamalaśīla]] at [[samye-debate|the Samyé debate]]. "Hva-shang" (a
transcription of *héshàng*, "monk") became in Tibetan scholasticism a byword for the quietist error.

## The Position Attributed to Him

As reported by his opponents (and by [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] in the [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim
Chenmo]] Vol. III): because *every* conceptual thought — good or bad — apprehends signs and binds
one to cyclic existence, the whole path is to **bring nothing to mind** and rest in non-conceptual
absorption; "those who think nothing and do nothing will be fully liberated." The graded practices
and analytical wisdom are, on this view, for the dull. Tsong-kha-pa preserves the vivid anecdote that
on reading the *Saṃdhinirmocana*'s plain statement that insight is *discerning wisdom*, Hva-shang
said "I don't know how this can be a sūtra!" and kicked it.

## Reception

The figure is known almost entirely through hostile sources. Tsong-kha-pa makes "the system of the
Chinese abbot Ha-shang" the recurring foil of his insight section, siding with
[[kamalasila|Kamalaśīla]]'s gradual–analytical path and his central thesis that
[[samatha-vipassana|insight]] requires active analysis. Whether the historical Moheyan held the
position as caricatured is a question for modern scholarship; in the wiki the **doctrinal foil** is
what the sources transmit. See [[samye-debate|the Samyé debate]] for the controversy and the
[[object-of-negation|over-negation]] link.
