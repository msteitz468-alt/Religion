# Hippolytus of Rome

The most learned member of the Roman church in the early third century, a disciple in the theological line of [[irenaeus]], biblical commentator, heresiologist, liturgist (the *Apostolic Tradition*), and antipope (in schism against Pope Callistus, whose laxity he denounced). He died a martyr c. 235. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. II, V, VI, VIII.

## Trinitarian Theology

Hippolytus is a representative of [[logos-christology|Logos theology]] and [[oikonomia|economic Trinitarianism]]. He explicitly uses the term *Logos endiathetos* (the immanent Word): "Though alone, He was multiple (*monos ōn polys ēn*), for He was not without His Word and His Wisdom, His Power and His Counsel." At creation God "engendered His Word"; at the incarnation there appeared "another" (*heteros*), a second *prosopon*. He guards monotheism: "when I speak of 'another', I do not mean two Gods, but as it were light from light… For there is only one Power." Judged by later standards his language has a [[subordinationism|subordinationist]] ring, and he was reluctant to call the Word "Son" in the full sense before the incarnation. He combated the modalists Noetus and [[sabellius|Sabellius]] (*Contra Noetum*, *Refutatio*), and regarded Pope Zephyrinus as "an out-and-out modalist."

## Christology and Soteriology

An exponent of [[spirit-christology|Spirit Christology]]; he was the first to introduce *hypostanai*/*hypostasis* into Christological discussion. He taught [[recapitulation]]: the Word was born of the Virgin "to restore and recapitulate in Himself the original Adam," conquering death by his death.

## Church and Sacraments

A rigorist ecclesiologist: the Church is "the holy society of those who live in righteousness," an earthly Eden from which backsliders are extruded — the basis of his clash with Callistus over the [[sacrament-of-penance|penitential discipline]]. He held the [[eucharist]] to be the "new sacrifice foretold by Malachi," "the oblation of the holy Church."
