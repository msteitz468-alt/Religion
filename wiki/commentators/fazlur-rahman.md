# Fazlur Rahman

**Fazlur Rahman Malik** (1919–1988) was a Pakistani-American philosopher of [[islam|Islam]]
and the most influential theorist of **Islamic neo-modernist hermeneutics** in the 20th
century. Trained at Punjab and Oxford (DPhil on Avicenna), he taught at Durham and McGill,
briefly directed Pakistan's **Central Institute of Islamic Research** under Ayub Khan — where
his reformist views provoked such opposition that he emigrated in 1968 — and spent his last
two decades at the **University of Chicago**, where he shaped a generation of scholars.

## Hermeneutical Method: The Double Movement

Rahman's central contribution, set out in *Islam and Modernity* (1982) and *Major Themes of
the Qur'an* (1980), is the **"double movement"** theory of interpretation:

1. **From the present to Qur'anic times** — understand a given injunction within its concrete
   seventh-century occasion, then abstract from it the **general moral principle** (the
   *ratio legis*, the underlying objective) it was meant to realize. Qur'anic rulings were
   "given expression relative to" their circumstances; the circumstances are the *particular*,
   the moral principle the *universal*.
2. **From Qur'anic times back to the present** — re-embody that principle in the changed
   circumstances of the present community.

This treats the Qur'an's moral values as "extra-historical, transcendental," recoverable from
behind the time-bound legal specifics — and it implies that a literal application that betrays
the underlying principle is a *misreading*. Rahman insisted, against the **atomistic** verse-
by-verse *tafsir* tradition, on reading the Qur'an as a **thematic, coherent whole** (the
program of *Major Themes*) — its world-view (*Weltanschauung*) governing the parts.

## Influence on the Wiki's Sources

Rahman is the acknowledged methodological predecessor of [[amina-wadud|Amina Wadud]], whose
[[wadud-quran-and-woman|*Qur'an and Woman*]] adopts the double movement wholesale and extends
his thematic holism into a "[[tawhid|hermeneutics of tawhid]]." Through Wadud, Barlas, and the
broader reform current, his method is the engine of [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] and
of *maqasid*-oriented reform more generally. His historicizing of the legal verses places him
on the reformist side of debates such as [[quran-4-34-male-authority|Q 4:34]] and is contested
by traditionalists who hold the rulings, not merely their objectives, to be binding.

## Characteristic Positions

- The Qur'an is a unified moral and religious world-view, not a law code to be mined verse by
  verse.
- Revelation is both entirely God's word and, in its *verbal* expression, bound up with the
  Prophet's historically situated experience — a position that drew accusations of denying the
  Qur'an's literal divine wording (the chief charge against him in Pakistan).
- *Sunna* is a "living, evolving" practice, not a fixed deposit; *ijtihad* must be reopened.
