# Cyprian of Carthage

Bishop of Carthage (248–258) and martyr under the Valerianic persecution, a leading figure of
Latin North African Christianity in the generation between [[tertullian|Tertullian]] and
[[augustine|Augustine]]. This page is anchored, so far, in his appearance in Augustine's
[[on-christian-doctrine|*De Doctrina Christiana*]] Book IV, where he is held up as a model of
Christian eloquence.

## In Augustine's *De Doctrina Christiana* (Book IV)

Augustine uses Cyprian as one of his two patristic exemplars (with [[ambrose|Ambrose]]) of the
three styles of [[christian-eloquence|Christian eloquence]] (Bk. IV, ch. 21):

- **Subdued/quiet style** (teaching/proving) — *Ad Caecilium* (Ep. 63), on the eucharistic cup
  (water vs. wine).
- **Temperate style** (delighting/praising) — *De habitu Virginum*, chs. vii and xviii, in
  praise of consecrated virgins.
- **Majestic style** (moving the will) — *De habitu Virginum*, ch. xii, denouncing the use of
  cosmetics.

Augustine also cites Cyprian's early letter *Ad Donatum* (Ep. 1) as a specimen of a **florid,
ornamented style that Cyprian later disciplined** once subject to Christian sobriety (Bk. IV,
ch. 14) — a contrast Augustine draws between youthful rhetorical excess and mature Christian
restraint.

Cyprian is named earlier (Bk. II) among the Christian writers — with Lactantius, Victorinus,
Hilary, and Optatus — who exemplify the [[spoils-of-egypt|appropriation of pagan learning]] for
Christian use.
