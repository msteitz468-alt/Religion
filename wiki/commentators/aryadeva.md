# Āryadeva

**Āryadeva** (c. 3rd c.) is the direct disciple of [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] and, with him, one of the
"fundamental" [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] authorities — "both the father and his spiritual son are
sources for the other Mādhyamikas." [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] ranks him in the authority chain
Nāgārjuna → Āryadeva → [[buddhapalita|Buddhapālita]] → [[candrakirti|Candrakīrti]] that culminates in
the [[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|Prāsaṅgika]] reading.

## Work and Positions (per the Lamrim Chenmo Vol. III)

His **Four Hundred Stanzas** (*Catuḥśataka*) — on which Candrakīrti wrote the *Catuḥśataka-ṭīkā* — is
cited repeatedly in the insight section for:

- **Ignorance as the seed of cyclic existence**: "The seed of worldly existence is a consciousness…";
  seeing that objects lack self ends that seed (for śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas
  alike).
- **The single door to peace**: the view of selflessness is "the one and only door"; "there is no
  second door to peace."
- **Dependent-arising entails no autonomy**: "That which arises dependently / Does not exist
  autonomously… Therefore, they have no self" — autonomy meaning what
  [[object-of-negation|emptiness negates]].
- **"See one, see all"**: "Who sees one thing is said to see everything; the emptiness of one thing is
  the emptiness of everything."
- **"No position to rebut"** — a verse the "no-thesis" Tibetans cite, which Tsong-kha-pa (via
  Candrakīrti) reads as denying only positions framed by *intrinsic character*.
