# Marius Victorinus

A celebrated Roman rhetorician and Neo-Platonist philosopher whose conversion (c. 355) [[augustine|Augustine]] recounts in the *Confessions*. His Trinitarian treatises (*Adversus Arium*) and hymns defended the Nicene *[[homoousion]]* in a philosophical idiom that influenced Augustine. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. X.

## Trinitarian Theology

Drawing on Plotinus (modified by Scripture), Victorinus held God to be essentially active (*esse* = *moveri*) and *tridynamos*, "possessing three powers — being, living, understanding" (*esse, vivere, intelligere*). The Father is divine essence as unconditioned, "prior to being" (*proōn*); the Son is the "form" by which the Godhead determines and reveals itself ("Word to eternal silence"); the Spirit is intelligence, "the link, or copula, between the Father and the Son." He preferred *tres subsistentiae* to *tres personae*, taught the mutual indwelling ("circuminsession") of the Persons, and is a documented influence on [[augustine|Augustine]]'s [[vestigium-trinitatis|psychological analogy]] of the Trinity.
