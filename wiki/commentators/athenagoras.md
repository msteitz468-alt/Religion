# Athenagoras of Athens

A Christian apologist styling himself a "philosopher of Athens," author of the *Legatio pro Christianis* (Embassy/Plea for the Christians), addressed to Marcus Aurelius, and a treatise *On the Resurrection of the Dead*. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|Kelly]] and [[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|Pelikan]].

## Contributions

- **Theory of inspiration.** Athenagoras gave the classic "instrument" image of prophetic [[inspiration-of-scripture|inspiration]]: the prophets uttered what they received "in ecstasy (*kat' ekstasin*), the Spirit breathing through them as a flautist breathes through his pipe" — a key statement of the ecstatic/possession model.
- **The Trinity in coordination.** He coordinated Father, Son, and Spirit, asking that Christians who "acknowledge God the Father, God the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and declare both Their power in union and Their distinction in order (*taxis*)," not be reckoned atheists — an early stage of [[trinity|Trinitarian]] articulation.
- **The resurrection.** Against the Greek doctrine of the soul's natural immortality, he argued the [[bodily-resurrection|resurrection of the body]] follows from the Creator's very purpose in making man, who is a body–soul composite destined for endless life.
