# Daniel C. Matt

Contemporary American scholar of Jewish mysticism, best known as the translator and annotator of the **Pritzker Edition** of the [[zohar|Zohar]] — the first complete scholarly English translation of the Zohar from a reconstructed critical Aramaic text. The edition (Stanford University Press, 12 volumes, 2004–2017; Matt translated the first nine, covering the Torah portions) is the standard scholarly English Zohar and the source for this wiki's Zohar pages.

---

## Biographical and Scholarly Context

Daniel Matt is the son of the Conservative rabbi and scholar Hershel Matt. He earned his doctorate at Brandeis University, writing on the early kabbalistic text *Sefer Mar'ot ha-Tsove'ot* of David ben Yehudah he-Ḥasid, and taught for over twenty years at the Graduate Theological Union in Berkeley, California. He has also taught at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and Stanford.

His major works:
- **The Zohar: Pritzker Edition** (2004–2017) — the monumental annotated translation, named for benefactor Margot Pritzker, who commissioned and underwrote the multi-decade project. Matt translated the first nine volumes, which comprise the main running Torah commentary (Genesis through Deuteronomy, the primary theosophic and theurgic parashah exegesis); Volume X (Midrash ha-Ne’lam, the earliest Zoharic stratum) and later volumes were translated by other scholars (e.g., Nathan Wolski). **Volumes 1–3 cover the whole of Genesis** (Vol 1: Be-Reshit–Lekh Lekha; Vol 2: the middle-Genesis parashot; Vol 3: Va-Yeshev–Va-Yeḥi), with **Volume 4** (ingested 2026-06-07) covering the first half of Exodus (Shemot through Yitro: enslavement, birth and call of Moses, plagues, deliverance, crossing of the Red Sea with revelation of the 72-letter Name, wilderness trials including Amalek, Jethro’s counsel, and the Sinai theophany / Ten Commandments). **Volume 5** (ingested 2026-06-07) continues with Parashat Mishpatim (civil and ritual laws, Exod 21–24) including the embedded *Sava de-Mishpatim* ("Old Man of Mishpatim" — first extensive Jewish treatment of gilgul/reincarnation, linked to levirate marriage, and the famous parable of Torah as a beautiful maiden gradually revealing herself), Parashat Terumah (beginning of Tabernacle instructions, Exod 25–27), and *Sifra di-Tsni’uta* (the "Book of Concealment"). The volume centers on the Dwelling (*mishkan*) as symbol of Shekhinah and the intimacy of "Kallat Moshe" (Bride of Moses). **Volume 6** (ingested 2026-06-07) covers Parashat Ki Tissa (the Golden Calf crisis, Exod 32, read as rejection of Shekhinah leading to Her departure; Moses intercedes by invoking the patriarchs), and Parashat Va-Yaqhel / Pequdei (the actual construction and completion of the Tabernacle under Bezalel; the glory of YHWH filling the Dwelling as the full indwelling of Shekhinah). **Volume 7** (ingested 2026-06-07) covers early-to-mid Leviticus: Parashat Va-Yiqra and Tsav (sacrificial offerings as sefirotic symbols, e.g. the olah/ascension offering as Shekhinah ascending to unite with the blessed Holy One), Shemini (inauguration of the sanctuary, Nadab and Abihu's death read as failure to include Shekhinah in divine contemplation and to imitate the divine male-female union through marriage), Tazria/Metsora (purity laws as maintaining sefirotic balance and allowing Shekhinah's presence), and Aḥarei Mot (Yom Kippur and related holiness). The volume emphasizes theurgy: human ritual action (sacrifice, purity, priesthood) stimulates and maintains the upper divine union (*zivvuga qaddisha*). (The wiki has now ingested **Vols 1–7**.) **Volume 8** (ingested 2026-06-07) covers the conclusion of Leviticus—Parashat Qedoshim (“You shall be holy” read as ethical and sexual sanctification that generates and imitates the *zivvuga qaddisha*; the adjacency of the licentiousness laws to Qedoshim teaches that vigilance in these matters produces holiness, and conjugal union should be timed to midnight when the blessed Holy One appears in the Garden of Eden and supernal holiness is aroused), Emor (priestly conduct and festivals), Behar (sabbatical and Jubilee as socio-economic expressions of holiness and release), and Behuqqotai (blessings/curses as outcomes of alignment with the divine flow); plus the opening of Numbers with Parashat Naso (nazirite vow, priestly blessing, *sotah*; the *Idra Rabba* theosophic assembly; Torah study for its own sake is better than all offerings and sacrifices—it tears up decrees and reconciles the blessed Holy One). The volume continues the theurgical emphasis: study, ethics, and ritual maintain Shekhinah and the upper union in the wilderness. (The wiki has now ingested **Vols 1–8**.) **Volume 9** (ingested 2026-06-07) completes Matt’s main running commentary on the Torah: the remainder of Numbers (beginning with Parashat Shelach Lekha—the spies/scouting of Canaan read as a mystical drama in which the bodies of the generation die in the desert while the souls resemble “denizens of the Garden”; subsequent parashot on rebellion, leadership, and preparations for the land) and the entirety of Deuteronomy (Moses’ farewell addresses as theurgic covenant renewal and transmission of the divine mystery; the visionary Rav Metivta section with its journey to the Garden of Eden, revelations about the afterlife, the tripartite soul, and secrets of the righteous; the colorful Yanuqa/Child narrative as a model of surprising wisdom and intergenerational teaching; theurgic readings of the Shema, the land as sefirotic inheritance, blessings/curses, and Moses’ death/ascent). With Volume 9, the Pritzker Edition Volumes 1–9 (translated by Matt) cover the Zohar’s full Torah commentary from Genesis through Deuteronomy. (The wiki has now ingested **Vols 1–9**; the main Torah commentary is complete.)
- **Zohar: The Book of Enlightenment** (1983) — an earlier annotated anthology of selected passages in the Classics of Western Spirituality series
- **The Essential Kabbalah** (1995) — a popular thematic anthology of Kabbalistic texts
- **God and the Big Bang** (1996) — a constructive work relating Kabbalah and modern cosmology

## Method and Significance of the Pritzker Edition

Matt's edition is significant for several reasons documented on the [[zohar|Zohar]] text page:

1. **A reconstructed critical text**: There was no reliable critical edition of the Zohar's Aramaic. Working from roughly twenty of the best manuscripts (14th–16th centuries) against the standard printed editions (Mantua 1558–60, Cremona 1559–60), Matt constructed an eclectic Aramaic text — published separately online (the "Pritzker Aramaic text") — restoring readings the printers had altered, smoothed, or censored. His translation thus often reads passages unavailable in earlier English versions (Sperling-Simon, Tishby).

2. **Dense scholarly annotation**: Each page carries extensive footnotes tracing the Zohar's biblical and rabbinic sources, parallel kabbalistic passages, and the sefirotic symbolism encoded in each image — making the cryptic text navigable for the first time in English.

3. **Attention to literary artistry**: Following his teacher's generation (Scholem) and especially the work of **Yehuda Liebes** and **Arthur Green** (who wrote the introduction to Volume I), Matt presents the Zohar not as a chaotic anthology but as a work of profound literary and mystical creativity.

Matt operates as a **historical-critical and philological** scholar (see the modern critical frameworks in the wiki). He treats the Zohar as a medieval Castilian composition (the work of [[moses-de-leon|Moses de León]] and his circle), not as an ancient work of [[shimon-bar-yochai|Shim'on bar Yoḥai]] — i.e., he stands on the modern side of the [[zohar-authorship|authorship controversy]]. At the same time, his translation is animated by deep sympathy for the text's spiritual vision, a combination characteristic of the Berkeley/GTU school of Jewish mysticism scholarship.

## Relation to the Scholarly Tradition

Matt's work stands in the lineage of modern academic Kabbalah scholarship:
- **Gershom Scholem** (1897–1982) — founder of the academic study of Jewish mysticism; established the de León authorship thesis on rigorous philological grounds
- **Isaiah Tishby** — produced *The Wisdom of the Zohar*, a thematic anthology with translation and commentary that Matt's edition supersedes in completeness
- **Yehuda Liebes** — argued for the Zohar as the product of a creative circle and illuminated its literary mythopoesis
- **Arthur Green** — wrote the scholarly introduction to Pritzker Volume I, situating the Zohar historically and theologically
