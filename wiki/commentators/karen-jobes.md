# Karen H. Jobes

Karen H. Jobes (Ph.D., Westminster Theological Seminary, Philadelphia) is an American evangelical
New Testament scholar and Septuagint specialist. At the time of her BECNT *1 Peter* (2005) she was
associate professor of New Testament at Westmont College (Santa Barbara, CA); she later became the
Gerald F. Hawthorne Professor of New Testament Greek and Exegesis at Wheaton College Graduate School
(the same institution as [[douglas-moo|Douglas Moo]]). She is the wiki's first solo-authored woman
commentator, and one of relatively few women to write a major exegetical commentary in a flagship
evangelical series.

She is best known in two areas: **Septuagint studies** — she co-authored, with her doctoral mentor
Moisés Silva, the widely used *Invitation to the Septuagint* — and the **General/Catholic Epistles**
(commentaries on 1 Peter and on 1–3 John, plus an earlier NIVAC volume on Esther). Her LXX expertise
decisively shapes her NT exegesis.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Grammatical-historical with a Septuagint-first lens.** Jobes's signature methodological commitment
is that the **Septuagint (LXX) is the primary scriptural and literary context** of 1 Peter. She
argues Peter does not "proof-text" the OT but imports the *context* of the LXX passages he cites and
applies them to his readers. Interpreting 1 Peter therefore means reading it against the LXX as Peter
and his readers would have known it (e.g., the saturating use of Ps. 33 LXX [34 ET] in the letter).

**Syntax criticism / bilingual interference.** Her most technically distinctive contribution
(developed in an excursus, "The Syntax of 1 Peter: How Good Is the Greek?"). Against the near-universal
claim that 1 Peter's Greek is "too good" for a Galilean fisherman, Jobes analyzes the *syntax* — which,
unlike deliberate rhetorical ornamentation, "flows almost subconsciously" — and finds **bilingual
interference** consistent with a Semitic-language author for whom Greek was a second language. In four
areas (use of prepositions; the genitive personal pronoun; position of attributive adjectives; the
dative with *en*) the author falls short of native writers like Polybius and even Josephus. Conclusion:
the Greek is no obstacle to Petrine authorship, and the author was probably *not* a Greek/Latin-native
Roman or Asian elder.

**Sociohistorical reconstruction.** She engages the sociological readings (Elliott, Balch, Volf) and
proposes her own historical scenario (the colonization theory, below), while resisting reductive
single-cause models of the letter's social setting.

**Evangelical-critical.** Like the BECNT series generally, she interacts seriously with critical
scholarship (Achtemeier, Elliott, Goppelt) without treating its conclusions as foregone, and defends
traditional authorship where she finds the critical case inconclusive.

## Characteristic Positions (1 Peter, BECNT 2005)

**1. The Roman colonization theory.** Her central new proposal. The recipients were not indigenous
Asian converts evangelized in situ (for which there is no historical evidence) but Christians
converted *elsewhere — probably Rome —* who were *displaced to Asia Minor*, plausibly through the
colonization policies of the emperor **Claudius** (who, perhaps uniquely, founded colonies in all
five regions named in 1:1) and/or the Claudian expulsion of Jews/Christians from Rome (Suetonius,
*Claudius* 25; Acts 18:2). This explains: how Christianity reached these remote regions quickly; the
absence of any named founding evangelist; the readers' deep LXX literacy; Peter's association with
them (via Rome) despite never having visited; and the *literal* force of "foreigners and resident
aliens" beneath its spiritual meaning. See [[1-peter-authorship|1 Peter: Authorship, Date, and the
Colonization Theory]].

**2. Petrine authorship and an early date.** She argues the four classic arguments for pseudonymity
(the Greek; the persecution; the church structure; dependence on Paul) and the "Babylon" code-word
are all inconclusive, and that epistolary pseudonymity in *personal correspondence* was not the
accepted, victimless convention often assumed (forged letters were censured when detected: 3
Corinthians, Galen). A date within Peter's lifetime — early 50s or mid-60s — becomes plausible.

**3. The "foreigner" motif as both literal and spiritual.** Against the consensus that *parepidēmoi*
/ *paroikoi* (1:1; 2:11) are *merely* metaphor, and against Elliott's *merely* literal-socioeconomic
reading, Jobes holds the terms had a real historical referent (displaced colonists/expellees) whose
power Peter transmutes into the spiritual truth that all Christians are resident aliens in a hostile
world. See [[christian-exile-diaspora|Christian Exile / Diaspora Identity]].

**4. 3:18–22: ascension victory, not descent into hell.** Christ's "going and preaching" to "the
spirits in prison" is his *ascension proclamation of triumph* over the disobedient fallen angels of
Noah's day (1 Enoch background), framed by the Noah–baptism typology — not a *descensus*, and not
(against Grudem) Christ preaching through Noah. See [[spirits-in-prison-1-peter-3|the controversy
page]].

**5. 4:6: no postmortem second chance.** "The gospel preached to the dead" means those now dead who
heard it while alive; she severs it from 3:19 and rejects postmortem conversion.

**6. Peter's creative, early Christology.** 1 Peter is not derivative of Paul; its theology is
independently creative and "antiquated" in the sense of standing close to the crucifixion — most
notably the **original identification of Jesus with the Isaiah 53 Suffering Servant** (2:21–25),
exegeting Jesus' death in light of Isa 53 rather than the reverse. See [[servant-songs|Servant
Songs]].

## Relationship to Other Commentators in the Wiki

- **Within BECNT**: she joins [[thomas-schreiner|Schreiner]] (Romans), [[douglas-moo|Moo]]
  (Galatians), and [[darrell-bock|Bock]] (Acts) as a BECNT contributor; her volume is the series'
  entry on 1 Peter. She later became Moo's colleague at Wheaton.
- **Septuagint method**: her LXX-first approach contrasts with the OT-template biblical theology of
  [[roy-ciampa-brian-rosner|Ciampa–Rosner]] (1 Corinthians) — both privilege the OT, but Jobes works
  specifically from the *Greek* OT's wording and context.
- **General Epistles**: alongside [[douglas-moo|Moo]] (James), [[gareth-lee-cockerill|Cockerill]]
  (Hebrews), and [[i-howard-marshall|Marshall]]/[[colin-kruse|Kruse]] (Johannine Epistles), she
  expands the wiki's coverage of the non-Pauline letters.
