# Asaṅga

**Asaṅga** (c. 4th c.) is the founder of the **[[yogacara|Yogācāra]]** ("mind-only" / Cittamātra)
school and, with [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]], one of the "two great trailblazers" (*shing rta chen po*)
between whom [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] divides the whole Mahāyāna: Nāgārjuna carries the lineage of
the **profound** view (emptiness), Asaṅga the lineage of **vast** conduct and, per the
[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]] colophon, the path "blazed by the two great trailblazers."

## Role in the Lamrim Chenmo (Vol. III)

Asaṅga is the principal authority for the **[[samatha-vipassana|serenity]]** section. His **Śrāvaka
Levels** (*Śrāvakabhūmi*, part of the *Yogācārabhūmi*) supplies the architecture Tsong-kha-pa
follows: the **nine mental states**, the **six forces** and **four attentions**, the objects of
meditation, the analysis of **pliancy** (*śin sbyangs*) as the dividing line of accomplished
serenity, and the **seven attentions** for freedom from the desire realm. Tsong-kha-pa explicitly
privileges Asaṅga's account over rival explanations ("although there are alternative explanations, do
not rely on them"). Also cited: his **Abhidharmasamuccaya** (*Compendium of Knowledge*, on pliancy,
mindfulness, and the divisions of insight), **Bodhisattvabhūmi**, and the *Viniścayasaṃgrahaṇī*.

In the **insight** section, Asaṅga's Yogācāra is named as one of the **two legitimate Mahāyāna
views** (the three-natures system — imaginary/contingent/perfectly real), distinct from but, on the
practice of sustaining insight, agreeing with Nāgārjuna's. Tsong-kha-pa recommends Ratnākaraśānti's
*Instructions for the Perfection of Wisdom* as the clearest presentation of Asaṅga's system.
