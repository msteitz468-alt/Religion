M. Daniel Carroll R. (Rodas, b. 1953) is a Latino Old Testament scholar whose work on Amos and social ethics bridges evangelical philology and Two-Thirds World realities. The “R.” stands for Rodas, his Guatemalan mother’s (or family’s) Hispanic surname in Spanish naming convention — he self-identifies as bilingual/bicultural, son of a Guatemalan mother and an American father. He taught OT at **El Seminario Teológico Centroamericano (SETECA)** in Guatemala City during Central American civil wars (Guatemala’s 1960–1996 war left up to ~200,000 dead), later at **Denver Seminary**, and at **Wheaton College** (as noted in the NICOT preface). Series editor Robert L. Hubbard Jr. invited the NICOT Amos volume years before its 2020 publication.

## Hermeneutical Method

Carroll’s NICOT *Amos* pursues a **primarily synchronic / final-form / canonical** reading against a plausible eighth-century backdrop. He concentrates on “the received, or canonical, text,” holding that “the book of Amos is a literary creation that goes back largely to the prophet himself,” with only minimal early additions (perhaps the superscription; third-person Amaziah narrative penned by another under the prophet’s influence).

**Four reasons** he gives for preferring synchronic over multi-century redaction as the *governing* method:
1. Diachronic layered conjecture is highly speculative even when practitioners admit uncertainty (Wolff, Jeremias, Hadjiev, Eidevall, etc.).
2. Form/redaction foundations are questioned — especially **pan-Deuteronomism** and weak evidence for a Dtr redaction of Amos.
3. Historical fit of most material in the first half of the eighth century.
4. Thematic coherence and literary art better explain “disjunctions” than accretions over centuries.

He is **sympathetic** to confessional/pastoral arguments for final form but insists they “do not constitute the rationale” for his approach — historical plausibility + literary coherence do.

**Distinctive instruments:**
- **Poetics and rhetorical criticism / speech-act theory** (Möller et al.) — deliberative aim; doom announcements can *function* as warnings aiming at repentance.
- **Interpretive anthropology (Geertz)** — culture as webs of significance; religion authorizes the social construction of reality (functionally a sacred canopy over the unjust order).
- **Latin American / Two-Thirds World horizon** — *Contexts for Amos* (1992) under John Rogerson at Sheffield forged an alternative to unsatisfying liberation-theology options while remaining justice-oriented; ongoing work on OT social ethics and immigration.

## Key Contributions

**Principled synchronic defense.** Carroll’s systematic engagement with Wolff’s six-stage theory, Jeremias’s four editions, Schart’s Twelve-linked stages, and Persian-period scribal theories is among the fullest in evangelical Amos commentary — without ignoring critical literature.

**Reversal of theological traditions.** Documenting how Amos turns exodus, election, cult, Day of Yahweh, and Divine Warrior traditions **against** Israel as weapons of indictment.

**Cult as world-authorization.** Judgment must begin at the altar (Bethel) because sanctuaries sanctify the unjust social imaginary; primary target of 5:21–23 is the **YHWH cult**, not foreign gods alone (with 5:26 expanding to other deities).

**Person of Amos as person of means.** *nōqēd* / *bôqēr* / *bôlēs šiqmîm* → not the romantic poor shepherd.

**ʾănāk as tin (7:7–8).** Soft-metal wall = fragile fortresses — not the medieval English “plumb line.”

**Eighth-century authenticity of 9:11–15.** Against Wellhausen’s “roses and lavender” late appendix: hope is extension of reversal, not contradiction; booth of David = frail Davidic kingdom/dynasty.

**Acts 15 and Amos 9:11–12.** Detailed intro treatment of James’s citation — Gentile inclusion under exalted Davidic rule, with midrashic liberty on the LXX-leaning text.

**Global / ethical appropriation.** MLK on 5:24; Latin American interface; explicit rejection of racist Cushite readings of 9:7 (Wellhausen, Harper, et al.).

## Characteristic Positions (attributed)

- **Date:** ministry ca. **760 / 765–750**; terminus ad quem ~750; Assyria unnamed.
- **Structure:** four primary book sections; nested chiasms in 5:1–17 (center: “Yahweh is his name,” 5:8d), 5:18–27 (center: **5:24** human justice jussive), 6:1–14 (center: death scene 6:9–10).
- **OAN:** comprehensive moral universe; *lex talionis*; completed book is not pure “rhetoric of entrapment.”
- **Election paradox (3:2):** unique *ydʿ* is ground of punishment, not insurance.
- **Repentance and doom:** not mutually exclusive (speech-act); “perhaps” (5:15) for remnant of Joseph; restoration after judgment (9:11–15).
- **Sieve (9:9):** corporate house of Israel exiled; 9:10 targets boastful sinners.
- **Cush (9:7):** distant peoples — not racial inferiority.
- **Book of the Twelve:** discrete superscriptions → read as separate compositions first; intertextual dialogue is second-level (against elaborate Twelve redaction as primary key).

## Biographical and Pastoral Horizon

Author’s Preface: civil-war Guatemala formed his reading of Amos’s violence, poverty, corruption, and racism. Unsatisfied with liberation theology as the primary Latin American option, he used Amos as a test case for an alternative approach (*Contexts for Amos*, 1992). He remains a “concerned evangelical” who wants the volume to serve “a world needing a moral compass and an authentic prophetic word.”
