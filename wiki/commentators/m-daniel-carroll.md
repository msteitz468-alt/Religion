M. Daniel Carroll R. (b. 1953) is Professor of Old Testament at Denver Seminary. The "R." stands for Rodas, his Guatemalan father's surname — a biographical fact that informs his distinctive attention to minority and Two-Thirds World perspectives on the biblical text. He has published extensively on Amos and social justice, including an earlier monograph (*Contexts for Amos*, 1992) explicitly addressing Latin American contexts.

## Key Contributions

**Principled synchronic approach**: Carroll's most methodologically significant move — a systematic, well-reasoned defense of final-form reading against the dominant redaction-critical tradition in Amos scholarship. His critique of Wolff's six-stage theory, Jeremias's four editions, and Persian-period scribal theories is the most thorough in the commentary.

**Social context using interpretive anthropology**: Rather than simple class analysis, Carroll uses Clifford Geertz's cultural theory to explain why the cult was the target of prophetic attack: it was the "sacred canopy" sanctifying the unjust social order. Corruption was not limited to elites but endemic across the society.

**The reversal strategy**: Documenting how Amos systematically takes every cherished Israelite theological tradition (exodus, covenant, Day of Yahweh, cult) and turns it against Israel as a weapon of indictment.

**Global perspective**: Carroll's own Latin American background informs his reading; he is one of the first NICOT commentators to explicitly engage with liberation theology, feminist criticism, and ideological readings — not capitulating to them, but engaging critically.

**Acts 15 and Amos 9:11-12**: Detailed treatment of James's citation of Amos at the Jerusalem Council — the "fallen booth of David" being rebuilt as the basis for Gentile inclusion.
