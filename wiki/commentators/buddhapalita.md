# Buddhapālita

**Buddhapālita** (c. early 6th c.) is the commentator on [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s
[[mulamadhyamakakarika|Mūlamadhyamakakārikā]] whose method of refutation by *consequence* seeded the
[[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|Svātantrika/Prāsaṅgika split]]. [[candrakirti|Candrakīrti]]
judged that Buddhapālita "had fully elucidated what the noble Nāgārjuna intended" and based his own
commentary on Buddhapālita's system; [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]] in turn names Buddhapālita and
Candrakīrti together as the authorities he follows for Nāgārjuna's meaning.

## Work and Method

His **Commentary on the Fundamental Treatise** (*Buddhapālita-mūlamadhyamaka-vṛtti*) negated the
four kinds of production (from self, other, both, neither) by drawing out the absurd consequences
(*prasaṅga*) of the opponents' own positions, rather than asserting autonomous counter-syllogisms.
**[[bhavaviveka|Bhāvaviveka]]** attacked this as powerless to establish a thesis; Candrakīrti's
defense of Buddhapālita is the birth of the Prāsaṅgika position. (Tsong-kha-pa notes it is not
obvious Buddhapālita himself consciously set up a "Prāsaṅgika system" — the label is retrospective.)

## Positions Attributed in the Lamrim Chenmo (Vol. III)

- **Existence vs. essential existence.** Time, causes, and effects cannot exist essentially, yet "are
  established as dependent designations" — the distinction that keeps Madhyamaka from nihilism.
- The **barren-woman's-son** analogy: the awareness that the self does not exist does not think "his
  ears don't exist," yet it eliminates reifying conceptions of what belongs to the self.
- The **"I have nothing / give me that nothing"** example: one can cure those who reify things, but
  not those who reify emptiness itself as a real thing.
- Three arguments against the self being intrinsically **one with the aggregates**; and the
  demonstration of pervasion **using what the opponent accepts** (earth perceived wet via water,
  etc.) — the model for the Prāsaṅgika consequence-becomes-inference method.
