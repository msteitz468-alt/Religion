# Aristotle

Aristotle of Stagira (384–322 BCE), "the Philosopher" (*al-faylasuf*) of the medieval
Islamic and Jewish traditions, is the authority whose physics and metaphysics frame the whole
debate of [[maimonides|Maimonides]]'s [[guide-for-the-perplexed|Guide for the Perplexed]]. He
is known to the medievals largely through the Arabic Peripatetics [[al-farabi|al-Farabi]] and
[[avicenna|Avicenna]] and the commentators (Themistius, Alexander of Aphrodisias).

## Reception in the Guide

Maimonides treats Aristotle as demonstratively reliable for the **sublunary** world (physics
"below the sphere of the moon") but holds that his claims about the **eternity of the world**
and the supralunary causes are *not* demonstrated — only persuasive. The Guide's Part II opens
with twenty-five Aristotelian **propositions** Maimonides grants as proven, withholding only the
twenty-sixth (the world's pre-eternity). This bounded acceptance of Aristotle is the hinge of
his reconciliation of philosophy with Torah. See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
