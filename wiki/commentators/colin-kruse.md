# Colin G. Kruse

Australian evangelical NT scholar. Senior Research Fellow at Melbourne School of Theology. Known for the capacity to assimilate complex exegetical debates and present them with clarity, evenhandedness, and fidelity to the text. Series editor D.A. Carson describes Kruse's work as "sure-footed and reasoned, clear and remarkably concise without being simplistic or condescending."

## Primary Work Ingested

**Colin G. Kruse, *The Letters of John*, Pillar New Testament Commentary (PNTC), Eerdmans (US) / Apollos/IVP (UK), 2000 (2nd ed. 2020).**

The second edition was substantially revised and updated in light of two decades of scholarship since the first edition. The commentary is based on the NIV 2011. Kruse added contextual explanations for each major section and explicit statements of theological content.

Series context: Pillar New Testament Commentary (PNTC) — evangelical commentary series edited by D.A. Carson, Eerdmans. "Rigorous exegesis and exposition, with an eye alert both to biblical theology and to the contemporary relevance of the Bible." Designed for "serious pastors and teachers" who want scholarship without being buried in it. Other PNTC volumes in the wiki include [[joel-green|Green]] on Luke and [[karen-jobes|Jobes]] on 1 Peter.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Framework**: Evangelical historical-grammatical exegesis with attention to rhetorical structure. Kruse explicitly identifies a central text (1 John 3:23: "And this is his command: to believe in the name of his Son, Jesus Christ, and to love one another as he commanded us") as the key to the entire corpus — encapsulating the two governing demands: right belief and practical love.

**Scenario-based historical reconstruction**: Kruse opens with a detailed "scenario" — a narrative reconstruction of the events behind the letters. Working hypothesis: the Gospel of John in early form existed first; the secession followed; 1 John (circular) was written first to address the crisis theologically; 2 John and 3 John followed as targeted responses to a specific church and an individual (Gaius).

**The Johannine community**: A group of loosely related churches in fellowship with the author's community, probably in and around Ephesus. Kruse resists the more theoretically elaborate constructions of the "Johannine community" model (Martyn, Brown) and treats the community simply as a set of interconnected churches, not a hermetically sealed separatist movement.

**Gospel-Epistle relationship**: Very close; same or near-same author. Key caveat: concepts carried over from the Gospel (light, darkness, truth, love, *paraklētos*) are used with different emphases in the letters — the exegete must not assume one-to-one equivalence. Example: *paraklētos* in the Gospel = the Holy Spirit; in 1 John 2:1 = Jesus as advocate before the Father.

**Authorship**: Kruse leans toward apostolic authorship as the most faithful reading of the internal evidence. The eyewitness language of 1:1–4 is literal. External testimony (Irenaeus, Tertullian, Dionysius of Alexandria) is unanimous in ascribing 1 John to the apostle John. The Papias problem (two Johns?) is real but not decisive. Jerome's notice that 2 and 3 John were attributed by some to "the elder" (distinguished from the apostle) may mean only that the apostle used the self-designation "the elder."

**Excursuses**: 24 excursuses on disputed topics make this the most analytically detailed commentary on the letters in English. Covered: sense perception language; "from the beginning"; *koinōnia*; first-person forms; light and darkness; truth; *hilasmos*; "children/fathers/young men"; antichrist; *chrisma* (Spirit or Word?); teaching authority of the church; God's "seed"; sinless perfectionism; the Spirit's role; *monogenēs*; the Son's preexistence; God's invisibility; the Savior of the world; witness; eternal life; sins that lead and do not lead to death; bases of assurance; hospitality; love and hate in the Christian community.

## Characteristic Positions

- **1 John 3:23 as central text**: right belief and love are the two inseparable demands of the Johannine gospel, stated in their most compressed form at this verse.
- **Secessionist teaching**: the secessionists denied (1) that Jesus is the Christ/Son of God come in the flesh; (2) the salvific necessity of Jesus's death; (3) obligation to obey God's commands; (4) importance of brotherly love. They claimed (1) sinlessness; (2) direct Spirit-given knowledge superior to received apostolic tradition.
- **"Water and blood" (5:6)**: anti-Cerinthian polemic. The secessionists denied that Jesus came "by blood," i.e., that his death was necessary for salvation. "He did not come by water only" targets a view like Cerinthus's that the divine Christ was present at baptism but absent from the crucifixion.
- **Sinlessness paradox**: Same resolution as Marshall. 1:8/10 addresses the secessionist claim to sinlessness; 3:6/9 addresses the ongoing, habitual character of sin incompatible with being born of God. Individual acts of sin remain real (2:1).
- **Diotrephes**: ecclesiastical problem, not doctrinal heretic. No theological charges are brought against him. His behavior (rejecting the elder's authority, refusing missionaries, expelling those who receive them) reflects the early development of local episcopal authority seeking autonomy from the wider Johannine network.
- **Johannine community and the seven churches of Asia**: Kruse suggests the churches of the Johannine community may overlap with the seven churches addressed in Revelation 2–3; the parallels in theme (false teaching, love for one another, testing of spirits, perseverance) are extensive.
- **Propitiation (*hilasmos*)**: favors propitiation over expiation (Dodd's preferred reading). God's righteousness and wrath are real categories for the author; the cross addresses them.

## Excursus Conclusions (PNTC)

The 24 excursuses are the volume's distinctive contribution; each argues a lexical or
theological thesis by surveying LXX + NT usage, declining to let word-study alone decide, then
letting the letter's context settle the point. Kruse's conclusions on the load-bearing ones
(all attributed to Kruse):

- **Language of sense perception (1:1; Exc. 1)**: the "we" of 1:1 are **genuine eyewitnesses**;
  Kruse rebuts Brown's "School of tradition-bearers" reading, showing each of Brown's alleged
  metaphorical parallels (Tacitus, 2 Pet 1:18, Polycarp, Irenaeus, Gregory Nazianzen, Amos) fails
  as a precedent for reading 1:1 non-literally.
- **"From the beginning" (*ap' archēs*; Exc. 2)**: in 1 John this usually points to **the
  incarnate** Word, not (as in the Gospel prologue) the pre-creation Logos — Kruse distinguishes
  1 John's *ap' archēs* from the Gospel's *en archē* while conceding echoes may imply identity.
- **"To be without sin" (1:8)**: **not** a claim to a sinless nature/freedom from the sin
  principle, but a claim to **not being guilty of committed sins** (probably: since conversion
  and reception of the anointing).
- **Propitiation, God-initiated (*hilasmos*; Exc. 7)**: neither expiation nor propitiation can
  be ruled out lexically; the **context** of 2:2 supports **propitiation** — but never in pagan
  terms, since 4:10 makes God himself the provider of the sacrifice. A mediating position against
  pure expiation (Dodd).
- **Antichrist (Exc. 9)**: the author **probably coined the term**; its background is the
  end-time God-opposing figure of **Jewish apocalyptic**; uniquely in 1–2 John the "antichrists"
  are **former members of the community** (2:19). See [[antichrist|Antichrist]].
- **Chrisma = the Holy Spirit (2:20; Exc. 10)**, against de la Potterie's "word of God" reading.
- **"Children / fathers / young men" (2:12–14; Exc. 8)**: **two age groups, not three levels of
  spiritual maturity** — "children" is used for all readers throughout the letter.
- **God's "seed" (3:9; Exc. 12)** = **the Holy Spirit** — a "most daring metaphor" of God
  begetting — not Christ and not the word.
- ***anomia* (3:4)** does **not** mean breaking the Mosaic law (which is wholly absent from
  1 John); with de la Potterie, it names the sinner's **rebellious alignment with the devil** —
  the resolution Kruse gives the sinless-perfection tension of 3:6, 9 (the impossible sin is
  *anomia*/rebellion, barred because God's "seed" abides).
- ***monogenēs* (4:9; Exc. 15)** = "**one and only**," not "only begotten"; the stress is on the
  Son's uniqueness, not on being "begotten."
- **"Water and blood" (5:6)**: "by water" = **Jesus's baptizing ministry** (not merely his own
  baptism); "by blood" = his **atoning death**. The polemic targets a secessionist
  baptizing-ministry-only Christology that denied the reality and saving necessity of the death;
  Kruse treats a precise **Cerinthian** identification as attractive but not exact (cf. the
  "anti-Cerinthian polemic" bullet above, which he would qualify).
- **Sins that do / do not lead to death (5:16–17; Exc. 21)**: decided by **who commits them** —
  the "brother or sister" commits sin *not* leading to death, so the sin leading to death is the
  **unbeliever's / secessionist's** denial of Christ come in the flesh; believers cannot commit
  it. Kruse holds the author "does not contemplate apostasy" by true believers.
- **"He is the true God" (5:20)**: the nearer antecedent is **Jesus Christ**; Kruse (with
  Schnackenburg) reads it as a climactic confession of Jesus's full identity with God.
- **Bases of assurance (Exc. 22)**: the seven grounds are **objective, not subjective** — right
  belief, righteous living, and love, not Spirit-experience or inner feeling.
- ***ekklektē kyria* (2 John 1)** = **a local church and its members**, not an individual woman
  ("Electa"/"Kyria"); the "sister… chosen by God" (v. 13) is the elder's own church.
- **3:19–22** read as continuation of the **charity** theme of 3:17 (persuading a reluctant
  heart to give generously), **not** a digression on conscience/assurance — *kardia* is not a
  synonym for *syneidēsis* and *peithō* means "persuade," not "reassure."
- **Rhetoric (with Watson)**: 1 John is **epideictic** (reinforcing adherence via amplification,
  not proving a thesis), 2 John **deliberative**, 3 John **epideictic** — hence Kruse declines to
  impose a linear-argument outline on 1 John.

## Relation to Other Commentators

- **Marshall (NICNT, 1978)**: [[i-howard-marshall|Marshall]] is the elder of the two ingested commentators. Kruse cites him frequently and shares his basic orientation (evangelical, apostolic authorship, literal eyewitness reading). Kruse is more detailed in historical reconstruction, provides more extensive excursuses, and reflects 20 more years of scholarship.
- **Brown, Schnackenburg, Bultmann**: the three Continental and American scholars whose work dominates the secondary literature. Kruse engages all three critically; he is sympathetic to Schnackenburg's exegetical rigor, skeptical of Bultmann's source theories, and opposed to Brown's Johannine-community theories where they overreach.
- **Smalley, Strecker, Lieu**: major interlocutors in the 2000 edition, now supplemented by Jobes (ZECNT, 2014), Parsenios (PCNT, 2014), and others in the second edition.
