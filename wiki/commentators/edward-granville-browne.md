# Edward Granville Browne

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from lines 8153–8193 unless noted.*

## Biographical Context

Edward Granville Browne (1862–1926) was an English orientalist who wrote extensively on the Babi and Bahá'í religions. He was Sir Thomas Adam's Professor of Arabic at the University of Cambridge from 1902, and is well known for his pioneering work on Persian literature and his support for the Iranian Constitutional Revolution of 1905–9 (lines 8155–8162).

He first made contact with Bahá'ís and Azalis during his visit to Iran in 1887–8 (described in *A Year Amongst the Persians*, 1893). In 1890 he visited both Subh-i-Azal in Cyprus and [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] at [[bahji|Bahjí]]. His well-known description of his first meeting with Bahá'u'lláh appears in *A Traveller's Narrative* (TN xxxix–xli) (lines 8163–8170). He described Bahá'u'lláh as "a wondrous and venerable figure" whose face he "could neither describe nor forget" and whose piercing eyes "seemed to read one's very soul" (cited in lines 6662–6668).

## Major Works on the Babi–Bahá'í Tradition

Browne collected a large number of manuscripts, publishing some and summarizing others (line 8171–8172). His major publications were:

1. Text and translation of 'Abdu'l-Bahá's *Traveller's Narrative*, with extensive notes (1891) (line 8174–8176).
2. Translation of *The Ta'ríkh-i-Jadíd* or *New History of Mírzá 'Alí Muḥammad the Báb*, with extensive notes (1893) (lines 8177–8180).
3. Persian text of the *Kitáb-i-Nuqtatu'l-Káf* with a lengthy English introduction (1910) (lines 8181–8184).
4. *Materials for the Study of the Bá'bí Religion* (1918) (lines 8184–8186).
5. Additional published materials on the Faith appear in *Browne, Selections*.

## Hermeneutical Position and Controversies

His position on the Bahá'í–Azali split is the subject of considerable controversy (line 8190). Smith notes that his major works on the subject date from 1889–1910 (line 5157). Although his work is recognized as foundational to academic Babi and Bahá'í studies, his later alignment with Azali materials and sources has been criticized by Bahá'í scholars. His encounter with Bahá'u'lláh in 1890 is the only known account by a Western observer of a meeting with the prophet-founder of the Bahá'í Faith.
