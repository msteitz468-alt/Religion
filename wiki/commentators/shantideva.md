# Shantideva

An 8th-century Indian [[mahayana|Mahayana]] monk of **Nalanda**, in the Madhyamaka tradition, and the
author of the most beloved poetic manual of the [[bodhisattva|bodhisattva]] path.

## Major Work — the Bodhicaryavatara

The **[[bodhicaryavatara|Bodhicaryāvatāra]]** ("Guide to the Bodhisattva Way of Life") is a verse
treatise in ten chapters on cultivating **[[bodhicitta|bodhicitta]]** (the Awakening Mind) and
practicing the six perfections (*pāramitās*). Its chapters move from arousing bodhicitta, through the
perfections of forbearance, vigour, and meditation — culminating in the
**[[exchange-of-self-and-other|equalizing and exchanging of self and other]]** (ch. 8) — to its famous
ninth chapter on **wisdom** (*prajñā*), a concise [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] argument for
[[sunyata|emptiness]], and a closing dedication of merit. It is treasured across Mahayana and
especially [[vajrayana|Tibetan Buddhism]], where it is a core study text (the Dalai Lama teaches from
it regularly). His companion anthology, the **Śikṣāsamuccaya** ("Compendium of Training"), gathers
and arranges scriptural passages on the same path.

## Hermeneutical Method

Shantideva is less a verse-by-verse commentator than a **systematizer-in-verse** of the bodhisattva
teaching drawn from the Mahayana sutras. His ninth chapter stands in [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]]'s
**Prāsaṅgika [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** lineage, applying the [[neyartha-nitartha|nitartha]]
(definitive) reading of emptiness — including the **[[sunyata#emptiness-of-emptiness|emptiness of
emptiness]]** and the [[two-truths|two truths]] — against substantialist (realist, Sāṃkhya, and
Yogācāra) opponents. His ethical argument for altruism (ch. 8) is grounded directly in
[[anatta|nonself]]: with no real "I," self-preference has no rational basis.

## Influence

The Bodhicaryāvatāra is one of the most studied and recited texts in Tibetan Buddhism; its verses on
patience (ch. 6) and on exchanging self for other (ch. 8) are the scriptural backbone of *lojong*
(mind-training) and *tonglen*.
