# Shantideva

An 8th-century Indian [[mahayana|Mahayana]] monk of **Nalanda**, in the Madhyamaka tradition, and the
author of the most beloved poetic manual of the [[bodhisattva|bodhisattva]] path.

## Major Work — the Bodhicaryavatara

The **Bodhicaryavatara** ("Guide to the Bodhisattva Way of Life") is a verse treatise on cultivating
*bodhicitta* (the awakening-mind) and practicing the six perfections (*paramitas*). Its chapters move
from arousing bodhicitta, through the perfections of patience and meditation, to its famous ninth
chapter on **wisdom** (*prajna*) — a concise Madhyamaka argument for [[sunyata|emptiness]] — and a
closing dedication of merit. It is treasured across Mahayana and especially [[vajrayana|Tibetan
Buddhism]], where it is a core study text (the Dalai Lama teaches from it regularly).

## Hermeneutical Method

Shantideva is less a verse-by-verse commentator than a **systematizer-in-verse** of the bodhisattva
teaching drawn from the Mahayana sutras; his companion anthology, the *Sikshasamuccaya*, gathers and
arranges scriptural passages. His ninth chapter applies the [[neyartha-nitartha|nitartha]] (definitive)
reading of emptiness against substantialist opponents.

## Influence

The Bodhicaryavatara is one of the most studied and recited texts in Tibetan Buddhism; its verses on
patience and on exchanging self for other are central to *lojong* (mind-training).
