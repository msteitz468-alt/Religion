# Boethius

Anicius Manlius Severinus Boethius (c. 477–524) was a Roman senator, philosopher, and theologian
whose translations of and commentaries on Aristotelian logic, his theological *opuscula sacra*,
and his *Consolation of Philosophy* (written awaiting execution) transmitted much of classical
philosophy to the Latin Middle Ages. He is a constant authority in [[thomas-aquinas|Aquinas]]'s
[[summa-theologica|*Summa Theologiae*]].

## Positions cited in the Summa (Prima Pars)

- **Definition of eternity** (*Consolation* V): "the simultaneously-whole and perfect possession of
  interminable life" (*interminabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio*) — adopted and
  expounded by Aquinas at *ST* I, Q10, A1. See [[eternity-of-the-world]].
- **Definition of "person"** (*Contra Eutychen*): "an individual substance of a rational nature"
  (*naturae rationalis individua substantia*) — the basis of Aquinas's treatment of the divine
  persons at *ST* I, Q29, A1.
- **Being and goodness / participation** (*De Hebdomadibus*): the distinction between what a thing
  *is* and *that it is*, and the doctrine that all things but God are good by participation — used
  throughout *ST* I, Q3–Q6. See [[divine-simplicity]].
