# Hilary of Poitiers

Bishop of Poitiers, "the Athanasius of the West," the leading Latin defender of Nicene orthodoxy. His exile in Asia Minor (356–359) gave him first-hand knowledge of the Eastern debate, which he mediated to the West in *De Synodis*. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. X, XII, XIII, XIV.

## Trinitarian Theology

Hilary insisted on identity of substance: "if considered as Father and Son the Persons are two and can properly be designated as 'like', the substance which They both possess, and are, is one and indivisible" (*Unum sunt, non unione personae sed substantiae unitate*). In *De Synodis* (359) he interpreted *[[homoousion]]* charitably and even allowed *homoiousios* — provided it meant "perfect equality, which strictly entailed unity of nature" — urging rapprochement between Nicenes and Homoeousians.

## Christology and Soteriology

He taught two natures in one Person, the incarnation an *exinanitio* (self-emptying) involving "no diminution of the Word's omnipotence," though his stress on Christ's *corpus coeleste* raised against him a charge of latent [[docetism]]. On the atonement Kelly counts him "one of the pioneers of the theology of satisfaction": Christ "offered Himself to the death of the accursed in order to abolish the curse of the Law… by the oblation of his holy and perfect sacrifice."

## Ayres on Hilary (Nicaea and Its Legacy)

Lewis Ayres ([[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its Legacy*]], 2004) makes Hilary the leading
Latin **[[pro-nicene-theology|pro-Nicene]] precursor** — one who "show[s] many of the features of
these later theologies but not all." Returning from his Eastern exile c. 360, he became
"standard-bearer for the pro-Nicene cause," shaping the Council of Paris and travelling with
Eusebius of Vercelli to promote it; Ayres notes Hilary claims to have **first heard the Nicene
creed recited publicly** only at Milan — evidence that the creed's text was barely known in the
West before the 350s. His *De synodis* (358–9) defended the orthodoxy of the
[[homoiousians|Homoiousians]] to Western bishops, reading their "likeness" as "perfect equality"
(*Synod.* 27.73) — a rapprochement parallel to [[athanasius|Athanasius's]]. His theology is
"deeply indebted to earlier Latin theology," grounding shared essence in the Son's perfect birth
("by the perfect birth of a living nature," *De trin.* 7.31); Book 7 shows Eastern influence in
arguing from continuity of *operation* to identity of nature. Ayres flags Hilary's terminological
fluidity (he never speaks of "three persons"; uses *substantia* for both unity and the persons)
and cautions that **Tertullian's *natura*/*persona* vocabulary was not yet a Latin given**. His
pneumatology is "clear about the function of the Spirit… and deeply austere about the place of
the Spirit in the Trinity itself."
