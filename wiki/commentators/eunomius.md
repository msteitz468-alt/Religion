# Eunomius of Cyzicus

Eunomius (c. 335 – c. 393), bishop of Cyzicus and disciple of Aëtius, was the leading theologian of the **anomoian** (ἀνόμοιος, "unlike") or **heteroousian** ("of different substance") movement — the radical "neo-Arian" wing of the fourth-century Trinitarian controversy. Though condemned, his sharply argued system was, in Johannes Zachhuber's account ([[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]]), the decisive catalyst for [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|Cappadocian philosophy]]: [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil]] and [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]] forged some of their most seminal ideas through polemical engagement with his writings, so that his thought "exerted considerable albeit unacknowledged influence on the cast of the Cappadocian intellectual system."

## Doctrine

Eunomius and Aëtius accepted that the first principle is *ousia*, but argued that because of its **absolute simplicity** the first *ousia* could not "beget" or "engender" a second *ousia* like itself. Drawing on long-standing reservations about the language of divine generation, they held that:

- The only acceptable paradigm for the first, simple *ousia* was **creation**: it produced the second principle through its **activity** (*energeia*), itself simple.
- The "ingenerate" first *ousia* and "the generate" (τὸ γέννημα, its only product) are therefore **unlike as to substance** — hence *anomoian* / *heteroousian*. Their closeness is grounded only in activity: "unlike according to ousia, like according to activity."
- The divine essence is **knowable** and is captured by the name "**ingenerate**" (*agennētos*): the term names the very substance of God, not merely a relation. (This claim — that we can know the essence of God — is the deep target of the Cappadocian and Nyssan emphasis on divine incomprehensibility.)

### The Predication Argument

Eunomius pressed a **binary logic of predication**: terms used of Father and Son in Scripture and tradition must be either univocal — in which case they would imply full equality and even render the Son "ingenerate" — or **equivocal (homonyms)**. Since (he held) the Father's pre-eminence is non-negotiable (John 14:28, "the Father is greater than I"), such terms must be equivocal. He taunted the Origenist "derivative" tradition that *its* logic must collapse into the full equality of the persons.

Zachhuber notes the irony that **Athanasius** independently deployed the same binary (reject *homoousios* and you may as well say the Son is wholly "unlike"). But it was **Basil's** response that was decisive: Basil accepted the binary yet drew the opposite conclusion — identical, **univocal** predicates said of Father and Son indicate the *absence* of subordination — and then needed particularizing properties (*idiōmata*) to distinguish the now co-ordinate persons. The whole [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|abstract account]] grew from this move.

## Major Work

- **Apology** (*Apologia*) — the one major writing surviving largely in full; the primary witness to anomoian doctrine. (Also a later *Apology for the Apology*, known mainly through Gregory of Nyssa's refutation.)

## Reception

Condemned as a "neo-Arian"/heteroousian; refuted at length by Basil (*Against Eunomius*, 3 books) and Gregory of Nyssa (*Against Eunomius*, 12 books), and opposed by Gregory of Nazianzus in the Theological Orations. The anomoians were among the targets of the settlement ratified at the Council of Constantinople (381). See [[arianism|Arianism]] for the broader movement.

## Ayres on Eunomius (Nicaea and Its Legacy)

Lewis Ayres ([[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its Legacy*]], 2004) places Eunomius (with his
teacher [[aetius|Aetius]]) at the head of the **[[heteroousians|Heteroousian]]** trajectory and
resists the label "neo-Arian": there are "significant differences from Arius," Eunomius makes "no
claim on Arius' legacy," and the Cappadocians do not present him as "Arius redivivus" (the
genealogy through Lucian of Antioch is a construction of the later Heteroousian historian
Philostorgius). Eunomius's theology turns on a causal hierarchy — **essence → activity →
product** — and the doctrine that the divine names (above all *agennētos*, "unbegotten") reveal
the very essence of God; since "unbegotten" cannot apply to the begotten Son, the Son is "unlike"
the Father in essence. He still uses the [[homoians|Homoian]] formula "like the Father according
to the Scriptures" even while pushing beyond it. Michel Barnes finds third-century antecedents
(Dionysius of Alexandria; [[eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]]) for the essence/activity
distinction. Eunomius's challenge provoked [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil's]] decisive
[[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|ousia/idiomata]] and [[divine-simplicity|simplicity]] arguments —
a foundation of [[pro-nicene-theology|pro-Nicene theology]].
