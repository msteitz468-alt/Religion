# Joel Hecker

Contemporary scholar of Jewish mysticism and Kabbalah, best known in this context as the translator and annotator of **Volume 11** of the Pritzker Edition of the [[zohar|Zohar]] — *Midrash ha-Ne’lam and Zoharic Compositions* (Stanford University Press, 2019). This volume presents a range of Zoharic genres beyond the main running Torah commentary, including additional strata of the *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* (on the Megillot: Song of Songs, Ruth, and Lamentations) and the independent “Zoharic Compositions” (notably the *Idra Zuta* or “Small Assembly,” *Matnitin*, Toseftot, and related theosophic units).

---

## Biographical and Scholarly Context

Joel Hecker is a leading academic specializing in medieval Jewish mysticism, with particular expertise in the literary and theosophic dimensions of the Zohar. His work on the Pritzker Edition focuses on the non-running-commentary sections of the Zoharic corpus — the independent homilies, assemblies, and concealed midrashim that constitute some of the most esoteric and influential material in the tradition. Unlike Daniel C. Matt’s volumes (the main parashah commentary, I–IX) or Nathan Wolski’s treatment of the Genesis-focused *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* (Vol. X), Hecker’s volume showcases the diversity of genres within what is often called the “Zoharic library.”

## Midrash ha-Ne’lam and Zoharic Compositions in the Pritzker Edition (Vol. XI)

Hecker’s translation and annotation make accessible a variety of Zoharic texts that reflect the range of genres in the early Zoharic corpus. Many of these sections first appeared in print under the title *Zohar Ḥadash* (“New Zohar”), a designation Hecker notes is misleading: “this title is misleading, however, in that the volume comprises some of the earliest Zoharic material.”

Key characteristics and contents (as presented in the volume):

- **Additional Midrash ha-Ne’lam on the Megillot**: Beyond the Genesis material in Vol. X, this volume includes concealed midrash on Song of Songs (a short but dense unit), Ruth, and Lamentations (Eikhah). These readings emphasize hidden dimensions of exile, redemption, the soul, angelic praise, and divine glory. Sample from *Midrash ha-Ne’lam on Ruth*: “In the days when the judges judged … (Ruth 1:1). The rabbis explained the verse *When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy* (Job 38:7). Who are the morning stars? Holy angels ruling by day. Who are the sons of God? Angels ruling by night. … Everything that the blessed Holy One created, He created only for His glory, as is said: *Everyone who is called by My name, whom I created for My glory* (Isaiah 43:7).”

- **Zoharic Compositions**: Independent theosophic units that stand apart from verse-by-verse Torah commentary. These include *Matnitin* (“Teachings”), Toseftot (supplements), a *Zohar on Song of Songs*, and above all the *Idra Zuta* (“Small Assembly”). These texts often take the form of mystical assemblies or concentrated discourses among Rabbi Shim’on bar Yoḥai and select Companions, revealing the most recondite aspects of the Godhead, the sefirotic structure in its dynamic and erotic dimensions, and the theurgic power of mystical speech and presence itself.

- **The *Idra Zuta* (Small Assembly)**: The culminating and most dramatic unit — the account of Rashbi’s deathbed discourse. In this “small assembly” of the most intimate circle, the Holy Lamp (Botsina Qaddisha) discloses ultimate secrets; his soul departs in the midst of the revelation. The *Idra Zuta* is widely regarded as one of the pinnacles of Zoharic theosophy and literature, a performative event in which the mystical fraternity enacts and reveals cosmic processes.

- **Hermeneutical and literary style**: “Concealed midrash” and assembly discourse — more dramatic and concentrated than the running commentary, yet sharing the core Sod-level concern with the inner life of the divine, the soul, and the theurgic efficacy of Torah study and mystical praxis. Hecker’s apparatus situates these units within the evolution of the Zohar, underscoring that the corpus contains multiple voices and strata from its earliest phases.

This volume thus complements the main Torah commentary (Vols. I–IX) and the Genesis *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* (Vol. X) by displaying the full generic and chronological range of Zoharic literature, highlighting both its “earliest” layers and its most intensely theosophic expressions.

## Contributions to Volume XII (Zoharic Compositions)

Hecker also translated and annotated the majority of the units in Volume XII of the Pritzker Edition (*Zoharic Compositions*, with Nathan Wolski). The volume carries a joint Introduction by Wolski and Hecker. Hecker handled Raza de-Razin, Sitrei Otiyyot, Qav ha-Middah, Merkevet Yeḥezqel, Zohar al ha-Parashot, and the passages (mis)labeled as Tosefta (Wolski covered the Heikhalot Be-Reshit, Heikhalot Pequdei, and Piqqudin). 

These units extend the concentrated theosophic register of the *Idra Zuta* and related compositions from Vol. XI: *Raza de-Razin* develops Zoharic physiognomy (with its distinctive pietistic emphasis on the mutability of bodily features through *teshuvah*); *Sitrei Otiyyot* explores the letters of the Tetragrammaton as generative of the sefirot (as “chariots”); *Qav ha-Middah* presents the luminous Line of Measure emanating from Ein Sof that structures the sefirot with scale, light, and color. The volume as a whole reinforces Hecker’s framing (already prominent in Vol. XI) that much of the material printed as *Zohar Ḥadash* (“New Zohar”) belongs to the earliest strata of the corpus. See [[zohar|The Zohar]] (Vol. XII section), [[raza-de-razin|Raza de-Razin]], and [[heikhalot|Heikhalot]] (for the units Wolski translated).

## Relation to the Broader Zohar and Wiki

Vol. XI deepens the wiki’s portrait of the Zohar as a developing, multi-layered corpus rather than a single uniform commentary. The additional *Midrash ha-Ne’lam* on Ruth, Lamentations, and Song of Songs extends the soul-centered, concealed exegesis to texts of exile and love, resonating with Shekhinah and *zivvuga qaddisha* themes already tracked in the main commentary and Vol. X. The Zoharic Compositions, especially the *Idra Zuta*, provide the dramatic literary and theological climax for the figure of [[shimon-bar-yochai|Rabbi Shim’on bar Yoḥai]] (his death as supreme revelation) and for the theurgic role of the Companions (*Ḥavrayya*) as a mystical circle whose discourse itself sustains and reveals the divine.

Hecker’s critical presentation (across Vols. XI and XII) reinforces the hermeneutical tracking of Sod/PaRDeS and the distinction between the running parashah theosophy/theurgy and the “assembly” or concealed-midrashic modes. It also supplies the scholarly English access to units long recognized (in Hebrew editions and later tradition) as among the most potent in the entire Zoharic literature. His work on the Compositions in both volumes completes (with Wolski) the non-running strata of the Pritzker Edition in the wiki.
