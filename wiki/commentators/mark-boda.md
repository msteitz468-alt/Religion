Mark J. Boda is Professor of OT at McMaster Divinity College (Hamilton, Ontario) and one of the world's leading specialists in postexilic prophetic literature. He has published extensively on Zechariah, Haggai, penitential prayer in the OT, and post-exilic community formation. His NICOT Zechariah (2016) is the most technically detailed commentary in the wiki — 864 pages covering a book of 14 chapters.

## Key Contributions

**Compositional history of Zechariah**: Boda's most technically sophisticated contribution — a three-level analysis of how chs. 1-8 came together (original vision/oracle collections; supplementary materials; editorial integration) plus mapping the two oracle collections in chs. 9-14. This nuanced compositional approach neither fragments the book into irrecoverable sources nor ignores the evidence of growth.

**Inner-biblical allusion mapping**: One of Boda's central methodological concerns — Zechariah is one of the most allusion-dense books in the OT, drawing on Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Job, Psalms, and Lamentations. Mapping these allusions reveals both how Zechariah understood itself in relation to earlier revelation and provides interpretive clues for otherwise opaque passages.

**Leadership theology**: The systematic tracking of how all three leadership streams (priestly/Joshua, royal/Zerubbabel-Branch, prophetic/Zechariah) are first affirmed and then progressively critiqued across the book — pointing toward an ideal eschatological leader who supersedes all three.

**Passion narrative connections**: Boda's detailed analysis of how Zechariah 9-14 shaped the NT Passion accounts — more concentrated quotation of Zechariah in Matthew's Passion than of almost any other OT source.

**Haggai-Zechariah as literary unit**: The two books function together; Boda has published a previous NIVAC commentary on both books and understands them as a coherent unit driving the Second Temple rebuilding.
