# Jaroslav Pelikan

> **Commentator/scholar page.** The leading 20th-century historian of the development of
> Christian doctrine, author of the five-volume *[[pelikan-emergence-catholic-tradition|The Christian
> Tradition]]* (1971–1989).

## Biographical Context

Jaroslav Pelikan (1923–2006) was a Lutheran historian of Christianity who taught for decades at
Yale; raised in a Slovak Lutheran pastoral family, he was received into the **Orthodox Church in
America** late in life (1998). His magnum opus, *The Christian Tradition: A History of the
Development of Doctrine*, traces doctrine from the patristic age to modernity across five volumes.

## Method and Characteristic Theses

- **Doctrine defined**: "what the church believes, teaches, and confesses on the basis of the word
  of God" — see [[development-of-doctrine]]. He distinguishes the history of *doctrine* from the
  history of *theology* and of *dogma*.
- **Regressive method**: reading back from confession to teaching to belief (citing Count Yorck to
  Dilthey: history, unlike antiquarianism, is "regressive").
- **Exegesis as engine**: doctrinal development is driven by the church's interpretation of
  Scripture; even Athanasius's *Orations against the Arians* are "explanations of a series of
  biblical passages."
- **The theologian's changing vocation**: bishops (100–600), monks (600–1500 West), professors
  (since 1500) — "Gregory I... was a bishop who had been a monk; Martin Luther... a monk who became
  a university professor."
- He famously distinguished **tradition** ("the living faith of the dead") from **traditionalism**
  ("the dead faith of the living").

## Relation to Other Theories of Development

Pelikan's project stands in dialogue with **[[development-of-doctrine|Newman's theory of
development]]**, **Harnack's** *Dogmengeschichte* (the "Hellenization" thesis), and Rahner — but
reframes the question around believing/teaching/confessing rather than around either decline or
inevitable unfolding.
