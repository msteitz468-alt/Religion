# Henri de Lubac

Henri de Lubac SJ (1896–1991) was one of the most influential Roman Catholic theologians of
the twentieth century and a principal architect of the *ressourcement* movement (the
"return to the sources") that fed the **nouvelle théologie** and shaped the Second Vatican
Council. Made a cardinal by John Paul II in 1983, he is read here primarily as a
**historian and theologian of biblical interpretation** — the author of the definitive study
of the Christian [[fourfold-sense|fourfold sense of Scripture]].

## Biographical and Intellectual Context

A Jesuit teaching at Lyon-Fourvière, de Lubac belonged to the mid-century French Catholic
revival (1930–1950) alongside Jean Daniélou, Yves Congar, Marie-Dominique Chenu, Louis
Bouyer, and (in association) Hans Urs von Balthasar, drawing on Möhler, Newman, Rousselot,
and Blondel. His program was to recover the riches of the Fathers and the medievals against
both a desiccated neo-scholasticism and a secularizing modernity. Several of his works
(notably *Surnaturel*, 1946) drew Vatican suspicion in the years around the encyclical
*Humani generis* (1950), and he was for a time restricted from teaching — yet the same
theology was substantially vindicated at Vatican II, where he served as a *peritus*.

## Hermeneutical Method

De Lubac's exegetical project is **spiritual exegesis recovered through historical
scholarship**. Its core conviction (developed in [[de-lubac-medieval-exegesis|*Medieval
Exegesis*]] and the earlier *Histoire et Esprit*, his study of [[origen|Origen]]) is that
**the doctrine of the senses of Scripture is identical with the doctrine of the
relationship between the two Testaments**. The spiritual senses are not arbitrary overlays
but the unfolding of the single divine economy: the *letter* of Old Testament history, its
fulfillment in Christ and Church (*allegoria*), its appropriation in the soul
(*tropologia*), and its eschatological term (*anagogia*). For de Lubac, spiritual
understanding is finally a *transformation of the reader* by the reality the text contains,
not a decoding trick — which is why he rejects both the Enlightenment caricature of allegory
as arbitrary and a flat historicism that misses the doctrine's depth. He upholds the
necessity of the literal sense as foundation (with Aquinas) while insisting the spiritual
senses grow organically from it.

## Characteristic Positions

1. **Senses = two Testaments.** The fourfold sense is the grammar of salvation history, not a fourfold filing system.
2. **Spiritual exegesis is transformative, not merely informational.** Reading Scripture spiritually assimilates the reader to Christ.
3. **History and spirit are allies, not rivals.** The letter is the indispensable foundation of the spiritual senses.
4. **Recovery of the corporate.** Salvation, the Church, and the reading of Scripture are communal realities (*Catholicisme*).
5. **Defense of the medieval tradition** against both Protestant/Enlightenment dismissal and neo-scholastic flattening.
