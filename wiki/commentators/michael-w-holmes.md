# Michael W. Holmes

## Biographical Context

Michael W. Holmes is a Protestant evangelical scholar specializing in New Testament textual criticism and the Apostolic Fathers. He served as professor of biblical studies and early Christianity at Bethel University (St. Paul, Minnesota) for several decades. He is best known for:

1. His revision and expansion of the Lightfoot-Harmer diglot edition of the Apostolic Fathers (3 editions: 1989, 1999, 2007)
2. His editorship of *The Greek New Testament: SBL Edition* (2010)
3. Scholarly contributions to the *Trajectories through the New Testament and the Apostolic Fathers* volume (Oxford University Press, 2005)

## Hermeneutical Method

Holmes operates within the **historical-critical tradition** with a commitment to rigorous **textual criticism**. His primary contribution is editorial and text-critical rather than interpretive:

- **Manuscript comparison**: Weighing Greek manuscript traditions against Latin, Syriac, Coptic, and other versions
- **Compositional history**: Evaluating the dates, provenance, and authorial situations of each Apostolic Father document
- **Tradition-critical analysis**: Identifying how these documents use (or fail to use) what became the NT canon
- **Comparative analysis**: His thematic analysis section in the collection identifies patterns across documents

Holmes does not write confessional commentary in the patristic tradition. His notes are descriptive and analytical; he presents competing scholarly positions without adjudicating where evidence is insufficient. He describes himself as committed to making these primary texts accessible to students and scholars across traditions.

## Characteristic Positions

- Accepts the **7-letter Middle Recension** of Ignatius as authentic (contra the Tübingen School, supporting Lightfoot)
- Treats 2 Clement as an **anonymous sermon**, not a letter by Clement (consensus position)
- Places Polycarp's martyrdom **ca. 155–160 CE** (against Eusebius' date of 167, based on evidence from the proconsul Statius Quadratus)
- Regards sections 11–12 of the *Epistle to Diognetus* as a **different document** accidentally bound with the apology (supported by internal evidence of a break in the manuscript)
- On Papias: resists over-reading the fragments; notes that Papias' tradition about Matthew's "Hebrew logia" need not refer to the canonical Gospel of Matthew

## Relationship to Other Sources

Holmes stands in the tradition of **J. B. Lightfoot** (whose edition he revised) and alongside scholars like Bart D. Ehrman (whose Loeb Classical Library edition of the Apostolic Fathers appeared in 2003). He is widely regarded as producing the most accessible and scholarly English diglot for classroom and research use.
