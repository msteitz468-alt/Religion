# G. Walter Hansen

American evangelical New Testament scholar, emeritus professor of New Testament at **Fuller Theological Seminary**. His major commentary ingested here is *The Letter to the Philippians* in the **Pillar New Testament Commentary** (PNTC; Eerdmans, 2009) — the wiki’s source for densified coverage of [[philippians|Philippians]]. He has also written extensively on **[[galatians|Galatians]]** (including work on Abraham in Galatians). D. A. Carson’s PNTC preface notes Hansen’s service as pastor, seminary professor, and missionary, and commends his clarity on complex matters.

## Hermeneutical Method

Hansen reads grammatico-historically within a confessional evangelical (Pillar) frame:

| Emphasis | Practice in the Philippians commentary |
|---|---|
| **Translation base** | TNIV for exposition; **own Greek translation** for the Christ hymn (2:6–11) |
| **Register split** | Body largely free of technical jargon; footnotes carry academic debate (ebook: notes relocated to end dump) |
| **Friendship + deliberative** | Letter as Hellenistic friendship letter (ten motifs) and deliberative speech for unity; declines forcing classical Latin rhetorical labels as *the* structure |
| **Colonial/imperial context** | Philippi as Roman colony; *politeuesthe* / *politeuma*; *kyrios* and Savior as subversion of Caesar titles with Isaiah/Jewish meaning |
| **Pastoral aim** | Accessibility for teachers and pastors; partnership with readers seeking to “live in a manner worthy of the gospel” (1:27) |

Dialogue partners named in his preface: Barth, Beare, Bockmuehl, Bonnard, Fee, Fowl, Hawthorne, Martin, Muller, O’Brien, Silva.

## Characteristic positions on Philippians (attributed)

- **Integrity:** single unified letter against partition theories.
- **Imprisonment locus:** Rome traditional/widespread; Ephesus strong alternative; not treated as closed.
- **1:27 dual citizenship** (with Hawthorne): earthly and heavenly.
- **2:5:** union-in-Christ *think* parallel preferred; ethical exemplar vs pure kerygma is a **false dichotomy**.
- **Hymn:** preexistence on narrative grounds; *morphē theou* via glory of God; *harpagmos* = possessed advantage not to exploit; kenosis without loss of deity; name = Lord; imperial subversion + christological monotheism.
- **3:2 vs 3:18–19:** Judaizing mutilators distinct from enemies of the cross (ethical libertines / earthly-minded).
- **NPP:** engages Sanders/Dunn/Wright; refuses reducing “my own righteousness” to ethnic boundary alone; objective genitive on *pistis Christou*.
- **4:13:** contentment in need and plenty — not prosperity omnipotence.
- **Caesar’s household (4:22):** imperial slaves/freedmen/civil service, not merely blood family of Caesar.
