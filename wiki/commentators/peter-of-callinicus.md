# Peter of Callinicus

Peter of Callinicus, miaphysite Patriarch of Antioch (fl. c. 581–591), is known to Johannes Zachhuber ([[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]], §6.2) chiefly through his vast polemic ***Contra Damianum***, directed against [[damian-of-alexandria|Damian of Alexandria]]. Despite the work's length, "Peter's own positive theory is harder to extract than Damian's"; his strength was "encyclopaedic learning" rather than original ideas.

## Hypostasis as Substrate

Peter rejected both **particular substances** (the source of the [[tritheist-controversy|tritheist controversy]]) and Damian's notion of **hypostatic properties**. He adopted Damian's term **'substrate' (ὑποκείμενον)** but redirected it from the *ousia* to the *hypostasis*: a hypostasis is "neither each characteristic property of the hypostasis nor... a collection of characteristic properties (as it pleases new-fangled literature to suppose) but... the substrate... it being understood that the properties appear on it" ("new-fangled literature" = Damian). The definition is based on [[gregory-of-nyssa|Gregory of Nyssa]], *Against Eunomius* III.5.56. The hypostasis is thus "not the property but the subject in which property inheres."

## Ousia as Concrete Universal

With [[severus-of-antioch|Severus]], Peter "unhesitatingly accepted the concrete universal" — ousia as the totality of the class. He rebuked Damian for speaking of the *presence* of ousia *in* the hypostasis, which would erase the difference between the hypostasis and the whole Trinity. Zachhuber reconstructs Peter's position as: ousia = totality of the class; hypostasis = the concrete "substrate" bearing individual properties, but **not itself a particular substance** — "hypostases [are] parts of the universal substance and, by that token, not themselves substances but hypostases."

## Assessment

This would amount to "a full retrieval of the concrete dimension of the classical theory" — both ousia and hypostasis concrete. But to avoid particular substances, Peter "insisted on a radical separation between hypostasis and ousia within the concrete account," dropping the **ontological continuity** between universal and particular that was the concrete dimension's chief tenet. Zachhuber's verdict: Peter "perceived the deep-seated conceptual problems inherent in earlier miaphysite theology, and while he made legitimate criticisms of Damian... he was unable to offer anything resembling a more viable alternative."
