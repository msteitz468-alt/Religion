# Buddhaghosa

The preeminent commentator of [[theravada|Theravada]] Buddhism — a 5th-century scholar-monk who
composed the **Visuddhimagga** ("Path of Purification") and revised the *atthakathā* (commentaries)
on most of the [[tipitaka|Pali Canon]]. His synthesis fixed the classical form of Theravada doctrine
and practice and is the single most authoritative non-canonical Theravada voice.

## Biographical Context

Tradition (preserved in the Mahavamsa chronicle) presents Buddhaghosa as a Brahmin from near Bodhgaya,
India, who converted to Buddhism and eventually traveled to Sri Lanka to access the Sinhala
commentaries at the **Mahavihara** monastery in Anurādhapura. The Mahavihara was the institutional
home of conservative Theravada — it had preserved the oral commentarial tradition in Sinhala since
the time of Mahinda (the monk who brought Buddhism to Sri Lanka under Ashoka, 3rd c. BCE). Buddhaghosa
translated and synthesized these Sinhala commentaries into Pali and, in the process, composed the
*Visuddhimagga* as a demonstration-piece of his method and learning before being granted access to
the full library.

The name "Buddhaghosa" means "voice (or sound) of the Buddha." His patron at the Mahavihara was
the Elder **Saṅghapāla**, a "true Vibhajjavādin" (Theravada partisan) "who lives in pure simplicity,
devoted to discipline's observance." The *Visuddhimagga* colophon identifies him as bearing the name
Buddhaghosa and being "of Moraṇḍacetaka."

## Major Work — the Visuddhimagga

The **[[visuddhimagga|*Visuddhimagga*]]** ("Path of Purification") is a comprehensive manual of
Theravada doctrine, meditation practice, and Abhidhamma analysis, organized around the
**[[seven-purifications|seven purifications]]** and the threefold training of virtue, concentration,
and wisdom. Key sections:

- **Part I (Virtue)** — the monastic code and thirteen ascetic practices (*dhutaṅga*)
- **Part II (Concentration)** — forty meditation subjects, temperament-matching, all eight jhānas
  (four material, four immaterial), supernormal powers, brahmaviharas
- **Part III (Understanding/Wisdom)** — Abhidhamma analysis of the five aggregates, twelve bases,
  eighteen elements, twenty-two faculties, four noble truths, dependent origination; then the
  progressive **[[vipassana-insight-knowledges|sixteen insight knowledges]]** (Chs. XVIII–XXI);
  the four supramundane paths and fruits (Ch. XXII); and the benefits of developing understanding
  (Ch. XXIII)

The opening verse sums the whole: "When a wise man, established well in virtue, / Develops
consciousness and understanding, / Then as a bhikkhu ardent and sagacious, / He succeeds in
disentangling this tangle."

### The Forty Meditation Subjects

The most-cited practical schema. Forty *kammaṭṭhāna* allocated to six temperaments:

| Temperament | Suitable subjects |
|---|---|
| Greedy | Ten foulnesses, body contemplation |
| Hating | Four brahmaviharas, four color kasinas |
| Deluded / speculative | Mindfulness of in-and-out breathing, four elements |
| Faithful | Six recollections of Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, deities |
| Intelligent | Mindfulness of death, recollection of peace, contemplation of food |
| Any | Ten kasinas, four immaterial states, mindfulness of body |

### The Seven Purifications as Path Map

Buddhaghosa organizes the entire path via the seven purifications from the Rathavinīta Sutta (MN 24):
(1) virtue → (2) mind/concentration → (3) view → (4) overcoming doubt → (5) knowing what is and is
not the path → (6) knowledge and vision of the way → (7) knowledge and vision (supramundane).

### The Insight Sequence

The most theologically influential section. The meditator moves through:

*Comprehension by groups* → *knowledge of rise and fall* (with the ten imperfections of insight) →
*knowledge of dissolution* → *appearance as terror* → *knowledge of danger* → *knowledge of
dispassion* → *knowledge of desire for deliverance* → *knowledge of reflection* → *knowledge of
equanimity about formations* → *conformity knowledge* → *change-of-lineage knowledge* →
*path knowledge* → *fruition knowledge*.

The **ten imperfections of insight** (*vipassanupakkilesa*) — illumination, knowledge, rapturous
happiness, tranquillity, bliss, resolution, exertion, assurance, equanimity, subtle attachment —
arise to a meditator keeping to the right course and must be recognized as defilements rather than
attainments. The story of Elder Dhammadinna and Elder Mahā-Nāga at Uccavālika illustrates the
danger of mistaking the illumination for liberation.

### Path Knowledge as Four-Function Simultaneous Event

At the moment of path knowledge, four functions occur simultaneously: full-understanding (of
suffering), abandoning (of origin), realizing (of cessation), and developing (of the path). Three
similes: lamp (burns wick / dispels darkness / makes light appear / uses up oil), sun, boat. The
four truths are penetrated in sixteen ways simultaneously (Paṭis II 107).

### Nibbāna as Object

Nibbāna (*nibbāna* — the unconditioned, signless, non-formation, cessation) is taken as object by
change-of-lineage knowledge and then by path knowledge. It is "void, signless, and desireless"
(three liberations) depending on whether the contemplation at emergence emphasized impermanent,
painful, or not-self. The three liberations of the Suttanta method vs. the two of the Abhidhamma
method are explicitly reconciled: the signless liberation "gives its name to the path by the way of
arrival" but is not recognized in the Abhidhamma schema because the faith faculty (dominant in
signless liberation) is not a path factor.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Systematic and harmonizing**: Buddhaghosa gathers scattered teaching of the suttas and
Abhidhamma into an ordered whole. He reads the Pali Canon largely literally — the *peshat*
analog — resolving apparent tensions by careful definition and taxonomy rather than by
allegorization or a two-truths hierarchy. The [[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]] distinction
(interpretable vs. definitive meaning) is acknowledged but used sparingly.

His primary methodological tool is the **Paṭisambhidāmagga** (*Paṭis*) — the canonical
"analysis" text of the Khuddaka Nikāya, which provides the detailed insight-knowledge framework
he systematizes in Chs. XVIII–XXII. Where the Suttanta and Abhidhamma methods diverge (most notably
on the number of liberations and the question of when defilements are abandoned), Buddhaghosa
records both positions with their reasons without adjudicating.

Within the Mahavihara tradition, he records three elder-variants on what governs differences in the
path's factors (Elders Tipiṭaka Cūla-Nāga, Mahā Datta, and Tipiṭaka Cūla Abhaya) and gives his
own position (governance by preliminary insight and insight leading to emergence) while respecting
all three.

## Influence

The *Visuddhimagga* remains the backbone of Theravada monastic training. The modern *vipassanā*
movement (Mahasi Sayadaw, S. N. Goenka, and related teachers) operates within the framework of
insight knowledges codified here. The question of whether the *Visuddhimagga*'s path requires jhāna
as a prerequisite is actively contested within contemporary Theravada: the text itself teaches a
"dry-insight" (bare insight / *sukkha-vipassaka*) path alongside the jhāna-based path.

Buddhaghosa's commentaries (*atthakathā*) on the Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, and Aṅguttara
Nikāyas — the *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī*, *Papañcasūdanī*, *Sāratthappakāsinī*, and *Manorathapūraṇī* —
are the standard exegetical reference in Theravada scholarship.
