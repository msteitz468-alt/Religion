# Buddhaghosa

The most important commentator of [[theravada|Theravada]] Buddhism — a 5th-century scholar-monk
(traditionally from India, working at the **Mahavihara** in Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka) whose synthesis
fixed the classical form of Theravada doctrine.

## Major Work — the Visuddhimagga

His masterpiece, the **Visuddhimagga** ("Path of Purification"), is a vast, systematic manual of
Theravada doctrine and meditation, organized around the threefold training: **ethics (*sila*),
concentration (*samadhi*), and wisdom (*panna*)**. It catalogues the meditation subjects (the
*kasinas*, mindfulness of breathing, the *brahmaviharas*), the stages of insight, and the Abhidhamma
analysis of mind and matter. It is the single most authoritative non-canonical text in Theravada — the
practical and doctrinal counterpart to the [[tipitaka|Pali Canon]] itself.

## Hermeneutical Method

Buddhaghosa also edited and composed the *atthakatha* (commentaries) on much of the canon. His method
is **systematic and harmonizing**: he gathers the scattered teaching of the suttas into an ordered
whole, reading the canon largely literally and resolving apparent tensions by careful definition rather
than by a [[neyartha-nitartha|neyartha/nitartha]] hierarchy (which Theravada uses sparingly). He is to
Theravada roughly what Rashi+Maimonides are to rabbinic Judaism: the indispensable classical
interpreter.

## Influence

The Visuddhimagga remains the backbone of Theravada monastic training and of the modern *vipassana*
movement.
