# Swami Gambhirananda

**Swami Gambhirananda** (1899–1988) was a monk of the **Ramakrishna Order** and its eleventh
President (1985–1988). He is best known not as an original thinker but as the **standard modern
English translator of [[shankara|Śaṅkara's]] commentaries (*bhāṣyas*)** — the channel through which
much of the English-reading world meets Advaita Vedanta in Śaṅkara's own words rather than in
paraphrase.

## Major Works

Faithful, literal renderings published by **Advaita Ashrama** (the Ramakrishna Order's publishing
house), still the reference translations:

- ***Bhagavad Gītā with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*** — the wiki's ingested source (see
  [[gambhirananda-bhagavad-gita|the source page]]).
- ***Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya of Śaṅkarācārya*** (complementing [[george-thibaut|Thibaut's]] older SBE
  rendering of the same [[brahma-sutras|Brahma Sutras]] commentary).
- ***Eight Upaniṣads with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*** and the *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* — Śaṅkara on
  the [[upanishads|Upanishads]].

## Hermeneutical Stance

Gambhirananda's method is **self-effacing translation**: he keeps his own voice to footnotes and lets
Śaṅkara's argument stand, preserving the technical vocabulary (*adhyāsa*, *avidyā*, *nirguṇa*) rather
than smoothing it into devotional English. His framing is orthodox [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] — he
presents Śaṅkara's reading as *the* meaning of the texts, in the neo-Vedanta lineage of the Ramakrishna
movement (Vivekananda).

## Significance

By making Śaṅkara's *Gītā-bhāṣya* and *Brahma-Sūtra-bhāṣya* available verbatim in English, Gambhirananda
let later readers see how radically Śaṅkara's Gita differs from the popular "scripture of devotion and
duty" — a Gita whose whole point is **knowledge of the actionless Self, preceded by the renunciation of
all action** (see [[jnana-karma-samuccaya|the knowledge-vs.-action controversy]]).
