# Wang Bi

**Wang Bi** (226–249) is the most important **philosophical** commentator on the [[daodejing|Daodejing]],
the leading figure of 3rd-century **Xuanxue** ("Dark/Profound Learning") — and one of the most precocious
minds in Chinese history, dead at 23.

> **Note:** the standard English translations (Rump/Chan; Lynn, *The Classic of the Way and Virtue*) are
> `[COPYRIGHT]` (see *Needed Sources.md*); this page is written from secondary knowledge pending a source.

## Major Works

Commentaries on the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] and on the *Yijing* (Book of Changes) — both foundational for
later Chinese philosophy.

## Characteristic Position — the Dao as Non-Being

Wang Bi reads the [[dao|Dao]] **metaphysically**, as **wu** (無, non-being/nothingness) — the
undifferentiated root from which all determinate being (*you*) springs. The myriad things depend on and
return to this "nothing." His is an abstract ontology of **root and branch, one and many**, drawn out of
the Daodejing's images. This is the **[[philosophical-daoism|philosophical]]** reading par excellence.

## Hermeneutical Method

A model of **commentary-as-transformation** in its *philosophical* mode: the commentary cultivates
metaphysical insight, training the reader to see being grounded in non-being. The polar opposite of
[[heshang-gong|Heshang Gong's]] longevity reading — see [[wang-bi-vs-heshang-gong|the controversy]].

## Influence

Wang Bi's text and reading became the standard "received" Daodejing for the literati tradition and
shaped Neo-Daoism, the reception of Buddhism (the *wu*/emptiness rapprochement), and Neo-Confucian
metaphysics.
