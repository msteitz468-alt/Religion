# Gene L. Green

Gene L. Green (Ph.D., University of Aberdeen) is an American evangelical New Testament scholar, for
many years professor of New Testament at Wheaton College (Wheaton, IL). Before Wheaton he taught for
years in **Latin America** (Costa Rica; Seminario ESEPA), and he is well known as an advocate for **Majority World /
global theology**, editing volumes that bring non-Western voices into biblical and theological
scholarship. His major exegetical works are the Pillar commentary on *The Letters to the
Thessalonians* (2002) and the BECNT *Jude and 2 Peter* (2008), the latter the object of the 2026-07-18 fidelity re-ingest ([[green-jude-2peter-becnt|source page]]). Spanish-language commentaries (*1 Pedro y 2 Pedro*; *1 y 2 Tesalonicenses*) and coauthorship of *The New Testament in Antiquity* also appear in his profile (About the Author paratext).

**Note on name:** Gene L. Green is *not* the same person as [[joel-green|Joel B. Green]] (the
Wesleyan author of the NICNT Luke). The two are distinct scholars who happen to share a surname.

## Hermeneutical Method

**Grammatical-historical with intensive cultural-contextual reconstruction.** Green's Author’s Preface states the concern to read Jude and 2 Peter "within their cultural and historical contexts" — the "web of Jewish and Hellenistic culture in which they are embedded" (L175). He reads the letters as a dialogue with both the
Jewish heritage (apocalyptic, midrash/pesher, the Enochic and Mosaic pseudepigrapha) and the wider
Greco-Roman philosophical and social world (Epicurean-flavored theodicy, *vituperatio*, honor-shame,
*imitatio*, secretarial composition).

**Sociology of the early church.** Green frames exegesis in terms of **honor and authority**. Why 2 Peter would borrow from the lesser-known Jude turns on Jude's honor as a member of the
**family of Jesus** (*desposynoi*); opponent rhetoric is read through ancient *vituperatio*.

**Evangelical-critical; minority authenticity for 2 Peter.** BECNT series frame (Yarbrough/Stein L147–L155): informed evangelical thought, pastors as primary audience, tradition not sacrosanct. Green defends authentic authorship of both letters — 2 Peter a minority academic position — by arguing standard pseudonymity arguments are inconclusive.

**Theology and history together.** “Theology and history can be and should be discussed together… not bifurcated into discrete domains” (L175–L177).

## Characteristic Positions (Jude and 2 Peter, BECNT 2008)

*Loci from 2026-07-18 digests; all positions attributed to Green.*

**1. Jude is authentic.** Written by **Jude, the brother of James and of Jesus** (one of the
"brothers of the Lord"), likely with secretarial assistance (answering the "good Greek" objection).
The heresy fits the *first* century (antinomianism, not second-century gnosticism); provenance is
**Palestinian Jewish Christianity**; date c. late 50s–early 60s. See
[[jude|Jude]] and [[jude-brother-of-jesus|Jude, the Brother of Jesus]].

**2. Jude genuinely cites 1 Enoch and the Assumption (Testament) of Moses as authoritative.** Green's
most distinctive position: against the apologetic alternatives (J. Charles's "even *your* Enoch" /
opponents-held-it reading; the "all truth is God's truth" minimizing; the "authentic pre-flood
tradition" defense), Green argues Jude's quotation formula places 1 Enoch (1.9, in Jude 14–15)
*alongside Torah and apostolic prophecy* as predictive, authoritative revelation — while noting Jude
uses such material *judiciously*, tying it to canonical bedrock (Gen. 6; Deut. 33–34; Zech. 3). He
follows Augustine's humble stance, holding both Jude's inspiration and the historical reality of its
sources. See [[jude-1-enoch-canon|Jude's Use of 1 Enoch and the Canon Question]].

**3. 2 Peter is authentic.** Green defends Petrine authorship (a minority academic view), arguing
that the four main pseudonymity arguments fail: the Hellenistic Greek/style is explicable by a
*different secretary* (Jerome's point about 1 vs. 2 Peter); the "testament genre = pseudepigraphy"
thesis (Bauckham) collapses because 2 Peter *lacks* the genre's hallmarks (no deathbed scene, no
burial, no dreams/visions, no blessings/curses — only a testamentary *section*, 1:12–15); the
opponents are not second-century gnostics; and "early Catholicism" (Käsemann) does not fit. The
self-designation **"Simeon Peter" (1:1)** actually argues *for* authenticity. See
[[2-peter-authorship|2 Peter: Authorship and the Testament Genre]].

**4. 2 Peter used Jude (imitatio).** With the majority, Green holds that 2 Peter is literarily
dependent on Jude (not vice versa, and not a common source). He frames the borrowing as ancient
*imitatio/mimēsis* (honorable re-use, not plagiarism), which implies Jude held a *higher* honor
status — explicable by Jude's place in Jesus' family. See [[2-peter-jude-relationship|The
Relationship between 2 Peter and Jude]].

**5. The opponents in 2 Peter are eclectic "scoffers," not gnostics.** They are apostates who deny
the **parousia and final judgment**, scoffing at its delay (3:3–4), with Epicurean-flavored
skepticism about prophecy, providence, and judgment — but assembled eclectically ("seed-pickers"),
not a single school. See [[2-peter|2 Peter]].

**6. "Partakers of the divine nature" (1:4) = moral transformation, not divinization.** Green reads
*theias koinōnoi physeōs* as participation in God's *moral* character (virtue), against both the
ontological-deification reading and Bauckham's "immortal and incorruptible." See
[[theosis-divine-nature|Theosis / Partakers of the Divine Nature]].

**7. Cosmic renewal, not annihilation (3:10–13).** The world is judged by fire, but the hope is the
*renovation* of creation — "new heavens and a new earth" (Isa. 65:17) — within a *linear* (not Stoic
cyclical) cosmology of three worlds: past (pre-flood), present, future. Green accepts the difficult
reading of 3:10 ("the earth and the works in it will be **found**/laid bare" before God). See
[[new-creation|New Creation]].

## Relationship to Other Commentators in the Wiki

- **Within BECNT**: joins [[thomas-schreiner|Schreiner]] (Romans), [[douglas-moo|Moo]] (Galatians),
  [[darrell-bock|Bock]] (Acts), and [[karen-jobes|Jobes]] (1 Peter) — and is now the wiki's third
  Wheaton-affiliated NT commentator (with Moo and Jobes). With Jobes, he completes BECNT's coverage
  of the Petrine letters.
- **On Petrine authorship**: Green defends authenticity for *both* 2 Peter and (with Jobes) the
  Petrine corpus generally; his secretary hypothesis parallels arguments used for 1 Peter and the
  [[pastoral-epistles-authorship|Pastoral Epistles]].
- **Majority World lens**: his global-theology commitments resonate with the contextual readings of
  [[peter-lau|Peter Lau]] (Ruth) and the "global Christianity" hermeneutic of
  [[robert-yarbrough|Robert Yarbrough]] (Pastorals).
