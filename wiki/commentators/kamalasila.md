# Kamalaśīla

**Kamalaśīla** (8th c.), pupil of Śāntarakṣita, is the Indian master whose **three *Stages of
Meditation*** (*Bhāvanākrama* I–III) are the backbone of [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]'s treatment of
both [[samatha-vipassana|serenity and insight]] in the [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]] (Vol. III).
The Tibetan tradition credits him with defeating [[heshang-moheyan|Hva-shang Moheyan]] at
[[samye-debate|the Samyé debate]], and his works were composed in that connection.

## Works (as cited in the Lamrim Chenmo)

- **Bhāvanākrama** I–III (*Stages of Meditation* / *Madhyamaka Stages of Meditation*) — the
  meditation manual underlying the serenity section (posture, the antidotes to laxity and excitement,
  the marks of accomplished serenity) and the central authority for the anti-quietist argument that
  insight requires analysis.
- **Madhyamakāloka** (*Illumination of the Middle Way*) — cited on the definitive/provisional
  ([[neyartha-nitartha|nītārtha/neyārtha]]) status of texts and on conventional intrinsic character.
- **Madhyamakālaṃkāra-pañjikā** — commentary on Śāntarakṣita's *Ornament for the Middle Way*.

## Positions and Role

- **Insight requires analysis** — his decisive contribution to Tsong-kha-pa's case: "the root of the
  sublime wisdom that knows reality is correct analytical discrimination"; to think of nothing is to
  "abandon the Mahāyāna." The **fire-from-two-sticks** image (analysis kindles the nonconceptual
  wisdom that then consumes the analysis) comes through his *Bhāvanākrama*.
- **Method and wisdom united** — the Mahāyāna path is twofold; against the
  [[heshang-moheyan|Hva-shang]] reduction of the path to thought-free absorption.
- On the [[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|Svātantrika/Prāsaṅgika]] map he is, with Śāntarakṣita, a
  **Yogācāra-Svātantrika** — holding (against [[candrakirti|Candrakīrti]]) that objects have
  intrinsic character conventionally, and that the *Saṃdhinirmocana*'s three-naturelessness teaching
  is definitive.
