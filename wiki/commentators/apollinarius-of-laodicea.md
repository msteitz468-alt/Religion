# Apollinarius of Laodicea

Apollinarius of Laodicea (c. 310 – c. 390) was a learned defender of Nicene *homoousios* whose philosophically ambitious Trinitarian thought directly shaped the young [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil]] — and whose later **Christology** (that in Christ the divine Logos replaced the human rational soul/mind) was condemned as the heresy of **Apollinarianism**. In Johannes Zachhuber's account ([[zachhuber-rise-of-christian-theology|*The Rise of Christian Theology*]]), Apollinarius is "particularly important for an account of Patristic philosophy": his letter to Basil offers a uniquely sophisticated philosophical defense of the *homoousion*, and his project of applying a single ontological model across Trinity *and* economy anticipated Gregory of Nyssa's systematic ambition.

## The Derivative Account of Ousia

Responding to Basil's request for a sound understanding of *homoousios* "where neither a common overlying genus, nor a pre-existent material substratum, nor a division of the first into the second" applies, Apollinarius distinguished three senses of *ousia*:

1. what is "one in number" (Aristotle's first substance — the concrete individual);
2. what is contained in one "description"/circumscription (second substance — species/genus);
3. **two or more united as a family** (κατὰ γένος) — and *this* third sense, he argued, is the one pertinent to the Trinity.

On the family model, the members are "the same" (*tauton*) in substance and "one" (*heis*): "all human beings are Adam," "the son of David is David." So the divine *ousia* is first the Father and then, by derivation, the Son — making the Son truly "God" and the two genuinely one, without violating monotheism. This is an **asymmetric, derivative** unity (cohesion through descent from a first principle), which Apollinarius explicitly distinguished from both the Platonic transcendent genus and the Stoic material substratum.

Zachhuber connects this to the Neoplatonic concept of ***ab uno* / *ad unum* (ἀφ' ἑνός / πρὸς ἕν) "derivative genera"** — series whose first member is also the genus term (e.g. the number series headed by the monad). Where the Neoplatonists prized such genera for *ontological gradation*, Apollinarius prized them for **cohesion**, which is why he could call originator and offspring *homoousios*. (His emphasis on unity over differentiation meant he never worked out a theory of distinct hypostases.)

## Influence on Basil and the Cappadocians

The (disputed but probably authentic) correspondence between Apollinarius and the young Basil is, for Zachhuber, "the intellectual point of origin of the Cappadocian's development." Basil did **not** simply accept Apollinarius's derivative model — he built instead on shared, univocal predication and ontological **co-ordination** (the [[ousia-hypostasis-distinction|abstract account]]) — but Apollinarius's example of an intellectually ambitious, philosophically explicit defense of *homoousios*, and his use of the universal/family model, set the agenda. [[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory of Nazianzus]]'s more Origenist, Father-as-cause emphasis stays close to Apollinarius's logic.

## Christology and Condemnation

Apollinarius is best known for the Christological doctrine later condemned: that in the incarnate Christ the divine **Logos took the place of the human rational soul/mind** (*nous*), so that Christ was not fully human. This "Word–flesh" Christology was rejected (the Cappadocians, esp. Gregory of Nazianzus: "what is not assumed is not healed") and anathematized at the Council of Constantinople (381). His Christological writings survived patchily, often transmitted under orthodox names, precisely because of this condemnation — which is why his earlier influence on Trinitarian philosophy is easy to overlook.
