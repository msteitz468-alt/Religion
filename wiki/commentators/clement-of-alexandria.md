# Clement of Alexandria (commentator)

Titus Flavius Clemens (c.150–c.215 CE), head of the Alexandrian catechetical school, the
first Christian writer to engage Greek philosophy systematically as a positive theological
resource and the first to deploy allegorical exegesis programmatically in a Christian key.
This page treats Clement **as a commentator / exegete / theologian** — his hermeneutical
method, characteristic positions, and influence. For his life and reception as a figure, see
[[clement-of-alexandria|Clement of Alexandria (figure)]]. His works have their own text pages:
[[protrepticus|Protrepticus]], [[paedagogus|Paedagogus]], [[stromata|Stromata]],
[[quis-dives-salvetur|Quis Dives Salvetur]], [[clement-fragments|Fragments]].

## Hermeneutical Method

Clement reads Scripture on multiple levels, privileging the **spiritual/allegorical** sense over
the literal, in a manner descended from [[philo-of-alexandria|Philo]] (cited by name) and
anticipating [[origen|Origen]]:

- **Allegory as the master key.** "Almost the whole Scripture gives its utterances in this way"
  (*Strom.* V). The patriarchs are read as types (Abraham/Sarah/Hagar = faith/wisdom/secular
  culture; Isaac "self-taught"; Jacob the "Exerciser"); the Rich Young Ruler's "possessions" are
  the soul's passions ([[quis-dives-salvetur|Quis Dives Salvetur]]); the Tabernacle, the Decalogue,
  and the parables are unfolded mystically. Reading "according to the syllables" (gnostic unfolding)
  is set against reading "according to the letter." Notably, Clement will keep an allegory's moral
  conclusion while *rejecting its literal factual premise* (the hyena/hare zoology in *Paed.* II.10).
- **The discipline of concealment (esotericism).** Truth is deliberately veiled — in Scripture, in
  the Egyptian *adyta* and Hebrew veil, in Pythagorean symbols — to provoke inquiry and to exclude
  the unworthy ("nor are the mysteries of the word to be expounded to the profane"). The
  [[stromata|Stromata]]'s scattered "patchwork" form enacts this. Clement claims an **unwritten
  apostolic tradition** of [[gnosis|gnosis]] transmitted "to a few."
- **The ecclesiastical rule / canon of truth.** Exposition is governed by "the concord and harmony
  of the law and the prophets in the covenant delivered at the coming of the Lord" (*Strom.* VI;
  see [[rule-of-faith|rule of faith]]). Heretics err by attending "to the names alone, while they
  alter the meanings."
- **Faith → knowledge → love.** Exegesis is an ascent: faith is the indemonstrable first principle
  ("all demonstration is traced up to indemonstrable faith," *Strom.* VIII), knowledge its
  superstructure, love its term. The true [[gnosis|Gnostic]] is the ideal interpreter.
- **Greek learning baptized.** "I call him truly learned who brings everything to bear on the
  truth; so that, from geometry, and music, and grammar, and philosophy itself, culling what is
  useful, he guards the faith." His method is programmatically **eclectic** — "whatever has been
  well said by each of those sects... this eclectic whole I call philosophy."

In the framework typology of the wiki this is a Christian **literal-plus-allegorical (Quadriga-style)**
reading weighted heavily toward the spiritual sense, fused with a distinctive **commentary-as-ascent**
model (faith→gnosis→love→deification) and the importation of Greek **logic/demonstration** as a tool
subordinate to faith.

## Characteristic Positions

- **Philosophy as preparation for the Gospel.** Greek philosophy was "a schoolmaster to bring the
  Hellenic mind to Christ," "a covenant peculiar to" the Greeks given by "the same God that furnished
  both the Covenants." See [[harmony-of-religion-and-philosophy|harmony of religion and philosophy]].
- **The theft of the Greeks.** The best Greek dogmas are "older," plagiarized from [[moses|Moses]] and
  the prophets — Plato "the disciple of the barbarian philosophy."
- **The Christian Gnostic and apatheia.** The perfected believer does good for its own sake, attains
  [[apatheia|passionlessness]], and is "assimilated to God" — "made a god going about in flesh." See
  [[gnosis|gnosis]], [[theosis-divine-nature|theosis]].
- **Apophatic theology.** God is known "knowing not what He is, but what He is not," "above space,
  time, name, and conception." See [[apophatic-theology|apophatic theology]].
- **Free will against natural election.** Faith is voluntary, not (as [[basilides|Basilides]] and
  [[valentinus|Valentinus]] held) a natural endowment of the elect. See [[freedom|free will]].
- **Wealth ethics.** Salvation turns on disposition, not dispossession — wealth is a neutral
  instrument. See [[use-of-wealth|use of wealth]], [[quis-dives-salvetur|Quis Dives Salvetur]].
- **Moderate marriage and against rash martyrdom.** Marriage and celibacy both God-given (against
  both libertines and encratites); voluntary martyrdom condemned.
- **Logos-Christology.** The pre-existent Word is creator, the "New Song," the Tutor, and the
  Teacher who deifies. See [[logos-christology|Logos-Christology]].

## Influence on Later Tradition

Clement's synthesis of faith and Hellenic learning, his allegorical method, and his ideal of the
Christian Gnostic were taken up and systematized by [[origen|Origen]] and through him shaped the whole
Alexandrian and later Eastern tradition (deification, apophaticism, contemplative ascent). His
speculative tendencies (pre-existence themes; the soul "not naturally immortal" in the fragments) also
drew later suspicion.

## Hillman's Revisionist Reading (Contradiction Protocol)

[[hillman-original-sin|Hillman (2012)]] reads Clement's *Exhortation to the Greeks* (*Protrepticus*) primarily as evidence of early Christianity's **political war against pagan mystery religions** — specifically the suppression of the female oracular authority associated with the cults of Aphrodite, Dionysus, and the Draco/Medusae tradition. Hillman treats Clement's sustained polemic against pagan drug use, erotic iconography, and theatrical entertainments not as theological ethics but as the literary arm of a political program designed to dismantle the oracular institutions of the Greco-Roman world. He also cites the *Rich Man's Salvation* passage describing God as "effeminate" (*thely-morpha*) as evidence that Alexandrian theology deliberately cultivated an androgynous Christology to absorb initiates from Cybele's hermaphroditic cult. This revisionist framing is not endorsed by mainstream patristics and is explicitly heterodox; see [[early-christian-sexual-ethics-pagan-critique|Early Christian Sexual Ethics and the Pagan Critique]].
