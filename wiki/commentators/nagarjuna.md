# Nāgārjuna

**Nāgārjuna** (c. 150 CE) is the founder of the **[[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]** ("Middle Way") school
and, after the Buddha himself, the most influential thinker in the [[buddhism|Buddhist]] tradition —
revered across [[mahayana|Mahayana]] and [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] as a "second Buddha." His
**[[mulamadhyamakakarika|Mūlamadhyamakakārikā]]** (MMK) gave the early Mahayana *Prajñāpāramitā*
proclamation that "all things are empty" its rigorous philosophical defense.

## Life (largely legendary)

Almost nothing is securely known. Traditional accounts make him a South Indian Brahmin convert,
associated with the **Sātavāhana** dynasty and later with **Nālandā**; legend has him **retrieving
the Prajñāpāramitā sūtras from the *nāgas* (serpent-dragons)** in their undersea realm — the source
of his name — and credits him with alchemy and a centuries-long life. Historians place his floruit
around 150 CE on the basis of the works confidently attributed to him.

## Works

The MMK is the keystone. Also generally accepted as authentic is the **Vigrahavyāvartanī**
("Dispeller of Disputes"), which answers the self-refutation charge — that the thesis "all things
are empty" must itself be empty and so say nothing — by distinguishing the [[two-truths|two
truths]]. A wider corpus (the *Yuktiṣaṣṭikā*, *Śūnyatāsaptati*, *Ratnāvalī*, etc.) is traditionally
ascribed to him.

## Method and Characteristic Positions

- **Emptiness ([[sunyata|śūnyatā]]) of *all* dharmas.** Nāgārjuna extends emptiness from the person
  (the only "empty" thing for [[abhidharma|Abhidharma]]) to the supposedly ultimate *dharmas*
  themselves: nothing whatever has **intrinsic nature ([[svabhava|svabhāva]])**.
- **Reductio, not assertion.** He advances no positive metaphysical thesis of his own (the
  *Prāsaṅgika* reading, after Candrakīrti, makes this definitive); he only draws out the
  contradictions in opponents' views — the [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] *prasaṅga* method, using
  patterns like infinite regress, "neither identical nor distinct," and the three times.
- **Emptiness = dependent origination = the middle path** (MMK 24.18), avoiding both eternalism
  (being) and annihilationism (nonbeing) — and **emptiness is itself empty**, no reified Absolute.
- **The two truths as the key to everything.** Misunderstanding emptiness for nihilism, or for a
  really-existing void, is the snake wrongly grasped (MMK 24.11). Conventionally, the path and the
  Four Noble Truths stand; ultimately, nothing is reified.
- **Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are not ultimately distinct** (MMK 25.19).

## Influence

Nāgārjuna founds a lineage running through Āryadeva, Buddhapālita, **Bhāviveka** (the *Svātantrika*
branch), and **Candrakīrti** (the *Prāsaṅgika* branch), into Yogācāra debate, the whole of Tibetan
scholastic philosophy (where Prāsaṅgika-Madhyamaka becomes the normative "highest view"), and East
Asian Buddhism (the Chinese *Sanlun* / "Three Treatise" school). He is a central figure in modern
comparative philosophy (compared to Sextus Empiricus, Wittgenstein, and Derrida).
