# Candrakīrti

**Candrakīrti** (c. 7th c.) is the commentator on [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] whom the Tibetan tradition
treats as the definitive interpreter of [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]], and the founding authority of the
**[[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|Prāsaṅgika]]** stream. [[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]], following
[[atisha|Atiśa]], makes Candrakīrti's the "main Madhyamaka system" and declares: "I will follow
[Buddhapālita and Candrakīrti] in making determinations about what the noble Nāgārjuna intended." In
the [[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]] (Vol. III) he is by far the most cited authority.

## Major Works (as cited in the Lamrim Chenmo)

- **Madhyamakāvatāra** (*Commentary on / Introduction to the Middle Way*) and its **auto-commentary**
  (*bhāṣya*) — the source of the **sevenfold chariot analysis**, the refutation of the four kinds of
  production, and the delineation of the two selflessnesses.
- **Prasannapadā** (*Clear Words*) — his word-commentary on the [[mulamadhyamakakarika|MMK]];
  Tsong-kha-pa takes the Prāsaṅgika/Svātantrika terminology to "agree with" this text.
- **Catuḥśataka-ṭīkā** — commentary on [[aryadeva|Āryadeva]]'s *Four Hundred Stanzas*.
- **Yuktiṣaṣṭikā-vṛtti** — commentary on Nāgārjuna's *Sixty Stanzas of Reasoning*.

## Characteristic Positions (per the Lamrim Chenmo)

- **No intrinsic character even conventionally** — the defining Prāsaṅgika thesis: nothing exists "by
  way of its own character" (*svalakṣaṇa*) at any level, against [[bhavaviveka|Bhāvaviveka]]. This
  grounds his refutation of **autonomous (*svatantra*) syllogisms** and his use of consequence
  (*prasaṅga*) arguments and "arguments based on what others accept."
- **"We are not nihilists."** Emptiness is dependent-arising; he refutes both the proposition of
  (intrinsic) existence and of nonexistence. The **robbery analogy** distinguishes the Mādhyamika
  from the Lokāyata nihilist even where conclusions coincide.
- **Conventional valid cognition** — the world knows objects through four valid cognitions (direct,
  inferential, scriptural, analogical), established in mutual dependence, none essentially.
- **The conception of true existence is *afflictive* ignorance** — a distinctive claim (other
  Mādhyamikas count it a merely cognitive obscuration), making it the first of the twelve links of
  [[dependent-origination|dependent-arising]]. He holds, accordingly, that śrāvakas and
  pratyekabuddhas realize the selflessness of phenomena, not only of persons.
- **External objects exist conventionally** — but in a manner unique to his system, not as the
  Sautrāntikas hold them (so Tsong-kha-pa rejects labeling him a "Sautrāntika-Mādhyamika").

## Influence

Through [[atisha|Atiśa]] the Candrakīrti–Prāsaṅgika reading entered Tibet and, via
[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]], became the normative "highest view" of the [[gelug|Gelug]] school. See
[[svatantrika-prasangika-distinction|the Svātantrika/Prāsaṅgika distinction]].
