# Ambrose of Milan

Bishop of Milan (374–397), one of the four traditional Latin Doctors of the Church, and the
figure who baptized [[augustine|Augustine]] in 387. This page is anchored, so far, in his
appearances in Augustine's [[on-christian-doctrine|*De Doctrina Christiana*]].

## In Augustine's *De Doctrina Christiana*

**As a model of eloquence (Book IV).** With [[cyprian|Cyprian]], Ambrose is Augustine's
patristic exemplar of the three styles of [[christian-eloquence|Christian eloquence]] (Bk. IV,
ch. 21):

- **Subdued/quiet style** — *De Spiritu Sancto*, Bk. I prologue, on the equality of the Holy
  Spirit.
- **Temperate style** — *De Virginibus*, Bk. II ch. i, the portrait of the model virgin.
- **Majestic style** — *De Virginibus*, Bk. II, denouncing cosmetics.

**On chronology (Book II).** Augustine reports Ambrose's argument that, on the dating of the
figures, Plato is more likely to have learned from the Hebrew prophets (Jeremiah) than the
reverse — used to support the [[spoils-of-egypt|"spoils of Egypt"]] claim that what is true in
pagan philosophy was ultimately drawn "from the mines of God's providence." (In the
*Retractations* Augustine corrects his memory of exactly what Ambrose said and that Plato and
Jeremiah were not in fact contemporaries.)
