# Amina Wadud

**Amina Wadud** (b. 1952, Bethesda, Maryland, as Mary Teasley) is an American Muslim scholar
of [[islam|Islam]], a pioneer of **gender-inclusive [[tafsir|tafsir]]**, and the most
influential single theorist of [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]]. An African-American
convert (she embraced Islam in 1972), she took her PhD in Islamic studies and Arabic at the
University of Michigan, taught at the International Islamic University Malaysia and for many
years at Virginia Commonwealth University, and was a founding figure of the Malaysian
women's-rights group **Sisters in Islam**.

## Hermeneutical Method

Wadud's method is set out in [[wadud-quran-and-woman|*Qur'an and Woman*]] (1992; Oxford 1999):
a **holistic, thematic** rereading of the [[quran|Qur'an]] that treats the text as a coherent
unity (a "**hermeneutics of [[tawhid|tawhid]]**") rather than a string of verses to be glossed
atomistically, combined with the **double-movement** historicism of [[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur
Rahman]] — extract the universal moral principle a verse expressed within seventh-century
Arabia, then re-apply it to the present. Her signature interpretive claim is that the
inequality Muslim women experience reflects an **androcentric "prior text"** read *into* the
Qur'an, not a teaching read *out* of it; her readings of the human-creation passages, the
eschatology, and above all [[quran-4-34-male-authority|Q 4:34]] aim to show a Qur'an in which
women and men are equal as *khalifa* (moral agents) before God. She treats **gender as a
category of thought**, not merely a topic.

She situates her work explicitly within *ijtihad* — independent reasoning that may override
the inherited male consensus — which is precisely what makes it contested.

## Major Works

- ***Qur'an and Woman: Rereading the Sacred Text from a Woman's Perspective*** (1992; Oxford
  University Press, 1999) — the foundational text of feminist tafsir; see the
  [[wadud-quran-and-woman|scholarship page]].
- ***Inside the Gender Jihad: Women's Reform in Islam*** (2006) — the activist sequel,
  reflecting on praxis and authority.

## Activism and Controversy (recorded, not adjudicated)

Wadud calls her project the **"gender jihad."** In March 2005 in New York she **led a
mixed-gender Friday (*jumu'a*) congregation in prayer as imam and delivered the *khutba***, an
act without modern precedent that drew worldwide attention, fatwas of condemnation, and threats,
and made her a lightning rod in the dispute over women's religious authority. (She had earlier,
in 1994 in Cape Town, delivered a "pre-khutba" address in a mosque.) Critics deploy "Western"
and "feminist" as disqualifiers; defenders see a recovery of the Qur'an's own egalitarianism.
Per the [Contradiction Protocol] the wiki records the dispute without ruling on it; the
exegetical core is carried on [[quran-4-34-male-authority|the Q 4:34 controversy page]].

## Influence

Wadud is the principal forerunner of a generation of feminist and reformist Qur'an scholars —
Asma Barlas (*"Believing Women" in Islam*), Kecia Ali, Sa'diyya Shaikh, Ziba Mir-Hosseini and
the *Musawah* movement — and of [[islamic-feminism|Islamic feminism]] as an interpretive
school. She both builds on and departs from earlier male reformists ([[fazlur-rahman|Fazlur
Rahman]], Muhammad Iqbal, Sir Sayyid Ahmad Khan).
