# Joseph

**Joseph** (Hebrew *Yosef*), the eleventh son of [[jacob|Jacob]] and the firstborn of Rachel, is the protagonist of the longest sustained narrative in Genesis (chs. 37, 39–50) — the dreamer sold into Egyptian slavery by his brothers who rises to become vizier of Egypt and, through forgiveness, the preserver of his family. In Jewish tradition he is uniquely titled **Joseph the Righteous** (*Yosef ha-Tsaddiq*), and in the [[zohar|Zohar]] this title is given its deepest meaning: Joseph is the human embodiment of **Yesod** (Foundation), the [[sefirot|sefirah]] of the covenant and the channel of all blessing — **"the Righteous One, the foundation of the world"** ([[tsaddiq|Tsaddiq]]).

---

## Biographical Overview

- **The favored son and the dreams** (Gen 37): the coat of many colors; the dreams of the sheaves and the stars bowing; the brothers' hatred; sold to Ishmaelite/Midianite traders and brought to Egypt.
- **Potiphar's house and prison** (Gen 39): serves Potiphar; **resists the advances of Potiphar's wife**, is falsely accused, and imprisoned — the episode on which his title "the Righteous" turns.
- **The interpreter of dreams** (Gen 40–41): interprets the dreams of the cupbearer and baker, then Pharaoh's dreams of the seven years; appointed vizier over Egypt; renamed Zaphenath-paneah; marries Asenath; fathers Manasseh and Ephraim.
- **The reunion and forgiveness** (Gen 42–45): the brothers come for grain; the testing; the disclosure ("I am Joseph"); "you intended evil against me, but God intended it for good" (50:20).
- **The descent and the death** (Gen 46–50): Jacob's family settles in Goshen; Joseph's death at 110; his bones to be carried up at the Exodus.

---

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 37, 39–50** — the "Joseph novella," widely regarded as a literary masterpiece and the climax of Genesis.
- **Exodus 13:19; Joshua 24:32** — Joseph's bones carried out of Egypt and buried at Shechem.
- **Qur'an, Sura Yusuf (Q 12)** — "the most beautiful of stories"; a complete, independent retelling emphasizing Yusuf's chastity, the women of the city, and divine providence.
- **Acts 7:9–16; Hebrews 11:22** — Joseph in the NT as a model of faith and a figure within the salvation-historical retrospect.

---

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Jewish**: *Yosef ha-Tsaddiq*, "the Righteous," a title earned by resisting Potiphar's wife — the paradigm of sexual self-mastery and covenant fidelity. Rabbinic Midrash dwells on his beauty, his temptation, and his bones. He is associated with the covenant of circumcision and with the messianic figure *Mashiaḥ ben Yosef* (the suffering, warring Messiah who precedes Messiah ben David).
- **Christian**: a major **type of Christ** — the beloved son rejected by his brothers, sold for silver, suffering unjustly, exalted, and becoming the savior of those who wronged him; his forgiveness prefigures Christ's. Patristic and medieval exegesis develops the typology extensively.
- **Islamic**: Yusuf, a prophet; Sura 12 is the Qur'an's most sustained single narrative, a story of trial, chastity (the famous scene of the women cutting their hands), patience, and the reunion of the family; a touchstone of Sufi devotional and poetic tradition (the beauty of Yusuf as the beauty of the divine Beloved).
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)** — see below; Joseph = Yesod, the Righteous Foundation.

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar)

Joseph is the Zohar's premier human embodiment of **Yesod** — the ninth [[sefirot|sefirah]], the Foundation, the channel that gathers all the upper energies and transmits them to the [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] (Malkhut). Yesod is symbolized by the **covenant of circumcision** and the generative organ; its human exemplar must therefore be the master of sexual holiness. Key Zoharic readings:

- **"The righteous one is the foundation of the world"** (Prov 10:25): the Zohar makes this verse the definition of Yesod and of Joseph. "All those pillars stand upon one single pillar… called Righteous"; Yesod is "the foundation of the world and its perfection, single point of the world, center of all," identified with **Zion**. See [[tsaddiq|Tsaddiq (the Righteous One, Foundation of the World)]].
- **Guarding the sign of the covenant**: Joseph earns the title *Tsaddiq* by resisting Potiphar's wife. At the decisive moment "**the image of his father Jacob** appeared to him" in the window, and he "withdrew" — because to have sinned would have defiled Yesod, the very sefirah he embodies. "This covenant is the mystery of Joseph." The verse "there was no man of the men of the house" (Gen 39:11) is read to *include* the image of Jacob ([[jacob|Tif'eret]]) present in the room, of which Joseph (Yesod) is the direct extension.
- **Joseph's coffin and the Shekhinah**: because Joseph guarded the covenant, "**Joseph's coffin accompanied Her**" — his bier travels together with the Shekhinah through the wilderness; the one who keeps Yesod is never separated from Malkhut.
- **Joseph and Benjamin / Mashiaḥ ben Yosef**: the Zohar links Joseph to the warring, suffering messianic figure and to the eschatological role of the tribe of his line.

The contrast with **Er and Onan** (Judah's sons, Gen 38), who "wasted seed" and so fed the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]], is pointed: Joseph is the positive pole of the same sefirah whose misuse is the gravest sin. See [[tsaddiq|Tsaddiq]].

### Pritzker Vol. III reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

Material below is grounded in Zohar III (Pritzker Vol. III; [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol3|Matt, Vol. III]]), Va-Yeshev through Va-Yeḥi.

**Generations of Jacob: Joseph; Yesod fruiting; *vav* image (Va-Yeshev, 1:180a–182b).** In the Zohar body (Pritzker III, 1:180a): “only that rung called Righteous One bears fruit; Joseph is the rung of Jacob bearing fruit… So, *These are the generations of Jacob: Joseph*.” Anyone gazing at Joseph’s image would say, “This is the image of Jacob!” — “Jacob’s entire image inhered in Joseph… the two of them proceeding as one. This is the mystery of the letter ו״ו (*vav*)” (1:180a, 1:182b). Joseph was seventeen when lost; another seventeen years of true life in Egypt with Joseph as king (1:180a; cf. Va-Yeḥi 1:216b). Through all the days of Joseph—image of his father—judgment did not befall the world; at Joseph’s death exile immediately (1:180a).

**Coat / sale / supernal covenant (Va-Yeshev, 1:182b–186a).** Excessive Jacob-love and the ornamented tunic inflicted exile upon them all “as well as upon Shekhinah” (1:183a). When the brothers sold Joseph they made [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] partner to the oath; until Joseph was revealed Shekhinah did not rest upon Jacob (1:183b–184a). “Joseph is supernal covenant… As soon as Joseph—supernal covenant—vanished from the world, then covenant, Shekhinah, and Israel all plunged into exile” (1:184a). The pit = Hell empty of Torah and Egypt lacking mystery of faith (1:185a). Goat-blood on the tunic is measure-for-measure for Jacob’s goat deception of Isaac (1:185b–186a).

**Potiphar / covenant / Righteous (Va-Yeshev–Mi-Qets, 1:189b–194b).** Joseph called Righteous who “guarded the holy covenant engraved in him,” joining Gevurah against the evil impulse (1:189b). “Until that incident befell Joseph, he was not called Righteous. Once he guarded that sealed covenant, he was called Righteous” (Mi-Qets, 1:194b). Guarding covenant = entire Torah; failure separates Israel from Father like other gods (1:189b). He left his garment and fled — model for abandoning fancy openings to the Accuser / “woman of evil” ([[sitra-ahra|Other Side]]) (1:190b–191a). *YHVH was with Joseph*; Shekhinah success-miracles in Potiphar’s house (1:189a–189b).

**Rise: river / Avrekh / sun–moon; famine seal (Mi-Qets, 1:193b–204a).** Every river pertains to Joseph the Righteous; Pharaoh’s Nile is “a dream of Joseph” (1:193b–194a). *YHVH lives* = Living One, Righteous One, Foundation of the World, Vitality of the Worlds (1:194a). Once Joseph went down, “he drew Shekhinah after him, for Shekhinah follows only the righteous one” (1:197a). *Avrekh* = “a link linking sun with moon, and all kneel to this place” (1:196b–197a); as governor, “sun governing moon, illumining her, nourishing her” (1:198a). “Joseph, mystery of covenant, sealed his spring in the year of famine, not enabling it to proliferate in the world” — so as not to draw fetus from the Other Side (1:204a). Error of “remember me” → place of forgetting / End of Left until two years restore remembering (1:193b).

**Life of Worlds; reunion; double *aron* (Va-Yiggash–Va-Yeḥi).** World stands on a single pillar named Righteous who waters the garden (1:208a). Joseph harnessed his chariot = Righteous One; meets Israel in Goshen in single union (1:211a). Jacob: “you are still alive”—“for you have abided by mystery of holy Covenant, called Life of the Worlds” (1:211a–211b). *He blessed et Joseph* = sign of covenant Joseph guarded → called Righteous (1:227b–228a). Joseph fruitful son / bow firm: would not stray from covenantal sign; blessings of father surpass all rest on Joseph’s head so Yesod becomes river from Eden (1:246b–247b). Embalming: Joseph modeled on the body of the patriarchs. **Double *aron***: *va-yiysem ba-aron* spelled with two *yods* — coffin below **and** ark of the covenant above; “inherited only by one who preserves the covenant”; Joseph preserved covenant below and Covenant above; soul joined Shekhinah; every *tsaddiq* inherits Holy Land (Isa 60:21) (1:250b–251a). ### Hamilton (NICOT) reception (2026-07-18)

Material attributed to [[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]] ([[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|Genesis 18–50 NICOT]]; intro theology in [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|1–17]]).

**Macrostructure and theology of the cycle.** Opens under Jacob’s *tôlĕdôt* (37:2a) though data are preponderantly about Joseph. No divine promise speeches in the Joseph story, yet famine threat and Egyptian placement fulfill means for Abrahamic descendants to survive: “If Joseph represents the means of preservation of God’s promises, then Judah represents the means of endangering those promises.” Intimacy→strife→reconciliation pattern (Coats) runs through Genesis including Joseph. God never clearly rebukes patriarchal aberrant behavior — “there is a ‘Why did Sarah laugh?’ but no … ‘Why did Joseph frame?’”

**Sale and favoritism (37).** Possible anticipatory paronomasia: “Joseph was shepherding his brothers” → adult provision. *Dibbâ rāʿâ* // spies’ bad report of the land. *Ketōnet passîm*: Mari garment parallel; AV “many colors” from LXX/Vulg. Jacob’s doting repeats the schism he suffered; syntax may allow brothers’ hate directed at Jacob as well. Dreams without God’s voice — ambiguity hubris vs prophecy; Hamilton: unsuspecting prophecy of destiny. Escalation of animosity (maligning, favoritism, dreams) continues Isaac–Ishmael / Jacob–Esau pattern — not three source traditions. Midianites/Ishmaelites as overlapping terms (Judg 8), not J/E conflation; brothers sell for twenty shekels (OB average male-slave price; fits Lev 27:5 age band). Joseph later says “sold” (45:4) but “stolen” to cupbearer (40:15) — deliberate adjustment, not dual tradition. Ironic goat-blood deception of the deceiver of Isaac.

**Judah–Tamar (38) and Potiphar (39).** Ch. 38 chronologically only possible between ages 17 and 30; linguistic/thematic links to 37 (recognize; goat; go down). *Yahweh was with Joseph* encapsulates story theology; only narrator uses YHWH (never characters); name at most uncertain moment. Blessing of Potiphar’s house fulfills 12:3a (// Laban via Jacob). Adultery refusal: *hārāʿâ haggeḏōlâ*, sin against God; second clothing loss. Against J/E divine-name split in 39–41: YHWH = narrator (presence/blessing); Elohim = Joseph speaking to Egyptians.

**Dreams and rise (40–43).** Providence in two modes: (1) interpreting life’s happenings as God’s control (45:5–8; 50:20); (2) conviction about God every time he interprets a dream (40:8; 41:16, 25, 28, 32).

**Revelation and remnant (44–45).** Judah’s plea longest human speech in Genesis; spiritual metamorphosis — once engineered sale, now offers self as slave for Benjamin. Self-revelation: “not you who sent me… but God”; *miḥyâ* life-preservation; remnant/survivors — more at stake than twelve lives: “the plan of redemption announced first to his great grandfather.” Clothes/silver gifts invert ch. 37 stripping.

**Settlement and testament (46–50).** Beer-sheba theophany permits Egypt for Jacob (denied Isaac). Agrarian program as fruit of Jacob’s blessing on Pharaoh. Crossed hands: Ephraim over Manasseh — another primogeniture disregard. Joseph’s bones / death at 110; NT appropriation of Joseph follows ch. 50 in Hamilton’s plan. Hamilton resists source-critical dismemberment of the unified literary design (Rudolph, Coats, Alter, et al.).

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Joseph is the bridge from the patriarchal age to the national story: his descent into Egypt sets up the Exodus, and his providential reading of his own suffering ("God intended it for good," 50:20) is Genesis's clearest statement of how divine sovereignty works through, not against, human wrongdoing. In the Zohar this providential, world-sustaining role is given metaphysical depth: as Yesod, the Tsaddiq, Joseph is *literally* the channel through which the world is "nourished" and held in being — the righteous human on whom, the Zohar says (with Prov 10:25), the world stands.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- The Joseph narrative is **not independently attested**; critical scholarship treats it as a novella (a "diaspora tale" akin to Esther and Daniel) shaped in the monarchic or post-exilic period, reflecting Egyptian local color of uncertain date. Source criticism finds J and E strands (the Ishmaelite/Midianite traders doublet, the Reuben/Judah doublet).
- The **two-Messiah tradition** (Mashiaḥ ben Yosef vs. ben David) is a later development the Zohar inherits and elaborates.
- Joseph/Yusuf's near-identical role across Genesis and the Qur'an makes him a frequent locus of comparative Jewish–Christian–Islamic study.

## Islamic Reception (Bukhari Evidence)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Book 55 (Prophets), Nos. 597–603. M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### The Most Honorable Lineage

Two hadiths establish Joseph as the supreme exemplar of prophetic lineage:
- "The most honorable person is Joseph, Allah's Prophet, the son of Allah's Prophet, the son of Allah's Prophet, the son of Allah's Khalil" (4:55:597, Abu Huraira — the four-generation chain: Joseph → Jacob → Isaac → Abraham)
- "The honorable, the son of the honorable, the son of the honorable, (was) Joseph, the son of Jacob! the son of Isaac, the son of Abraham" (4:55:603, Ibn Umar)

When Abu Huraira pressed further and Muhammad listed himself, he was told "you are asking me about nobility" — implying Joseph's family chain is unmatched for concentrated prophetic lineage.

### Patience as Paradigm

The Prophet himself measured his own capacity for patient endurance against Joseph's standard: "If I were to stay in prison (for a period equal to) the stay of Joseph (prison) and then the offer of freedom came to me, then I would have accepted it" (4:55:601, Abu Huraira — said in context of praising Lot). This elevates Joseph as the primary Islamic exemplar of patience in unjust suffering.

### Drought and Famine Association

The Prophet invoked Joseph's era as a paradigm of divine punishment through famine: "O Allah! Let them suffer from years (of drought) similar to that inflicted during the life-time of Joseph" (4:55:600, Abu Huraira — praying against the Mudar tribe). The seven-year famine of Genesis is here absorbed into Islamic prophetic memory as a template for divine chastisement.

### Aisha Compared to Joseph's Female Companions

The Prophet compared Aisha (and women who insist on their viewpoint despite evidence) to "the female companions of Joseph" (4:55:598–599, Abu Huraira) — a pointed reference to Zulaikha's household.
