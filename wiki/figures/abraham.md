# Abraham

**Biographical Overview**:
The foundational patriarch of Israel (originally Abram), called by God from Ur of the Chaldeans (Gen 11:31–12:1) to journey to a land God would show him. He received the covenant promises of land, descendants as numerous as the stars/dust/sand, and blessing to all nations (Gen 12:1-3; 15; 17). God changed his name to Abraham ("father of a multitude," Gen 17:5). He entered covenants (Gen 15 ritual, Gen 17 circumcision as sign), fathered Ishmael through Hagar (Gen 16) and Isaac through Sarah (Gen 21), faced the Akedah test (Gen 22), negotiated for Sodom (Gen 18), encountered Melchizedek (Gen 14), and died at 175 (Gen 25:7-8). He is portrayed as sojourner, altar-builder, and intercessor.

From [[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]] (NICOT Genesis): Abraham's story (Gen 11:27–25:11, the sixth *tôlĕdôt*) is the heart of the patriarchal narrative. Hamilton stresses his call as divine initiative breaking into a pagan context (Ur/Chaldeans), the unconditional nature of the promises despite human failure (e.g., the Hagar episode, the wife-sister deceptions in 12 and 20), and the dual covenant ratifications. The Akedah (Gen 22) is the climactic test of faith; Abraham's obedience ("here I am") and provision of the ram underscore substitution and divine provision. Hamilton notes Abraham as "righteous" by faith (Gen 15:6) yet still needing growth in trust (e.g., the laughter in 17:17 and Sarah's in 18:12). See densified **Hamilton (NICOT) reception** below.

**Primary Source Appearances**:
- **Genesis 11-25**: The narrative of his call, journeys, family tensions (Lot, Hagar/Ishmael, Isaac), covenants, and tests. Hamilton divides into call (12:1-9), sojourns and deceptions (12:10–13:18), covenant and Hagar (14–16), covenant renewal and circumcision (17), Sodom intercession and birth of Isaac (18–21), Akedah (22), wife for Isaac and death (23–25).
- **Hebrews 11:8-19**: The paradigm of living by faith as an alien and stranger, waiting for the heavenly city. His faith is demonstrated in his obedience to leave his homeland, his sojourning, and his willingness to sacrifice Isaac, trusting in God's power to raise him from the dead. (See also Melchizedek encounter in Heb 7.)
- **Galatians 3 & Romans 4**: Paul uses Abraham as the paramount example of justification by faith apart from works of the law, quoting Gen. 15:6 ("Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness"). In Galatians, Abraham's true "seed" is identified as Christ and those incorporated into Him by faith.
- **Other**: James 2:21-23 (justified by works when offering Isaac, complementing Paul); 1 Peter allusions to Sarah's obedience.

**Tradition-Specific Reception**:
- **Jewish**: Abraham as first monotheist, model of *ḥesed* and hospitality (e.g., the three visitors in Gen 18), father of the covenant people. Rabbinic midrash expands his early life (idols, Ur furnace). The Akedah is central to Rosh Hashanah liturgy and Jewish martyrdom traditions. Philo allegorizes him as the soul's journey from sense to virtue.
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)**: At the **Sod** level, Abraham is the human embodiment of the fourth [[sefirot|sefirah]], **Ḥesed** (Lovingkindness) — the right arm of the divine body, the "south," the boundless flow of grace ("Abraham My lover," Isa 41:8, read as Abraham's identification with the loving side of God). His life literally *enacts* Ḥesed: his hospitality, his intercession for Sodom (the lovingkindness that Noah lacked — see [[noah|Noah]]), and his outward, expansive journey "to the land I will show you" are the descent of divine grace into the world. In the patriarchal triad of the second sefirotic triad, Abraham (Ḥesed/right) is balanced by [[isaac|Isaac]] (Gevurah/left) and synthesized by [[jacob|Jacob]] (Tif'eret/center).
  - *Lekh Lekha (Zohar Vol II)*: the Zohar dwells on Abraham's **[[circumcision|circumcision]]** (Gen 17) as the decisive turning point — "*before Abraham was circumcised, one rung spoke with him* (the outer grade, Shaddai); *once he was circumcised, all those rungs rested upon him*." Circumcision is his entry into the covenant of [[sefirot|Yesod]] that opens him to the **full sefirotic influx** and the higher revelation. The Zohar also reads "Abraham, who aroused from the East" (Isa 41:2) as Ḥesed drawing the lower world (Malkhut/[[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]) into a "sea," and treats the indwelling [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] as "abiding constantly with [[sarah|Sarah]]."
  - *Va-Yera*: the **Akedah** (Gen 22) stages the cosmic emergence of judgment — "water (Abraham/Ḥesed) versus fire ([[isaac|Isaac]]/Gevurah)… until Jacob appeared and everything harmonized." Abraham the compassionate father "turned cruel" only so that the left side (Din) could manifest and then be reconciled in the central pillar. See [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah]], [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]], [[sefirot|Ten Sefirot]].

### Pritzker Vol. II reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

Locus-anchored densification from the fidelity reingest of Pritzker *Zohar* II (Lekh Lekha–Va-Yetse body; Matt English). Claims below are attributed to the Zohar body, not bare wiki voice.

**Lekh lekha — self-refinement, conduits, South/Ḥesed.** In the Zohar body (Pritzker II, 1:78a), *Lekh lekha* is read as “Go for yourself, to refine yourself, to perfect your rung” / “to know yourself, to refine yourself.” God grants Abraham “a spirit of wisdom, so he discovered and tested the conduits of the inhabited world” (powers of stars/constellations); he fails at the central point of Canaan until God reveals it (1:78a–78b). The four promises map right / left / middle / land of Israel: “Here is the throne of four legs, all comprised in Abraham” (1:78b). Journeying “toward the Negev—the South, share of Abraham” (1:80a); he is “right hand of the world.” Two altars mark revealed vs. concealed rungs (1:80a). “Do not read *be-hibbare’am*… but rather *be-Avraham*, through Abraham” (1:86a–86b) — the world established through him.

**Egypt as refinement (not fall).** In the Zohar body (1:83a), R. Shim’on: “If Abram had not gone down to Egypt and been refined there first, he would not have become the share of the blessed Holy One” — contrast Adam and Noah, who were seduced; Abraham is not. Descent without permission is linked (R. Yehudah) to four hundred years’ enslavement (1:81b–82a). He receives *nefesh*, then *ruaḥ*, then *neshamah* via altars and journeys; returns “very heavily laden” (1:83a–83b). [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] abides with [[sarah|Sarah]] in Egypt; “She is my sister” joins the literal claim to Prov 7:4, speech to Wisdom/Shekhinah (1:82a).

**Melchizedek encounter.** After the war of kings, Melchizedek (two-worlds reading) blesses Abram and receives the tenth (1:87a–87b); see [[melchizedek|Melchizedek]].

**Before / after circumcision — speech and seed.** In the Zohar body (1:89a), “Before Abraham was circumcised, one rung spoke with him… *maḥazeh*… Once he was circumcised, all those rungs rested upon this rung.” Va-Yera restates: before, speech on a low rung with no high rungs; after, “voice was revealed, joining speech” and “YHVH appeared to him” (1:98a); “As soon as he was circumcised, all was revealed—and Shekhinah rested upon him completely” (1:98b–99a). “Abram does not engender; Abraham does engender” — while Abram he engenders below ([[ishmael|Ishmael]]); after name and covenant he engenders above ([[isaac|Isaac]]) (1:90a–90b; cf. 1:109b–110a: Isaac as “fire from water”). At ninety-nine, *I am El Shaddai*: uncircumcised marked *shin–dalet*; circumcision reveals *yod*, completing *Shaddai* (1:95b). After the *brit* he stands erect, fearless (1:96a–96b). See [[circumcision|Circumcision]].

**Akedah completes Ḥesed with Gevurah.** In the Zohar body (1:119b–120a), “Elohim tested Abraham—not Isaac… previously Abraham had contained no judgment at all. Now water was embraced by fire.” *Et Abraham* = Isaac in low power until binding crowns him; “Then division became apparent: water versus fire… until Jacob appeared and everything harmonized—triad of patriarchs completed” (1:120a). Compassion transforms to judgment at the binding: “not appear as a father but rather as his adversary” (1:120a); double “Abraham! Abraham!” marks incomplete then complete (1:120b). Hospitality even in post-circumcision pain; water-fetching “buttress[es] that rung holding water” (1:101a–102b). Intercession for Sodom begins with fifty and stops at ten — partial justice vs. Moses who “did not budge” (1:106a). See [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah]].

**Ḥayyei Sarah — days, Machpelah, Keturah.** Abraham “coming into days” = rung-by-rung approach into “supernal days… mystery of faith,” blessed “with everything” (1:129a). He alone (as destined owner) sees light and Garden opening at Machpelah; Ephron sees darkness (1:127a–128a). Gives all to Isaac including Sarah’s image / “mystery of supernal faith” so fire and water complete; gifts to sons of concubines = low impure rungs sent eastward (1:133b). Does not bless Isaac himself so Esau would not inherit those blessings; God’s blessing after Abraham’s death; Abraham’s image remains stamped in Isaac (1:135a; Toledot).
- **Christian (evangelical, per Hamilton and NT sources)**: Exemplar of faith (Rom 4, Gal 3, Heb 11); recipient of unconditional covenant fulfilled in Christ (the "seed"). Hamilton emphasizes the promises' universal scope (blessing to nations) and the Akedah as supreme test of unreserved obedience and divine provision (with NT center on Abraham’s faith/works rather than Isaac-as-Christ typology as primary). In Hebrews, pilgrim faith; in Paul, justification by faith; in James, faith completed by works.
- **Islamic**: Ibrāhīm, model monotheist and builder (with Ishmael) of the Kaaba (Quran 2:124-141; 3:95-97; 37:99-113). The Akedah is often attributed to Ishmael. Venerated as *Khalīl Allāh* (friend of God).
- **Samaritan**: Central to their Pentateuch; Mount Gerizim as true site of sacrifice (vs. Jewish Moriah/Jerusalem).
- **Critical scholarship**: Historicity debated (patriarchal narratives as etiological or late compositions); ANE parallels (Nuzi customs for adoption/covenant, but Hamilton cautions against over-reliance).

### Hamilton (NICOT) reception (2026-07-18)

Locus-anchored densification from [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Hamilton, Genesis 1–17 (NICOT)]] and [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|Hamilton, Genesis 18–50 (NICOT)]]. Claims attributed to Hamilton.

**Primeval hinge and election.** For Hamilton, Gen 1–11 (Babylonia) → 12–36 (Palestine) → 37–50 (Egypt) brackets the world-history frame; election of Abraham is so that “the nations of the earth … might find the knowledge of God and his blessing.” After 1–11’s alienation, chs. 12ff. are “Reconciliation Regained” / “Hope Regained,” not Paradise Regained: covenant with a family augments the Noachic covenant with humankind; Abraham’s obedient model contrasts sorry models before him. Four-fifths of Genesis treat four generations (Abraham–Joseph) vs one-fifth on twenty (Adam–Abraham).

**Call and heptad of blessing (12:1–3).** First divine words open with imperative + triple object (// final imperative stack at 22:1). Command and promises antedate the *berîṯ* of ch. 15; faith already operates. Seven-clause blessing: great *gôy* (nationhood), bless you (against Sarai’s barrenness), great name (contrast Babel self-naming — gift not achievement), and climactic “by you all earth’s clans blessed.” Hamilton retains Niphal *niḇreḵû* as **passive** (not “bless themselves”); illustrations include Laban and Potiphar blessed because of the patriarch’s family. Silent obedience; Lot as “presumed heir” teasing motif until land-to-seed at Shechem eliminates him.

**Wife-sister, Lot, and military Abram (12–14).** Egypt sojourn: half-truth sister ruse; Pharaoh takes Sarai — actual adultery in Hamilton’s reading of 12:19; Yahweh’s plagues; Egyptian more morally sensitive than the patriarch; pattern anticipates Exodus. Post-Lot separation, Abram becomes magnanimous; land promise expands to Abram *and* seed forever (13:14–18). Gen 14: only military activity; Melchizedek of Salem blesses and receives tithe of booty (// Heb 7); Abram refuses Sodom’s spoils (ANE straw/string formula).

**Gen 15: faith credited; unilateral covenant.** First *heʾĕmîn*; *ḥāšaḇ…ṣeḏāqâ* as Yahweh reckoning faith as deciding factor (Rom 4 / Gal 3; Jas 2 complementary for Hamilton). Animal-pieces ceremony: only deity-symbol (smoking pot, flaming torch) passes between — unilateral gift, not self-curse on God; 400-year exile prophecy; land bounds river of Egypt → Euphrates.

**Hagar / Gen 17.** Ch. 16: Sarai’s surrogacy as initiative to force the promise; Abram’s “false neutrality.” Ch. 17 reconfirms one ongoing covenant (Hiphil *qûm*), not a second bilateral covenant replacing ch. 15; demands of circumcision **ringed by** promise speeches. El Shaddai and name-change; walk before me and be *tāmîm* as ethical charge within unilateral covenant.

**Hospitality, intercession, Gerar (18–20).** Daytime visit contrasts cool-of-day garden visit to Adam (3:8); hospitality vs Sodom’s hosting. Abraham precursor of mediating prophet: does not debate Sodom’s guilt or preach repentance to Sodom, but asks divine mercy. “Voice of conditionality and mutuality is occasionally heard” (18:19) amid overwhelming unilaterality. Post-covenant wife-sister (ch. 20) shows spiritual maturation does not erase precovenant patterns; first biblical “prophet” = intercessor who prays for Abimelech despite duplicity.

**Akedah (22).** Frame is divine *test* (*nāsâ*), not demonic temptation; real test = relinquishing the *promise-bearer*, not only a beloved son; continuity with Ishmael’s dismissal (21). Whole narrative (ram, halt, subsequent promises) frames God as merciful and Abraham’s race as owing existence to mercy + obedience — not v. 2 alone. “We will return” read with Heb 11:17–19 as faith that God can restore Isaac. Isaac’s wood // John 19:17; cooperating faith. *yirʾat ʾĕlōhîm* = unreserved obedience that yields even the promised future; Yahweh-yireh names **God’s provision**, not Abraham’s obedience. Hamilton defends integrity of 22:15–18: novel *conditionality* (“because you… obeyed”) postponed until last divine speech so that performance is subordinated to promise/grace; reappears at 26:5 for Isaac. NT appropriation centers Abraham (faith/works/obedience); Isaac-as-Christ typology is late/allusory — “the son is spared, but in the Christian story the Son is not spared.”

**Machpelah, Rebekah, death (23–25).** Ch. 23’s center = costly full-price acquisition of first legal foothold in promised land (model for later costly possession). Ch. 24: longest Genesis chapter is about marriage; “I will bless” (12:2) actualized as “Yahweh had blessed” (24:1); endogamy protects inheritance, not confessional Yahwism of the bride. All-to-Isaac after Keturah’s sons sent east echoes all-to-God at Akedah. Isaac and Ishmael bury him; God blesses Isaac immediately after Abraham’s death.

Sources: [[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot]], [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot]], [[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]]. Related controversies: [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah]], [[covenant|Covenant]].

**Theological and Narrative Significance**:
Abraham is the answer to the primeval crisis (Gen 1–11): from universal degeneration to the particular family through whom "all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (12:3). His story structures the book (the *tôlĕdôt* of Terah/Abraham). Theologically, he models election by grace, faith as trust in promise (credited as righteousness, 15:6), covenant as divine commitment (unconditional yet with sign), and the tension of sojourning (land promised but not possessed). The younger-son reversals, deceptions, and Akedah highlight divine sovereignty over human frailty. In the canon, he is father of faith (NT), first Hebrew, and type of the believer's pilgrimage.

**Historicity and Interpretive Controversies**:
- **Ur and migration**: Traditional early date vs. critical late (Amorite or later); Hamilton defends substantial historicity within 2nd-millennium ANE context while noting literary shaping.
- **Covenant(s)**: One covenant in stages (Gen 15/17) or two? Source criticism (J vs. P) vs. canonical unity (Hamilton favors unity).
- **Akedah (Gen 22)**: Historical event? Test of faith vs. polemic against child sacrifice? Typological (Christ, substitution)? Ethical issues (divine command to kill son). Hamilton sees it as supreme test revealing Abraham's fear of God and God's provision.
- **Wife-sister stories (12, 20, 26)**: Duplicate traditions? Etiological for Gerar/Philistines? Moral problems for patriarchs. Hamilton treats as distinct episodes showing recurring failure and divine protection.
- **Melchizedek encounter (14)**: Historical king-priest or later insertion? Typological (Heb 7: superior priesthood).

**Influence on Commentary Traditions**:
- **Hamilton (Genesis)**: Abraham as the pivotal figure resolving primeval failure; detailed philological and theological exegesis of call, covenants, Akedah as faith's climax, and providence in family stories. Defends evangelical readings against source criticism while engaging ANE data.
- **Philo**: Allegorical journey of the soul (from astrology/Ur to virtue).
- **Patristic**: Abraham as type of faith and hospitality; Akedah as prefiguring cross (e.g., Isaac carrying wood).
- **Reformation**: Justification by faith (Luther on Gen 15:6; Rom 4).
- **Modern critical**: Form-critical (Gunkel: saga cycles); tradition-historical (von Rad: Yahwist epic); canonical (Childs).
- **Jewish**: Midrashic expansions; Akedah in liturgy as merit for Israel.
- Cross-tradition: Comparisons of allegory (Philo/Origen) vs. literal-historical (Hamilton); Abraham as common father in interfaith dialogue (Judaism/Christianity/Islam).

## Islamic Reception (Bukhari Evidence)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Book 55 (Prophets). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Muhammad's Resemblance to Abraham

The Prophet declared: "I resemble prophet Abraham more than any of his offspring does" (4:55:607, Abu Huraira; repeated 4:55:647). The claim is cosmological rather than genealogical — Muhammad is the closest embodiment of the Abrahamic original, not merely a descendant.

### Al-Khalil ("Friend of God")

Abraham is identified as "Allah's Khalil" in the honorific genealogical chain: Joseph is called "the most honorable," son of Jacob, son of Isaac, "son of Allah's Khalil" (4:55:597, Abu Huraira). The *Khalil* title — also applied to Muhammad in contexts where the Prophet declines it for himself to avoid rivalry — is Abraham's defining Islamic epithet.

### First Dressed on the Day of Resurrection

"The first to be dressed will be Abraham" (4:55:656, Ibn Abbas). At the resurrection, before any other human being is clothed, Abraham receives his garments — a preeminence that echoes his role as the father of the prophetic line.

### The Ka'ba's Primacy over Jerusalem

"Al-Masjid-ul-Haram was built first; Al-Masjid-ul-Aqsa was next; forty (years) between them" (4:55:636, Abu Dhar). Though not explicitly attributing the Ka'ba's foundation to Abraham in this hadith, the context positions the Ka'ba's antiquity (associated Islamically with Abraham and Ishmael, Q 2:127) as prior to the Jerusalem sanctuary.
