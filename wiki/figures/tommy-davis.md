# Tommy Davis

**Tommy Davis** is the son of actress **Anne Archer** and the former **chief spokesperson for the Church of Scientology International** (approximately 2004–2011). He is one of the central figures in Lawrence Wright's *Going Clear*, serving as both the Church's primary interface with Wright's fact-checking process and a subject of the book itself.

## Biographical Overview

Davis attended **Columbia University for one semester** before dropping out to join the Sea Org (approximately 1990). His mother Anne Archer is a prominent Scientologist; Davis told Wright she "took him to Scientology" and was instrumental in his entry into the Church.

He signed a **billion-year Sea Org contract** at approximately age 18 and tattooed the Sea Org logo on his arm — Miscavige reportedly berated him for the "copyright violation." He was known internally as "Tom" before changing to "Tommy." In the early 2000s, he was assigned to handle Tom Cruise — managing press and access — before becoming the Church's public spokesperson.

## Career as Spokesperson

Davis was the public face of Scientology through some of the organization's most turbulent media periods:

- **BBC Panorama confrontation** (March 2007): Famously confronted reporter John Sweeney, following him and his crew with a video camera; the BBC produced footage of Sweeney losing his temper. Davis followed up with an "ambush" video in which he challenged Sweeney's journalistic ethics.
- **CNN disconnection denial**: Appeared on CNN and denied that the disconnection policy existed — an assertion Wright identifies as a direct lie, given Deborah Rennard's parents' experience.
- **New Yorker fact-checking meeting** (September 2010): Arrived with wife Jessica Feshbach, four attorneys, and 48 three-ring binders containing 971 questions/responses for Wright's article. Acknowledged that the musical chairs incident had occurred. Claimed that homophobic passages in Hubbard's works were inserted by "a bigot" (not Hubbard) — a claim Wright characterizes as implausible.
- **Hubbard military records defense**: Presented what two St. Louis archivists subsequently identified as a forged "Notice of Separation" document; insisted Hubbard was a decorated war hero despite contradictions with the official Navy record.

Davis "blew" (escaped from the Sea Org) to Las Vegas in March 2007, was recovered, and returned. By 2011 he and Feshbach had stopped representing the Church and were living in Texas. Davis told Wright their divorce was unrelated to the Church; ex-colleagues dispute this.

## Marriage

Married **Nadine van Hootegem**, a Sea Org member who became "a non-person" in the Church's eyes after the Katie Holmes situation. Later married **Jessica Feshbach** (also a Sea Org spokesperson), who co-handled press with him.

## Significance

Davis exemplifies a pattern Wright traces throughout the book: a second-generation Scientologist raised in a celebrity/Church household, deeply committed, who eventually exits. His career also illustrates the Church's PR strategy: fronting articulate, well-connected young figures while Miscavige and senior executives remain inaccessible and unaccountable.
