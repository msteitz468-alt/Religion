# Amelia Collins

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 9245–9278.*

## Biographical Overview

American [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]] of Lutheran background. She became a Bahá'í in 1919 and was elected onto the American national spiritual Assembly in 1924, serving on that body for over twenty years (lines 9247–9252).

Wealthy as a result of her marriage, she was able to make large donations to various Bahá'í projects (lines 9252–9254), including:
- The Western Pilgrim House in [[haifa|Haifa]]
- The buildings for the original Geyserville Summer School in the USA
- The superstructure of the [[shrine-of-the-bab|Shrine of the Báb]]
- The land for the future Bahá'í Temple on Mount Carmel
- The International Archives building

She travelled extensively in Latin America and Europe to promote the Faith (lines 9264–9266).

## Administrative Role

In January 1951 she was appointed as Vice-President of the newly established International Bahá'í Council (lines 9269–9272). Already privately named as a Hand of the Cause in 1947 by Shoghi Effendi, she was publicly appointed to this rank in December 1951 (lines 9274–9277).
