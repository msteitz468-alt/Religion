# Bilal ibn Rabah

## Biographical Overview

Bilal ibn Rabah appears in [[sahih-al-bukhari]] primarily in his functional role as the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s first and primary **mu'adhdhin** (caller to prayer). The text does not in this range provide a narrative biography of Bilal — his Ethiopian origin, enslavement, early conversion, or manumission are not in the source range covered and are therefore not attributed here.

## Primary Source Appearances in Sahih al-Bukhari (Books 10–12)

### Institution of the Adhan (Vol. 1, Book 11, Nos. 577–578)

The most theologically significant appearance of Bilal concerns the establishment of the call to prayer in Medina. Two parallel hadiths narrate the event:

**No. 578** (narrated by Ibn 'Umar): When the Muslims settled in Medina they "used to guess the time" of prayer. A communal discussion ensued. Some proposed a bell like the Christians; others a trumpet/horn like the Jews. 'Umar was the first to suggest that a man should call the people vocally. The Prophet then "ordered Bilal to get up and pronounce the Adhan for prayers."

**No. 577** (narrated by Anas): A parallel, briefer account: the people discussed fire and bell as signals, associating each with Jews and Christians, "and then Bilal was ordered to pronounce Adhan for the prayer by saying its wordings twice, and for the Iqama by saying its wordings once."

These are the founding hadiths for the Adhan as an institution. Bilal is not merely among those who could have been chosen — he is the named individual directly commanded.

### The Form of the Adhan: Bilal's Instructions (Nos. 579–581)

The Prophet gave Bilal specific instructions on the call's format. The Adhan's phrases are doubled; the Iqama's are said once — except "Qad-qamat-is-Salat" which, according to one transmission (No. 581), is said twice. This variant is the source of a jurisprudential disagreement between the Hanafi and Shafi'i/Hanbali schools on the Iqama's form.

### Physical Manner During the Adhan (Nos. 606–607)

**No. 607** (narrated by 'Aun bin Abi Juhaifa, from his father): "I saw Bilal turning his face from side to side while pronouncing the Adhan for the prayer." This documents the physical practice of calling.

**No. 606**: Before a prayer at al-Abtah, Bilal "came out with an Anza and planted it in front of Allah's Apostle at al-Abtah and pronounced the Iqama." The Anza (short stick or spear) served as a sutra (screen/barrier) for the Prophet's prayer.

### The Two-Adhan System for Fajr (Nos. 591, 594–596)

Bilal and Ibn Um Maktum served complementary roles for the Fajr prayer. The Prophet said: "Bilal pronounces the Adhan at night, so keep on eating and drinking (Suhur) till Ibn Um Maktum pronounces the Adhan." Bilal's night adhan was intended to wake those performing tahajjud and signal the last portion of permissible suhur; it did not signal the arrival of true dawn. Ibn Um Maktum, who was blind, "would not pronounce the Adhan unless he was told that the day had dawned" (No. 591). The Prophet distinguished the horizontal spread of true dawn from the false vertical dawn (No. 595, Ibn Mas'ud). This system reveals Bilal's role as the regular, night-time caller rather than the dawn-specific caller.

### Journey Incident: Bilal's Failed Vigil (Vol. 1, Book 10, No. 569)

On a journey, "Bilal said, 'I will make you get up.'" He rested against his riding saddle but fell asleep. The Prophet woke when the sun had already risen and said: "O Bilal! What about your statement?" Bilal replied: "I have never slept such a sleep." The Prophet said: "Allah captured your souls when He wished, and released them when He wished. O Bilal! Get up and pronounce the Adhan for the prayer." Bilal called the Adhan; the Prophet performed ablution and prayed. This hadith is significant for jurisprudence on missed prayers and is the only passage in this source showing Bilal's personal fallibility as a mortal.

### Bilal and the Prophet's Fatal Illness (Vol. 1, Book 11, No. 681)

During the Prophet's last illness: "When Allah's Apostle became seriously ill, Bilal came to him for the prayer." Bilal came in his normal role — informing the Prophet when prayer time arrived — until the Prophet could no longer attend, at which point he appointed Abu Bakr as prayer leader.

### 'Id Prayer: Collecting Alms (Vol. 1, Book 12, No. 822)

After an 'Id prayer, when the Prophet asked the women to give charity, "the woman would bring her hand near her neck and take off her necklace and put it in the garment of Bilal." Bilal served as the collection vessel for charitable gifts at this communal event.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Within the Bukhari texts of this range, Bilal functions as the **originary figure of the Adhan** — the human institution through which Islam distinguishes its call to prayer from both Jewish (trumpet/horn) and Christian (bell) practices. The choice of Bilal as the person commanded is recorded as a direct Prophetic order, not a community election, giving the office of mu'adhdhin and the Adhan's form normative Prophetic authority.

The texts in this source range do not develop Bilal's story allegorically or typologically; he is presented as a legal-liturgical actor establishing the Sunnah of the Adhan's format, timing, and physical manner of delivery.
