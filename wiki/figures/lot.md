# Lot

## Biographical Overview
Lot was the nephew of Abraham who traveled with him from Ur to Canaan. After a dispute over grazing land, Lot separated from Abraham and settled in the region of the Jordan valley, eventually residing in Sodom. He was rescued by angels prior to the city's destruction by fire and brimstone.

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Genesis 11-19**: The primary narrative, detailing his relationship with Abraham, his life in Sodom, the angelic visitation, the rescue, the loss of his wife, and the subsequent incestuous origins of Moab and Ammon.
- **2 Peter 2:7-8**: Characterizes Lot as a "righteous man" who was deeply distressed by the sensual conduct of the wicked inhabitants of Sodom.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In the New Testament, particularly in 2 Peter, Lot serves alongside Noah as a paradigm of divine rescue. While the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah stands as the ultimate type of judgment on the ungodly, Lot's rescue demonstrates God's ability to deliver the righteous from trial. The description of Lot as a "righteous man" whose soul was vexed day after day reflects later Jewish traditions (like Wisdom 10:6) that emphasized his piety in a corrupt environment, contrasting with the sometimes ambiguous portrayal of his character in Genesis.

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar, Va-Yera)
At the **Sod** level the Zohar reads the **overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah** as the public execution of **Din** (Gevurah, judgment) upon a society of total corruption — the unleashing of the left side against a place that had wholly given itself to the [[sitra-ahra|Other Side]] (the Sodomites' cruelty and sexual disorder being the opposite of the [[abraham|Ḥesed]] and hospitality Abraham embodies). [[abraham|Abraham]]'s **intercession** (Gen 18) is Ḥesed pleading to temper Din — the right side seeking to restrain the left — though here the measure of judgment prevails. Lot, drawn out before the destruction, is a lesser righteous one "hidden from the Destroyer" (cf. the pattern on [[noah|Noah]]), saved by Abraham's merit rather than fully on his own account. See [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]], [[sefirot|Ten Sefirot]].

### Pritzker Vol. II reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

Locus-anchored densification from Pritzker *Zohar* II body (Matt English).

**Taken for David’s line; imperfect disciple.** In the Zohar body (1:79a–79b, Lekh Lekha), Abram takes Lot because “he foresaw through Holy Spirit that David was destined to issue from him.” Lot “went with him… to learn… though he did not learn so well.” After parting he chooses the Jordan plain and tents toward Sodom (1:84a).

**Separation / idolatry / Holy Spirit.** The quarrel is *riv* spelled *rv* (missing *yod*): Lot sought to revert to local idolatry while Canaanites/Perizzites still dwelled (1:84a). *Mi-qedem* = “from the Primordial One of the world” (parallel Gen 11:2) — turning from that One (1:84a). Abraham: “Please separate… you are not worthy to join me.” While Lot is with Abraham, Holy Spirit departs from Abraham; after parting, it returns (1:85a).

**Sodom rescue — merit, hospitality, Destroyer.** In the Zohar body (Va-Yera), “God remembered Abraham and sent Lot out… it is not written: God remembered Lot” — merit of prior hospitality to the three angels (1:104b). “Because Lot joined Abraham… he learned to render kindness to creatures by imitating Abraham” (1:105a). Lot sees Shekhinah / radiant aura and spells *Adonai* as Abraham did (1:106b–107a). Hospitality order inverted for safety: first house/night, then wash feet — so townspeople would not notice (1:107a). One Destroyer with dual mission (1:106b–107a).

**Wife / behind him.** The verse should read “behind her,” but the Zohar body reads “behind Shekhinah” or behind the Destroyer: “upon seeing the Destroyer, she became a pillar of salt… as long as the Destroyer doesn’t see a person’s face, he can’t destroy him” (1:108a–108b).

**Daughters / Moab / Messiah; Abraham’s silence.** Older daughter (Moab, “from father”) impudent; younger (Ben-Ammi) decorous; older line → Messiah (dotted *vav* reading) (1:109a–111a). Abraham risked life for Lot in Gen 14 yet will not pray him out of Sodom — will not have God forgive for Abraham’s sake after improper conduct (1:111a–111b).

## Hamilton (NICOT) reception (2026-07-18)

Attributed to [[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]] ([[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Genesis 1–17]], [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|18–50]]).

- **Presumed heir then eliminated:** double Lot notation in call narrative as teasing motif; separation (13) — wealth friction; magnanimous Abram; Lot chooses Jordan plain outside Canaan, confirming he is not the seed-heir of 12:7. Captured with Sodom’s kings (14); rescued by “Abram the Hebrew.”
- **Sodom (19):** messengers conceived as human beings, not winged; shares uncle’s courtesy but not concern for Sodom’s future. Offer of daughters inexcusable (// Abraham’s Egypt/Gerar failures) — not exonerated by hospitality custom. Wife dies for ignoring “do not look back.” Deliverance: “An individual who does not deserve to be spared (Lot) is spared because of another (Abraham)” — Abraham is to Lot what the hypothetical righteous remnant would have been to Sodom.
- **Cave/incest:** daughters reverse the father’s earlier sexual use of them without consent. Moab and Ammon origins.
- **2 Pet 2:7–8 “righteous”:** Hamilton: nothing in Gen 18–19 makes the adjective appropriate on narrative grounds; Peter applies it because Abraham argued on righteousness grounds and Lot was delivered — theological reading, not character whitewash of Genesis.
