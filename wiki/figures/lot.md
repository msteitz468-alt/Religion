# Lot

## Biographical Overview
Lot was the nephew of Abraham who traveled with him from Ur to Canaan. After a dispute over grazing land, Lot separated from Abraham and settled in the region of the Jordan valley, eventually residing in Sodom. He was rescued by angels prior to the city's destruction by fire and brimstone.

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Genesis 11-19**: The primary narrative, detailing his relationship with Abraham, his life in Sodom, the angelic visitation, the rescue, the loss of his wife, and the subsequent incestuous origins of Moab and Ammon.
- **2 Peter 2:7-8**: Characterizes Lot as a "righteous man" who was deeply distressed by the sensual conduct of the wicked inhabitants of Sodom.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In the New Testament, particularly in 2 Peter, Lot serves alongside Noah as a paradigm of divine rescue. While the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah stands as the ultimate type of judgment on the ungodly, Lot's rescue demonstrates God's ability to deliver the righteous from trial. The description of Lot as a "righteous man" whose soul was vexed day after day reflects later Jewish traditions (like Wisdom 10:6) that emphasized his piety in a corrupt environment, contrasting with the sometimes ambiguous portrayal of his character in Genesis.

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar, Va-Yera)
At the **Sod** level the Zohar reads the **overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah** as the public execution of **Din** (Gevurah, judgment) upon a society of total corruption — the unleashing of the left side against a place that had wholly given itself to the [[sitra-ahra|Other Side]] (the Sodomites' cruelty and sexual disorder being the opposite of the [[abraham|Ḥesed]] and hospitality Abraham embodies). [[abraham|Abraham]]'s **intercession** (Gen 18) is Ḥesed pleading to temper Din — the right side seeking to restrain the left — though here the measure of judgment prevails. Lot, drawn out before the destruction, is a lesser righteous one "hidden from the Destroyer" (cf. the pattern on [[noah|Noah]]), saved by Abraham's merit rather than fully on his own account. See [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]], [[sefirot|Ten Sefirot]].
