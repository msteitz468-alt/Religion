# Chaitanya

**Chaitanya** (1486–1533 CE), also called **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** ("Great Lord Who Awakens
Consciousness"), was a Bengali Brahmin saint whose ecstatic devotion to [[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]] and
[[radha-krishna-relationship|Rādhā]] gave rise to **Gaudiya Vaishnavism** — one of the most
influential devotional movements in Hindu history, with global reach today through ISKCON.

## Biographical Overview

Born into a Brahmin family in Navadvip (Bengal), Chaitanya received a sound Sanskrit education. At
twenty-two, after making a pilgrimage to Gaya to perform his father's funeral rites, he had a
religious experience of profound intensity. He returned transformed: he stopped teaching grammar and
began leading ecstatic *kirtana* — communal singing and dancing in a trance-state to Krishna's names.
People flocked to him; the movement spread rapidly.

Against his mother's wishes, he settled at **Puri** in Orissa (at the great Jagannatha temple, not at
Vrindavan) so he could stay in contact with her. He suffered **epileptic seizures** during ecstatic
states; Doniger notes that he may have died by drowning while in religious ecstasy.

He converted the Muslim governor of Orissa back to Hinduism (from which the governor had previously
converted) and reportedly converted many Pathans (Afghan Muslims) as well.

## Theological Identity: Dual Avatar

Chaitanya and his followers believed he was an **incarnation of both Krishna and Radha in one body**
— a solution to the theological problem of their separation (*viraha*): in his person, the two could
simultaneously experience the bliss of both sides of the divine couple in union. His golden complexion
(hence "Gauranga," "the Golden One") was explained as Krishna taking on Radha's complexion out of
longing for her love.

This theology is the foundation of **Gaudiya Vaishnavism's** characteristic emotional structure:
the devotee participates in the divine love of Krishna and Radha not as an outsider but by identifying
with Radha's experience.

## The Goswamins: Chaitanya's Main Disciples

After Chaitanya renounced the world, his main disciples were the **Goswamins** — renunciant theologians
who systematized his ecstatic practice into doctrine:

- **Nityananda**: considered the incarnation of Krishna's brother Balarama; in efforts to convert
  Bengali Tantrics, "consorted with prostitutes, drunkards, and others of dubious character" — justified
  by Balarama's known excesses
- **Rupa Goswami**: developed the theology of *raganuga bhakti* ("the devotion that follows from
  passion") — spontaneous, impassioned devotion contrasted with *vaidhi bhakti* (scriptural devotion)
- **Sanatana Goswami**, **Jiva Goswami**: the theological systematizers of the movement in Vrindavan

The Goswamins debated: was Radha **svakiya** (Krishna's wife, "his own") or **parakiya** (his
mistress, "someone else's")? In 1717, the Bengali Vaishnava community officially decided that
**adulterous love (*parakiya*) is orthodox** — that the very transgression of social norms intensifies
the love that serves as the model for the soul's relationship to God.

## The Sahajiyas

A more radical branch of Bengali Vaishnavas — the **Sahajiyas** ("Naturalists") — went further:
they believed Krishna and Radha were united not only in Chaitanya but in every man and every woman.
Their goal was not to *worship* or *imitate* Krishna and Radha (dualistic bhakti) but to **become**
them — to realize both male and female divine powers within their own bodies (monistic, Tantric bhakti).

They praised adulterous love, low-caste love, and unmarried love *because of* their social
transgression — making "addictive vice" (lust) into a "positive virtue" (devotion). Their practice
was the counterpart of the Kapalika antinomianism: transgression sacralized, but now in the register
of erotic devotion rather than skull-bearing.

The mainstream Goswamins reacted against the Sahajiyas by sanitizing the myth: where earlier texts
had the Gopis leave shadow-selves in bed with their husbands while they danced with Krishna, some
Goswamins reversed this — the real Gopis remained with their husbands, and only their shadow selves
went to Krishna.

## The Radhavallabhas

Yet another branch — the **Radhavallabhas** ("Radha's Darlings") — rejected renunciation entirely and
held that Krishna was not the supreme deity but **the servant of Radha**. A British scholar summarized:
"Krishna may do the coolie-work of building the world, but Radha sits as Queen. He is at best but
her Secretary of State." This tradition drew on earlier Bangla poetry (Chandidas, Vidyapati) in which
Radha is more powerful than Krishna.

## Hermeneutical Significance

The Bengali Vaishnava tradition following Chaitanya developed a rich system of **rasa theology**
(*rasika vaisnavism*):

- The devotee chooses which *rasa* (emotion/relational register) to inhabit with Krishna: mother
  (*vatsalya*), lover (*madhurya*), servant (*dasya*), friend (*sakhya*)
- Women, who can be god's mothers and lovers, were considered naturally better suited to bhakti
- Male devotees who identified with the lover role sometimes adopted female dress and simulated
  menstruation monthly — embodying the chosen identity literally

## Influence

- **Gaudiya Vaishnavism** became one of the largest and most institutionally organized Hindu
  devotional movements; it spread across Bengal, Orissa, Manipur, and (from the 20th c.) globally
- **ISKCON** (International Society for Krishna Consciousness, founded 1966 by A. C. Bhaktivedanta
  Swami Prabhupada) is the direct descendant of Chaitanya's movement and the most visible Hindu
  organization in the Western world
