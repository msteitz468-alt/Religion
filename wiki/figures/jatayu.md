# Jaṭāyu

**Jaṭāyu** (Jatayu) is the aged king of the vultures in the [[ramayana|Rāmāyaṇa]], a lifelong friend of [[dasaratha|Daśaratha]] and the witness who sacrifices his life resisting [[ravana|Rāvaṇa]]'s abduction of [[sita|Sītā]].

## In William Buck's Retelling

In Buck's Rāmāyaṇa, Jaṭāyu is described as 60,000 years old and a lifelong companion of Daśaratha — "while Dasaratha lived I was his old friend, we flew side by side across the sky" (line 6968). When Rāvaṇa carries off Sītā, Jaṭāyu attacks him in mid-air and fights him to a standstill until Rāvaṇa severs his wings. He falls dying to the forest floor. Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa find him still alive; he gives his dying testimony — Sītā was taken south by Rāvaṇa — and then dies in Rāma's arms. Rāma performs Jaṭāyu's funeral rites as he would for a father, "in Dasaratha's name."

Indra's tribute to Jaṭāyu encapsulates his theological role: he "over and over, again and again gives his life for what's right, never once wondering should he do it or not" (lines 7238–7240). He represents righteous self-sacrifice as the purest form of action — dharmic reflex without deliberation.

## Significance

Jaṭāyu's testimony is the pivot of the narrative: without his dying words, Rāma would not have known Sītā was taken to Laṅkā. His death also establishes that Rāvaṇa's act was witnessed and condemned even at the cosmic level, since the king of the birds is a creature of the sky and therefore a divine witness.
