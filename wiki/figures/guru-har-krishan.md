## Biographical Overview

Guru Har Krishan was the eighth Guru of the Sikhs, the second son of [[guru-har-rai|Guru Har Rai]]. He received the title in 1661 at the age of six, after his elder brother Ram Rai was disinherited by their father. McLeod describes his period plainly: "Just what he was doing in Delhi continues the tale of obscurity which covers most of the mid-seventeenth-century Panth. It is, however, certain that he was in Delhi (there is unanimity on this point) and that he died there from smallpox" in 1664 (McLeod ~L218–222).

## Succession: *Bābā Bakāle*

Guru Har Krishan's dying words were the cryptic *Bābā Bakāle*, from which it was concluded that the ninth Guru would be found in the village of Bakala (between Amritsar and the Beas river). Tradition records that twenty-two claimants converged on Bakala, creating a crisis of succession resolved by the merchant Makhan Shah (McLeod ~L246–265).

## Historical Significance

Although his own tenure was brief and historically obscure, Har Krishan's dying pronouncement launched the process by which [[guru-tegh-bahadur|Guru Tegh Bahadur]] was identified as his successor.
