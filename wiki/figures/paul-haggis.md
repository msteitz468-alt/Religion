# Paul Haggis

**Paul Haggis** (born 1953, London, Ontario, Canada) is an Academy Award-winning screenwriter and director (*Crash*, *Million Dollar Baby*, *Casino Royale*, *Flags of Our Fathers*) who joined Scientology at age 22 (1975) and spent **34 years** in the Church before resigning publicly on **August 19, 2009**. He is the primary narrative frame of Lawrence Wright's *Going Clear*.

## Engagement with Scientology

Haggis joined Scientology in London, Ontario after being introduced via a dissemination drill by Sandy Kent (later Anderson). He was drawn by the description of communication courses as a way to overcome shyness and improve writing. Over 34 years he progressed through auditing, completed significant OT levels, and became a Celebrity Centre regular. He appears on the cover of *Celebrity Centre* magazine (1986).

Despite his prominence and contributions, Haggis reports never reaching OT VII/VIII — his career demands and intermittent finances interrupted his Bridge progress.

## Break with the Church

Two triggers, both reported in *Going Clear*:

**1. Proposition 8 (2008):** When the Church of Scientology lent its official support to California's anti-gay-marriage Proposition 8, Haggis — with two lesbian daughters — found this unconscionable. He spent ten months emailing [[tommy-davis|Tommy Davis]] demanding the Church retract its support and address the issue.

**2. Disconnection:** Davis appeared on CNN and denied the disconnection policy existed. Haggis knew this was false from direct experience: his wife Deborah Rennard's parents had been declared Suppressive Persons (August 2006 goldenrod) and Deborah had been forced to disconnect from them.

After online research — including the anonymous website **Exscientologykids.org** (founded by Jenna Miscavige Hill) and accounts from the St. Petersburg Times' "Truth Rundown" series — Haggis sent his **resignation letter to Tommy Davis** on August 19, 2009. Marty Rathbun published it on his blog; it received 55,000 hits in one afternoon.

## Significance Within the Scientology Studies Wiki

Haggis is unusual among celebrity defectors in that he (a) resigned publicly with a written explanation, (b) named specific institutional failures, (c) continued to speak to the press despite knowing the Church's reputation for retaliation, and (d) cooperated fully with Lawrence Wright's two-year reporting process.

His arc — sincere convert, long-term practitioner, gradual disillusionment, public break — illustrates the book's central argument about Scientology's capacity to hold educated, reflective people through a combination of genuine insight, incremental commitment, and social structure.

Wright quotes his self-assessment: "I was in a cult for thirty-four years. Everyone else could see it. I don't know why I couldn't."
