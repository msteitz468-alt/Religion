# Poseidon (Neptune)

**Poseidon** (Roman **Neptune**) is the god of the **sea, earthquakes ("the Earth-Shaker"), and horses** —
brother of [[zeus|Zeus]] and [[hades|Hades]], with whom he divided the cosmos, receiving the **sea**.

## Myths and Character

Wielder of the **trident**, moody and violent like his element. He contended with [[athena|Athena]] for
**Athens** (she won with the olive tree, he offered a salt spring/horse) and pursued his great vendetta
against **Odysseus** for the blinding of his son the Cyclops Polyphemus (the engine of the [[homer|Odyssey]]).
Father of many monstrous and heroic offspring; said to have created the **horse**.

## Cult and Roman Identification

Worshipped at the **Isthmus of Corinth** (the Isthmian Games) and by sailors throughout the Greek world. As
**Neptune** he was an old Italic water-god assimilated to Poseidon, honored at the Neptunalia.

## Myths in Detail

**The contest for Athens.** When the new city of Athens sought a patron, Poseidon and [[athena|Athena]]
competed for it. Poseidon struck the Acropolis rock with his **trident** and brought forth a spring (salt
water) or the first horse; Athena planted the **olive tree**. The gods (or the citizens) judged the
olive — peace and prosperity — the greater gift, and Athena won. The salt sea Poseidon left, and his anger,
remained in the well of the Erechtheion.

**The wrath against Odysseus.** Poseidon is the great divine antagonist of the **[[homer|Odyssey]]**:
Odysseus blinds the Cyclops **Polyphemus**, Poseidon's son, who curses him; thereafter the sea-god harries
Odysseus's homeward voyage for ten years with storms and shipwreck, the engine of the whole epic. He is
likewise hostile to Troy (having built its walls for King Laomedon, who cheated him of his pay), yet his
fury is set within the limits the [[fate-moirai|Fates]] and [[zeus|Zeus]] allow.

**Creator of the horse; father of monsters.** Lord of horses (Poseidon Hippios), he produced the first
horse and the winged **Pegasus** (from the slain Medusa, whom he had lain with in Athena's temple — earning
Athena's curse that transformed her). His many unions produced giants and monsters as turbulent as the sea
itself.
