# Osiris

**Osiris** is the [[egyptian-religion|Egyptian]] god of the **dead, of resurrection, fertility, and rightful
kingship** — the murdered-and-revived god whose myth is the very heart of Egyptian religion. Eldest child of
[[geb|Geb]] and [[nut|Nut]], brother and husband of [[isis|Isis]].

## The Osiris Myth (in detail)

**The good king.** Osiris was the first king of Egypt, who taught humanity agriculture, law, and the worship
of the gods, civilizing the land with his sister-wife **[[isis|Isis]]**. His jealous brother **[[set|Set]]**
plotted his death.

**The murder.** Set secretly took the **exact measurements of Osiris's body** and built a magnificent
ornamented **chest**. At a banquet he offered it as a prize to whoever fit it perfectly. When Osiris lay down
in it, Set and his conspirators slammed the lid, sealed it with molten lead, and **cast it into the Nile**,
which bore it out to sea. (It drifted to Byblos in Lebanon, where it lodged in a tree that grew around it.)

**Isis's search.** The grieving [[isis|Isis]] wandered the world searching for her husband, finally
recovering the chest from the pillar of the king of Byblos's palace and bringing the body home. But while she
was away, **Set found the body and tore it into fourteen pieces**, scattering them the length of Egypt.

**The gathering and the conception of Horus.** Isis, with her sister **[[nephthys|Nephthys]]** and the jackal-
god **[[anubis|Anubis]]** (who invented embalming for the occasion), searched out and **reassembled the
pieces** — all but the phallus, which a fish had eaten, so Isis fashioned a replacement. By her powerful
magic she revived Osiris just long enough to **conceive a son, [[horus|Horus]]**, before he passed fully into
death.

**King of the dead; Horus the avenger.** Osiris did not return to the world of the living; instead he became
the **king and judge of the underworld**, presiding over the [[maat|weighing of the heart]]. His son [[horus|
Horus]], raised in secret, grew up to **avenge his father**, contending with Set for the throne of Egypt (see
[[horus|Horus]] and [[set|Set]]) — so that the living king is **Horus** and the dead king becomes an
**Osiris**.

## Significance

Because Osiris conquered death, every Egyptian hoped to share his resurrection: through proper rites the
dead person **becomes "an Osiris"** and lives forever (see [[osirian-cult|the Osirian cult]]). His myth makes
him the archetypal **dying-and-rising god** of fertility and the afterlife — a major comparandum for other
such figures ([[dionysus|Dionysus]], Adonis, [[magna-mater-cybele|Attis]], and contested comparisons with
the resurrection of Christ), recorded without asserting dependence.
