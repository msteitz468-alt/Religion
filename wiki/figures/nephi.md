# Nephi (son of Lehi)

**Nephi**, fourth son of [[lehi|Lehi]] and Sariah, is the primary narrator of 1–2 Nephi in the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] and the eponymous founder of the [[nephites|Nephite]] people.

## Biographical Overview (textual)

- Born of "goodly parents"; trained in the learning of the Jews and the language of the Egyptians (1 Ne 1:1–2).
- Believes Lehi's prophecies when Laman and Lemuel murmur; promised to be a ruler and teacher if obedient (1 Ne 2).
- Obtains the **brass plates** from Laban (including the Spirit-commanded killing of Laban, 1 Ne 4); builds a ship "not after the manner of men" (1 Ne 17–18); leads the voyage to the promised land.
- Sees the **tree-of-life** vision and its interpretation (1 Ne 11–14): love of God, the Messiah's ministry and cross, Gentiles, plain and precious things, the apostle John.
- After Lehi's death, separates from Lamanite faction; builds a temple after the manner of Solomon; refuses kingship but serves as ruler/teacher (2 Ne 5).
- Installs brothers Jacob and Joseph as priests; keeps dual plate traditions; delivers the **doctrine of Christ** (2 Ne 31) and farewell (2 Ne 33).

## Theological Significance

- Model of obedient faith ("I will go and do," 1 Ne 3:7).
- Hermeneut: **likens** Isaiah to his people; delights in **plainness** (2 Ne 25).
- Frames the book's Christology before the American Christophany of 3 Nephi.
