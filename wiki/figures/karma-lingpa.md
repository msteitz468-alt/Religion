# Karma Lingpa

**Karma Lingpa** is the 14th-century [[nyingma|Nyingma]] **treasure-finder** (*gter-ston*) who
revealed the [[bardo-thodol|kar-gling zhi-khro]] cycle — the Tibetan Book of the Dead. (The name
*kar-gling* abbreviates "Karma Lingpa.")

## Biographical Overview

His exact dates are unknown but placed in the 14th century. He was the son of the accomplished
master **Nyinda Sangye** and revered as an emanation of the great translator Chokrolui Gyeltsen.
In his fifteenth year he is said to have extracted the cycle of the **Peaceful and Wrathful
Deities: Natural Liberation of Enlightened Intention** from **Mount Gampodar** in Dakpo, "which
resembles a dancing god." Tradition holds that he died young, having shown a yellow treasure-scroll
to a student before the appointed time.

## Primary Source Appearances

He is named in the colophons throughout the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]] as the
treasure-finder who brought the cycle forth ("a treasure of Karma Lingpa!"). The teaching was then
transmitted through his son **Nyinda Choje (Lingpa)** — author of Chapter 1 — to **Lama Nyinda
Özer** (Guru Sūryacandra), and to **Gyarawa Namka Chokyi Gyatso**, the first to teach the cycle
publicly.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

For the Nyingma he is one of the prophesied treasure-finders foretold by
[[padmasambhava|Padmasambhava]], alongside Nyangrel Nyima Özer and Guru Chöwang. His revelation
is the textual basis of Tibetan death-ritual practice down to the present; the shorter funerary
anthology was later standardised by **Rigdzin Nyima Drakpa** (1647–1710).
