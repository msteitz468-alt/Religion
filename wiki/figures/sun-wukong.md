# Sun Wukong (the Monkey King)

**Sūn Wùkōng**, the **Monkey King**, is the irrepressible **monkey-god and trickster** of the Ming novel
*Journey to the West* (*Xīyóujì*) — one of the most beloved figures in all of Chinese culture, retold in
[[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's]] chapter "How the Monkey Became a God." He embodies the
[[chinese-folk-religion|folk-religious]] theme of **apotheosis** in its most rebellious key: a creature who
**storms Heaven** and is finally tamed into a Buddha.

## Myths in Detail

### Birth, power, and rebellion

Born from a **stone egg on the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit**, Sun Wukong becomes king of the monkeys, then
seeks out a Daoist master who teaches him the **72 transformations**, **cloud-somersaulting** (108,000 li in a
leap), and the secret of immortality. He seizes from the Dragon-King of the sea his magic weapon, the
**Ruyi Jingu Bang** — a golden cudgel that shrinks to a needle behind his ear or grows to pillar the sky — and,
furious at being listed for death, **storms into the [[diyu|underworld]] and strikes his name out of the
registers of the dead**.

### War on Heaven

The **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]**, in classic bureaucratic style, tries to **contain the troublemaker with
office** — first the menial post of Keeper of the Heavenly Horses, then the hollow title **"Great Sage Equal to
Heaven."** Slighted and unsupervised, Monkey **eats the peaches of immortality** at [[xi-wangmu|the Queen
Mother's]] feast, swallows **Laozi's elixir pills**, and wrecks Heaven; he survives being smelted in Laozi's
furnace (gaining his **fiery golden eyes**) and fights the entire celestial army to a standstill. Only the
**Buddha** subdues him: wagering that Monkey cannot somersault out of the Buddha's palm, Sun Wukong flies to
the ends of the earth — only to find he has never left the Buddha's hand, and is **pinned beneath the Mountain
of Five Phases for 500 years**.

### Pilgrimage and Buddhahood

Released to **escort the monk Xuánzàng (Tripitaka)** on the pilgrimage to fetch the Buddhist scriptures from
India, Monkey — restrained by a **golden headband** that tightens at the monk's word — defeats demon after
demon along the way with his companions **Zhū Bājiè (Pigsy)** and **Shā Wùjìng (Sandy)**. At journey's end his
merit earns him **enlightenment**: he becomes the **"Victorious Fighting Buddha."**

## Significance

Sun Wukong is the great Chinese **trickster-and-pilgrim**: anarchic genius disciplined into a vehicle of
salvation. His story is read as Daoist immortality-cultivation, Buddhist taming of the "monkey-mind," and
sheer popular delight; he is venerated as a god in folk temples (especially in the south and overseas) as the
**Great Sage**.
