# Mishkín-Qalam (1812–1912)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 22664–22688.*

## Biographical Overview
Mírzá (Muḥammad-) Ḥusayn, eminent Iranian calligrapher and Bahá'í. He was renowned for his art and was given the title Mishkín-Qalam ('Musk-scented' or 'jet-black pen') by Náṣiru'd-dín Sháh. He developed the technique of drawing with his fingernail ('Nail script', *Khaṭṭ-i-nakhúní*). He was a Sufi of the Ni'matu'lláhí order (see [[sufism|Sufism]]) and seems to have become a Bahá'í in the 1860s (lines 22664–22678).

## Bahá'í Significance
His Bahá'í calligraphy, including his representation of the [[greatest-name|Greatest Name]], is well known. He met [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] in [[edirne|Edirne]] and was one of the Bahá'ís sent to accompany [[subh-i-azal|Subh-i-Azal]] to Cyprus. He was able to rejoin the companions of Bahá'u'lláh in [[akka|Akká]] in 1886 (lines 22680–22685).

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] named him as one of the Apostles of Bahá'u'lláh (line 22686).
