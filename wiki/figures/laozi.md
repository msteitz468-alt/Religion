# Laozi

**Laozi** ("the Old Master") is the legendary author of the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] and the traditional
founder of [[daoism|Daoism]] — a figure whose historicity is doubtful but whose iconic image (the sage
departing west on an ox, persuaded to write down his teaching at the frontier pass) is central to the
tradition.

## Sources and Historicity

The earliest "biography," in Sima Qian's *Records of the Grand Historian* (1st c. BCE), is already
uncertain — it offers several candidates and admits no one knows. Most scholars treat Laozi as **legendary
or composite**, and the [[daodejing|Daodejing]] as an **anthology** compiled over time rather than the work
of one 6th-century sage. The traditional story of his meeting with Confucius (whom he rebukes for pride) is
a Daoist literary motif asserting Daoism's priority over Confucianism.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[philosophical-daoism|Philosophical Daoism]]** — Laozi is the human author-sage, the voice of the
  Daodejing's wisdom.
- **[[religious-daoism|Religious Daoism]]** — Laozi is **deified** as **Taishang Laojun** ("Supreme Old
  Lord"), a high god of the Daoist pantheon who revealed scriptures (his revelation to Zhang Daoling founds
  the Celestial Masters); in some texts an eternal being who incarnates in successive ages.

## Significance

Laozi anchors Daoism much as a founder-figure, even as critical scholarship dissolves the historical
person — a useful parallel to the wiki's other legendary/semi-legendary founders and authors (e.g.
[[shimon-bar-yochai|Shim'on bar Yoḥai]] and the Zohar; the attributed authorship debates generally).
