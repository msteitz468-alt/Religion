# Yan Wang (King Yama of the Chinese underworld)

**Yán Wáng** (or **Yánluó Wáng**) is the **king and chief judge of the dead** in [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese
folk religion]] — the supreme magistrate of **[[diyu|Diyu]]**, the underworld. His name and office are adapted
from the Indian/Buddhist **[[yama|Yama]]**, naturalized into the Chinese **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial
bureaucracy]]** as the head of its **judicial branch**: a stern, imperial figure who judges every soul,
consults the **registers of life and death**, and assigns it to reward, to one of the punishing hells, or
straight to rebirth.

In the developed system Yan Wang presides over (or heads) the **Ten Courts of Diyu**, each court trying
particular sins under its own Yama-King. He is bureaucratic and **corruptible in folk imagination** only up to
a point — the registers can be checked, clerks bribed with spirit-money, life-spans occasionally extended by
clerical error (as when **[[sun-wukong|Sun Wukong]]** simply crosses his own name out of the ledger) — but his
office stands for the **certainty of post-mortem judgment** proportioned to one's deeds. He delivers the
purified soul, by way of **Mèng Pó's broth of forgetfulness**, to the Wheel of Rebirth.
