# Loki

**Loki** is the [[norse-religion|Norse]] **trickster** — a shape-shifting, ambiguous figure, **companion of
the gods who is also their betrayer**, and ultimately the agent of their doom at [[ragnarok|Ragnarök]]. Of
giant descent but counted among the Æsir, he is cunning, amoral, and the source of both the gods' greatest
treasures and their greatest catastrophe.

## Father of Monsters

By the giantess Angrboða, Loki fathered the three **monsters fated to destroy the gods**: the wolf
**[[fenrir|Fenrir]]**, the world-serpent **[[jormungandr|Jörmungandr]]**, and **[[hel|Hel]]**, queen of the
dead. (As a mare, he also bore [[odin|Odin's]] eight-legged horse Sleipnir.) His trickery repeatedly **causes
a crisis and then resolves it** — he gets the gods' treasures forged (Thor's hammer, Odin's spear) and gets
Thor's hammer back from Þrymr, but also nearly loses [[freyja|Freyja]], the goddess [[idunn|Iðunn]], and her
apples of youth to the giants.

## Myths in Detail — the Death of Baldr and the Binding

Loki's defining act is the **murder of [[baldr|Baldr]]**, the beloved, radiant god. When Baldr's mother
[[frigg|Frigg]] had extracted an oath from **every thing in the world** not to harm him — overlooking only
the lowly **mistletoe** — the gods made a game of throwing weapons at the invulnerable Baldr. Loki, learning
of the one exception, fashioned a **mistletoe dart** and guided the hand of the **blind god Höðr** to throw
it; Baldr fell dead. Then, when the goddess Hel agreed to release Baldr **if every being would weep for him**,
Loki (as the giantess **Þökk**) **alone refused to weep** — keeping Baldr among the dead.

For this the gods finally **bound Loki**: with the entrails of his own son, they fastened him to three rocks
beneath a **serpent dripping venom** onto his face (his faithful wife Sigyn catches the drops in a bowl, but
when she turns to empty it, his agonized writhing causes **earthquakes**). There he lies **until Ragnarök**,
when he breaks free to lead the giants against the gods and falls, with [[heimdall|Heimdall]], each slaying
the other.
