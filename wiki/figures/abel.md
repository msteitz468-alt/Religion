# Abel

The second son of [[adam|Adam]] and [[eve|Eve]], a keeper of sheep, whose acceptable offering provoked
the envy of his brother [[cain|Cain]], by whom he was murdered — the first death and, in Christian
reading, the first martyr.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 4** — Abel's offering of the firstlings of his flock is accepted while Cain's is not; Cain
  rises up and kills him; his blood "cries from the ground."
- New Testament reception: Abel as the first of the righteous (Matt. 23:35; Heb. 11:4; Heb. 12:24, whose
  blood "speaks better than that of Abel").

## Augustine's Reading (City of God XV)

For [[augustine|Augustine]], Abel is the **first citizen of the [[two-cities|city of God]]** and its
foundational type. Where [[cain|Cain]] "built a city," Abel "was a sojourner" who built none — "for the
city of the saints is above, although here below it begets citizens, in whom it sojourns till the time
of its reign arrives." The birth order (Cain first, then Abel) typifies the rule that **the natural
precedes the spiritual** (1 Cor. 15:46): each person is "first of all born of Adam evil and carnal, and
becomes good and spiritual only afterwards." Augustine also reads Abel as a **type of Christ** — "the
shepherd of the flock of men," slain by Cain who figures "the Jews who slew Christ" — though he marks
this as the allegorical sense and defers its full exposition. Abel's name, glossed "grief," is paired
with [[seth|Seth]]'s "resurrection" to prefigure the death and rising of Christ.

## Theological Significance

Abel is the archetype of the innocent sufferer and of the pilgrim citizen of the heavenly city, whose
righteousness is vindicated only beyond this world. His murder inaugurates the antagonism of the
[[two-cities|two cities]] in human history.
