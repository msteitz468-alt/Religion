# Ares (Mars)

**Ares** (Roman **Mars**) is the god of **war** — but the Greek and Roman conceptions differ sharply, making
him the best illustration of the limits of [[interpretatio-romana|interpretatio romana]].

## Greek Ares vs. Roman Mars

- **Ares** is the embodiment of the **brutal, bloody, chaotic** side of war — disliked even by his father
  [[zeus|Zeus]], routinely bested by [[athena|Athena's]] strategic skill, and famous chiefly for his affair
  with [[aphrodite|Aphrodite]] (caught in [[hephaestus|Hephaestus's]] net). Son of Zeus and [[hera|Hera]].
- **Mars**, by contrast, was one of **Rome's greatest gods** — dignified, a guardian of the state, also a
  god of **agriculture and spring** (his month, March, opened the campaigning and farming season), and the
  **father of Romulus and Remus**, hence ancestor of the Roman people. Augustus built the temple of **Mars
  Ultor** (the Avenger).

## Myths in Detail

**The affair with Aphrodite.** Ares' most famous tale is his adultery with **[[aphrodite|Aphrodite]]**, wife
of the lame smith **[[hephaestus|Hephaestus]]**. Warned by [[helios|the all-seeing Sun]], Hephaestus forged a
net of **invisible, unbreakable chains** finer than spider-silk and spread it over the bed; when the lovers
lay down, the net snared them fast, and Hephaestus called all the gods to come and **laugh** at the trapped
pair. Their union produced **Harmonia** and the war-spirits **Phobos** (Fear) and **Deimos** (Terror) who
attend their father in battle, and — in some accounts — [[eros|Eros]].

**Bested again and again.** For all his ferocity, Ares is repeatedly **humiliated**: at [[homer|Troy]],
fighting for the Trojans, he is wounded by the mortal **Diomedes** (guided by [[athena|Athena]]) and runs
bellowing to [[zeus|Zeus]], who rebukes him as the most hateful of the gods; the giant Aloadae once stuffed
him into a **bronze jar** for thirteen months. He was tried for murder on the Athenian hill that took his
name, the **Areopagus** (acquitted of killing Halirrhothius, who had assaulted his daughter).

**Roman Mars, father of Rome.** As **Mars**, by contrast, he is a great and dignified god: he fathered, on
the Vestal **Rhea Silvia**, the twins **Romulus and Remus** — so Mars is the **divine ancestor of the Roman
people**, honored in spring (his month March) and avenged at the temple of Mars Ultor.
