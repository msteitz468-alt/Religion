# Guanyin (the Goddess of Mercy)

**Guanyin** (older **Kuan Yin**) — full name **Guānshìyīn**, "the One Who Perceives the Cries of the World" —
is the **bodhisattva of compassion** and the most beloved deity of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese]] popular
religion. She originates as the Indian bodhisattva **Avalokiteśvara**, but in China was, over centuries,
**transformed into a female "Goddess of Mercy"** — the merciful mother who **hears every cry of suffering and
saves all who call her name**. (In Japan she is **Kannon**.)

## Myths in Detail — the legend of Miao Shan

[[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner]] records her most famous Chinese legend, the story of **Princess Miào Shàn**.
The third daughter of a cruel king, Miao Shan refuses to marry and insists on becoming a **nun**. Enraged, her
father subjects her to humiliation and hard labor, and at last orders her **executed**; but the executioner's
sword shatters, and she descends instead into the **underworld**, where her presence is so holy that **the
hells turn into paradise** and Yama must send her back. Reborn on **Putuo island**, she achieves
enlightenment. When her tyrant father later falls fatally ill — curable only by a medicine made from **the eyes
and hands of one without anger** — Miao Shan **gives her own eyes and hands** to heal him. In reward (and to
restore her), she is granted **"a thousand eyes and a thousand hands"** — the origin of her iconic
**thousand-armed, all-seeing form**, each hand an instrument of mercy. She thus embodies **boundless compassion
returned even for cruelty**.

## Forms and devotion

Guanyin appears in many forms: the **white-robed Guanyin**; the **thousand-armed** form; the **child-granting
Guanyin (Sòngzǐ)**, to whom the childless pray for sons; and the **Guanyin of the Southern Sea** on Putuo. She
is invoked in danger on land and sea (often paired with **[[mazu|Mazu]]**), and the *Lotus Sutra*'s promise —
that whoever calls her name is saved from fire, water, demons, and the sword — underlies her cult. She is the
**compassionate, accessible counterweight** to the awful majesty of the [[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]] and the
[[diyu|courts of the dead]].

## Significance

Guanyin is the supreme Chinese figure of **mercy and salvation by grace** — the deity of the desperate, the
suffering, and the childless. Her female refiguring of a male Indian bodhisattva is one of the great cases of
**religious adaptation**, and she became, with [[mazu|Mazu]], the saviour-mother of [[chinese-folk-religion|folk
religion]].
