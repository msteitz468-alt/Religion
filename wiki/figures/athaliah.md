Athaliah, daughter of the house of [[ahab|Ahab]] and mother of King Ahaziah of Judah, is the
only woman in the biblical record to rule Judah in her own name, and the single break in the
Davidic succession between [[david|David]] and the exile. On her son's death she destroyed the
whole royal seed and reigned six years, until the priest Jehoiada produced the hidden child
Joash and had her killed.

## Biographical Overview

Athaliah enters the narrative as a marriage-alliance link between the northern house of Omri
and the southern house of David: she is the daughter of [[ahab|Ahab]] (2 Kgs 8:18 associates
her with the house of Ahab; 2 Kgs 8:26 calls her "daughter of Omri," a usage the texts do not
resolve) and the wife of Jehoram of Judah, and her son Ahaziah "walked in the way of the house
of Ahab." When [[jehu|Jehu]]'s purge of the Omrides killed Ahaziah, Athaliah "arose and
destroyed all the royal seed" of Judah and took the throne herself.

One infant escaped. Jehosheba hid Ahaziah's son Joash in the temple, where he was concealed for
six years while Athaliah reigned. In the seventh year the priest Jehoiada armed the guards,
brought out the boy, crowned and anointed him, and the people acclaimed him king. Athaliah,
hearing the noise, came into the temple, tore her clothes, and cried "Treason! Treason!"
Jehoiada ordered her taken out between the ranks, and she was put to death at the horses'
entrance to the palace. The covenant renewal that followed included the tearing down of the
temple of Baal, the smashing of its altars and images, and the killing of its priest Mattan
before the altars (2 Kgs 11).

## Primary Source Appearances

- **2 Kings 8:16–27**: her marriage into the Davidic house; Jehoram and Ahaziah judged as
  walking in the ways of the house of Ahab.
- **2 Kings 11**: the killing of the royal seed; Jehosheba's rescue of Joash; the six-year
  concealment; Jehoiada's coup, the coronation, Athaliah's cry of treason and execution at the
  horses' entrance; the covenant and the destruction of the Baal temple.
- **2 Chronicles 22–23**: the parallel account, with the Chronicler's characteristic expansion
  of the Levitical and priestly roles in the coup and the temple guard arrangements.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[iain-provan|Provan]] (NIBC Kings, as recorded on [[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]])**: the survival
  of Joash under Athaliah belongs to Provan's governing theme of **salvation within judgment** —
  the wiki's Kings page records the episode in his frame as "David" surviving "Jezebel," the
  Davidic line preserved by a thread through the Elijah–Elisha era rather than by dynastic
  strength.
- **[[peter-leithart|Leithart]] (BTCB Chronicles, as recorded on
  [[1-2-chronicles|1–2 Chronicles]])**: Athaliah is the carrier of the Ahab "poison." Leithart
  reads Jehoshaphat's marriage-alliance with [[ahab|Ahab]] as a contamination that is "genetic
  and contagious," spreading *through Athaliah* and organizing the next four reigns of Judah —
  part of the larger Chronicles movement in which "Judah becomes Israel."

No Jewish, Islamic, or patristic reception of Athaliah has been ingested into this wiki; none is
asserted here.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Athaliah is the point at which the Davidic promise comes nearest to extinction from within.
The threat is not Assyrian or Babylonian but dynastic: the queen mother of the line destroys the
line. That the promise survives in a single hidden infant, sheltered in the temple and produced
by a priest, locates the continuity of the [[davidic-covenant|Davidic covenant]] in cult and
divine preservation rather than in royal power — the reading recorded above from Provan.

Her reign also marks the deepest penetration of Omride religion into Judah: a Baal temple with
its own priesthood stood in [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] and was demolished only at her fall.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **"Daughter of Ahab" vs "daughter of Omri"** (2 Kgs 8:18 // 8:26): the texts use both
  descriptions. Whether "daughter of Omri" means granddaughter, dynastic shorthand, or a
  different filiation is disputed; the wiki records the tension rather than resolving it.
- **Chronology**: the length and absolute dates of her reign depend on the contested synchronisms
  of the divided monarchy; the figures given here are approximate.
- **Kings vs Chronicles emphasis**: Chronicles foregrounds the Levites and the ordering of the
  temple guard in Jehoiada's coup where Kings foregrounds the royal guard units. Both accounts
  are recorded; neither is treated here as correcting the other.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Within this wiki's ingested sources Athaliah functions chiefly as a node in two larger arguments:
Provan's Kings theme of grace preserving the Davidic line through judgment, and Leithart's
Chronicles theme of Ahab's contagion moving southward through marriage. She is also a standing
test case for how the biblical historians narrate female rule, and for the relation between
priestly authority and royal legitimacy — the priest, not the army or an oracle-prophet, makes
the king.
