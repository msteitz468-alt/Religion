# Nüwa

**Nüwa** (older **Nü Kua**) is the great **creator-goddess** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese]] myth — often
depicted with a **woman's upper body and a serpent's or dragon's tail**, sometimes paired with her brother-consort
**Fúxī**. Two deeds define her.

**The creation of humanity**: in the world newly made by [[pangu|Pángǔ]] but still empty of people, Nüwa
**molds human beings out of yellow clay** by hand, shaping each carefully. Growing tired of the slow work, she
**dips a cord in mud and flicks it**, and the spattered droplets become more humans — a folk etiology, Werner
notes, of social rank: the hand-crafted are the **nobles and rich**, the cord-flicked the **common multitude**.

**The repair of Heaven**: when the monster **Gòng Gōng**, defeated in a cosmic battle, **smashed his head
against Mount Buzhou**, one of the **pillars holding up the sky**, Heaven cracked and tilted, fire and flood
poured through, and beasts devoured the people. Nüwa **smelted stones of five colours to patch the hole in the
sky**, **cut the legs off a giant tortoise to prop up its four corners**, dammed the floods with reed-ash, and
slew the black dragon — restoring the cosmos. The sky's permanent **northwest tilt** (why the stars wheel that
way and the rivers run southeast) is the lasting mark of the damage. Nüwa is thus both **mother of humankind**
and the goddess who **mends the broken world**.
