# Mazu

**Mazu** (also **Matsu**; titled **Tiānhòu**, "Empress of Heaven," and **Tin Hau** in Cantonese) is the great
**sea-goddess** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]] and a model case of **apotheosis** — the
deification of a virtuous human. She is venerated as the **protectress of seafarers, fishermen, and travelers**,
and is among the most widely worshipped deities of coastal and overseas China.

Mazu is the apotheosis of **Lín Mòniáng**, traditionally a 10th-century fisherman's daughter of **Meizhou
island, Fujian**, famed for piety and uncanny powers. The central legend tells how, in a trance while her
father and brothers were at sea in a storm, she **spirit-flew to save them**, gripping the men — but her mother,
startled, woke her, so that she dropped (and lost) her father even as she saved her brothers. After her early
death (often said at 28, while climbing a mountain to ascend to Heaven), sailors reported her **appearing in red
robes above the waves to guide ships to safety**, and her cult spread rapidly with Fujianese maritime trade
across the southeast coast, **Taiwan**, Hong Kong, and the Chinese diaspora.

The **imperial state repeatedly canonized her**, raising her title step by step from "Lady" to "Heavenly
Consort" to **"Empress of Heaven" (Tiānhòu)** — promotion through the ranks of the
**[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]** exactly as a human official was promoted. Her temples
(*Tianhou* / *Mazu* temples) and her **pilgrimage processions**, especially on her birthday (23rd day of the
3rd lunar month), remain among the largest popular religious events in the Chinese world. With
**[[guanyin|Guanyin]]**, she is the merciful **saviour-mother** of the people.
