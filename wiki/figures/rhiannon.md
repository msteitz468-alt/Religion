# Rhiannon

**Rhiannon** is the radiant **sovereignty- and horse-goddess** of the Welsh **[[mabinogion|Mabinogion]]** — her
name from Brittonic ***Rigantona***, "**Divine / Great Queen**," and widely understood as a reflex of the
Gaulish horse-goddess **Epona** (the only Celtic deity the Romans themselves worshipped). She belongs to the
**[[the-otherworld|Otherworld]]** and confers kingship through marriage, the classic
**[[sacred-kingship|sovereignty goddess]]**.

## Myths in Detail

### The uncatchable rider

In the **First Branch**, **[[pwyll|Pwyll]]**, lord of Dyfed, sees a woman in shining gold riding a
**pale-white horse** past the mound of Arberth. He sends riders after her, then gallops himself — yet however
fast they go, **she is always just ahead at the same gentle pace, and cannot be overtaken**, until Pwyll at
last simply **asks her to stop**, and she does. She is Rhiannon, come of her own will to refuse the husband
chosen for her and to **choose Pwyll instead**. After a contest with her rival suitor **Gwawl** (trapped in a
magic bag in the game of "badger-in-the-bag"), she marries Pwyll and becomes queen.

### The vanished child and the false guilt

Rhiannon bears a son, but on the night of his birth her **attendant women fall asleep and the infant
vanishes**. To save themselves the women smear the sleeping Rhiannon with the **blood of puppies** and lay
bones beside her, accusing her of **devouring her own child**. Rhiannon does not contest it; she accepts a
**penance**: for seven years she must sit at the horse-block outside the gate, **tell every stranger her
"crime," and offer to carry them on her back into the court like a beast of burden** — the horse-goddess
literally made a horse. The child, meanwhile, has been carried off by a monstrous claw and rescued by
**Teyrnon**, who raises him; recognizing the boy's likeness to Pwyll, he restores him. The boy is named
**Pryderi** ("care / anxiety"), Rhiannon is **vindicated**, and her penance ends.

In the **Third Branch** Rhiannon — now widowed and married to **Manawydan** ([[manannan|Manannán]]'s Welsh
counterpart) — is caught with Pryderi in an enchantment that empties Dyfed, and the two are held in an
Otherworld captivity (made to wear the **collars of asses**) until Manawydan's cunning lifts the spell.

## Significance

Rhiannon fuses the **horse-goddess** (her birds, "the Birds of Rhiannon," sing so sweetly they wake the dead
and lull the living, and can make seven years pass like a moment) with the **wronged-and-vindicated
sovereignty queen**. Her unjust punishment and patient endurance made her, for later readers, one of the most
sympathetic figures in Welsh myth, and a model of the slandered queen.
