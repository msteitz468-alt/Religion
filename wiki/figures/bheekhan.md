# Bheekhan

Bheekhan (c. 16th century) was a North Indian [[sant-tradition|Sant]] poet with apparent Sufi background whose two compositions appear in the **[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]** under Raag Sorat'h as **Bhagat bāṇī**. Very little biographical information is preserved.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib (Raag Sorat'h, Angs 659–660)

Bheekhan's two compositions in Raag Sorat'h are marked by intense devotional longing expressed through physical symptoms, and by a pervading omnivision of the Lord:

> "Tears well up in my eyes, my body has become weak, and my hair has become milky-white. My throat is tight, and I cannot utter even one word." (L7510–7518, Ang 659)

> "The Name of the Lord, the ambrosial, immaculate water, is the best medicine in the world. By Guru's Grace, says servant Bheekhan, I have found the Door of Salvation." (L7537–7546, Ang 660)

> "Says Bheekhan, my eyes are content; wherever I look, there I see the Lord." (L7572–7575, Ang 660)

The physical symptoms of longing (weeping eyes, weakened body, whitened hair, constricted throat) form an embodied register for divine yearning — a mode found also in classical Sufi poetry. The resolution in omnivision ("wherever I look, there I see the Lord") follows the classic Sufi pattern of *fanā* (annihilation of self) giving way to *baqā* (subsistence in God's presence).
