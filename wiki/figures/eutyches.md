# Eutyches

The aged archimandrite (head of a monastery) at Constantinople whose extreme "one nature" Christology provoked the Council of [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedon]] (451). Kelly calls him "muddle-headed," the rallying-point for opponents of the 433 Formula of Reunion. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. XII.

## Teaching and Condemnation

Eutyches was condemned at the Standing Synod of Constantinople (8/22 November 448) by Eusebius of Dorylaeum, who found him a "follower of Valentinus and Apollinarius." His declared position: "after the union I confess one nature" — accepting "two natures *before* the union" but only one after. He admitted Christ was perfect God and perfect man, born of the Virgin, but refused to allow that the Lord's flesh was "consubstantial with us," and was reported to teach that the humanity was "swallowed up" (*katapothēnai*) and "transformed into the substance of the divinity."

Kelly's assessment is sympathetic on intent: "He was no Docetist or Apollinarian… His hesitations about 'consubstantial with us' were due to his exaggerated suspicion that it might be twisted to imply the Nestorian conception." His fatal error was to omit [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s saving qualification "made flesh" from the *mia physis* formula. [[leo-the-great|Leo]] judged him *multum imprudens et nimis imperitus*. He was briefly rehabilitated at the "Robber Synod" of Ephesus (449) before Chalcedon condemned his teaching as the root of [[oriental-orthodoxy|monophysitism]].
