# Mormon (Book of Mormon historian)

**Mormon** is the prophet-historian for whom the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] is named — abridger of the large plates of Nephi and father of [[moroni|Moroni]].

## Biographical Overview (textual)

- Charged as a youth with the sacred records (Ammaron's charge); later chief captain of the Nephites in the final wars (Mormon 1–6).
- Edits and abridges centuries of Nephite history; inserts the **small plates** of Nephi for a "wise purpose" (**Words of Mormon**).
- Self-identification: "I am a disciple of Jesus Christ, the Son of God" (3 Ne 5:13); pure descendant of Lehi.
- Laments the day of grace past; refuses vengeance ethics; records the fall at **Cumorah** (~230,000 of his people slain in the final numbered tally, Mormon 6).
- Addresses the latter-day remnant of the Lamanites (Mormon 7): believe in Christ, the dual records, baptism, one God.
- Turns the plates to Moroni (Mormon 8).

## Theological Significance

- Embodies the book's **editorial self-consciousness** (cannot write a hundredth part; lesser things to try faith — 3 Ne 26).
- Title-page purpose statement is attributed to his and Moroni's framing.
- Type of the tragic prophet who both fights for and chronicles a collapsing covenant people.
