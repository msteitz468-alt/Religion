# Janus

**Janus** is one of the most distinctively **Roman** gods — with **no Greek equivalent** — the two-faced god
of **beginnings, doorways, transitions, and time**. He looks **both ways at once** (to past and future, inside
and outside), and stands at every threshold and turning point.

## Character and Cult

Invoked **first in every prayer and rite** (even before [[zeus|Jupiter]]), because he opens the way to the
gods; he presides over all beginnings — the start of the day, the month (the **Kalends**), and the year (the
month **January** is his). The doors of his shrine in the Forum, the **Janus Geminus**, stood **open in time
of war and closed in time of peace** — and were famously closed only a handful of times in all Roman history.
God of gates (*ianua*, "door"), comings and goings, and new undertakings.

## Significance

Janus exemplifies Roman religion's genuinely **native** layer — its concern with **thresholds, time, and the
correct opening of action** — beneath the Hellenized pantheon. A unique conceptual deity with few parallels.
