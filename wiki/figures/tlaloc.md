# Tlaloc

**Tlaloc** is the [[aztec-religion|Aztec]] god of **rain, water, lightning, and agricultural fertility** — one
of the oldest and most important gods of [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerica]] (his cult reaches back to
Teotihuacan and beyond), the Aztec counterpart of the Maya **[[chaac|Chaac]]**. On the great **Templo Mayor**
of Tenochtitlan, a shrine to Tlaloc stood beside that of [[huitzilopochtli|Huitzilopochtli]] — **water and war
together** at the empire's sacred heart, for the rains that fed the maize were as vital as the sun.

## Character

A **life-giving but terrifying** god: his rains bring the harvest, but he also sends **drought, flood,
lightning-strike, and hail**. He rules **Tlalocan**, a green paradise of abundance where those who died by
water, lightning, or certain diseases go (a rare blessed afterlife in a tradition with little hope beyond
death). His worship demanded grim offerings — including the **sacrifice of children**, whose tears were thought
to bring the rain. Goggle-eyed and fanged, attended by the rain-spirits (Tlaloque), he embodies the
**ambivalent power of water** on which all life in the dry highlands depended.
