# Abu Hurairah

**Abu Hurairah** (c. 603–681 CE, *kunya* meaning "Father of the Kitten") is the single most
prolific transmitter of hadiths in the entire Sunni corpus. His birth name was Abd al-Rahman ibn
Sakhr al-Dawsi al-Zahrani. He accepted Islam in 7 AH (628 CE) and spent roughly three years in
the Prophet's company — a short but intense period of close proximity that gave him an
extraordinarily large hadith store.

In [[sahih-al-bukhari]] alone he is the narrator of thousands of hadiths, appearing in virtually
every topic book. Classical sources credit him with transmitting over 5,000 hadiths, earning him
the title of the single most authoritative Companion for prophetic traditions.

## Content Profile in Sahih al-Bukhari Vol. 1–3

*(Source: Subagent D notes from 2026-06-27 ingest.)*

### Zakat (Book 24)
Abu Hurairah's transmissions in the Zakat book are predominantly:
- **Eschatological punishment for zakat refusal**: Untithed camels and sheep trample their owners
  on Resurrection Day (No. 485); untithed wealth becomes a poisonous snake with two black spots
  encircling the owner's neck, citing Q 3:180 (No. 486).
- Zakat as two-way reward: both the giving husband and the wife who distributes receive full reward
  (Nos. 507–508).
- Sadaqa (voluntary charity) principles: giving with the right hand such that the left does not know.

### Hajj (Book 26)
- Transmits hadiths on the merits of Hajj and its complete forgiveness of past sins (No. 594).
- The Ka'ba: pre-Islamic naked tawaf prohibited (No. 689).
- Madinah as sanctuary (No. 763).
