# Yamata-no-Orochi

**Yamata-no-Orochi**, "the eight-forked serpent," is the great **chaos-dragon** of [[shinto|Shinto]] myth — a
colossal serpent with **eight heads and eight tails**, its body **spanning eight valleys and eight hills**, its
eyes red as winter-cherries, moss and trees growing on its back, and its belly always **bloody and inflamed**.
It is the monster slain by the storm-god **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]** in the [[kojiki|Kojiki]]'s Izumo cycle.

Each year the serpent has devoured one of an old couple's daughters; when Susanoo, newly banished from heaven,
descends to Izumo, only the last daughter, **Kushinada-hime**, remains. He agrees to slay the serpent in
exchange for her hand, and does so by **cunning rather than force**: he has the couple brew **eight vats of
strong rice-sake** and set them out behind **eight gates**, so that the serpent's **eight heads each plunge
into a vat, drink, and fall down drunk and asleep** — whereupon Susanoo **hews it to pieces**. Cutting through
the tail, his blade strikes something hard: the great sword **Kusanagi ("Grass-Cutter")**, which he draws out
and presents to **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]**, and which becomes one of the **Three Sacred Treasures** of the
imperial regalia. Yamata-no-Orochi is the Shinto form of the **[[the-combat-myth|combat myth]]** — the
storm-god's defeat of the many-headed chaos-serpent (cf. [[tiamat|Tiamat]], [[jormungandr|Jörmungandr]], the
Hydra) — and the myth's origin-story for the imperial sword. (It is often read as a flood- or river-control
myth, the serpent being a ravaging river tamed.)
