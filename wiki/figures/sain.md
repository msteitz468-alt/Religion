# Sain Bhagat

Sain Bhagat (c. 15th century) was a North Indian [[sant-tradition|Sant]] poet of barber (*nai*) background whose compositions were included in the **[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]** as **Bhagat bāṇī**. He is one of the lowest-caste figures among the Bhagats — barbers occupied a service caste considered ritually impure — and his inclusion reinforces the SGGS's anti-caste structural theology.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib

Sain does not have independent compositions in the Raag Aasaa Bhagat bāṇī section of Part 2. His presence in the Granth is primarily documented through the **meta-commentary** by Guru Arjan (Mehl 5), which explicitly names his social background and his transformation:

> "Sain, the barber, the village drudge, became famous in each and every house. The Supreme Lord God dwelled in his heart, and he was counted among the devotees." (L8869–8878, Ang 488)

The phrase "village drudge" (*vill-u saar*) emphasizes the social lowliness of the barber's role, while "became famous in each and every house" is a reversal — the servant of all households through his trade became spiritually renowned in all households through his devotion.

## Theological Significance

Sain's primary theological significance is structural rather than textual: his inclusion among the named Bhagats in Mehl 5's internal endorsement (alongside Namdev, Kabir, Ravidas, and Dhanna) establishes that the SGGS's endorsement of low-caste devotion explicitly extends to the barber caste — traditionally considered impure through association with hair-cutting and ritual service at births and deaths.
