# Varuna

**Varuna** is the great **cosmic sovereign** of [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]] — the god who **upholds
[[rita|rita]]** (the cosmic and moral order), guardian of truth, and the most **ethically serious** of the
Vedic gods. With his partner **[[mitra|Mitra]]** he forms the sovereign pair (the *Adityas*) who watch over
the world's order.

## The Moral Sovereign

Where [[indra|Indra]] is the heroic warrior, Varuna is the **majestic, all-seeing ruler** who maintains the
laws of the cosmos: he set the sun in the sky, hollowed the channels for the rivers, and keeps the order of
day and night. Above all he is the **omniscient guardian of moral truth** — "with his thousand eyes" he sees
every human deed, even thoughts, knows every lie, and **binds the sinner** with his nooses (disease, especially
dropsy, was Varuna's punishment). The penitential hymns to Varuna, confessing sin and begging forgiveness
("loose us from the sins of our fathers… let me not yet go to the house of clay"), are among the most morally
profound and personally devout in the Rig Veda — strikingly close in tone to the Hebrew penitential Psalms (a
noted comparandum). His exalted ethical sovereignty has also been compared to the Zoroastrian **Ahura Mazda**.

## Decline

Like [[indra|Indra]], Varuna **fades in later [[hinduism|Hinduism]]**, dwindling into a lesser god merely of
the **waters and the ocean** — a remarkable demotion for the once-supreme moral sovereign.
