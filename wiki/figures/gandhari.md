## Biographical Overview

Gandhari is the wife of the blind king [[dhritarashtra]] and mother of the Kauravas, including Duryodhana, Duhsasana, and Vikarna. She is the sister of [[shakuni]]. She voluntarily blindfolded herself at her marriage to Dhritarashtra as an act of solidarity with her husband — a detail noted in the tradition (not in Rajagopalachari's account in this section) that signals her character of complete identification with her husband's lot.

## Primary Source Appearances (lines 3100–5900)

Gandhari's presence in this portion of Rajagopalachari's retelling is minimal. She appears only in one brief narrative moment during the dice game episode: when Duhsasana dragged Draupadi to the assembly by her hair, Draupadi had initially "started to fly for refuge to the inner apartments of Dhritarashtra's queen" (Ch. XXV, line 3184). The phrase identifies Gandhari's quarters as the place a wronged woman would naturally seek protection — but Duhsasana cut off Draupadi's flight before she could reach them.

Gandhari does not speak in any of the incidents narrated in this range. She is not present in the councils, the conversations between Dhritarashtra and Vidura, or the encounters with sages.

## Notes on Limited Coverage

Rajagopalachari's retelling in this section focuses on [[dhritarashtra]]'s anxieties and moral failures rather than Gandhari's response. Her active role in the epic — particularly her grief over the Kauravas' fate and her famous curse on Krishna following the Kurukshetra war — falls outside this source range (lines 3100–5900) and cannot be attributed to Rajagopalachari's account here.

Further content on Gandhari should be drawn from other sections of the retelling or from separate scholarly sources when ingested.
