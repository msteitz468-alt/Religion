# Atiśa

**Atiśa Dīpaṃkara Śrījñāna** (982–1054), the Bengali master whose journey to Tibet inaugurated the
"later diffusion" reform and whose *Bodhipathapradīpa* (**Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment**)
established the **graded-path (lamrim)** framework of three capacities that
**[[tsongkhapa|Tsong-kha-pa]]** later expanded into the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Great Treatise]]**. His
followers formed the **Kadam** lineage, the seedbed of the [[gelug|Gelug]] school.

## Biographical Overview (as Tsong-kha-pa narrates it, Lamrim Chenmo Vol. I, ch. 1)

The whole of the **[[lamrim-chenmo|Lamrim Chenmo]]**'s first chapter is a life of Atiśa, establishing
"the greatness of the author" of its root text. Tsong-kha-pa draws chiefly on Nag-tso's *Eighty Verses
of Praise*.

- **Birth and lineage.** Born in Bengal (Za-hor), in "a great city, Vikramaṇipura," son of King
  Kalyāṇaśrī and Queen Śrīprabhā; his birth-name was Candragarbha. Educated young in the sixty-four
  arts, Sanskrit, and logic; said to have bested a non-Buddhist logician in debate at fifteen.
- **The three trainings.** Tsong-kha-pa structures Atiśa's greatness around his possessing all three:
  **ethics** (ordained by the Mahāsāṃghika abbot Śīlarakṣita as Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna; guardian of even
  the minor monastic rules; holder of bodhisattva and tantric vows); **concentration** (a serviceable
  mind through meditative serenity); and **wisdom** (a union of serenity and insight).
- **Teachers.** Many, including Rāhulagupta, Śāntipa, Bhadrabodhi, and Jñānaśrī — but above all
  **[[suvarnadvipa-serlingba|Ser-ling-ba (Suvarṇadvīpa / Dharmakīrti of Suvarṇadvīpa)]]**, from whom
  he received the **[[bodhicitta|spirit of enlightenment]]** lineage. Although Ser-ling-ba held the
  (inferior, per Atiśa) "true-aspectarian" Cittamātra view while Atiśa held [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]],
  Atiśa revered him as "unrivaled amongst his gurus" precisely because through him he obtained
  bodhicitta and the stages of the Mahāyāna path — Tsong-kha-pa's prime example that the guru who
  transmits bodhicitta is the kindest, whatever his philosophical view.
- **Tibet.** Invited by the royal renunciate Jang-chup-ö and the translators Nag-tso and
  Gya-dzön-seng, he taught in Upper Nga-ri and at Nye-tang; he composed the **Lamp for the Path to
  Enlightenment** in Tibet, and corrected the lingering errors of the Chinese abbot **Hva-shang**
  (Ha-shang), who "did not understand emptiness correctly and thereby denigrated the factor of
  method." His chief Tibetan disciple and lineage-holder was **Drom-tön-pa (Gyel-way-jung-nay)**,
  founder of the **[[kadam|Kadam]]** order.

## Significance for the Lamrim

Atiśa's **Lamp for the Path** (*Bodhipathapradīpa*) is the declared root text of the
[[lamrim-chenmo|Great Treatise]]. It supplies the architecture of **[[three-types-of-persons|three
capacities]]** (small, medium, great) and "condens[es] all the key points of the sūtra and mantra
vehicles." Per D. Seyfort Ruegg's introduction to the edition, the *Lamp* was itself partly based on
instruction Atiśa received from Ser-ling-ba; and Atiśa's Kadam lineage (Drom-tön-pa, dGon-pa-ba,
Po-to-ba) is the proximate seedbed of the *lam rim* genre that the [[gelug|Gelug]] inherits. His
counsel that in a degenerate age one may accept a teacher with "one eighth" of the full qualities,
and his insistence to Drom-tön-pa that "you need faith, faith, faith," are both quoted in the Treatise.
