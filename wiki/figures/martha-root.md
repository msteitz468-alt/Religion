# Martha Root

*Populated from Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000). All claims from that source unless marked.*

## Biographical Overview

Martha Root (1872–1939) was a prominent American Bahá'í itinerant teacher and writer. A journalist and former school teacher, she became a Bahá'í in Pittsburgh in 1909 (lines 27131–27135).

## Teaching Travels

In 1915 she made a lengthy visit to various Bahá'í communities in Asia and elsewhere, supporting herself by publishing articles in the United States. In 1919, in response to 'Abdu'l-Bahá's appeal in the [[tablets-of-the-divine-plan|Tablets of the Divine Plan]], she began what was to become her life's work with an extended visit to promote the Faith in several countries of Latin America (lines 27135–27147).

Travelling from city to city she gave talks — often to Theosophist and Esperanto groups — wrote articles for newspapers and magazines, put Bahá'í books in libraries, and contacted government officials, educators and others. Other lengthy tours followed: between 1923 and 1930 she visited the Far East, Australasia, Europe, the Middle East and India. Then after a year in the United States and Canada she went again to Europe (1932–4) and then to East Asia, Ceylon, India and Australasia (1937–9). She died in Honolulu on the return leg of her journey (lines 27148–27163).

## Notable Achievements

- Disregarding her own health and living much of the time in poverty, she pushed herself to seize every opportunity to promote the Faith, making myriads of new contacts, including her meetings with Queen [[marie-of-romania|Marie of Romania]]
- She was the first person to make a radio broadcast about the Bahá'í Faith (in Perth, Australia in 1924)
- In Europe she helped set up the [[international-bahai-bureau|International Bahá'í Bureau]]
- She enormously expanded Bahá'í links with the [[esperanto|Esperanto]] movement

(Lines 27163–27175.)

## Recognition

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] posthumously named her a [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]] and described her as a Bahá'í immortal (lines 27175–27179).
