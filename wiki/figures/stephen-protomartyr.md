# Stephen the Protomartyr

The first Christian martyr ("protomartyr"), one of the seven deacons of the Jerusalem church (Acts 6),
whose preaching and stoning are narrated in **Acts 6–7**. He dies praying "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit"
and "lay not this sin to their charge," with Saul ([[paul-apostle|Paul]]) consenting and minding the cloaks of the
witnesses.

## In the City of God

[[stephen-protomartyr|Stephen]] is the central figure of [[augustine|Augustine]]'s account of
contemporary miracles in [[city-of-god|*City of God*]] Book XXII. About two years before Augustine wrote,
**relics of Stephen** were brought to Hippo-regius, and an outpouring of healings followed at his shrines
in the districts of Hippo and Calama — "they would fill many volumes," with nearly **seventy** recorded by
the time of writing. Augustine narrates specific cases: blind sight restored, fistulas and gout cured,
several **raisings of the dead** (the children of Bassus, Irenaeus, and Eleusinus; the priest Eucharius),
the conversion and holy death of the aged pagan **Martial of Calama** — who died with Stephen's own dying
words, "Christ, receive my spirit," on his lips — and the famous cure of the trembling Cappadocian siblings
**Paulus and Palladia** before Augustine's Easter congregation.

For Augustine these miracles are not ends in themselves but **witnesses** (the literal sense of *martyr*)
to "the faith which preaches Christ risen in the flesh," tied to his defense of the bodily
[[resurrection|resurrection]] and his account of the [[relic-veneration|cult of the martyrs]] — "our
martyrs are not our gods."
