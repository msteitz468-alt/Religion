# Ravidas

Ravidas (c. 1450–1520) was a low-caste (Chamar/cobbler) [[sant-tradition|Sant]] poet from Varanasi whose compositions were included in the **[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]** as **Bhagat bāṇī**. His hymns emphasize direct devotion to God beyond caste hierarchy and are significant in anti-caste spiritual traditions. He is venerated by the Ravidassia community.

## Ravidas in the Guru Granth Sahib

The Granth includes at least one Ravidas composition in Siree Raag (Ang 93, Thind/Khalsa translation), attributed at the closing verse: "O Ravi Daas, one who understands that the Lord is equally in all, is very rare." The composition articulates a non-dual theology grounded in divine omnipresence:

> "You are me, and I am You — what is the difference between us? We are like gold and the bracelet, or water and the waves." (L20827–20833, Ang 93)

> "If I did not commit any sins, O Infinite Lord, how would You have acquired the name, 'Redeemer of sinners'?" (L20834–20840, Ang 93)

The term **sam dal** ("equally in all" / equal vision) is Ravidas's signature theological marker: God is equally present in all beings, cutting across caste hierarchy. His composition's inclusion in the Granth — immediately following Trilochan (Maharashtra) and Baynee — signals the Sikh Gurus' explicit affirmation of a low-caste Bhagat's spiritual authority. Mehl 3 within the Granth itself (Ang 67) validates the Bhagats' salvific standing: "Naam Dayv the printer, and Kabeer the weaver, obtained salvation through the Perfect Guru... Their Banis are sung by the angelic beings."

## In the Guru Granth Sahib — Raag Goojaree (Part 2)

Ravidas's compositions in Raag Goojaree state his low-caste identity explicitly — "I am a low-caste cobbler" — and argue that God's grace elevates the lowly beyond the Brahmin's ritual scholarship. The Goojaree compositions articulate the same *sam dal* theology as the Siree Raag compositions: God is equally present in all.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib — Raag Sorat'h (7 compositions, Angs 658–659)

Ravidas's most extensive section in the Granth. The 7 compositions range across non-dual theology, Hindu mythological reference, and his most explicit low-caste self-declarations:

**Non-dual theology (ego and union)**:
> "When I am in my ego, then You are not with me. Now that You are with me, there is no egotism within me. The wind may raise up huge waves in the vast ocean, but they are just water in water." (L7176–7184, Ang 658)

The ego-dissolution passage is one of the clearest advaita-inflected statements in the SGGS Bhagat bāṇī, often compared to Kabir's wave-ocean formulations. The mechanism of union is ego-dissolution, not ritual practice.

**Maya as dream**:
> "It is like the king, who falls asleep upon his throne, and dreams that he is a beggar. His kingdom is intact, but separated from it, he suffers in sorrow. Such is my own condition." (L7195–7202, Ang 658)

**Devotional audacity** (bold direct address to God):
> "If I am bound by the noose of emotional attachment, then I shall bind You, Lord, with the bonds of love. Go ahead and try to escape, Lord; I have escaped by worshipping and adoring You." (L7226–7233, Ang 659)

> "If You are the mountain, Lord, then I am the peacock. If You are the moon, then I am the partridge in love with it. If You are the lamp, then I am the wick. If You are the sacred place of pilgrimage, then I am the pilgrim." (L7385–7402, Ang 659)

**Low-caste self-declaration**:
> "My social status is low, my ancestry is low, and my life is wretched. I have come to Your Sanctuary, O Luminous Lord, my King; so says Ravi Daas, the shoemaker." (L7468–7476, Ang 659)

> "I am a shoemaker, but I do not know how to mend shoes... Without wasting my time mending, I have found the Lord." (L7478–7493, Ang 659) — the cobbler's trade turned into a spiritual metaphor: Ravidas used his time in spiritual practice rather than in his hereditary trade, and this is presented not as negligence but as wisdom.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib — Raag Dhanaasaree (3 compositions, Ang 694) — The Naam-Aartee

The final Ang of SGGS Part 2 belongs to Ravidas. His three compositions in Dhanaasaree close the section with intense longing and the most internalized Aartee in the Granth:

> "There is none as forlorn as I am, and none as Compassionate as You; what need is there to test us now?" (L14658–14660, Ang 694)

> "For so many incarnations, I have been separated from You, Lord; I dedicate this life to You." (L14674–14677, Ang 694)

> "Without the Saadh Sangat, the Company of the Holy, love for the Lord does not well up; without this love, Your devotional worship cannot be performed." (L14712–14716, Ang 694)

**The Dhanaasaree Naam-Aartee** (final composition, L14723–14779, Ang 694): A systematic replacement of every element of the traditional *āratī* (lamp-ceremony) with the Naam:

> "Your Name, Lord, is my adoration and cleansing bath." (L14723–14728)

> "Your Name is the prayer mat, and Your Name is the stone to grind the sandalwood." (L14730–14734)

> "Your Name is the lamp, and Your Name is the wick. Your Name is the oil I pour into it. Your Name is the light applied to this lamp, which enlightens and illuminates the entire world." (L14745–14755)

> "Says Ravi Daas, Your Name is my Aartee, my lamp-lit worship-service. The True Name is the Lord's offering of food." (L14775–14779, Ang 694) — **the final line of SGGS Part 2 belongs to Ravidas, the lowest-caste Bhagat**.

The fact that SGGS Part 2 ends with Ravidas is structurally significant: the Granth's compilers gave the last word of a major textual division to the cobbler-saint, inverting the caste hierarchy that would have assigned him the most marginal position.

## Theological Significance

Ravidas's corpus in the SGGS spans three raag sections and develops four interconnected themes:
1. **Ego-dissolution as union**: the wave-and-ocean, king-dreaming formulations articulate non-dual theology in an accessible register
2. **Explicit low-caste self-declaration**: Ravidas names his caste identity more persistently than any other Bhagat, and in each case pairs the low-caste declaration with equal or greater spiritual attainment
3. **Devotional audacity**: the "I will bind You" and "I have escaped" verses claim an intimacy with God that inverts conventional power relations
4. **Internalized worship**: the Naam-Aartee (Dhanaasaree, Ang 694) completely displaces ritual forms with the Name
