# The Dragon Kings (Lóng Wáng)

The **Dragon Kings** (**Lóng Wáng**) are the **divine rulers of the waters** in [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese
folk religion]] — the **water-ministry** of the **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]**. Unlike the
malevolent dragon of Western myth, the Chinese **lóng** is an **auspicious, benevolent** being of rain, rivers,
and the deep, and its kings command **clouds, storms, floods, and the rain on which all farming depends**.

Chief among them are the **Four Dragon Kings** of the **Four Seas** (East, South, West, North), with **Ào Guǎng**,
Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, the most prominent. Each rules a crystal undersea palace, commands legions of
aquatic creatures as his officials and soldiers, and **carries petitions for rain** up to the
**[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]**, on whose orders they release (or withhold) it. In drought, villages
**process the Dragon King's image, pray, and sometimes punish or expose it** to coerce rain — divine
accountability in action. In legend they are often **bested by upstart heroes**: **[[nezha|Nezha]]** kills the
Eastern Dragon King's son, and **[[sun-wukong|Sun Wukong]]** bullies Ào Guǎng out of his iron pillar-cudgel,
the *Ruyi Jingu Bang*. The Dragon Kings express the [[chinese-folk-religion|folk-religious]] sense that the
**life-or-death gift of rain** is administered, like everything else, by a heavenly **bureau** that can be
petitioned — and held to account.
