# David Miscavige

**David Miscavige** (born 1960, Willingboro, New Jersey) is the **Chairman of the Board of the Religious Technology Center (RTC)** and the effective supreme leader of the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] since the late 1980s. He succeeded [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]] through an internal power struggle following Hubbard's death in January 1986. He has declined all interviews with journalists and has never given a full authorized account of his rise to power.

## Biographical Overview

### Early Life and Entry into Scientology

Born in **Willingboro, New Jersey** (near Philadelphia). Suffered severe childhood asthma treated with steroids, which he credits Scientology with curing. His father, Ron Miscavige Sr., joined Scientology when David was young. Miscavige was sent to Saint Hill (England) at approximately age 12, joined the Sea Org at age 16 (1976), and became Chief Cinematographer at 17.

Wright (*Going Clear*) notes he **dropped out of tenth grade** to join the Sea Org full-time and never returned to conventional education. He was nicknamed "the Wonder Kid" by Karen de la Carriere and others around Hubbard.

### Rise to Power (1981–1987)

Miscavige positioned himself as a gatekeeper to Hubbard during the early 1980s, when Hubbard was in hiding and increasingly inaccessible. Key moves:

- **Dismantling the Guardian's Office** (1981–82): Miscavige organized the "Mission Holder" purge and the removal of the Guardian's Office executives (including Jane Kember), displacing Mary Sue Hubbard's power center. Mary Sue was sent to prison in January 1983 (after Operation Snow White convictions) and released after one year; Miscavige reportedly told her she had been "disfellowshipped" while Hubbard was still alive. Mary Sue cursed him openly.
- **The Denny's phone booth coup** (c. 1981): Coup against CMO executive Gale Irwin, conducted via a pay phone at a Denny's restaurant — Miscavige physically seized control of the organizational hierarchy in the field.
- After Hubbard's death (January 24, 1986), Miscavige moved against **Pat Broeker** — Hubbard's last personal handler, who had announced himself as "Loyal Officer" with access to secret OT IX–X materials. Miscavige placed Broeker under continuous surveillance (a private investigator monitored him for over 25 years, per the Tampa Bay Times) and stripped him of all authority by 1988.
- By **1987**, Miscavige had consolidated control as RTC Chairman.

### Marriage

Married **Shelly Barnett** (December 1982). Shelly Miscavige disappeared from public view in June 2006, after she reportedly reassigned staff positions without Miscavige's authorization. She has not been seen publicly since; the Church maintains she is alive and on a private assignment. A missing persons report was filed by Jenna Miscavige Hill (David's niece) in 2013; police investigated and were satisfied she was alive.

## Documented Abuses (Wright's Account)

Wright compiled a list of **11 named individuals** who told him they had been physically assaulted by Miscavige, and over **20 witnesses** to such assaults (including Mike Rinder, Gale Irwin, Marty Rathbun, Jefferson Hawkins, Tom De Vocht, Mark Fisher, Bruce Hines, Bill Dendiu, Guy White, Marc Headley, and Stefan Castle, among others).

Specific incidents documented:
- Beat Marty Rathbun repeatedly; punched Tom De Vocht; beat Mike Rinder (January 2004)
- The **"musical chairs" incident** (c. 2004): ordered the entire Watchdog Committee to play musical chairs to Queen's *Greatest Hits*; the loser would be "assigned to the Hole"
- **Imposed divorces** on Sea Org couples (2004)
- Managed the **Hole** (see [[the-hole|The Hole]]) — a double-wide trailer where up to 70 senior executives were confined for months/years

The Church has consistently denied all allegations of physical violence, calling the accusers "bitter apostates." Recorded without adjudication per Contradiction Protocol.

## Lifestyle and Resources

Wright's portrait of Miscavige's lifestyle at Gold Base (from interviews with former chefs Sinar Parman and Lana Mitchell, and others):
- Food costs: $3,000–$20,000 per week for Miscavige personally (disputed by Church)
- Custom suits from Richard Lim; custom shoes from John Lobb
- $150,000 custom stereo
- Macallan Scotch whisky
- Personal chef, full-time dog handler
- Dogs with captain's epaulettes
- Multiple motorcycles and custom vehicles

This is set against the Sea Org rank-and-file at Gold Base: 75¢ per meal allotment, $13–$50 per week pay, two sets of clothing as a lifetime allotment.

## Relationship to Hubbard's Canon

Miscavige's authority rests on his stewardship of "standard tech" — the claim that he is protecting Hubbard's exact scripture from corruption. Yet Wright reports he cancelled Flag Order 3879 (Hubbard's posthumous directive naming Pat Broeker as heir) in April 1988, demonstrating willingness to override Hubbard when necessary. He also authorized the "Golden Age of Tech" revisions to training materials in 1996, which many defectors regard as a departure from "standard tech."

## Public Profile

Miscavige appeared on **ABC Nightline** (Ted Koppel interview, February 14, 1992) — his most significant public appearance. He has appeared at International Association of Scientologists events, the most famous being the **October 8, 1993** victory speech after the IRS settlement: "The war is over!"

He awarded Tom Cruise the **Freedom Medal of Valor** (October 2004) and served as best man at Cruise's weddings to both Nicole Kidman (Telluride, December 1990) and Katie Holmes (Rome castle, November 2007).

## Controversy

The central controversy is whether Miscavige is a legitimate religious leader running a legitimate religion, or a megalomaniacal authoritarian using religious corporate structures to insulate himself from accountability. See [[scientology-religion-or-commercial-enterprise|Scientology: religion or commercial enterprise?]].

Numerous lawsuits have been filed against Miscavige personally; the Church has generally succeeded in having him dismissed as a defendant. Multiple former senior executives have spoken publicly about abuse; the Church calls them "apostates" whose testimony is unreliable.

## Atack's Account (*A Piece of Blue Sky*, 1990)

Atack provides the earliest sustained documented account of Miscavige's rise, drawing on the Armstrong case record and ex-member interviews:

- Physical description: **"just over five feet tall and asthmatic"**; joined Sea Org at 16 (1976); became Chief Cinematographer at 17 (CMO Cine Org).
- The **All Clear Unit**: Miscavige headed this unit, ostensibly tasked with clearing Hubbard's legal situation so he could return from hiding — in practice, it centralized Miscavige's control over the Hubbard connection.
- **Confronted Mary Sue Hubbard, May 1981**: organized the displacement of her GO authority; by August 1981 had effectively taken over the Guardian's Office. [[mary-sue-hubbard|Mary Sue]] cursed him openly.
- **Chairman of ASI** (Author Services Incorporated, for-profit): Miscavige's formal title before his RTC role; gave him control over Hubbard's finances and literary estate.
- **RTC incorporated January 1, 1982**: [[david-mayo|David Mayo]] signed the incorporation documents; Mayo later claimed the terms were altered after his signature.
- **ASI target: $85 million** as Hubbard's "Christmas present" (1982 period).
- Atack notes the Aznaran testimony about a **schism in February 1987** between Miscavige and the Broekers over the "upper level Holy Scriptures" — Miscavige ultimately prevailed and had Broeker placed under surveillance (confirmed by later Tampa Bay Times reporting: a private investigator monitored Broeker for over 25 years).

## Headley's Account (*Blown for Good*, 2009)

Marc Headley worked at [[gold-base|Gold Base]] for approximately 15 years (c. 1990–2005), giving him close-range observation of Miscavige's personal management style. Headley's account adds the following to Wright's and Atack's portraits:

### Physical Incidents
- **Punched Marc Headley** in A/V Manufacturing — Headley records this as one of multiple assaults; he treats it as a characteristic occurrence rather than an exceptional one
- Miscavige's physical confrontations with staff were frequent enough that Headley mentions them matter-of-factly alongside his narrative of daily operations

### The Musical Chairs Incident (~2004)
Headley provides an eyewitness account that supplements and specifies Wright's summary:
- Approximately **70 senior staff and managers** were summoned
- Forced to play musical chairs to **Queen's *Greatest Hits*** — specifically "Bohemian Rhapsody"
- The losing game would result in assignment to the Hole; Miscavige also assigned "losers" to remote continental posts (Africa, etc.) during the game itself
- The results were **cancelled the next morning** — but the reshuffled assignments were partially imposed anyway
- The incident is treated by Headley as characteristic of Miscavige's management by arbitrary threat

### New Year's 2000 Overboarding
Miscavige revived the ship-era disciplinary practice of **overboarding** for the New Year's 2000 event at Gold Base's pool — throwing staff into the pool as a disciplinary and motivational measure. This directly echoes the Corfu overboardings of 1968 documented by [[atack-a-piece-of-blue-sky|Atack]].

### Secret Nighttime Office Searches
Miscavige conducted regular **unannounced nighttime sweeps** through Gold Base offices, including executives' personal workspaces. Staff who left their desks in disarray faced consequences. The searches were conducted with no advance warning and at irregular hours.

### Building 50
The **$70 million** construction project (completed ~2003) representing Miscavige's flagship office complex at Gold Base. Three workers died during construction, including a woman named **Stacey**, who was electrocuted. Headley describes the scale as exceeding any practical function.

### Tom Cruise Management
Headley documents Miscavige's personal management of Tom Cruise's Church involvement:
- **Freewinds birthday party** (2004): full production event on the Church's cruise ship; Cruise told Miscavige it was the "best birthday ever"
- **Freedom Medal of Valor** ceremony: October 2004; Miscavige personally presented it to Cruise at a major IAS event
- **Girlfriend search**: RTC arranged over 100 auditions of Scientology actresses as candidates for a Cruise relationship; Greg Wilhere was dispatched to New York to conduct candidate interviews. The audition/selection process was managed at the RTC level
- **Global funding plan**: Miscavige planned to use Cruise's film profits to fund Scientology expansion worldwide

### Lifestyle Details at Gold Base
- **Macallan Scotch** whisky: confirmed in Headley's account as a personal staple
- **Silk pajamas and sheepskin slippers**: Headley observed these during encounters in Miscavige's quarters
- **Seasonal furnishing of Bonnie View**: Miscavige directed the seasonal rotation of Hubbard's mansion's furnishings
