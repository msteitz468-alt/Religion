# The Morrígan

The **Morrígan** — "**Great Queen**" or "**Phantom Queen**" — is the Irish **goddess of war, fate, and
death**, and one of the most charged figures of [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]]. She is rarely a combatant
herself; she is the **shaper of battle and prophet of its outcome**, appearing over the field as a
**crow or raven**, washing the bloody gear of men about to die, and **inciting or paralyzing** armies with
terror. She embodies the Celtic **triplism**: often a trio, the **Morrígna** — **Badb** ("the crow / scald-crow"),
**Macha**, and **Nemain** ("battle-frenzy") or **Anand**.

## Myths in Detail

In **[[gods-and-fighting-men|Gods and Fighting Men]]**, before the **Second Battle of Mag Tuired** the Morrígan
**unites with the [[the-dagda|Dagda]]** as she stands astride a river at **Samhain** — a sacred coupling by
which she pledges her power against the **Fomorians** and prophesies their defeat. She then **chants the
victory** of the [[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]] and afterward proclaims a **prophecy of peace** and
a darker prophecy of the **end of the world** — the goddess who governs both the war's outcome and the long
fate beyond it.

In the Ulster Cycle she is the great antagonist-lover of the hero **Cú Chulainn**: she offers him her love,
and when he scornfully refuses, she opposes him in battle in the shapes of an **eel, a wolf, and a heifer**;
later, mortally foreshadowing his death, she appears as the **Washer at the Ford**, rinsing his bloodstained
armor, and as a **crow** that settles on his shoulder when he dies bound upright to a stone.

## Significance

The Morrígan fuses **war, prophecy, sovereignty, and death**. As "Great Queen" she carries the
**sovereignty-goddess** charge (the land conferring or denying victory and kingship; cf.
[[sacred-kingship|sacred kingship]] and [[rhiannon|Rhiannon]]); as the raven over the slain she is the Celtic
face of **fate and slaughter**, a goddess who decides battles before they are fought.
