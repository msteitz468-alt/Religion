# Seth

The third son of [[adam|Adam]] and [[eve|Eve]], born after the murder of [[abel|Abel]], through whom
the genealogical line of the [[two-cities|city of God]] descends to [[noah|Noah]] and the patriarchs.
Eve names him as the seed "appointed … instead of Abel, whom Cain slew" (Gen. 4:25).

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 4:25–5:8** — Seth's birth; his son Enos, in whose days "men began to call on the name of the
  Lord"; Seth heads the genealogy of Genesis 5 (Adam → Seth → Enos → … → Noah).

## Augustine's Reading (City of God XV)

[[augustine|Augustine]] makes Seth the head of the **heavenly line**, set against [[cain|Cain]]'s
earthly line — "these two series of generations … the one of Cain, the other of Seth, represent the two
cities." He glosses **Seth** as "resurrection" and his son **Enos** as "man" / "the child of the
resurrection," reading the note that Enos "hoped to call on the name of the Lord" as a prophecy of the
people who would call on God by the election of grace. Paired with [[abel|Abel]] ("grief"), Seth
("resurrection") prefigures the death and rising of Christ. Crucially, Augustine argues that the **"sons
of God" of Genesis 6 are Seth's line** — godly men who sinned by marrying the "daughters of men" (Cain's
line) — **not angels**, against the interpretation drawn from the non-canonical *Book of Enoch*.

## Theological Significance

Seth embodies the **continuity of the godly remnant** through which the promise is carried past the
violence of the earthly city — the thread that survives the flood in Noah and runs to Abraham.
