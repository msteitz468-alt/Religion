# Dumuzi (Tammuz)

**Dumuzi** (Sumerian; Akkadian **Tammuz**) is the **shepherd-god of fertility and vegetation** — and the
**dying-and-returning god** whose annual descent and return to the underworld mark the cycle of the seasons,
the withering and greening of the land. He is the husband of **[[ishtar|Inanna/Ishtar]]**.

## The Substitute

In the **Descent of [[ishtar|Inanna]]**, when Inanna returns from the dead she must provide a **substitute**
to take her place below. Finding Dumuzi enthroned and **not mourning her**, she hands him over to the demons.
His devoted sister **Geshtinanna** offers to share his fate, so that Dumuzi spends **half the year in the
underworld and half above** — his return bringing the spring's renewal, his descent the summer's drought and
death of vegetation. The **weeping for Tammuz** (lamentation at his seasonal death) was a widespread rite —
even condemned in the Hebrew Bible, where the prophet sees "women weeping for Tammuz" at the Temple gate
(Ezekiel 8:14).

## Significance

Dumuzi/Tammuz is the classic Mesopotamian **dying-and-rising vegetation god**, a major comparandum (with
[[osiris|Osiris]], [[dionysus|Dionysus]], Adonis, and [[magna-mater-cybele|Attis]]) in the study of such
figures and their relation to later resurrection ideas — recorded without asserting dependence.
