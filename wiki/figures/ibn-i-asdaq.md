# Ibn-i-Asdaq (Mírzá 'Alí-Muḥammad)

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17731–17775.*

## Biographical Overview

Mírzá 'Alí-Muḥammad Ibn-i-Asdaq (c.1850–1928) was an Iranian [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]] and son of the revered Bábí and Bahá'í teacher Mullá Ṣádiq Khurásání (l. 17749–17751). He visited [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] in Baghdad with his father in 1861–63, and father and son were imprisoned together as Bahá'ís shortly after their return to Iran (l. 17733–17736).

## Designation as Martyr

Longing for the station of a martyr for the Faith, he wrote to Bahá'u'lláh to supplicate for this. Bahá'u'lláh replied (in 1880) that what was called for was service to the Cause, and particularly teaching it with wisdom — that such endeavour might have the status of martyrdom even though no blood was shed (l. 17737–17744). In 1882 Bahá'u'lláh gave Ibn-i-Asdaq the title "Martyr, son of a martyr" (*Shahíd ibn-i-shahíd*) (l. 17745–17747).

## Service

He devoted himself to extensive travels within Iran to promote the Faith (l. 17752–17754). In 1887 he was designated a Hand of the Cause by Bahá'u'lláh and was involved in the work of that body in Tehran (l. 17754–17756). Through his marriage to 'Udhrá Khánum (d. 1923), a member of the Qájár nobility, he was able to contact members of the ruling stratum and inform them about the Faith; he also delivered 'Abdu'l-Bahá's "Treatise on Politics" to the Shah (l. 17758–17764).

He later travelled widely in India, Burma, the Caucasus, and Russian Turkistan (l. 17764–17766). In 1919, together with Aḥmad Yazdání, he delivered [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]'s letter to the Central Organization for a Durable Peace in The Hague (l. 17766–17770).

## Legacy

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] named him one of the [[apostles-of-bahaullah|Apostles of Bahá'u'lláh]] (l. 17771–17773).
