# Inari

**Inari** is the immensely popular [[shinto|Shinto]] **kami of rice, agriculture, fertility, prosperity, and —
in modern times — business and commerce**. With around **a third of all Japanese shrines** (some 30,000)
dedicated to Inari, headed by **Fushimi Inari Taisha** in Kyoto (with its famous tunnels of vermilion *torii*),
Inari is one of the most widely worshipped of all kami. The deity's gender and identity are fluid — Inari is
variously depicted as an old man with rice, a young woman, or an androgynous figure, and identified with rice-
and food-kami such as **Uka-no-Mitama** (a grain-deity descended in the [[kojiki|Kojiki]] from
[[susanoo|Susanoo]]) and **Ukemochi**.

Inari is inseparable from the **fox (*kitsune*)**, which serves as the deity's **sacred messenger** — the white
fox statues flanking Inari shrines, often holding a rice-sheaf, key, or jewel in their mouths. (Popular usage
loosely calls Inari "the fox god," though strictly the foxes are servants, not the deity.) Originally a kami of
the **rice harvest** petitioned by farmers, Inari's blessing of "abundance and increase" naturally extended to
**wealth and trade**, so that merchants, sake-brewers, and modern corporations alike maintain Inari shrines for
prosperity. Inari exemplifies Shinto's intimate bond with **agriculture, [[musubi|fertility/increase]], and
everyday flourishing**.
