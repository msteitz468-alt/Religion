# Aphrodite (Venus)

**Aphrodite** (Roman **Venus**) is the goddess of **love, beauty, desire, and sexual procreation** —
irresistible to gods and mortals alike.

## Origins and Myths

[[hesiod-theogony|Hesiod]] gives her a cosmic birth: she rose from the **sea-foam** (*aphros*) around the
severed genitals of [[uranus|Uranus]] — older than the Olympians. Wife of [[hephaestus|Hephaestus]] but
lover of [[ares|Ares]]; her power drives much of myth (she awards herself the golden apple by promising
**Helen** to Paris, igniting the Trojan War). Mother of [[eros|Eros]] and of the Trojan hero **Aeneas**.

## Cult and Roman Identification

Worshipped at Cyprus, Cythera, and Corinth. As **Venus**, she was supremely important to Rome: through
Aeneas she was the **divine ancestress of the Roman people** and of the **Julian family** (Julius Caesar
and Augustus claimed descent), honored as **Venus Genetrix** ("the Mother").

## Myths in Detail

**Birth from the sea.** When [[cronus|Cronus]] castrated his father [[uranus|Uranus]] and cast the severed
genitals into the sea, a white **foam** (*aphros*) gathered around them, and from it rose the fully-grown
**Aphrodite**, who drifted on a shell to Cyprus (or Cythera), the earth blooming under her feet. Older than
the Olympians, she is desire as a cosmic force.

**The Judgment of Paris.** At the wedding of Peleus and Thetis, **Eris (Strife)** threw down a golden apple
inscribed "**to the fairest**"; [[hera|Hera]], [[athena|Athena]], and Aphrodite each claimed it. [[zeus|Zeus]]
sent them to the Trojan prince **Paris** to judge. Each offered a bribe — Hera, royal power; Athena, victory
and wisdom; **Aphrodite, the most beautiful woman in the world**. Paris awarded her the apple, and she gave
him **Helen** of Sparta, another man's wife — the abduction that ignited the **[[homer|Trojan War]]** and
earned Aphrodite's protection of Troy (and Hera's and Athena's lasting hatred).

**Loves and the wound of Adonis.** Aphrodite's own loves are many: by [[ares|Ares]] (caught in the net), and
by the mortal **Anchises**, to whom she bore **Aeneas** — making her the ancestress of Rome ([[virgil-aeneid|
the *Aeneid*]]). Her great grief is the beautiful youth **Adonis**, gored to death by a boar; from his blood
sprang the anemone, and his yearly death and return became a vegetation cult. She punishes those who scorn
love (Hippolytus, the women of Lemnos) and rewards devotion (Pygmalion's statue brought to life).
