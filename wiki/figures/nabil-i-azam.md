# Nabíl-i-A'ẓam (1831–1892)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23374–23410.*

## Biographical Overview
Mullá Muḥammad Zarandí, prominent Iranian Bahá'í. Of humble origins — he was a shepherd — Nabíl became a Bábí around 1847, later becoming one of those who transcribed and distributed the Báb's writings (lines 23374–23380).

In the early 1850s he put forward claims to leadership of the Bábís on the basis of his receipt of divine inspiration, but withdrew this claim after meeting [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] in [[baghdad|Baghdad]] and giving him his allegiance. After Bahá'u'lláh moved to Edirne, he dispatched Nabíl to visit the Iranian Bábís to announce his open claim to be [[he-whom-god-shall-make-manifest|He Whom God Shall Make Manifest]], and also on one occasion to be the first to perform the newly revealed [[bahai-pilgrimage|pilgrimage]] rituals to the House of the Báb in [[shiraz|Shíráz]] and the House of Bahá'u'lláh in Baghdad (lines 23381–23392).

He eventually joined the companions of Bahá'u'lláh in [[akka|Akká]]. He was so overcome with grief after Bahá'u'lláh's passing that he drowned himself in the sea (lines 23393–23401).

## As Chronicler
He was renowned as a poet, but is best known to English readers as the author of the historical narrative of the Bábí period which [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] translated as the *[[dawn-breakers|Dawn-Breakers]]* (Nabíl's Narrative) (lines 23401–23405).

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] named him as one of the Apostles of Bahá'u'lláh (lines 23406–23409).
