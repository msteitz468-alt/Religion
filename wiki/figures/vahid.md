# Vahid

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Biographical Overview

Vahid ("Peerless") was the religious title of Sayyid Yahyá Dárábí (d. 1850), a distinguished cleric and son of Sayyid Ja'far, known as Kashfi ("discloser") for his skill in the esoteric interpretation of the Quran (lines 32017–32022). He was reportedly delegated by [[muhammad-shah|Muhammad Sháh]] to investigate the claims of the Báb while the latter was still in Shiraz, converting following the revelation of the Báb's commentary on the Quranic súra of Kawthar (autumn 1845) (lines 32023–32029).

His conversion was significant both because of his social prominence and because he was one of the first non-Shaykhis to accept the Báb (lines 32029–32032). The Báb appointed him his representative to the Shah (lines 32032–32034). He traveled extensively visiting Babis, and early in 1850 proceeded to Yazd, where his open proclamation of Babism provoked great controversy (lines 32034–32039).

## Nayriz Upheaval and Death

He then proceeded to the small town of Nayriz, where he already had a local following. A large number of inhabitants converted, exacerbating tensions with the town governor. An armed struggle developed, which ended with a truce broken by the killing of Vahid (29 June 1850) and a number of his companions; he was then aged about forty (lines 32040–32048).
