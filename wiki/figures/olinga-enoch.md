# Enoch Olinga (1926–1979)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23896–23928.*

## Biographical Overview
The only black African [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]]. Olinga was a Christian Ugandan of the Teso tribe. He became a Bahá'í in 1952 in Kampala, and shortly afterwards returned to his home district to teach the Faith (lines 23896–23903).

## Pioneering in Africa
In 1953 he was the first Bahá'í [[bahai-pioneering|pioneer]] to British Cameroon, and was named as [[knights-of-bahaullah|Knight of Bahá'u'lláh]] for that territory. In 1956 he was elected as chairman of the newly formed Regional Spiritual Assembly for North-West Africa. In 1957 he became the first of the new African Bahá'ís to make the pilgrimage to the [[bahai-world-centre|Bahá'í World Centre]] (lines 23903–23915).

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] named him 'Father of Victories' (*Abu'l-Futúḥ*) for his services to the Faith, and in October 1957 appointed him as a Hand of the Cause; at thirty-one he was the youngest of those appointed. Extensive world-wide travels followed (lines 23915–23921).

## Death
He served as a rallying point for the Ugandan Bahá'ís during the troubled 1977–9 period. He, his wife, and three of his children were murdered on 16 September 1979 by unknown assailants (lines 23922–23927).
