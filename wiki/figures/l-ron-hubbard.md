# L. Ron Hubbard

**Lafayette Ronald Hubbard** (1911–1986) is the founder of [[scientology|Scientology]] and the sole author
of its scripture — known within the movement simply as **"Source."**

## Biographical Overview

Before 1950 Hubbard was a prolific **pulp-fiction** writer (adventure and science fiction). In May 1950 he
published **[[dianetics|Dianetics]]**, a self-help system that became a bestseller; when the Dianetics
foundations failed financially and were rejected by professionals, he reorganized the movement as a
**religion**, founding the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] (1953–54). He developed
[[auditing|auditing]], the E-meter, the [[thetan|thetan]] doctrine, and the graded **Bridge** with its
confidential **OT levels**. In the late 1960s he ran the movement at sea aboard a fleet (the origin of the
**Sea Org**, where he was "the Commodore"); he spent his last years in seclusion and died in 1986, after
which **David Miscavige** consolidated leadership.

## Significance

Hubbard is the **author of an entire living scriptural corpus** and the designer of its practice — a 20th-
century parallel to founder-revelators like [[joseph-smith|Joseph Smith]]. His exact words are treated as
infallible "Source," to be applied verbatim ("standard tech").

## Historicity and Controversies

A historical figure, but his **biography is fiercely contested**: the Church promotes a heroic account
(war hero, nuclear physicist, explorer) that journalists and ex-members ([[reitman-inside-scientology|
Reitman]], [[shelton-a-to-xenu|Shelton]]) and court records dispute on many points (his military and
academic record, his health, and the development of the "tech"). The gap between the official hagiography
and the documentary record is itself a major theme of the scholarship. Recorded without adjudication
(Contradiction Protocol).
