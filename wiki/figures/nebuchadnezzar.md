## Biographical Overview
The powerful king of the Babylonian empire who conquered Jerusalem and exiled its inhabitants. He experienced God's power through Daniel's dream interpretations, the fiery furnace, and a personal period of divinely ordained madness that humbled his pride.

## Primary Source Appearances
Major historical figure in Daniel chapters 1-4.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
Nebuchadnezzar illustrates that human power, no matter how absolute, is subject to God’s sovereignty. His humbling and subsequent restoration serve as a profound theological lesson on the dangers of pride and the necessity of acknowledging God's ultimate rule.

## In Ezekiel's Oracles (Block)

Daniel Block ([[block-ezekiel-25-48-nicot|Block, Ezekiel 25–48 (NICOT)]]) notes that the
form *nĕbûkadreʾṣṣar* ("Nebuchadrezzar"), "found in the OT only in Ezekiel and Jeremiah,
corresponds to the Babylonian *Nabû-kudurri-uṣur*," whereas "Nebuchadnezzar … is a corruption
of the Babylonian original" (on 26:7). There he bears the title *melek melākîm*, "king of
kings" (Akk. *šar-šarrāni*), though Block observes that "neo-Babylonian texts attest the
title only for the god Marduk." Block reads Ezekiel as consistently casting him as Yahweh's
instrument: in the siege of Tyre he "is merely a tool in Yahweh's hands," and in 29:17–21 the
*śākār* ("wages") language "suggests that Nebuchadrezzar is the agent of a third party. The
portrayal of Yahweh as a superior who has engaged mercenaries to carry out his agenda is
remarkable" — the unpaid wages of the thirteen-year Tyrian siege are compensated with Egypt,
which Block connects to a fragmentary cuneiform text on the king's 37th year (568 BCE)
marching against Egypt. In 31:11 the *ʾêl* ("ram") of nations who fells the great cedar is
"undoubtedly Nebuchadrezzar," and in 32:11 the king of Babylon's sword is Yahweh's own sword.
Block explains Ezekiel's lack of any Babylon oracle by "the prophet's pro-Babylonian stance
in political matters. To him Babylon was the sword in Yahweh's hand." See
[[babylon|Babylon]].

## Historical Context (Coogan ingest)
The historical Nebuchadrezzar II (the spelling closer to Akkadian *Nabu-kudurri-uṣur*) was the great king of the Neo-Babylonian (Chaldean) Empire who defeated Egypt at Carchemish (605 BCE), deported Judah's elite in 597, and **destroyed [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] and the First Temple in 586 BCE**, inaugurating the Babylonian Exile. See [[babylonians]] (empire and deportation policy), [[babylon]] (the capital and place of exile), and [[lamentations]]. Cf. the more candid **Babylonian Chronicle** as a source (M. Cogan, Coogan Ch. 7).
