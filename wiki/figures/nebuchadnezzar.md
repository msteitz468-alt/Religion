## Biographical Overview
The powerful king of the Babylonian empire who conquered Jerusalem and exiled its inhabitants. He experienced God's power through Daniel's dream interpretations, the fiery furnace, and a personal period of divinely ordained madness that humbled his pride.

## Primary Source Appearances
Major historical figure in Daniel chapters 1-4.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
Nebuchadnezzar illustrates that human power, no matter how absolute, is subject to God’s sovereignty. His humbling and subsequent restoration serve as a profound theological lesson on the dangers of pride and the necessity of acknowledging God's ultimate rule.

## Historical Context (Coogan ingest)
The historical Nebuchadrezzar II (the spelling closer to Akkadian *Nabu-kudurri-uṣur*) was the great king of the Neo-Babylonian (Chaldean) Empire who defeated Egypt at Carchemish (605 BCE), deported Judah's elite in 597, and **destroyed [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] and the First Temple in 586 BCE**, inaugurating the Babylonian Exile.
