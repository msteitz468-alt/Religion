# Joseph Smith

**Joseph Smith Jr.** (1805–1844) is the founder and first prophet of the [[latter-day-saints|Latter Day Saint movement]] — the figure through whom Mormonism's scriptural canon and church order came.

## Biographical Overview (from the standard works)

- Born **23 December 1805**, Sharon, Windsor County, Vermont; family moves to Palmyra/Manchester, New York (JS-H 1:3–4).
- **First Vision** (early spring 1820): two Personages; command to join none of the contending sects; creeds condemned (JS-H 1:14–20) — see [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great Price]].
- **Moroni** and the plates (1823–1827); translation with Urim and Thummim; Martin Harris / Anthon episode; Oliver Cowdery as scribe (JS-H).
- **Aaronic Priesthood** restored by John the Baptist, 15 May 1829 (JS-H 1:68–72; D&C 13); Melchizedek keys promised via Peter, James, and John.
- Publishes the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] (1830); organizes the church 6 April 1830 (D&C 20–21).
- Ohio (Kirtland): high doctrinal revelations including [[degrees-of-glory|D&C 76]], priesthood (§84, §107), Word of Wisdom (§89), temple dedication and keys (§109–110).
- Missouri: Zion center at Independence (D&C 57); expulsions; Liberty Jail letters (§121–123).
- Nauvoo: temple and baptism for the dead (§124, §127–128); teachings on Godhead corporeality (§130); **new and everlasting covenant** / plural marriage (§132).
- **Killed** with brother Hyrum at Carthage Jail, Illinois, **27 June 1844** (D&C 135). Succession crisis splits the movement (Brigham Young / Utah vs. later [[community-of-christ|Community of Christ]] line under Joseph Smith III, etc.).

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Smith is simultaneously **translator**, **revelator**, **prophet**, and **institutional founder**. Through him the movement claims:
- restoration of priesthood after the [[great-apostasy|Great Apostasy]];
- an open canon ([[continuing-revelation|continuing revelation]]);
- distinctive cosmology (premortal life, degrees of glory, exaltation);
- temple theology (dead, sealing, marriage).

D&C 135:3 (John Taylor) states he "has done more, save Jesus only, for the salvation of men in this world, than any other man that ever lived in it" — a superlative *within* the LDS canon, contested outside it.
