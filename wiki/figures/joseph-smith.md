# Joseph Smith

**Joseph Smith Jr.** (1805–1844) is the founder and first prophet of the [[latter-day-saints|Latter Day
Saint movement]] — the figure through whom Mormonism's entire scriptural canon and church order came.

## Biographical Overview

Born in Vermont and raised in the "burned-over district" of revivalist upstate New York, Smith reported a
**First Vision** (c. 1820) of the Father and the Son, and later visits from the angel **Moroni** directing
him to golden plates, which he published as the **[[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]]** (1830) and on which
he organized the church the same year. He led the Saints through successive settlements (Kirtland, Ohio;
Independence, Missouri; **Nauvoo**, Illinois), receiving the revelations collected in the
[[doctrine-and-covenants|Doctrine and Covenants]] and producing the [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great
Price]]. He was **killed by a mob** at Carthage Jail, Illinois, in **1844**, precipitating the succession
crisis that split the movement (Brigham Young's Utah church vs. the later Community of Christ).

## Significance

Few individuals have authored a living scriptural canon: Smith is at once translator, revelator,
prophet, and institutional founder — the source of the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]], the
[[doctrine-and-covenants|D&C]], the [[pearl-of-great-price|Pearl of Great Price]], and the **Joseph Smith
Translation** of the Bible. He frames Mormonism's claim of a **Restoration** of the true church and
priesthood after a Great Apostasy.

## Historicity and Controversies

A historical figure abundantly documented. The disputed questions are about the **origin and nature** of
his revelations and translations — divine (the believer's view) vs. 19th-century composition (the critical
view) — including the Book of Abraham's relationship to the surviving Egyptian papyri and the historicity
of the Book of Mormon. His practice of **plural marriage** is also historically contested. Recorded
without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
