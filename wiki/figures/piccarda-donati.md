# Piccarda Donati

A Florentine woman of the Donati family — sister of Dante's friend Forese
Donati and of his enemy Corso — and the **first soul to speak** to
[[dante-alighieri|Dante]] in the [[paradiso|Paradiso]], in the sphere of the Moon
(Canto 3), among those whose religious vows were broken.

## In the Divine Comedy

Having entered the Poor Clares, Piccarda was dragged from the cloister by her brother
Corso and forced into a political marriage — "we did neglect our vows. These were in
some part void" (3:55–56). The Moon's souls appear faint, "less strongly than a pearl
on some white brow." She delivers the canticle's first great doctrinal speech, on the
**contentment of the blessed will**: "we in will are brought to rest / by power of
caritas that makes us will / no more than what we have, nor thirst for more… In His
volition is the peace we have" (3:70–72, 85). She points out the Empress **Constance**,
likewise veiled against her will, who "never let the veil fall from her heart."

Her case sets up Beatrice's doctrine (Canto 4) of the **absolute vs. conditioned
will**: Piccarda's absolute will never consented to leave the veil, though under
duress her conditioned will yielded — so she is genuinely, if "least," blessed, all
the souls in fact dwelling in the Empyrean.

## Significance

Piccarda dramatizes the poem's vision of beatitude as the perfect alignment of the
will with God's, where degree of glory provokes no envy. Kirkpatrick links her,
[[francesca-da-rimini|Francesca]], and La Pia as three women undone by the men who
controlled them — but Piccarda alone speaks from blessedness.
