# Nezha

**Nézhā** is the **rebellious child-god** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]], a central figure
of the **[[investiture-of-the-gods|Fengshen Yanyi]]** and of [[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's]] chapter on
"The Guardian of the Gate of Heaven." Born to the general **Li Jing** (the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King) after
a three-year pregnancy, Nezha emerges as a **ball of flesh** that opens to reveal a miraculous boy, already
armed with magical treasures — the **Universe Ring**, the **Red Armillary Sash**, and **Wind-Fire Wheels** on
which he flies.

His defining myth is one of **defiance, sacrifice, and rebirth**: as a child he kills the **Dragon-King's son**
(and flays him) after a quarrel at the sea, bringing the wrath of the Dragon-Kings, who threaten to flood his
people. To save his family and town, Nezha **takes his own life — returning his flesh to his mother and his
bones to his father**, severing the debt of his body to his parents (a startling inversion of filial piety).
His master then **resurrects him with a body fashioned from lotus root and flowers**, so that he is reborn
free, **owing his existence to no one**. He goes on to become a mighty **demon-queller** and, in the
investiture, a heavenly **marshal** — the "Marshal of the Central Altar," a protective deity especially beloved
in **Taiwan** (where his youthful, motorcycle-riding "Third Prince" form appears in modern temple processions).
Nezha embodies **youthful rebellion, self-sacrifice, and the tension between filial duty and the freedom of the
reborn self**.
