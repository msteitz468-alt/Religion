Goliath of Gath, the [[philistines|Philistine]] champion who defies the ranks of Israel in the
Valley of Elah for forty days and is killed by [[david|David]] with a sling-stone and then
beheaded with his own sword, is both the most famous single combat in the Hebrew Bible and one of
its sharpest textual problems: the manuscripts disagree about how tall he was, and
[[2-samuel|2 Sam 21:19]] credits his death to another man entirely.

## Biographical Overview

The narrative introduces him as a champion from Gath who comes out from the Philistine camp
morning and evening, armored in a bronze helmet, a coat of mail, bronze greaves and javelin, with
a spear whose shaft is like a weaver's beam and an iron spearhead of six hundred shekels, preceded
by a shield-bearer. He proposes representative combat: if he is beaten, the Philistines will serve
Israel. [[saul|Saul]] and Israel are dismayed. David, sent by Jesse with provisions for his
brothers, hears the taunt, refuses Saul's armor, takes five smooth stones, and kills him with the
sling; having no sword himself, David takes Goliath's, cuts off his head, and the Philistines
flee. Goliath's sword is later kept at the sanctuary at Nob and given to David by the priest
Ahimelech (1 Sam 21:9).

## Primary Source Appearances

- **1 Sam 17**: the challenge, the combat, the decapitation, the rout.
- **1 Sam 18:6-7**: the women's song, "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten
  thousands" — the aftermath that turns Saul against David.
- **1 Sam 21:9**: "the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of
  Elah," wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod at Nob.
- **2 Sam 21:15-22**: a list of Philistine giant-killings in David's later wars — including
  **21:19, where [[elhanan|Elhanan]] son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite "struck down Goliath the Gittite,
  the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam."**
- **1 Chr 20:5**: the parallel notice reads instead that Elhanan struck down **Lahmi the brother
  of Goliath** the Gittite.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Historical-philological / evangelical ([[david-tsumura|Tsumura]], NICOT)**: the Goliath
  narrative falls within Tsumura's block on David's introduction and rise (1 Sam 16–18), covered
  in the wiki's ingest of the commentary. What the wiki records from Tsumura on this material is
  methodological rather than apologetic: an **MT-first text criticism** that resists easy
  LXX/4QSam emendation on the grounds that many alleged corruptions are phonetic spellings,
  brachylogy, or misconstrued grammar. The wiki also records, on the [[1-samuel|1 Samuel]] page,
  the narrative tension between 1 Sam 17:55-58 (Saul asking whose son David is) and 1 Sam 16:14-23
  (David already at court), preserved side by side without harmonization.
- **Later reception**: no source ingested in this wiki treats the rabbinic, patristic, or Islamic
  Goliath (Jālūt) traditions at length. The Qur'anic Jālūt material is not covered by any ingested
  source and is therefore not summarized here.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

The combat is the narrative demonstration of the Song of Hannah's programme as the wiki records it
from Tsumura — the reversal of human fortunes, the bows of the mighty broken. Israel's tallest man
([[saul|Saul]], "a head taller than any of the people") does not fight; the youngest and least
regarded son of Jesse does, and does so explicitly without sword or spear. David's speech frames
the outcome as evidence "that all this assembly may know that YHWH saves not with sword and
spear." The episode is thus positioned as the public confirmation of the private anointing in 1
Sam 16 — and as the trigger of Saul's jealousy.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

**[CONTRADICTION] — two live disputes, recorded as positions without adjudication.**

1. **Goliath's height.** MT 1 Sam 17:4 gives "six cubits and a span" — roughly nine feet nine
   inches. The Septuagint tradition and 4QSam^a read **four cubits and a span** — roughly six feet
   nine inches: still exceptionally tall for the period, but not a giant. Position A takes MT as
   original; position B takes the shorter reading as original and MT as an exaggerating
   development. Tsumura's stated general method is MT-first (recorded above); McCarter/Tov-style
   eclectic reconstruction, the wiki's recorded alternative, weights LXX + 4QSam^a where MT is
   judged corrupt. This wiki does not decide between them.
2. **Who killed Goliath.** 1 Sam 17 credits David; 2 Sam 21:19 credits **Elhanan** the
   Bethlehemite, with the same "weaver's beam" spear formula. Recorded positions include: (a) the
   Chronicler's reading at 1 Chr 20:5 ("Lahmi the brother of Goliath") preserves the original and
   2 Sam 21:19 is textually damaged; (b) 1 Chr 20:5 is a harmonizing correction of an older,
   embarrassing tradition in which Elhanan killed Goliath and the deed was later transferred to
   David; (c) two different Philistine champions bore or were given the same name or title. None
   is adjudicated here.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Goliath is a standard test case in Samuel textual criticism (the long MT vs. short LXX form of
1 Sam 17 as a whole, not only the height figure), in debates over the composition of the
David-rise narrative, and in the moral-typological reading of David as the unarmed deliverer.
