# Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17582–17686.*

## Biographical Overview

Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í (c.1814–1849) was the first of the Báb's disciples, called by the Báb the *Bábu'l-Báb* ("Gate of the Gate") (l. 17587–17588). He was born in the hamlet of Zirak near Bushrúyih in the north-eastern Iranian province of Khurásán (l. 17589–17591). His father appears to have been a wealthy cloth dyer who was also a local cleric; his mother was a respected poet (l. 17592–17594).

He furthered his religious studies in Mashhad and Iṣfahán, then went to Karbalá as a student of the Shaykhi leader Sayyid [[kazim-rashti|Kázim Rashtí]], becoming so highly respected that some thought he might be his successor (l. 17595–17600). He was sent as Rashtí's representative to defend Shaykhi views before Mullá Muḥammad-Báqir Shaftí, "the pre-eminent Shí'í cleric of his age" (l. 17601–17606).

## Role in the Founding of Babism

Returning to Karbalá shortly after Rashtí's death (31 December 1843/1 January 1844), he became leader of a group of younger Shaykhis holding messianic expectations (l. 17607–17611). After a period of religious retreat he travelled to Shíráz, perhaps intending to meet Karím Khán, one of the claimants to Shaykhi leadership. Instead, he met the Báb and, after initial hesitation (doubting whether a non-cleric could hold a leadership station), accepted his claims (l. 17611–17620). The night of his acceptance (22 May 1844) effectively marked the start of Babism as a religious movement (l. 17619–17621). Others of his companions followed his lead.

He then journeyed to Iṣfahán, Káshán, and Tehran (where he forwarded a letter from the Báb to Muḥammad Sháh and Ḥájí Mírzá Áqásí), gaining converts including Mullá Ṣádiq Khurásání and [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]], before returning to his home province, establishing himself in Mashhad as a focal point for Babi mission (l. 17624–17634). He travelled extensively, visited the Báb again in Shíráz and Mákú. In July 1848, at the Báb's instructions, he left Mashhad at the head of a group of followers carrying the messianic symbol of the Black Standard (l. 17641–17648).

## Death at Shaykh Ṭabarsí

Eventually reaching Bárfurúsh, the party was attacked, leading to the Shaykh Ṭabarsí upheaval, during which Mullá Ḥusayn was killed on 2 February 1849 (l. 17649–17662). Accounts note his physical frailty and contrast his former scholarly life with his new life as a "fearless and much-feared warrior" (l. 17664–17669).

## Station in Babi–Bahá'í Tradition

The Bábís accorded Mullá Ḥusayn an exalted station:
- "Gate" to the Báb, acting as his deputy (l. 17678)
- "The first to believe" (l. 17679)
- "The primal mirror" (l. 17680)
- Regarded by some as the "return" of the Prophet Muḥammad and of the Imám Ḥusayn, as well as the Qá'im of Khurásán (l. 17681–17684)
- The Báb's eulogies and prayers for him amount to three times the volume of the Quran (l. 17684–17686)

[[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] stated that if it had not been for Mullá Ḥusayn, God "would not have ... ascended the throne of eternal glory" (l. 17638–17642). Among items in the [[international-archives|International Archives]] is Mullá Ḥusayn's sword (l. 18392–18393).
