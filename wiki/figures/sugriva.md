# Sugrīva

**Sugrīva** is the monkey king of Kiṣkindhā in the [[ramayana|Rāmāyaṇa]], and the principal political ally of [[rama|Rāma]] in the campaign to rescue [[sita|Sītā]] from [[ravana|Rāvaṇa]]'s Laṅkā.

## In William Buck's Retelling

Sugrīva is living in exile on Ṛṣyamūka Hill when Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa arrive, his one loyal companion being [[hanuman|Hanumān]] — son of the wind god Vāyu, whose powers protect Ṛṣyamūka from Sugrīva's brother Vali (who cannot cross the hill's boundary). Sugrīva was driven from his throne and his wife Rumā was taken by his older brother Vali. Rāma kills Vali (by shooting him from concealment during a staged duel, an act the dying Vali challenges as unjust) and installs Sugrīva as king of Kiṣkindhā. Sugrīva in turn commits the monkey and bear armies to Rāma's cause, dispatching search parties in all four directions.

Sugrīva's killing of Vali is one of the Rāmāyaṇa's persistent ethical cruxes: Vali argues (on his deathbed) that the shot from hiding was dishonorable; Rāma argues that Vali had sinned against dharma by seizing his brother's wife. The text does not fully resolve the dispute.

## Theological Significance

Sugrīva's alliance with Rāma establishes the cooperation between the human/divine avatar and the animal kingdom that is the Rāmāyaṇa's central military logic. The monkey and bear armies are not caricatures of humans but full agents in their own right, whose loyalty to their human leader is both the narrative engine and a devotional model for readers.
