# Jimmu (Emperor Jimmu)

**Jimmu** (Kamu-Yamato-Iware-biko, posthumously **Jimmu Tennō**) is, in [[shinto|Shinto]] and Japanese
tradition, the **first Emperor of Japan** — the figure in whom the **age of the gods passes into the age of
men**. He is the **great-grandson of [[ninigi|Ninigi]]**, and thus a direct descendant of the sun-goddess
**[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** through the imperial line she established.

The [[kojiki|Kojiki]] and [[nihongi|Nihongi]] narrate his **eastward campaign** from Himuka in Kyūshū to the
**Yamato** plain (around modern Nara), where he establishes his capital and is enthroned — an expedition marked
by divine aid: a giant **three-legged crow (Yatagarasu)** sent by the heavenly kami to guide him through the
mountains, and a **sacred sword** dropped to him to overcome hostile local deities and chieftains. The Nihongi
assigns his accession the precise date traditionally reckoned as **660 BCE** — a chronology with **no
historical support** (the figure is regarded by historians as **legendary**, the dating an 8th-century
construction to lend the throne antiquity).

Jimmu's significance is almost entirely **dynastic and ideological**: he is the **hinge of the imperial-descent
myth**, the human founder who makes the reigning emperor the unbroken heir of Amaterasu. This claim, latent for
centuries, was made the cornerstone of **[[state-shinto|State Shinto]]**: modern Japan celebrated **Kigensetsu**
(National Foundation Day) on the supposed anniversary of Jimmu's accession, and the "unbroken line of ten
thousand generations" became central to wartime nationalist ideology.
