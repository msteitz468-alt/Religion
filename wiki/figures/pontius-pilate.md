# Pontius Pilate

**Pontius Pilate**, Roman prefect of Judea (26–36 CE), condemned [[jesus-of-nazareth|Jesus]]
to crucifixion on the charge of claiming to be "king of the Jews."

## Historical Character vs. Gospel Portrayal

[[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]] ([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]]) contrasts the
**extra-biblical** Pilate with the increasingly sympathetic Gospel figure. Josephus records
the affair of the imperial standards smuggled into Jerusalem and the violent suppression of
protest over the aqueduct funded from the Temple treasury (*Antiquities* 18.3). Philo, a
contemporary, describes "his venality, his violence, his thefts, his assaults … his frequent
executions of untried prisoners, and his endless savage ferocity" (*Embassy to Gaius* 302). On
this basis Ehrman judges it "highly unlikely" that Pilate would have granted a council member's
request to give a crucified insurgent a decent, identifiable burial (see
[[burial-of-jesus|the burial controversy]]).

By contrast the Gospels progressively **exonerate** Pilate — reluctant in Matthew, thrice
declaring Jesus innocent in Luke and John, and finally converting in later non-canonical texts.
Ehrman reads "this progressive exoneration of Pilate" as serving "clear anti-Jewish purposes,"
shifting responsibility for Jesus's death onto the Jewish authorities (cf.
[[guilt-for-christs-death|guilt for Christ's death]]).
