# Indira Gandhi

Prime Minister of India whose handling of the Punjab crisis — the ordering of
[[operation-blue-star|Operation Blue Star]] and her subsequent assassination by her Sikh bodyguards — is
the pivot of the final chapters of Khushwant Singh's *A History of the Sikhs* Vol. 2. She is not a Sikh
figure but is central to modern Sikh history; the page records Singh's (markedly critical) account.
Singh, himself a Sikh, **returned his Padma Bhushan** in protest at the army's assault on the Golden
Temple.

## The Punjabi Suba and "Duplicity"

As chair of a post-1965-war cabinet sub-committee, she recommended the Punjabi Suba, then as PM
implemented the 1966 reorganization while, in Singh's reading, "loading the dice against it" — making
[[punjab|Chandigarh]] a shared Union Territory and attaching the Fazilka–Abohar corridor to Haryana.
Singh charges her with "a kind of duplicity more becoming of a small-time politician than a farsighted
statesman," citing her own memoir *My Truth*: to concede the Akali demand "would mean… letting down its
Hindu supporters."

## The Bhindranwale Strategy and the Blocked Settlements

Singh argues she "created Bhindranwale" as a wedge against the moderate [[akali-movement|Akalis]] (citing
Surjeet and Kuldip Nayar; see [[jarnail-singh-bhindranwale|Bhindranwale]]). Through 26 negotiating
meetings (1981–84), he insists, "it was not the Akalis but Mrs Gandhi who resiled from her commitments…
on five occasions the terms of a settlement were almost finalized." His diagnosis: "It did not suit Mrs
Gandhi to agree to any settlement which might be construed as a concession made to Sikhs at the expense
of Hindus. She had her eyes firmly fixed on her party's electoral prospects in the neighbouring states."
On 2 June 1984 she broadcast an appeal to "shed hatred" while — Singh notes — "she had already authorized
the army to do precisely the opposite." See [[anandpur-sahib-resolution|the Anandpur Sahib Resolution]].

## Operation Blue Star and Assassination

She ordered the June 1984 army assault on the Golden Temple ([[operation-blue-star|Operation Blue Star]]),
which Singh calls "a grievous error of judgement, a blunder for which she personally and the country
generally would have to pay a heavy price for decades to come." On **31 October 1984**, having declined
advice to remove Sikhs from her guard, she was shot dead by two of her Sikh bodyguards, **Beant Singh**
and **Satwant Singh** — "eighteen bullets into her frail body." A note attributed to her the day before:
"If I die a violent death as some fear and a few are plotting, I know the violence will be in the thought
and the action of the assassin, not in my dying." Her killing triggered the
[[1984-anti-sikh-violence|November 1984 anti-Sikh massacres]].
