# Moses

**Biographical Overview**:
The great leader and lawgiver of Israel (Moshe), born to Levite parents during Egyptian oppression, hidden in a basket, raised in Pharaoh's court, fled to Midian after killing an Egyptian. Called by God at the burning bush (Exod 3), returned to confront Pharaoh, mediated the ten plagues and Passover, led the exodus through the Red Sea, received the Torah at Sinai (Decalogue, Book of the Covenant), interceded after the golden calf, oversaw construction of the tabernacle, and led the people through the wilderness for forty years (grumbling, manna/quail, water from rock, spies, Korah rebellion, etc.). Died on Mount Nebo, buried by God (Deut 34). Never entered the Promised Land due to striking the rock (Num 20).

From [[james-bruckner|James K. Bruckner]] (UBCS Exodus): Moses is the central human figure embodying God's creational and redemptive purposes. Bruckner highlights the progressive revelation of YHWH's name and character through Moses (burning bush "I AM", plagues as "signs", Sinai theophany, golden calf crisis resolved by the "thirteen attributes" in Exod 34:6-7). Moses as mediator: reluctant at first ("who am I?", "what if they do not believe?"), empowered by signs and Aaron, intercessor par excellence (golden calf: "if you will forgive... blot me out"; Exod 33 "show me your glory"). The narrative arc moves from oppression → deliverance → covenant → crisis/forgiveness → presence (tabernacle filled with glory, Exod 40).

**Primary Source Appearances**:
- **Exodus**: Dominant figure — birth (ch. 2), call (3-4), plagues/exodus (5-15), wilderness to Sinai (15-18), theophany and law (19-24), golden calf intercession (32-34), tabernacle (25-31, 35-40). Bruckner structures around six sections with Moses as the human pivot: exit from Egypt (plagues, Passover, sea), journey (provision), law/covenant, instructions, crisis/forgiveness (name proclaimed), building/presence. Zohar Vol. IV (Shemot–Yitro) focuses on the call, plagues, sea crossing (with 72-Name), Amalek, Jethro, and Sinai revelation as the Moses–Shekhinah romance and sefirotic theurgy.
- **Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy**: Continues as lawgiver, census leader, spy story, rebellions (Korah, water from rock), farewell discourses and death (Deut). Central to the Pentateuch as mediator of Torah. [[timothy-ashley|Ashley]] (NICOT Numbers) densifies Numbers-specific loci: unique **mouth-to-mouth** prophecy vs Miriam/Aaron (Num 12); multi-factorial **Meribah** sin (20:1–13)—inexact obedience, anger, self as miracle-worker, formally *lack of trust* to hold Yahweh holy publicly, parallel to the people’s unbelief in ch. 14; intercession pattern across rebellions; commissioning of Joshua with Eleazar/Urim (27:12–23) while Moses remains senior leader through Numbers’ end and Deuteronomy; Midian war as unfinished business before death (31:2 with 27:13).
- **Psalms**: Psalm 90 is attributed to Moses, serving as the foundational opening for Book Four of the Psalter in response to the exile.
- **Hebrews 3:1-6**: Compared with Jesus; Moses faithful as "servant" in God's house, Christ as "Son" over it.
- **Hebrews 11:23-29**: Faith in identifying with God's people, choosing reproach over Egyptian treasures, keeping Passover, crossing sea "as on dry land".
- **Other NT**: Acts 7 (Stephen's speech: Moses as rejected deliverer, type of Christ); 2 Cor 3 (veil on Moses' face vs. unveiled in Christ); Jude 9 (dispute over Moses' body).

**Tradition-Specific Reception**:
- **Jewish**: Moshe Rabbenu ("our teacher Moses"), greatest prophet (Deut 34:10), mediator of Torah. Rabbinic tradition: 613 mitzvot from Sinai; face-to-face with God (Exod 33). Central to Passover Haggadah, Talmudic law (Oral Torah as explanation of written). Philo: Moses as ideal philosopher-king, allegorical lawgiver.
- **Christian (evangelical, per Bruckner and NT sources)**: Type of Christ (deliverer, mediator, lawgiver vs. grace in Christ); faithful servant (Heb 3); intercessor prefiguring Jesus. Bruckner (believing criticism, creational theology): Exodus as paradigm of liberation and God's desire to dwell with people; Moses embodies the tension of human frailty + divine empowerment. Golden calf crisis reveals God's forgiving character (34:6-7 as key text). In Hebrews (Cockerill): honored faithfulness, yet surpassed by the Son; tabernacle as shadow of heavenly reality. Liberation theology: Moses as model for oppressed peoples' leaders.
- **Islamic**: Musa, major prophet (mentioned more than any other in Quran); receives Tawrat (Torah) on Sinai; confronts Fir'aun (Pharaoh); parts the sea; golden calf episode. Emphasizes monotheism and prophethood; not lawgiver in same way.
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)**: Moses holds a unique place in the Zohar's prophetology. Whereas all other prophets receive their visions refracted through Netsaḥ and Hod (the lower sefirotic "legs," which in turn channel from above), **Moses alone prophesies from [[sefirot|Tif'eret]]** — the central pillar, the "blessed Holy One" — directly. The Zohar expresses this with the rabbinic image of the **"clear mirror" / "luminous glass"** (*aspaklarya ha-me'irah*): Moses saw through the one transparent, undimmed mirror, while other prophets saw through nine dim, refracting mirrors. From this flows his most striking Zoharic epithet: Moses is the **"bridegroom of the [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]"** (*ba'al ha-Matronita*) — the one human who, by reaching Tif'eret, became the partner/intimate of the divine Bride; he could approach her "whenever he wished," which is read into his unique face-to-face intimacy with God (Exod 33:11; Num 12:6–8) and his celibacy after Sinai. As the embodiment of the harmonizing center (Tif'eret/Daat), Moses is the cosmic **mediator** — the role the Zohar foregrounds in his attempt to reconcile the [[korah|Korah]] rebellion, which it reads as the left side (Gevurah) refusing the right (Ḥesed); Moses, who "contemplated the act of Creation" and grasped how the central pillar reconciles left and right, tried to mediate as God did at Creation.

**Volume IV (Exodus opening, Shemot–Yitro)** deepens the Moses–Shekhinah romance and applies it to the historical redemption. Matt’s preface to Pritzker Vol. IV highlights “the romance of Moses and Shekhinah” as one of the volume’s central themes alongside the nature of evil and the inner meaning of the Ten Commandments. Moses’ call at the bush and his role as plague-mediator and sea-splitter enact the drawing of Shekhinah / Malkhut out of constriction (Mitzrayim) and exile. At the splitting of the Red Sea he is the human agent through whom the 72-letter Name (216 letters, 72 triads from Exod 14:19–21) is revealed — a disclosure of sefirotic power that parts the waters of the left side for the people (the bride). The Song of the Sea is theurgic praise in which Moses (Tif’eret) and Israel (with Miriam leading the women as feminine aspect) unite the sefirot. Jethro’s counsel and the Sinai theophany / Ten Commandments are read as the establishment of structures (judicial and revelatory) that mirror and maintain the sefirotic organism; the “ten words” correspond to the ten sefirot. Moses remains the unique “clear mirror” mediator who can approach the divine Bride and transmit the theurgical means (commandments) by which the divine union is sustained.

**Volume V (Mishpatim + Terumah; standards reingest 2026-07-18, [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol5|Matt Pritzker Vol V]])** densifies the romance in the Dwelling. The Zohar reads the *mishkan* as Shekhinah’s earthly home. At 2:140b–141a, Num 7:1 *kallot Mosheh* is re-read *kallat Mosheh*: “the **Bride of Moses** descended to earth”; Shekhinah breaks through “hard chunks of ice” of accusatory darkness while *va-yhi* marks the pain of opposing armies. Offering midrash (Exod 25:2) has Israel take Matronita from the Righteous One with divine permission. Moses, as “clear mirror” from Tif’eret, is the human partner of the Bride through Mishpatim laws (including *Sava de-Mishpatim* on [[gilgul|gilgul]]) and sanctuary theurgy. See [[tabernacle|Tabernacle (Mishkan)]].

**Volume VI (Tetsavveh–Pequdei; standards reingest 2026-07-18, [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol6|Matt Pritzker Vol VI]])** dramatizes rupture and provisional restoration. At the Calf, Mother/Shekhinah (who “always grasps the arm of King, so the lash is suspended”) is absent because Israel sought left-side *Elohim* (2:191a–193b). Moses **takes Her place**: three prods of Exod 32:10 map to seizing the King’s **right arm**, **left arm**, and **body** so “He could not move in any direction at all” (2:193b). Afterward he pitches the Tent outside the camp. At erection, Bezalel fashions by letter-permutations but **only Moses erects** the Dwelling (Exod 40:18; 2:235a); the glory fills as the bride arrays and the husband must not enter mid-adornment (Exod 40:35; 2:235b). Dual Dwelling above/below; death and Samael **withdrawn**, not abolished forever (Isa 25:8 reserved for the Messiah). See [[zohar|The Zohar]], [[tabernacle|Tabernacle]], [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]], [[bezalel|Bezalel]], [[aaron|Aaron]].
- **Samaritan**: Central to their Pentateuch; Mount Gerizim (not Zion) as true worship site; Moses as supreme prophet.
- **Critical scholarship**: Historical Moses debated (13th cent. BCE context, possible Egyptian name "Mose"); sources (J/E for early life, P for tabernacle); form-critical (Mosaic traditions as etiological). Bruckner engages moderately, focusing on final form's theological message.

**Theological and Narrative Significance**:
Moses is the human face of the exodus paradigm: from slavery to covenant people, from Egypt to Sinai/tabernacle (God's dwelling). Bruckner: the book answers "Will Yahweh go with them?" through Moses' intercession and the glory filling the tabernacle (Exod 40:34-38). Moses as mediator reveals God's name/character progressively (I AM → compassionate/gracious/slow to anger/abounding in ḥesed → "will by no means clear the guilty"). Typologically (NT/Hebrews): servant vs. Son; law vs. grace; shadow vs. reality. In canon: Torah given through Moses (John 1:17); greatest OT prophet, yet points beyond (Deut 18:15 "a prophet like me"). Narrative arc of Pentateuch: Genesis (promise) → Exodus-Deut (fulfillment through Moses, yet wilderness as tension).

**Historicity and Interpretive Controversies**:
- **Moses' historicity**: Traditional: 13th cent. BCE, author of Pentateuch (with later editing). Critical: composite traditions, possible Midianite/Kenite origins for YHWH cult; no direct extra-biblical evidence for exodus (debated "Habiru", Merneptah stele). Bruckner: focuses on theological truth over reconstruction; accepts redaction in 6th cent. crisis (Babylon) but affirms ancient roots.
- **Hardening of Pharaoh's heart**: Who hardens? (Pharaoh himself, God, or both). Bruckner: after Pharaoh's initial resistance, God intensifies; purpose: "that my name be proclaimed in all the earth" (9:16). Not predestination but judgment on persistent rebellion.
- **Numbers in exodus**: 600,000 men (Exod 12:37) — logistical issue; often taken as "clans" or hyperbolic.
- **Tabernacle**: P source? Post-exilic fiction? Bruckner: ancient roots; theological (new creation, God's presence among sinners).
- **Moses' death (Deut 34)**: Authorship issue (how did Moses write his own death?); traditional: Joshua or prophetic addition.
- **Golden calf**: Polemic vs. Jeroboam's calves (1 Kgs 12)? Historical syncretism?

**Influence on Commentary Traditions**:
- **Bruckner (Exodus, UBCS)**: "Believing criticism" — moderate on sources (redacted in 6th cent., views of Fretheim creational theology + Childs canonical), strong on final form's message of hope, liberation, God's desire to dwell. Moses as model of reluctant but empowered leader; plagues as "signs" revealing God; crisis at Sinai as hinge for forgiveness and presence. Engages liberation theology (limits of exodus paradigm re: Canaanites) and modern slaveries.
- **Cockerill (Hebrews)**: Moses' faithfulness honored but surpassed; tabernacle as copy; "outside the camp" (Heb 13:13) echoes Moses' choice.
- **Jewish**: Foundation of halakha; midrashic expansions (e.g., Moses' reluctance, face shining).
- **Patristic/Christian**: Typology (Irenaeus, Origen: Moses/Christ); law vs. gospel (Augustine, Luther).
- **Modern**: Liberation (Gutierrez, Cone: Moses as revolutionary); canonical (Childs); creational (Fretheim, via Bruckner).
- Cross-tradition: Abraham (promise) → Moses (fulfillment/mediation); comparisons with other lawgivers (Hammurabi, Muhammad as prophet).

## Islamic Reception (Bukhari Evidence)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Books 55–56 (Prophets; Virtues of the Prophet). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Physical Descriptions (from the Mi'raj encounters)

Four hadiths describe Moses from Muhammad's night-journey visions, with minor variations:
- "Moses was a thin person with lank hair, looking like one of the men of the tribe of Shanua." (4:55:607, Abu Huraira)
- "The prophet Moses was brown, a tall person as if from the people of the tribe of Shanu'a." (4:55:608, Ibn Abbas)
- "I saw Moses. He was a tall person with lank hair as if he belonged to the people of the tribe of Shanu's." (4:55:647, Abu Huraira)
- "Moses was of brown complexion, straight hair and tall stature as if he was from the people of Az-Zutt." (4:55:648, Ibn Umar)

### The Al-Khadir Narrative (4:55:612–614)

The most extensive narrative in this range. Moses, the most learned among Bani Israel, was directed by Allah to find a servant more learned than he at "the junction of the two seas." He traveled with Yusha' bin Nun, carrying a fish in a basket as a sign — when the fish escaped (into the sea "straight as in a tunnel"), they retraced their steps and found Al-Khadir. Three episodes: (1) Al-Khadir damaged a ship's plank; (2) Al-Khadir killed a boy; (3) Al-Khadir repaired a wall in a hostile village. Moses protested each; Al-Khadir parted after the third, explaining each act's hidden wisdom. The Prophet added: "We wished that Moses could have remained patient by virtue of which Allah might have told us more about their story." Al-Khadir's name derives from sitting on barren white land that "turned green with plantation after (his sitting over it)" (4:55:614).

### The Angel of Death Episode

Moses strikes the angel of death in the eye when the angel comes to take his soul; Allah restores the angel's eye and instructs him to tell Moses to place his hand on an ox and he will live as many years as hairs his hand covers. Moses asks to be made to die near the Holy Land ("a stone's throw") rather than live those years. The Prophet declared: "I wish I could show you his grave beside the road near the red sandhill." (4:55:619, Abu Huraira)

### Shyness and the Stone Miracle (4:55:616)

Moses was so modest that he always covered his body. Bani Israel accused him of skin disease or scrotal hernia. Allah cleared his name: the stone on which he laid his clothes fled; Moses chased it naked before a group of Bani Israel "who saw him naked then, and found him the best of what Allah had created." The stone stopped; Moses struck it with his stick. Quranic cross-reference: 33:69.

### Moses's Patience Under Criticism

"May Allah bestow His Mercy on Moses, for he was harmed more (in a worse manner) than this; yet he endured patiently" — said by the Prophet when accused of unjust distribution, invoking Moses as the paradigm of patience under false accusation. (4:55:617, Abdullah)

### The Adam-Moses Predestination Debate (4:55:621)

Adam and Moses argued: Moses accused Adam of causing humanity's expulsion from Paradise through his sin; Adam replied, "you blame me for a thing which had already been written in my fate before my creation." **"So Adam overcame Moses."** This hadith is a key text for Islamic qadar (divine decree) — predestination defeats moral censure.

### The 'Ashura Fast (4:55:631 area; cf. 4:56:592)

The Prophet found Jews fasting on 'Ashura and was told it was the day Moses was saved from Pharaoh. He declared: "We are more entitled to Moses than you," and instituted the fast for Muslims. This claim — that Muslims inherit Moses more than the Jews do — is a significant supersessionist move within Islamic prophetology.

### Eschatological Role

In the Mi'raj, Moses is encountered in the sixth heaven (accounts vary on the exact heaven). Crucially, Moses's intercession drives the reduction of the daily prayers from 50 to 5: each time Muhammad passed Moses returning from Allah, Moses urged him to ask for reduction ("your community cannot bear it"). The 50→5 reduction thus passes through Moses's advocacy. Additionally, Moses is described as holding the leg/corner of Allah's Throne in at least one hadith (cited in the Mi'raj complex).

## Bruckner (UBCS Exodus) reingest anchors (2026-07-18) — intercession and friendship

*Source: [[bruckner-exodus-ubcs|Bruckner, Exodus (UBCS)]].*

The full re-ingest densifies the intercession material. [[james-bruckner|Bruckner]] structures Exod 32–34 around God's four major decisions (not to consume; whether the presence would go; whether to forgive; how to start over), and makes Moses' friendship the hinge: "Now leave me alone" (32:10) "revealed that God would not act in judgment unless Moses refused to intercede... God asked Moses' permission and opened a window of hope." Moses' first intercession deploys "two warrants: God's reputation as a redeemer and the Creator's promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Israel" — quoting "God's own promises back to God." The second ("please forgive... but if not, blot me out") is refused on Moses' terms; God is selective: "Whoever has sinned against me I will blot out of my book." The attempted atonement "had nothing to do with blood or sacrifice. Moses' best possibility for restoration was by means of God's friendship and through conversation," and "Moses' personal friendship with God was the basis on which the Lord reversed his second decision": "Friendship was the foundation on which the restoration of sinful Israel was built." "Show me your glory" is "not an expression of Moses' ecstasy, daring, impertinence, or spiritual longing" but a request for reassurance; "The Lord gave Moses what he wanted (glory) and what he needed (knowledge of God's goodness; 34:6–7)."

On the radiant face (34:29–35): "a visible sign of the Lord's forgiveness, presence, and restored communication"; the veil kept Moses' ordinary words "from being confused with the Lord's revelation" — the opposite of a cultic mask (with Durham). Bruckner notes the Vulgate's *cornuta* ("horned") behind Michelangelo's horned Moses and subsequent antisemitic rumor. On Exod 2:12 he argues against the premeditated-murder reading (a verse with "a long history of being misunderstood and even of anti-Semitic interpretation"): the context "leaves open the possibility that Moses did not intend to kill, but that he was guilty of unintentional manslaughter in his zealousness for justice." At the calf confrontation Moses "began by standing at the gate, the traditional place of court proceedings" — due process, not wanton zealotry.

## Moses as a Divinized Human ([[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]])

[[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]] ([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]]) gathers Jewish

### Pritzker Vol. IV reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

*Source: [[matt-zohar-pritzker-vol4|Matt, *The Zohar: Pritzker Edition*, Vol. IV]] (Exodus / Shemot–Yitro). Claims attributed to Zohar body discourse; folio refs Exodus-side (2:xx). Links: [[zohar]], [[shekhinah]], [[sitra-ahra]].*

**Birth and clinging of [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] (Shemot, ~2:11a–12a).** On Exod 2:1–3 the Zohar offers multiple readings. Biographically: Amram and Jochebed couple after a divine echo; **Shekhinah is poised above their bed**, desire joined with Her, so Shekhinah never withdraws from the son (Lev 11:44 typology of sanctifying below/above). *Saw that he was good*: R. Ḥiyya — born circumcised (covenant = good); R. Yose — radiance of Shekhinah in him, the house filled with light (Gen 1:4). Theosophically: Moses existed above before birth; three months map harsh Judgment months before recognition in supernal radiance (Sinai pattern of third new moon). Basket = ark typology; mark of *vav he* (King and Matronita) appears so that Pharaoh’s daughter shows compassion. Faithful Shepherd: in the messianic Garden array Moses stands above all righteous.

**Husband of the house / Name / shining speculum (Va-Era, ~2:22a–23b).** On Exod 6:2–3 the Zohar casts Moses as **master of the house, husband of Elohim**: had he not been, his bold complaint to Adonai (*Why have You done evil…*) would have been punished; as husband he commands without fear. King–daughter parable: patriarchs spoke only through unmarried daughter (**El Shaddai** / Female still in Father’s house); Moses speaks openly on the rung of **YHVH** after the wedding — “something I have done for no one else.” Jacob possessed the house below and arrayed the house above in twelve tribes; Moses possessed the **house above** and abandoned the house below. Unseen colors / sky of Moses: only Moses perceived colors that are not seen; classic formula — Moses attained the **speculum that shines**, patriarchs the one that does not shine (*va-era* = vision of non-shining; *I was not known* = knowing of the shining).

**Voice, speech, partner (Va-Era, ~2:25b).** *Uncircumcised of lips*: Moses is **voice**; **speech** (his word) is in exile because She ([[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] / Assembly of Israel) is in exile — “I have no word! … **Aaron his partner**.” Until Sinai, voice without word; at Sinai voice unites with speech (*Elohim spoke all these words*, Exod 20:1). Moses incomplete at first because he comes to bring speech out of exile; healed when voice and speech are one.

**Staff holiness (Va-Era, ~2:28a).** Moses’ staff is holier — engraved with the Holy Name in the supernal Garden — so God will not defile it among sorcerers’ staffs; Aaron’s staff (right) performs the serpent sign.

**Afraid of the great serpent (Bo, ~2:34a).** *Bo, Come* (not *Lekh, Go*) to Pharaoh: God brings Moses chamber after chamber to the mystery of the **great sea serpent** (Ezek 29:3). Moses feared him rooted above, approaching only streams and rungs; God alone wars (*I, YHVH*, Exod 12:12). Other Bo traits: fairest of humans (Ps 45 hierarchy beyond other prophets); *zeh* man Moses as male pole; *ka-ḥatsot* as knowing night divides before decrees; withheld *peri’ah* (uncovering) so Israel would not delay healing at the exodus.

**Song of the Sea and preexistence (Be-Shallaḥ, ~2:53b–55a).** At the appointed time a holy spirit from a sapphire quarry is crowned (248 / 173 keys / five crowns / open *mem* / *shin* of patriarchs); ships of the Great Sea give weapons against Pharaoh; *Before I formed you…*. *Az yashir*: *alef* of Ancient One’s sparkle joins *zayin* (Sword of YHVH full of blood) → miracles — *Then sang Moses*. *Yashir* (will sing) suspended for that time and the time to come; Moses destined to sing again when wonders are shown. **This song is Matronita’s song to the King**, uttered by Moses from below to above; daily intent chanting merits singing it in the world to come / days of Messiah. Moses and Israel praise on behalf of Matronita.

**Seventy-two-letter Name (Be-Shallaḥ, ~2:50b–52b, 58a–b).** Matnitin: by seventy-two letters stones/waters return and split. At the sea the moon fills with seventy names on three sides (Ḥesed / Gevurah / Tif’eret); three letter arrays — first straight (Ḥesed), second backward (Gevurah/weapons), third colors (Tif’eret) — culminate as the **Holy Name of seventy-two letters** crowning the patriarchal Chariot. Trunk *א-נ-י*, branches *ו-ה-ו*. Cloud-ascent tradition: Moses opens with twelve engraved letters then seventy-two before Hadraniel — revealed what angels lack. Splitting depends on the **Holy Ancient One** (*Why cry to Me?* — all depends upon the Ancient One; “as difficult as splitting of the Red Sea”).

**Hands vs Amalek (Be-Shallaḥ manna–Amalek, ~2:66a).** Moses withdraws from the first battle to array war **above**; Joshua battles below. Hands *kevedim* = glorious, venerable, holy, never defiled — worthy of war above; stone under him to share Israel’s distress. Aaron and Ḥur support from each side → hands *emunah* (faith): each arouses his side so assistance appears above. Raise hand = right above left → **Israel above** prevails; lower hands when Israel below wanes from prayer → Amalek prevails. Shekhinah is “married and joined to Moses,” so Joshua occupies the **Youth** rung beneath him, not Shekhinah herself. Altar *YHVH Nissi*; write remembrance — wiping out above / below.

**Sinai face-to-face (Yitro, ~2:69b–70a, 82a–b).** When Jethro arrives, Moses is coupling with a supernal place / speaking with [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] — “her two sons” out of respect for that coupling. At Sinai: Moses goes up to Elohim = wings of Shekhinah. Vision hierarchy: Israel face-to-face / light in a glass lantern; Moses through an *ispaqlarya* that shines; other prophets through one that does not; Ezekiel from behind many walls (Shekhinah and chariots only). Pure from impure: Moses inverse of Balaam — through immense radiance sees a thin streak of darkness “as from behind a wall” (shell/kernel).
