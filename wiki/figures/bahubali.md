# Bahubali

Bahubali ("strong-armed"), also called **Gommatesvara**, is a legendary figure of the first cosmic
age in Jain sacred history — a son of the first [[tirthankara|fordmaker]] [[rshabha|Rshabha]].

## Narrative

In the Jain Universal History, Bahubali and his brother **Bharata** contest the universal sovereignty
their father renounced. On the verge of defeating Bharata, Bahubali recoils from the violence of
victory, renounces the world, and stands in prolonged motionless meditation (*kayotsarga*, the
"abandonment of the body") so long that creepers grow up his legs. Pride at being, technically, on his
elder brother's land is said to have briefly blocked his enlightenment until he overcame it.

## Significance and Reception

Bahubali is the great exemplar of the renunciant ideal, especially for the [[digambara|Digambaras]]. His
most famous embodiment is the **57-foot monolithic colossus at [[shravana-belgola|Shravana Belgola]]**
(Karnataka), dedicated by the Ganga general **Camundaraya** in 981 CE and ritually bathed in the
spectacular *mastakabhiseka* head-anointing ceremony held every twelve years.
