# Týr

**Týr** (Anglo-Saxon **Tiw**, whence **"Tuesday"**) is the [[norse-religion|Norse]] god of **war, justice,
law, and oaths**. Linguistically he is the **oldest and highest** of the gods: his name is the exact cognate
of Latin *deus*, Greek **[[zeus|Zeus]]**, and Vedic **[[dyaus|Dyaus]]** — the **Proto-Indo-European sky-father
(*Dyeus*)** — though in the surviving Norse myths he has been **overshadowed by [[odin|Odin]]** (much as the
Vedic Dyaus was eclipsed by Indra), a revealing Indo-European demotion.

## The Binding of Fenrir

Týr's defining myth is the **binding of the wolf [[fenrir|Fenrir]]**. As the monstrous wolf grew, the gods
resolved to fetter it with the magical ribbon **Gleipnir**, but Fenrir, suspecting a trick, would only let
itself be bound if a god **placed a hand in its mouth** as a pledge of good faith. **Only Týr was brave (and
honorable) enough** to do so. When the wolf found it could not break free and realized the betrayal, it **bit
off Týr's hand**. So the god of **oaths and justice** sacrificed his sword-hand to keep a binding pledge — a
perfect emblem of law's cost. At [[ragnarok|Ragnarök]] Týr and the hound Garmr slay each other.
