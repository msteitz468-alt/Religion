# Eve

The first woman of Genesis, formed from [[adam|Adam]]'s side, "the mother of all living," and — with
Adam — a first parent of the human race. She is central to the narrative of the fall and to Christian
doctrines of sin.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 2–4** — her formation from Adam's rib; the serpent's temptation and the eating of the
  forbidden fruit; the curse and expulsion from Eden; the bearing of Cain, Abel, and Seth.

## Augustine's Reading (City of God XIV)

In [[city-of-god|*City of God*]] Book XIV, [[augustine|Augustine]] reads Eve through the doctrine of
[[original-sin|the fall]]. Citing 1 Tim. 2:14, he holds that **Eve was deceived** by the serpent while
**Adam was not deceived** but yielded knowingly, unwilling to be parted from his companion — so Adam
"sinned with his eyes open" and bears the greater culpability for transmitting sin, though Eve was first
in the transgression. Augustine takes her formation from Adam's side "in a divine manner" to teach "how
dear the bond between man and wife should be," and insists on the **literal historicity** of Eden even
while permitting figural readings. Generation in paradise, had the pair not sinned, would have proceeded
without [[original-sin|concupiscence]], by the calm rule of the will.

## Theological Significance

Eve functions in Christian typology as a foil to Mary (the "second Eve") and, with Adam, as the head of
fallen humanity. Her deception became a locus of later debate on the relative culpability of the first
parents and on the transmission of [[original-sin|original sin]].
