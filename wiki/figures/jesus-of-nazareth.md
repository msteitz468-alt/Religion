**Biographical Overview**: Born in Bethlehem to Mary, raised in Nazareth of Galilee. Baptized by John the Baptist. Conducted a traveling ministry of teaching, healing, and exorcisms primarily in Galilee, before journeying to Jerusalem where he was crucified under Pontius Pilate. His followers proclaimed his resurrection on the third day.

**Primary Source Appearances**: 
- *Gospel of Mark*: The suffering Son of Man, misunderstood by his disciples, whose messianic identity is paradoxically revealed through the cross.
- *Gospel of Matthew*: The fulfillment of the Old Testament, the Davidic Messiah, the new Moses who delivers the definitive Torah (Sermon on the Mount).

**Tradition-Specific Reception**: 
- *Christianity*: The incarnate Word of God, second person of the Trinity, whose death and resurrection provide salvation for humanity.
- *Judaism*: Historically a Galilean preacher and wonder-worker; not accepted as the Messiah.
- *Islam*: Isa al-Masih, a major prophet and messenger, but not divine.

**Theological and Narrative Significance**: 
In Mark, Jesus is the paradoxical king whose kingdom comes through suffering, teaching his disciples the way of the cross. In Matthew, he is the climax of Israel's history, bringing the law and the prophets to their intended completion.

**Historicity and Interpretive Controversies**: 
His historical existence is virtually universally accepted by critical scholars. Interpretive controversies focus on his self-understanding (did he see himself as divine?), the nature of his miracles, and the historicity of his resurrection.

**Influence on Commentary Traditions**: 
The central figure of the entire Christian commentary tradition. Interpreted both historically and allegorically (e.g., as the true fulfillment of all Old Testament types).
