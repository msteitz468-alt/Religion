# Hachiman

**Hachiman** is the [[shinto|Shinto]] **kami of war, archery, and the warrior** — the divine protector of the
imperial house and, above all, the **patron deity of the samurai**. With Inari and the Ise/Izumo deities,
Hachiman heads one of the largest shrine networks in Japan (tens of thousands of Hachiman shrines, headed by
**Usa Jingū** in Kyūshū and **Iwashimizu** and **Tsurugaoka** Hachiman-gū). He is a **deified human**:
identified with the legendary **Emperor Ōjin**, together with his mother the **Empress Jingū** (famed for a
legendary conquest of Korea) and the sea-kami, making Hachiman a classic case of the **apotheosis of an
imperial ancestor**.

Hachiman is also the supreme example of **[[folk-shinto|shinbutsu-shūgō]]**, the long Shinto–Buddhist fusion:
he was titled **"Hachiman Daibosatsu," the Great Bodhisattva Hachiman** — a kami honored as a Buddhist
**bodhisattva** — one of the earliest and most influential kami so identified. From the rise of the warrior
class he became the **tutelary god of the Minamoto (Genji) clan** and then of the **samurai** generally;
warriors invoked him before battle, and his name (*"Namu Hachiman Daibosatsu"*) was a battle-cry. Hachiman thus
fuses **war-god, deified emperor, and Buddhist bodhisattva**, and embodies the militarized and syncretic strands
of Japanese religion.
