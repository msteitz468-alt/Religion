# Prometheus

**Prometheus** ("Forethought") is the [[cronus|Titan]] who became the great **benefactor and champion of
humanity** against the will of [[zeus|Zeus]] — and paid for it with one of the most famous punishments in
myth. Son of the Titan Iapetus, brother of the slow-witted **Epimetheus** ("Afterthought") and of
[[atlas|Atlas]].

## Myths in Detail

**The trick at Mecone.** When gods and mortals were settling how sacrifices should be divided, Prometheus
was chosen to apportion a great ox. Cunningly, he made two portions: one was the good meat and entrails
hidden inside the ugly stomach; the other was the bare bones wrapped in glistening, appetizing fat. He
invited [[zeus|Zeus]] to choose. Zeus chose the fat — and found only bones. From then on humans burned the
bones and fat for the gods and kept the meat for themselves. (Hesiod adds that Zeus saw through the trick but
chose the bones deliberately, to have a pretext for his anger.)

**The theft of fire.** In fury, Zeus **withheld fire** from mortals, leaving them cold and helpless.
Prometheus stole a spark from heaven (or from Hephaestus's forge / the sun), hid it in a hollow **fennel
stalk**, and carried it down to humanity — the gift that made civilization, craft, and cooking possible.
Prometheus is thus the archetypal **culture-hero and trickster-benefactor** who steals a divine secret for
mankind.

**Pandora — Zeus's revenge on mankind.** To punish humans for receiving fire, Zeus had [[hephaestus|
Hephaestus]] mold the first **woman, Pandora** ("all-gifts") from earth and water; [[athena|Athena]] clothed
and taught her, [[aphrodite|Aphrodite]] gave her allure, and [[hermes|Hermes]] gave her a deceitful,
thieving heart. She was sent to the foolish Epimetheus, who — forgetting Prometheus's warning never to
accept a gift from Zeus — married her. Pandora opened the great **jar (pithos)** in her keeping, and out
flew all the evils, diseases, and sorrows that now plague the world; only **Hope** remained inside when she
shut the lid. (In some tellings Prometheus also shaped the first humans from clay.)

**The punishment and the deliverance.** For his defiance Zeus had Prometheus **chained to a peak in the
Caucasus**, where each day an **eagle tore out his liver**, which grew back each night, so the torment was
endless. He bore it unbroken — and held a secret Zeus needed (the prophecy that a certain union would
produce a son greater than his father). After ages, **[[heracles|Heracles]]** shot the eagle and freed him,
and Prometheus revealed the secret (warning Zeus away from the sea-nymph Thetis), and the two were
reconciled. Aeschylus's *Prometheus Bound* makes him the great tragic rebel and benefactor.

## Significance

Prometheus is the supreme myth of **the gift of culture won by defiance of the gods** — and a perennial
symbol of the rebel benefactor of humanity (later taken up by the Romantics, and by Mary Shelley's
*Frankenstein*, "the Modern Prometheus"). The Pandora story is the Greek counterpart to a "fall" myth — the
origin of human suffering — inviting comparison with [[genesis|Genesis 3]] (recorded without adjudication).
