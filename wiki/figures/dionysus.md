# Dionysus (Bacchus)

**Dionysus** (Roman **Bacchus** or **Liber**) is the god of **wine, ecstasy, fertility, theatre, and divine
madness** — the most paradoxical Olympian, both joyous liberator and source of terrifying frenzy.

## Myths and Character

The **"twice-born"** god: son of [[zeus|Zeus]] and the mortal **Semele**, he was rescued from her ashes and
sewn into Zeus's thigh until birth. In [[orphism|Orphic]] myth, as the infant **Zagreus**, he is torn apart
and devoured by the **Titans** and reborn — the basis of Orphism's doctrine of the soul. He wanders the
world spreading the vine, attended by **maenads** and satyrs, rewarding his followers and destroying those
who deny him (the fate of Pentheus in Euripides' *Bacchae*).

## Cult and Roman Identification

His [[dionysian-mysteries|mystery cult]] offered ecstatic union and afterlife hope; Athenian **theatre** grew
from his festivals. As **Bacchus** his ecstatic **Bacchanalia** were suppressed by the Roman Senate in 186
BCE as subversive.

## Myths in Detail

**The twice-born god.** [[zeus|Zeus]] loved the Theban princess **Semele**; the jealous [[hera|Hera]],
disguised as her nurse, persuaded her to make Zeus swear to appear in his true form. Bound by his oath, Zeus
came as **lightning and thunder**, which consumed the mortal Semele — but he snatched the unborn child and
**sewed him into his own thigh** until the babe was ready to be born. So Dionysus is "twice-born," of woman
and of god. (In the [[orphism|Orphic]] version, the infant **Zagreus**, son of Zeus and [[persephone|
Persephone]], is lured, dismembered, and **eaten by the Titans**; Zeus blasts them, and from their ashes —
part Titanic, part divine — humanity is made: the basis of Orphism's doctrine of the soul and a kind of
original sin.)

**The wandering god and his vengeance.** Driven mad by Hera, Dionysus wandered the world — to Egypt, India,
and back through Greece — spreading the cultivation of the **vine** and his ecstatic rites, attended by
**maenads** and satyrs. He rewards those who receive him and destroys those who deny his divinity: the
pirates who kidnapped him he turned into **dolphins** (their ship sprouting vines); and in Euripides'
*Bacchae*, King **Pentheus** of Thebes, who forbade the god's worship, was torn apart by his own mother and
the frenzied women, who in their madness took him for a lion. He went down to the underworld to bring back
his mother **Semele**, raising her to Olympus as the goddess Thyone.
