# Ōkuninushi

**Ōkuninushi**, "the Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land," is the great **earthly kami** of [[shinto|Shinto]] — the
**builder and ruler of the Central Land of Reed-Plains** (the terrestrial world) before its cession to the
heavenly gods, and the chief deity of the great shrine of **Izumo**. A descendant of **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]**, he
is the protagonist of the [[kojiki|Kojiki]]'s "Izumo cycle," the earthly counterweight to **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]'s**
heavenly line.

## Myths in Detail

His youth is a tale of **persecution and resurrection**: the kindly youngest of **eighty brothers**, he wins
the maiden they all court (after saving the **White Hare of Inaba**, which he heals — making him a god of
**medicine**), and the jealous brothers **murder him twice** — crushing him with a fire-heated boulder and
trapping him in a split tree — only for his mother and the maternal kami (the *musubi* power, see
[[musubi|musubi]]) to **revive him each time**. Fleeing to **Susanoo** in the netherworld, he undergoes
ordeals — a chamber of snakes, of centipedes and wasps, a burning field — surviving by the help of Susanoo's
daughter **Suseri-hime**, whom he weds; he then **steals Susanoo's sword, bow, and harp** and escapes, and
Susanoo, half in anger and half in blessing, names him **Ōkuninushi** and bids him rule the land. With the
dwarf-deity **Sukuna-biko-na** he then **builds and orders the land**, establishing agriculture, medicine, and
the means of human life.

## The cession of the land (kuni-yuzuri)

When **Amaterasu** resolves that her heavenly descendants shall rule the earth, she sends envoys demanding that
Ōkuninushi **yield the land**. After negotiation (and his sons' submission), Ōkuninushi **agrees to cede
political rule** to the heavenly line — on condition that a **great shrine be built for him at Izumo** and that
he be given charge of the **"hidden things" / the unseen world** (religious and spiritual matters, and the
realm of the dead), while the heavenly kami rule the "visible" affairs of state. This **kuni-yuzuri**
("transfer of the land") clears the way for the **[[ninigi|descent of Ninigi]]** and the imperial line, and
explains the enduring prestige of **Izumo Taisha** as the shrine of the lord of the unseen — where, in the
tenth lunar month, all the kami of Japan are said to gather.

## Significance

Ōkuninushi is the kami of **nation-building, medicine, agriculture, and the unseen**, and the great
**Izumo** counterpole to Ise. His peaceful **cession of the land** is the mythic hinge between the earthly and
heavenly orders, legitimating the imperial descent while preserving Izumo's sacred dignity.
