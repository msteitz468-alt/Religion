# Zeus (Jupiter)

**Zeus** (Roman **Jupiter** / Jove) is the **king of the gods** of the [[greek-religion|Greek]] and
[[roman-religion|Roman]] pantheons — god of the **sky, thunder, and storm**, and the supreme guarantor of
**order, justice, oaths, and hospitality**.

## Genealogy and Key Myths

Youngest son of the [[cronus|Titan Cronus]] and Rhea, Zeus escaped being swallowed by his father (Rhea hid
him on Crete), grew up, freed his swallowed siblings, and led the **Olympians to victory over the Titans**
(the **Titanomachy**), dividing the cosmos by lot: Zeus the **sky**, [[poseidon|Poseidon]] the **sea**,
[[hades|Hades]] the **underworld**. He reigns from **Mount Olympus**. His countless unions — with [[hera|Hera]]
(his wife and sister), and with goddesses and mortal women (Leto, Leda, Europa, Danaë, Semele, Alcmene) —
father much of the pantheon and many heroes ([[apollo|Apollo]], [[artemis|Artemis]], [[athena|Athena]],
[[hermes|Hermes]], [[dionysus|Dionysus]], [[heracles|Heracles]], the Muses).

## Cult and Significance

The supreme god of festival and oath: **Zeus Olympios** (the Olympic Games), **Zeus Xenios** (guardian of
guests/strangers), **Zeus Horkios** (oaths), the oracle of **Dodona**. As **Jupiter Optimus Maximus**, he
was the chief god of the Roman state, worshipped on the **Capitoline** with [[hera|Juno]] and [[athena|
Minerva]] (the Capitoline Triad), invoked for Rome's victory and law. Even Zeus, however, stands in a
contested relation to **[[fate-moirai|Fate]]**.

## Myths in Detail

**The secret birth.** [[cronus|Cronus]], warned that one of his children would overthrow him as he had
overthrown his own father [[uranus|Uranus]], swallowed each child at birth. When Zeus, the sixth, was due,
Rhea fled to **Crete** and bore him in a cave on Mount Ida (or Dikte); she gave Cronus a **stone wrapped in
swaddling clothes** to swallow in his place. Hidden Zeus was nursed on the milk of the goat **Amaltheia**
while the **Curetes** clashed their shields to drown his infant cries.

**The overthrow of Cronus and the Titanomachy.** Grown to strength, Zeus forced Cronus to **vomit up** the
swallowed children — [[hestia|Hestia]], [[demeter|Demeter]], [[hera|Hera]], [[hades|Hades]],
[[poseidon|Poseidon]] — now his full-grown allies. He freed the **Cyclopes** (who forged him his
**thunderbolts**) and the Hundred-Handers from Tartarus, and in the ten-year war of the
**[[hesiod-theogony|Titanomachy]]** the Olympians cast the Titans down into Tartarus. The three brothers
then drew lots for the cosmos: Zeus the **sky**, Poseidon the **sea**, Hades the **underworld** — the earth
and Olympus held in common, but Zeus supreme.

**Typhon.** [[gaia|Gaia]], enraged at the Titans' defeat, bore the monstrous **Typhon** — a hundred-headed
storm-giant who nearly toppled the gods. In a cataclysmic battle Zeus blasted him with a hundred
thunderbolts and crushed him beneath **Mount Etna**, securing his reign for good.

**Lord of justice — and of love.** As king, Zeus is the guarantor of oaths, hospitality, and order; he holds
the scales of [[fate-moirai|fate]] and hurls the thunderbolt at the impious. Yet he is famous for his
**innumerable loves**, by which he fathers gods and heroes — often in disguise and against [[hera|Hera's]]
jealous fury: **Metis** (whose child, prophesied to surpass him, he forestalled by swallowing Metis, so that
[[athena|Athena]] sprang from his head); **Leto** ([[apollo|Apollo]] and [[artemis|Artemis]]); **Semele**,
whom he came to as lightning, mother of [[dionysus|Dionysus]]; **Danaë**, whom he reached as a shower of
gold (Perseus); **Europa**, whom he carried off as a white bull; **Leda** as a swan (Helen); **Alcmene**
(Heracles); and the boy **Ganymede**, whom his eagle bore up to be cupbearer of the gods.
