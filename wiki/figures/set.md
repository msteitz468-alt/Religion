# Set (Seth)

**Set** is the [[egyptian-religion|Egyptian]] god of **chaos, violence, storms, the desert, and disorder** —
the most ambivalent figure in the pantheon: both the **murderer of [[osiris|Osiris]]** and, paradoxically,
the indispensable **defender of [[ra|Ra]]**. Son of [[geb|Geb]] and [[nut|Nut]], brother of Osiris, [[isis|
Isis]], and [[nephthys|Nephthys]] (his wife). He is shown with the head of an unidentifiable "Set animal."

## Myths in Detail

**The murder of Osiris and the contest for the throne.** Out of jealousy, Set tricked his brother [[osiris|
Osiris]] into the fitted chest and killed him, then dismembered the recovered body (see [[osiris|the Osiris
myth]]). When Osiris's son [[horus|Horus]] grew up, Set fought him through the long **Contendings** — tearing
out Horus's eye, contesting the throne by violence and trickery — until the gods awarded the kingship to
Horus and consigned Set to the **deserts and the margins**. Set thus personifies everything that threatens
[[maat|ma'at]]: the killing brother, the usurper, the storm, the barren waste, the foreign land.

**The defender of the sun.** Yet Set is **not simply evil**. Strong and fearless, he stands at the **prow of
[[ra|Ra's]] solar barque** each night and **spears the chaos-serpent [[apep|Apep]]** — the one god mighty
enough to repel the ultimate chaos. Egyptian thought needed Set's violent power turned outward, against the
deeper disorder. Some dynasties (the Ramessides) honored him as a war-god and royal patron.

## Reception

Set's reputation **darkened over time**: in later periods, especially after foreign invasions, he was
increasingly demonized and identified with the enemies of Egypt (and by the Greeks with the monster
**Typhon**). His ambivalence — necessary strength that is also the source of disorder — makes him one of the
most psychologically interesting gods of antiquity, and a contrast to the simpler good/evil dualisms
elsewhere.
