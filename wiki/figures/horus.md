# Horus

**Horus** is the falcon-headed **sky-god** of [[egyptian-religion|Egypt]], god of **kingship** and the sun —
the son of [[osiris|Osiris]] and [[isis|Isis]] who **avenged his father** and won the throne. Every living
**pharaoh** was the embodiment of Horus; every dead one became an [[osiris|Osiris]]. (An older "Horus the
Elder" was a sky-god whose eyes were the sun and moon; he was largely merged with the son of Isis.)

## Myths in Detail — The Contendings of Horus and Set

Raised in hiding by [[isis|Isis]], Horus grew up to claim his murdered father's throne against his usurping
uncle **[[set|Set]]** — beginning the long, often bizarre **"Contendings of Horus and Set,"** an 80-year
dispute argued before the tribunal of the gods (presided over by [[ra|Ra]] and the Ennead):

- **The legal case** — Isis argues that the throne belongs by right to the son of Osiris; Set argues that
  rule should go to the strongest. The gods waver for decades.
- **The contests** — they compete in a series of trials: a race in stone boats (Horus wins by trickery), a
  battle as hippopotami, and crude attempts by each to humiliate the other. In one violent episode **Set tears
  out Horus's eye** (or both eyes); the eye is healed and restored by [[thoth|Thoth]] — the **"Eye of Horus"**
  (*wedjat*), the great symbol of healing, wholeness, protection, and royal power.
- **The verdict** — at last the gods consult the dead [[osiris|Osiris]] himself, who insists on his son's
  right; **Horus is awarded the throne** of the living, and Set is given the deserts (and, in some versions,
  a place at [[ra|Ra's]] side fighting [[apep|Apophis]]).

## Significance

Horus's victory is the mythic **charter of Egyptian kingship**: the rightful king is Horus, his dead
predecessor is Osiris, and the orderly succession of [[maat|ma'at]] triumphs over the chaos of Set. The **Eye
of Horus** became the most common protective amulet in Egypt.
