# Pángǔ (P'an Ku)

**Pángǔ** (older transliteration **P'an Ku**) is the **primordial giant** of [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese]]
cosmogony — the first living being, whose growth and death **make the world**. His is the principal Chinese
**creation myth** in [[myths-and-legends-of-china|Werner's compilation]].

## Myths in Detail

### The hatching of the cosmos

In the beginning the cosmos was an undifferentiated **chaos shaped like a hen's egg**, in which **yīn and
yáng** lay mingled and inert. Within this egg **Pángǔ came into being and slept for 18,000 years**. When he
awoke and broke the egg, the **clear, light (yang) elements rose to become Heaven** and the **heavy, turbid
(yin) elements sank to become Earth**. Fearing the two would close together again, **Pángǔ stood between them
and pushed them apart** — and as he grew **ten feet a day for another 18,000 years**, Heaven rose ever higher
and Earth thickened beneath, until they were fixed **30,000 miles apart**. Then, his work done, **Pángǔ died**.

### The world from his body

At his death **Pángǔ's body became the world** (a classic **cosmic-body creation**, cf. the dismemberment of
[[ymir|Ymir]] and the Vedic Purusha): his **breath became the wind and clouds**, his **voice the thunder**, his
**left eye the sun and right eye the moon**, his **limbs and trunk the mountains**, his **blood the rivers**,
his **veins the roads**, his **flesh the soil**, his **hair the stars and the plants**, his **sweat the rain**,
and — in a humbling coda — the **parasites on his body, touched by the wind, became human beings**. In a variant,
the chisel-and-mallet-wielding Pángǔ carves out the valleys and heaps the mountains himself.

## Significance

Pángǔ embodies the Chinese cosmological intuition that the world **emerges from chaos by the separation of yin
and yang**, and that the cosmos and the human body are **continuous** — the macrocosm is a great body, the body
a small cosmos. He is comparatively **late** as a written myth (attested c. 3rd c. CE), but became the standard
account of the world's origin in [[chinese-folk-religion|folk religion]].
