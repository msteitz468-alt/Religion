# Tiamat

**Tiamat** is the primordial goddess of the **salt sea** and the embodiment of watery **chaos** — the great
mother from whose body the gods (and later the world) are formed, and the **chaos-dragon** that the young
[[marduk|Marduk]] must defeat to create the ordered cosmos.

## In the Enuma Elish

At the beginning there were only **[[apsu|Apsu]]** (fresh water) and **Tiamat** (salt water), whose mingling
waters bore the first gods. When the gods killed her consort Apsu, Tiamat, enraged, **spawned an army of
eleven monsters** under the commander **Kingu** and made war on the gods. **[[marduk|Marduk]]** faced her in
single combat, caught her in his net, filled her with the winds, and **split her corpse in two** to make the
**sky and the earth** — her waters becoming the seas and her eyes the sources of the Tigris and Euphrates. So
Tiamat is **both the matrix of creation and the chaos that must be subdued**: the world is literally made from
the body of defeated disorder.

## Significance

Tiamat is the archetypal **chaos-monster of the primeval waters** — a major comparandum for the cosmic
dragon/sea motifs in other traditions ([[zeus|Typhon]], Vritra in the [[rig-veda|Rig Veda]], and the biblical
**Leviathan/Rahab** and the *tehom* ("the deep," philologically related to *Tiamat*) of [[genesis|Genesis
1:2]]). See [[genesis-ancient-near-east|the ANE comparison]].
