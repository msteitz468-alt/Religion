Jehoiada the priest sheltered the infant [[joash|Joash]] through the six years of
[[athaliah|Athaliah]]'s reign, armed the temple guard, crowned and anointed the boy, had Athaliah
put to death, and then bound king, people, and YHWH in covenant, demolished the Baal temple in
[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]], and set the temple repair program in motion. He is the one figure in Kings
in whom priestly authority, rather than an oracle-prophet or the army, makes a king.

## Biographical Overview

Jehoiada enters the narrative in the seventh year of Athaliah's reign, though his role begins
earlier: Jehosheba — in 2 Chronicles 22 identified as his wife — had taken Ahaziah's infant son
Joash from among the king's sons about to be killed and hidden him with his nurse, and the child was
concealed in the house of YHWH for six years while Athaliah reigned.

In the seventh year Jehoiada acts. He summons the commanders of the guard units, brings them into
the temple, makes a covenant with them, shows them the king's son, and assigns them their stations
around the boy with orders to kill anyone who breaks the ranks. He gives them the spears and shields
of King [[david|David]] kept in the temple. Joash is brought out, given the crown and the testimony,
anointed, and acclaimed with clapping and the cry "Long live the king." Athaliah hears the noise,
comes into the temple, tears her clothes, and cries "Treason! Treason!" Jehoiada orders her taken
out between the ranks — the temple is not to be the place of her death — and she is killed at the
horses' entrance to the palace.

He then makes the covenant: between YHWH, the king, and the people, that they should be YHWH's
people, and between the king and the people. The people go to the house of Baal, tear it down,
smash its altars and images, and kill Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars. Jehoiada posts
officers over the house of YHWH and the king is brought to the palace and seated on the throne.

Under his direction the temple repair program is organized (2 Kgs 12): the priests are first
assigned to collect the money and carry out repairs, and when after many years the work is not done,
Jehoiada takes a chest, bores a hole in its lid, and sets it beside the altar so that the money
brought in is counted out and paid directly to the workmen — carpenters, builders, masons,
stonecutters — who are said to deal honestly. 2 Chronicles 24 gives the same reform as a levy
recalling the Mosaic collection, with the Levites sent out to gather it.

2 Chronicles 24 adds that Jehoiada arranged two wives for Joash, that he died old and full of days,
and that he was buried in the city of David among the kings, because he had done good in Israel and
toward God and his house — a burial honor the Chronicler gives to no other priest.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **2 Kings 11** — the six-year concealment; the covenant with the guard commanders; David's spears
  and shields; the coronation and anointing; Athaliah's execution outside the temple; the threefold
  covenant; the destruction of the Baal temple and the killing of Mattan.
- **2 Kings 12** — the temple repair: the failure of the first arrangement, the chest by the altar,
  the honest workmen, the guilt and sin money reserved to the priests.
- **2 Chronicles 22:10–12** — Jehoshabeath identified as Jehoiada's wife; the rescue of Joash.
- **2 Chronicles 23** — the parallel coup, with the Levites and priests foregrounded in the guard
  arrangements and the ordering of the temple courts.
- **2 Chronicles 24:1–16** — the repair levy through the Levites; the wives arranged for Joash;
  Jehoiada's death and burial among the kings.
- **2 Chronicles 24:17–22** — after his death, the princes' turn to idolatry and the stoning of his
  son Zechariah in the temple court (see [[joash|Joash]]).

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[iain-provan|Provan]] (NIBC Kings, as recorded on [[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]])** — the survival of
  Joash under Athaliah belongs to Provan's governing theme of **salvation within judgment**: on the
  wiki's Kings page the episode is recorded in his frame as "David" surviving "Jezebel," the Davidic
  line preserved by a thread rather than by dynastic strength. Jehoiada is the human agent of that
  preservation.
- **[[peter-leithart|Leithart]] (BTCB Chronicles, as recorded on [[1-2-chronicles|1–2 Chronicles]])**
  — in Leithart's reading of the reign, Joash "restores the temple under the priest Jehoiada" and
  apostatizes after Jehoiada's death; the priest's lifetime is the boundary of the faithful phase in
  the Chronicler's faithful-early / faithless-late pattern.
- No Jewish, Islamic, or patristic treatment of Jehoiada has been ingested into this wiki; none is
  attributed here.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Jehoiada is the point at which the temple functions as a political institution: it hides the heir,
arms the coup, hosts the coronation, and is deliberately kept clean of the queen's blood. The
covenant he mediates is threefold — YHWH, king, people — and it is followed immediately by the
demolition of the Baal shrine, so that the restoration of the Davidic line and the purge of Baal
worship in Judah are one act.

His stewardship of the repair money is narrated with unusual procedural detail. In the Kings
account the reform lies in removing the priests from the handling of the funds and in the
observation that no accounting was required of the men in charge because they dealt honestly — a
rare positive institutional notice in the book.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Kings vs Chronicles emphasis.** Kings foregrounds the royal guard units in the coup; Chronicles
  foregrounds the priests and Levites and the ordering of the temple courts. Both accounts are
  recorded; neither is treated here as correcting the other.
- **Jehosheba's marriage.** Only Chronicles identifies her as Jehoiada's wife. Whether this is
  independent tradition or the Chronicler's inference is disputed.
- **The mechanics of the repair fund.** Kings and Chronicles describe the collection differently —
  a chest beside the altar in Kings, a Mosaic-style levy gathered by Levites in Chronicles. Recorded
  as differing accounts.
- **Jehoiada's effective regency.** How far the text presents him as ruling in Joash's name, and
  whether 2 Kgs 12:2 ("all his days, because Jehoiada the priest instructed him") conditions Joash's
  goodness on the priest's tutelage, is an interpretive dispute bearing directly on the Kings /
  Chronicles divergence about Joash's later reign.
- **Chronology.** His dates depend on the contested synchronisms of the divided monarchy.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Jehoiada is the standing test case for the relation between priestly and royal authority in the
Hebrew Bible — the priest who makes, instructs, and outlives the usefulness of a king — and for the
theme of the temple as sanctuary in the political as well as the cultic sense. His burial among the
kings in Chronicles is a recurring point in discussions of the Chronicler's evaluation of the
priesthood. Cross-links: [[joash|Joash]], [[athaliah|Athaliah]], [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]],
