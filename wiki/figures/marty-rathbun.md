# Marty Rathbun

**Mark "Marty" Rathbun** is a former senior official of the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] who served as **Inspector General for Ethics of the Religious Technology Center** — the second-most powerful position in the Church under [[david-miscavige|David Miscavige]]. He defected in 2004 and became one of the most prominent public critics of Miscavige's leadership, while continuing to practice and advocate for Scientology as an "independent" outside the institutional Church.

## Biographical Overview

### Personal Background and Entry

Rathbun's brother Bruce died — the Church's magazine *Freedom* misidentified the cause; Wright documents that Bruce's wife **Diane Colletto** was murdered (shot) on **August 19, 1978** by John Colletto, who then killed himself three days later. This loss appears to have set Rathbun's direction toward Scientology. He **signed a billion-year contract** in January 1978, shortly before his sister-in-law's murder.

He used the alias **"Marty"** during sensitive operations; Wright notes he "became 'All Clear'" — i.e., head of the unit working to clear Hubbard's legal exposure — and adopted the alias operationally.

### Rise to Power

Rathbun became one of Miscavige's most trusted lieutenants in the 1980s–2000s, serving as Inspector General for Ethics and the Church's primary intelligence and legal strategy officer. Key roles documented by Wright:

- **IRS negotiations**: Walked unannounced into IRS headquarters with Miscavige to negotiate the landmark 1993 tax exemption
- **Lisa McPherson case**: Directed destruction of log books and other evidence; organized the campaign to neutralize the medical examiner; managed settlement negotiations (*Going Clear* and the Tampa Bay Times "Truth Rundown" series)
- **Tom Cruise's auditor**: Conducted Cruise's OT-level auditing sessions at Gold Base; sessions secretly filmed by Miscavige
- **Strangulation incident**: After the IRS victory in 1993, briefly blew (escaped); when he returned, Miscavige strangled him (witnessed by Stefan Castle); awarded the "Kha-Khan" status — a Hubbard term for a pardon-able offense
- Organized the surveillance of Pat Broeker after Hubbard's death

### Defection and Aftermath

Rathbun escaped Gold Base **by motorcycle through the gate** (c. 2004), after years of being confined to the Hole and witnessing/participating in systematic abuse. He moved to **Ingleside on the Bay, near Corpus Christi, Texas**, with his wife Monique Carle.

After defection:
- Began blogging at *Moving On Up a Little Higher* (2009) — became a rallying point for "independent" Scientologists and ex-members
- Gave extensive interviews including to Wright (2010–2012)
- Participated in the St. Petersburg Times' "Truth Rundown" series (June 2009)
- Counseled ex-members as an independent auditor
- Published *The Scientology Reformation* and *What Is Wrong with Scientology?*

The Church deployed a sustained harassment campaign: "Squirrel Busters" (private investigators who moved into his neighborhood for 199 days, wearing video cameras on their hats, patrolling by golf cart and paddleboat), hacking of computers, theft of phone records.

## Theological Position

Rathbun occupies a distinctive middle position between full rejection and full acceptance:

> "There are those who reject the teachings of L. Ron Hubbard entirely, such as Paul Haggis; and those who still believe entirely, but think that the church under David Miscavige has taken Scientology away from the original, true teachings of the founder. There is a third category… those people who are willing neither to swallow all the dogma nor to throw away the insights they gained from their experience." (*Going Clear*, Epilogue)

He quotes a Zen proverb: "When the master points at the Moon, many people never see it at all, they only look at the master" — implying Hubbard's insights (the Moon) are separable from Hubbard's personality and Miscavige's institution (the master).
