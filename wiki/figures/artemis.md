# Artemis (Diana)

**Artemis** (Roman **Diana**) is the virgin goddess of the **hunt, wild animals, the wilderness, and the
moon**, and a protectress of young girls and women in **childbirth**. Daughter of [[zeus|Zeus]] and Leto,
twin of [[apollo|Apollo]].

## Myths and Character

A fierce, chaste huntress roaming the mountains with her nymphs and hounds, swift to punish those who
violate her purity or her creatures — she turned **Actaeon** into a stag for seeing her bathe, and demanded
the sacrifice of Iphigenia. With Apollo she slew the children of Niobe. Often identified with the moon (and
with [[selene|Selene]] and [[hecate|Hecate]] in a triple lunar goddess).

## Cult and Roman Identification

Her temple at **Ephesus** (the many-breasted Artemis) was one of the Seven Wonders — and the flashpoint of
Acts 19. As **Diana** she had a famous grove at Lake Nemi (the "King of the Wood" priesthood) and was
patron of the lower classes and slaves.

## Myths in Detail

**Actaeon.** The hunter **Actaeon** stumbled upon Artemis bathing naked in a forest pool. Furious that a
mortal had seen her, the chaste goddess splashed him with water and turned him into a **stag** — whereupon
his own fifty hounds, not knowing him, tore him to pieces.

**The vengeance for her mother and her purity.** When **Niobe** boasted that her fourteen children made her
superior to Leto with only two, Artemis and [[apollo|Apollo]] shot down all of Niobe's children (Artemis the
daughters, Apollo the sons), and Niobe wept herself into a weeping stone. Artemis likewise punished
**Callisto**, one of her nymphs, when [[zeus|Zeus]] seduced her and she lost her virginity (she was turned
into a bear); and she demanded the sacrifice of **Iphigenia** to release the becalmed Greek fleet bound for
[[homer|Troy]] (rescuing her at the last moment in some versions).

**The eternal maiden of the wild.** As a little girl (in Callimachus's hymn) she asked her father Zeus for
**eternal virginity**, a bow and arrows, a band of nymphs, and the mountains as her domain — and got them. She
roams the wilderness hunting with her companions, fiercely guarding her chastity and theirs, and as a moon-
and birth-goddess she eases (or ends) women's labor. Identified with [[selene|Selene]] and [[hecate|Hecate]]
as a triple moon-goddess.
