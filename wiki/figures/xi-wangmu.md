# Xi Wangmu (the Queen Mother of the West)

**Xī Wángmǔ**, the **Queen Mother of the West**, is the great **goddess of immortality** in
[[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]] and Daoism — queen of the immortals and **keeper of the peaches
of eternal life**. In her earliest, archaic guise (in the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*) she is a fearsome,
**part-feline being with tiger's teeth and a leopard's tail**, a goddess of plague and the wild west; but she
was progressively refined into a **majestic, beautiful sovereign**, the highest goddess of the Daoist pantheon,
dwelling in a jade palace in the **Kunlun mountains** beside the **Lake of Gems**.

Her most famous attribute is the orchard of **immortality-peaches (pántáo)**, which **ripen only once every
3,000 (or 9,000) years**; when they do, she hosts the **Peach Banquet** for the gods and immortals — the feast
that **[[sun-wukong|Sun Wukong]]** gate-crashes and ruins, gorging on the peaches before his war on Heaven. She
grants elixirs and peaches of immortality to worthy mortals and emperors (famously to **King Mu of Zhou** and
**Emperor Wu of Han**), and presides over the **[[the-eight-immortals|Eight Immortals]]** and the company of
the deathless. Her male counterpart in later Daoism is the **Lord King of the East (Dōngwánggōng)**. As the
divine source of **longevity and immortality**, Xi Wangmu is a hugely auspicious figure in art and birthday
symbolism, embodying the deepest Daoist hope — to **escape death itself**.
