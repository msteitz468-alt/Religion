# Sabellius

The most sophisticated exponent of [[modalism|modalistic Monarchianism]] (hence "Sabellianism" as the standard name for the heresy), active at Rome around 215. He enjoyed the confidence of Pope Callistus before being excommunicated by him. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], ch. V.

## Teaching

Sabellius "regarded the Godhead as a monad" — his name for it was *huiopatōr* ("Son-Father") — "which expressed itself in three operations," using the analogy of the sun emitting both warmth and light. He may have exploited the idea of the "expansion" or "dilation" (*platysmos*) of the divine monad, the Father projecting himself first as Son and then as Spirit. Kelly cautions that much of the surviving evidence is confused with [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus of Ancyra]], and that the traditional attribution to Sabellius of *prosopa* understood as "masks" is "erroneous."

"Sabellianism" became a standard charge of overstressing divine unity at the expense of the distinct Persons — levelled at [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus]], and feared by [[athanasius|Athanasius]]'s critics in the *[[homoousion]]* debate.
