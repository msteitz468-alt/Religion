# Ja'far al-Sadiq

**Ja'far al-Sadiq** ("the Truthful One," 80/700 – 148/765) is the **sixth Imam** of Twelver and
Isma'ili [[shia-islam|Shia Islam]] and one of the most consequential figures of the second Islamic
century: a Qur'anic exegete, jurist, and hadith transmitter whose authority rests on both his
**lineage** (*beata stirps*) and his learning. The Twelver legal school, the **Ja'fari** *madhhab*,
bears his name.

## Biographical Overview

By Shi'ite reckoning his authority was conferred by **bloodline**: descended from the Prophet
**[[muhammad|Muhammad]]** through his daughter **Fatima** and **Ali ibn Abi Talib** (the first Imam),
the line running through Ali's martyred son **Husayn** to Ja'far, the great-grandson of Husayn — and
through a second line back to the Prophet's wife **A'isha**. He lived and taught in **Medina** at a
moment when Sunni and Shi'ite identities were not yet hardened camps: among his students were **Abu
Hanifa** and **Malik ibn Anas**, founders of two of the four Sunni law schools, and the sources
record warm collaboration across what later became a sharp divide.

He was a **political quietist**. Through the Abbasid revolution (132/750) and the protests of other
branches of the Prophet's household, he neither rebelled nor joined rebels. When the Abbasid caliph
**al-Mansur** proclaimed himself Imam-Caliph (c. 760), claiming the Imamate via descent from the
Prophet's uncle Abbas, Ja'far opposed him not politically but **intellectually**, through the Qur'an —
holding that the Imamate must remain distinct from the Caliphate and belong only to a designate of
the previous Imam, who inherits a special inner knowledge (*'ilm*). He was summoned to Samarra,
returned to Medina, and (in Shi'ite tradition) was poisoned on al-Mansur's order in 148/765. He was
buried in **Jannat al-Baqi** in Medina — a tomb later destroyed by the Saudi-Wahhabi authorities in
the twentieth century, in their campaign against shrine cults.

## Hermeneutical Method and Significance

Ja'far is credited (though no commentary survives intact; it is reconstructed from later Shi'ite
*tafsir*) with a **threefold reading** of the Qur'an:

1. **Literal** — the plain Arabic sense, increasingly requiring translation of Qur'anic into
   vernacular Arabic even by his day;
2. **Historical** — the circumstances of the Prophet and the early community (including the contested
   line of succession);
3. **Allegorical / esoteric** — "the most creative and open-ended" level, of imagination, myth, and
   intuition.

He intensified the Sunni-shared distinction between **clear** and **ambiguous** verses by assigning
the **ambiguous** verses' true meaning to the **Imams alone** as authoritative interpreters — far
more expansively than [[al-tabari|al-Tabari]] would later allow. On this reading "a quarter of the
Qur'an is about the Imams": verses naming the "Truthful Ones" (Q 9:119), the "possessors of
knowledge," the "Straight Path," the "Firmest Handle," and above all the **Light of God** (Q 64:8 as
*locus classicus*) are read as designating the Imams and their quasi-divine character. This is the
foundation of Shi'ite **[[allegorical-reading|ta'wil]]** and of the doctrine of the Imam as the
living interpreter of revelation — see [[shia-islam|Shia Islam]] and [[tafsir|Tafsir]].

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Twelver Shia**: the sixth of the Twelve Imams; the eponym of Ja'fari jurisprudence; a central
  hadith authority (much of *al-Kafi* transmits from him).
- **Isma'ili Shia**: pivotal — the succession dispute over his sons **Isma'il** and **Musa al-Kazim**
  splits the Isma'ilis (who follow Isma'il) from the Twelvers (who follow Musa).
- **Sunni Islam**: respected as a reliable transmitter and teacher of the Sunni founders; not regarded
  as an infallible Imam. The Shi'ite *exegetical* claims (the Imams as exclusive interpreters, the
  "light" verses) Sunnis reject — recorded without adjudication (Contradiction Protocol).
- **Sufism**: revered as an early master of esoteric Qur'anic insight; a *tafsir* attributed to him is
  a touchstone of the mystical tradition that runs toward [[ibn-arabi|Ibn Arabi]].
