# Quetzalcoatl (Kukulkan)

**Quetzalcoatl** (Aztec; Maya **Kukulkan**; K'iche' **Gucumatz**) — the **Feathered Serpent** — is one of the
greatest and most widespread gods of [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerica]]: a **creator-god** and god of
**wind, wisdom, learning, the calendar, the priesthood, and life**, and the **most benevolent** of the great
gods (the one **least associated** with human sacrifice, preferring offerings of jade, snakes, and butterflies).

## Myths in Detail

**Creator.** As **Gucumatz**, the Feathered Serpent is one of the makers who **speak the world into being** in
the [[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]]. In Aztec myth, after the present world's destruction, **Quetzalcoatl descends into
the underworld (Mictlan)** to retrieve the **bones of the dead** of the previous age from [[mictlantecuhtli|
Mictlantecuhtli]]; he tricks and flees the death-god, and though the bones are broken in his escape, he grinds
them and **sprinkles them with his own blood** to create the **humanity of the Fifth Sun** — a self-sacrificing
creator. He is also credited with giving humanity **maize** (stealing it as an ant) and the arts of
civilization.

**Rival of Tezcatlipoca; the exiled king.** Quetzalcoatl is the eternal **rival of [[tezcatlipoca|
Tezcatlipoca]]**, the dark fate-god; their cosmic struggle drives the cycle of the [[five-suns|Five Suns]]. A
parallel legend tells of a **priest-king of the Toltecs named Quetzalcoatl** (Topiltzin Ce Acatl), a wise and
gentle ruler who **opposed human sacrifice**; tricked and shamed by Tezcatlipoca's sorcery, he went into exile
to the east, departing on a raft of serpents (or immolating himself to become the **morning star, Venus**),
with a **prophecy that he would return**. (The notion that the Aztecs took **Cortés** for the returning
Quetzalcoatl is a famous but historically contested claim.)
