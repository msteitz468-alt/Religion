# Pharaoh

**Biographical Overview**:
The unnamed King of Egypt who enslaved the Israelites, ordered the death of Hebrew male infants, and obstinately refused to let God's people go despite ten devastating plagues. He finally relented after the death of the firstborn but then pursued the Israelites, resulting in the destruction of his army in the Red Sea.

**Primary Source Appearances**:
- **Exodus**: The primary antagonist (chs. 1-15). Engages in a contest of wills and power against YHWH and Moses. His heart is repeatedly "hardened."
- **Psalms**: His defeat is celebrated in poetic retellings of the Exodus (e.g., Ps 136).
- **Romans**: Used by Paul as a theological archetype for divine sovereignty and election (Rom 9:17).

**Tradition-Specific Reception**:
- **Jewish**: The archetypal tyrant and oppressor of the Jewish people. His arrogance ("Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice?") is the ultimate expression of human pride against divine authority.
- **Christian**: Represents bondage to sin and the devil. Paul uses Pharaoh to illustrate God's sovereign right to use rebellious human rulers to display His glory (Rom 9).
- **Islamic**: Fir'aun is the quintessential tyrant in the Quran, claiming divinity for himself ("I am your Lord, the Most High"). His drowning and the preservation of his body are presented as a sign for later generations.

**Theological and Narrative Significance**:
Pharaoh serves as the foil to YHWH's sovereignty. The narrative uses him to demonstrate that no earthly power, even one claiming divine status, can thwart God's redemptive purposes. According to James K. Bruckner, the contest is not merely political but theological: proving who is truly God.

**Historicity and Interpretive Controversies**:
- **Identity of the Pharaoh**: The Exodus text does not name him. Traditional/conservative scholars often identify the Pharaoh of the Exodus as either Thutmose III/Amenhotep II (15th century BCE) or Ramesses II (13th century BCE).
- **The Hardening of the Heart**: A major exegetical issue. Did Pharaoh possess free will? The text states Pharaoh hardened his own heart, but also that God hardened it. Bruckner argues that God intensified Pharaoh's existing resolve as a judgment, bringing his rebellion to its ultimate conclusion so that God's name might be proclaimed globally.

**The Pharaoh of Ezekiel's Oracles (Block)**:
The Egypt oracles of Ezekiel 29–32 target a *named* historical pharaoh: Daniel Block
([[block-ezekiel-25-48-nicot|Block, Ezekiel 25–48 (NICOT)]]) identifies him as **Hophra
(Apries, 589–570 BCE)**, the Saite king whose relief expedition emboldened Zedekiah (Jer
37:5–11; 44:30). Block reads 29:3 and 32:2 — Pharaoh as *hattannîn haggādôl*, "the great
monster" — as "concretely a marine creature… in this instance a crocodile" carrying
"mythological overtones… the mythical sea monster, the chaos god, known elsewhere as Rahab
and Leviathan," yet insists "Ezekiel's *tannîn* has been thoroughly historicized." In 32:2 the
pharaoh's lion self-image (Saite kings depicted themselves as sphinxes) is countered by the
prophet's thrashing monster. On 30:21 Block finds "a deliberate wordplay on his *nbty* name":
Hophra styled himself *nb ḫpš*, "Possessor of a Strong Arm," so "I have broken the arm of
Pharaoh" — citing Freedy and Redford's point that Yahweh "had offered his own *ḫpš*-sword to
Nebuchadrezzar, not to Apries." Block also records the contradiction between Josephus (Ant.
10.9.7: Nebuchadrezzar slew Hophra) and Herodotus (Hist. 2.163ff.: slain by his successor
Amasis after the 570 civil war). The series ends with Pharaoh's descent into
[[sheol|Sheol]], laid "among the uncircumcised and the criminal victims of the sword"
(31:18; 32:19–32) — see [[circumcision|Circumcision]] on the metaphor.

**Influence on Commentary Traditions**:
- Bruckner highlights how Pharaoh's oppressive system is a complete anti-creation, countered by the plagues, which deconstruct the Egyptian cosmic order.

### Bruckner (UBCS Exodus) reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

*Source: [[bruckner-exodus-ubcs|Bruckner, Exodus (UBCS)]].*

[[james-bruckner|Bruckner]] reads Pharaoh as a skilled and deceitful negotiator throughout: "Readers should not assume that Pharaoh became alternately soft and hard as the plagues progressed... He began with a hard heart and negotiated with skill." His "Thus says the pharaoh" (5:10) mirrors — and parodies — the prophetic transmission formula; his confessions are calculated: 9:27's "in the right"/"in the wrong" (*tsaddiq/rashaʿ*) "are legal admissions, not moral confessions," and 10:16's "I have sinned" is "primarily for the benefit of the reader." "Pharaoh is an imposter Lord" whose two anxieties (Israel's numbers, their departure) directly oppose God's two promises to Jacob (progeny, land): the stage "for the battle between the God of creation... and the great and powerful Pharaoh." Oppression escalates from "forced residency in a totalitarian state" through forced labor to infanticide. In ch. 14 Pharaoh and the sea "are both chaos threats in Exodus... The Lord ironically used one to drown the other" (with Ezek 29:3; 32:2); the final hardening "intensif[ies] his resolve, but not 'creating' his intent." His last words in Exodus are not a command but a request: "And also bless me" (12:32). Historically, Bruckner places the oppression under Rameses II with Merneptah as the likely exodus pharaoh.

### Pritzker Vol. IV reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

**New king / prince / separation (Shemot, ~2:5b–7b, 10a).** *A new king arose*: (1) innovator of decrees who “did not know Joseph,” arose on his own like Ahasuerus, sought to eliminate Israel; (2) supernal — Egypt’s **princely authority** newly raised over nations when Israel enters exile (*new* = no prior dominion); (3) issued from the place of **separation** (*From there it divides*, Gen 2:10) — river of Egypt first to separate — so he “did not know” Joseph = the Righteous, place of union. King Himself comes for Egypt (not mere decree) to bring out **Matronita** ([[shekhinah|Shekhinah]]). Pharaoh’s daughter at the Nile = left / harsh Judgment (not the sea); when Israel ceases from Torah, Judgment bathes in Israel’s blood; yet she sees mark *vav he* and shows compassion (infant Moses unit).

**Great sea serpent (Va-Era ~2:27b–28a; Bo ~2:34a–35b).** Core mapping: Ezek 29:3 — Pharaoh is the **great sea serpent sprawling amidst his streams**; Egyptian power and sorcery hang from/under that serpent, on **lowest rungs**, *behind the millstones*. Evil serpent mythos: red as a rose, iron scales, seventy-year cycles, muzzled with hooks in jaws (Ezek 29:4), cast to the abyss. Cosmogony (Bo): nine rivers + calm river; tanninim/Leviathan; firstborn of Egypt as fish “Nights”; *I am against you*. Moses feared the serpent “rooted above”; God alone fights him in the sea. Job as closest adviser (spare life, take labor) shields Pharaoh until Job is weakened — then vengeance.

**Plagues as judgment on dominion-rungs (Va-Era ~2:28b–32a; Bo).** Blood: left suckling; Nile as false god struck first (water dominion). Frogs: unending voices because Pharaoh seized a people of unending praise; bed/bedroom first (successive Pharaohs enjoyed painted image of Sarah). *Arov*: confounds sorcerous species above and below — “Egypt against Egypt.” Lice from dust (seventy-zone map). Animal plagues: finger vs full **hand** of five fingers; *dever* letters reverse to *barad*. Ten signs from the **mighty hand** muddle minds of Egyptian rungs from **lowest to firstborn** apex. Bo Passover: firstborn spectrum as lower crowns; Destroyer barred by blood/Name; leaven = Other Dominion.

**Hardened heart (Be-Shallaḥ sea, ~2:52b–53b).** Harder than Sihon/Og — witnessed mighty acts daily yet would not repent; gazed crowns/spheres but did not know the bond of **Faith** above; **the Name YHVH hardened his heart** — that Name not found in his wisdom as ruling the earth. Great hand: after five fingers completed, *YHVH hardened* Pharaoh (not under his control). *Va-Era* digests: *hazaq/kaved lev* **not** treated as a lemma there (related structure via serpent dominion and muddled rungs only).

**Sea pursuit and fall (Be-Shallaḥ ~2:45a–53b).** Escort of Israel/Shekhinah to Etham then pursuit; three night plagues including Pharaoh killing his own advisors; *hiqriv* draws Israel to prayer; Samael lends six hundred attacking chariots; upper Egyptian prince deprived of dominion (River of Fire) before troops below; chariots into sea / Kingdom; sea-crown of revenge on Pharaoh’s chariots later used for Messiah (Shemot messianic unit).
