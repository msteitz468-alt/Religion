# Brigid

**Brigid** (*Brighid*) is the Irish **triple goddess** of [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]] — daughter of
the **[[the-dagda|Dagda]]** and one of the **[[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]]**. Cormac's Glossary
describes her as **three Brigids**, sisters bearing one name: a goddess of **poetry and learning** (the
patroness most revered by the *filid*), a goddess of **smithcraft**, and a goddess of **healing**. She is thus
a deity of the **inspired fire** in all its forms — the poet's, the smith's forge, the healer's hearth — and is
associated with the spring festival **Imbolc** (1 February), the quickening of the year and of the flocks.

Brigid is also the great **bridge into Christianity**: her cult was very largely absorbed into **St. Brigid of
Kildare**, whose monastery kept a perpetual sacred fire and whose feast falls on Imbolc — one of the clearest
cases of a Celtic goddess surviving under a saint's name. In **[[gods-and-fighting-men|Gods and Fighting
Men]]** she is remembered as the one who **invented keening (the lament for the dead)** when her son **Ruadán**
was killed at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired. As a pan-Celtic figure she stands behind the Brittonic
**Brigantia**, tutelary goddess of the Brigantes.
