# May Maxwell (1870–1940)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 22037–22077.*

## Biographical Overview
Prominent early American Bahá'í. Born May Ellis Bolles (1870). She joined the first group of Western pilgrims to visit [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]], herself reaching [[akka|Akká]] in February 1899. She then returned to Paris where she had been living and established the nucleus of a Bahá'í community there. Those introduced to the Faith by her included C.M. [[remey|Remey]], Thomas [[breakwell|Breakwell]], Hippolyte [[dreyfus|Dreyfus]], and Laura Barney ([[dreyfus-barney|Dreyfus]]) (lines 22039–22052).

She married William Sutherland [[maxwell-william|Maxwell]] in 1902 and moved with him to Montreal. The couple had one child, Mary ([[ruhiyyih-khanum|Rúhíyyih Khánum]]). 'Abdu'l-Bahá stayed with the family during his visit to Canada (1912), their home later being accorded the status of a national Bahá'í shrine. Despite poor health, May became extensively involved in Bahá'í teaching and administrative activities in both the United States and Canada, and later in Europe. In 1940 she visited South America to support Bahá'í activities in Argentina, but died shortly after reaching Buenos Aires (lines 22053–22076).

## Status
[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] accorded her the status of a [[bahai-martyrdom|martyr]] (line 22077).
