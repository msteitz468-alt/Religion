# Sayyid Kázim Rashtí

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 20021–20054.*

## Biographical Overview

Sayyid Kázim Rashtí (c.1790s–1843/44) was the Shaykhi leader who succeeded Shaykh Aḥmad al-Aḥsá'í. Born in the northern Iranian city of Rasht, he joined Shaykh Aḥmad (according to his own account as a result of a visionary dream) while the latter was in Yazd, and subsequently became al-Aḥsá'í's most trusted companion and disciple (l. 20025–20031).

## Leadership and Teaching

Appointed as the Shaykh's successor, Rashtí established himself in the Iraqi holy city of [[karbala|Karbalá]], where he remained for the rest of his life, expounding and developing the Shaykh's teachings, consolidating the widespread network of Shaykhi followers in Iraq, Iran, and Arabia, and becoming one of the major notables of the city (l. 20033–20041).

During his leadership, opposition to Shaykhi views mounted and Shaykhism assumed an increasingly sectarian cast (l. 20042–20044). According to Bábí–Bahá'í accounts, he taught the near advent of the promised messianic figure of the Qá'im (l. 20045–20047). After his death, many Shaykhis followed the example of [[mulla-husayn|Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í]] — one of the Sayyid's leading disciples — and became Bábís (l. 20047–20051).

## Death

Various dates are given for Rashtí's death: 31 December 1843 and 1 January 1844 (l. 20052–20054).

## Significance

Rashtí was the teacher under whom [[mulla-husayn|Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í]] and the other future [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] had studied. His messianic teachings and the expectant community he gathered made the Shaykhi network a primary recruiting ground for the Báb's early movement.
