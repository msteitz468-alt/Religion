# Ibn-i-Abhar (Ḥájí Mírzá Muḥammad-Taqí Abharí)

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17700–17730.*

## Biographical Overview

Ḥájí Mírzá Muḥammad-Taqí Abharí (Ibn-i-Abhar) was an Iranian [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]] born into a family of respected *'ulamá* in the village of Abhar (between Qazvín and Zanján) (l. 17701–17704). His father became a Bábí in 1847 and later a Bahá'í. After the murder of his father in 1874 and threats to his own life, Ibn-i-Abhar moved to Zanján and brought the large Bábí community there to an acceptance of Bahá'u'lláh (l. 17705–17710).

## Service and Activities

He travelled extensively in Iran, consolidating the network of Bahá'í communities and teaching the Faith (l. 17710–17712). [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] appointed him a Hand of the Cause in about 1886 (l. 17712–17714). He was imprisoned for his Bahá'í activities in 1878–79 and again between 1890 and 1894 (l. 17714–17716). He was one of a group that travelled to India to promote the Faith in 1907, and also visited Turkistan and the Caucasus (l. 17716–17719).

His wife, Munífih Khánum (d. 1957), daughter of Ḥájí Akhúnd, was involved in establishing a Bahá'í school for girls in Tehran; both she and her husband were members of a Bahá'í committee for the advancement of women formed in 1909 (l. 17719–17727).

## Legacy

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] named him one of the [[apostles-of-bahaullah|Apostles of Bahá'u'lláh]] (l. 17727–17729).
