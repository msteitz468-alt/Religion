# Gaius (addressee of 3 John)

Gaius is the named recipient of 3 John, addressed by "the elder" as "my dear friend" and "the
beloved." [[i-howard-marshall|Marshall]] (NICNT) notes that Gaius bore one of the commonest
names in the Roman world and cautions that "we know nothing about him beyond what can be gleaned
from the letter"; there is no reason to identify him with any of the other New Testament bearers
of the name (Acts 19:29; 20:4; Rom. 16:23; 1 Cor. 1:14).

## Biographical Overview

Gaius is a member of a church under the elder's translocal oversight and, unlike
[[diotrephes|Diotrephes]], a model of Christian faithfulness. He is commended for:

- **Walking in the truth** — the elder's "greatest joy" is to hear that "my children are
  walking in the truth" (vv. 3–4);
- **Hospitality to strangers** — "you are faithful in what you are doing for the brothers, even
  though they are strangers to you" (v. 5). The "brothers" are itinerant missionaries who "went
  out for the sake of the Name," taking "no help from the pagans" (vv. 6–8), and depended on
  believers' hospitality (food, money, provisioning for the journey "in a manner worthy of
  God").

Marshall infers from the elder's prayer that Gaius "may enjoy good health… even as your soul is
getting along well" (v. 2) "some probability that Gaius was not in the best of health," and
speculates (flagging it explicitly as "one of several possible reconstructions") that Gaius may
have lived at some distance from Diotrephes' church, or been too unwell to travel and confront
Diotrephes himself.

## Primary Source Appearances

3 John 1–8, 11–15. Gaius is the letter's positive foil to [[diotrephes|Diotrephes]] and is
urged to "imitate what is good" (v. 11) and to continue supporting the missionaries, including
Demetrius, who is commended to him by a threefold testimony (v. 12; cf. the "three witnesses" of
Deut. 19:15).

## Significance

Gaius exemplifies the early-church network of hospitality that sustained itinerant Christian
mission, and stands as the counter-model to Diotrephes' refusal of the brothers. Marshall reads
the letter as showing that supporting legitimate missionaries makes the host a fellow-worker
"for the truth" (v. 8).
