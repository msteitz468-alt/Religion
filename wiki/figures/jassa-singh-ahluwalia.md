# Jassa Singh Ahluwalia

Supreme commander of the [[misls|Dal Khalsa]] and the foremost Sikh leader of the mid-eighteenth century,
hailed as *Sultān-ul-Qaum* — "King of the Nation." This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the
Sikhs*, Vol. 1, Chs. 7–9**.

## Biographical Overview (Khushwant Singh)

"Jassa Singh Kalal of the village Ahl (hence Āhlūvāliā) was born in 1718. He lost his father, Badar Singh,
when he was only five years old, and was brought up by Guru Gobind Singh's widow, Mata Sundari, and later by
Nawab Kapur Singh" (Singh, *History of the Sikhs* Vol. 1, fn.7). He gradually took over leadership from the
ageing Kapur Singh, was made supreme commander of the Dal Khalsa and all the [[misls|misls]] on its
formation (1748), and "came to be addressed as the *pādśāh* (king) of the Sikhs." On the Sikh occupation of
[[lahore|Lahore]] in 1761 he "was hailed as the *Sultān-ul-Qaum* (the King of the nation)" and minted a new
currency. He wrested Kapurthala in 1774 and died at [[amritsar|Amritsar]] in 1783, succeeded by Bhag Singh.

## Significance

Singh names Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, Hari Singh Bhangi, and Charhat Singh Sukerchakia as the leaders whose
quality enabled Sikh survival and recovery against [[ahmad-shah-abdali|Ahmad Shah Abdali]]. Across the
persecution years he organized the misldars against the Afghans, arbitrated disputes within the confederacy
(including the threat to excommunicate Ala Singh of Patiala), and led the recovery after each disaster,
including the [[ghallughara|Vaḍā Ghallūghārā]] of 1762.
