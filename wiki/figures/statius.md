# Statius

Roman poet (c. 45–c. 96 CE), author of the *Thebaid* (on the fratricidal wars of
Thebes) and the unfinished *Achilleid*. In Dante's [[divine-comedy|*Commedia*]] he
is both a literary source and a character.

## In the Divine Comedy

The [[inferno|Inferno]] draws heavily on the *Thebaid* — Thebes is Dante's
archetypal city of corruption — especially in the canto of the violent and the
treacherous (Inf. 12, 32–33; cf. the Ugolino–Tydeus parallel). Statius himself
appears in [[purgatorio|Purgatorio]] 21–25, where an earthquake and a *Gloria*
announce that his purgation is complete, and he joins [[dante-alighieri|Dante]] and
[[virgil|Virgil]] for the ascent — the only soul released from penance during
Dante's three days on the Mountain.

He had been purged on two terraces: more than 400 years for **sloth/lukewarmness**
(having hidden his faith) and a long span for **prodigality** (the opposite of
avarice, purged on the same terrace), corrected by a verse of the *Aeneid* (3:55–8).
Without historical warrant, Dante makes Statius a **secret Christian convert** of
the age of Domitian's persecution, whose conversion was catalysed by Virgil's
Fourth *Eclogue* — read in the Middle Ages as a prophecy of Christ's birth: "I was,
through you, a poet and was Christian, too" (22:73). The *Aeneid*, he says, was "a
mum to me, to me my nurse in poetry" (21:97–98). When he learns his interlocutor is
Virgil himself he stoops to clasp his feet, but Virgil stops him: "Brother, don't! /
You are a shadow and you see a shade."

In **Purgatorio 25** Statius delivers the poem's densest piece of philosophy — on
**embryology and the soul**: the body is *generated* from "perfect blood," but the
rational soul is *created* directly by God (against [the Averroist view] that the
intellect is a separable communal pool), and after death the soul radiates an
"aerial body" (the shade). He accompanies Dante through the Earthly Paradise to the
drinking of Eunoë (Canto 33).

## Significance

Statius embodies Dante's theme of [[virgil|Virgil]] as the pagan who unwittingly
heralds Christ — saving others (Statius) while himself remaining in [[limbo|Limbo]].
