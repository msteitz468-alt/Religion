# Nabíl-i-Akbar (1829–1892)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23348–23373.*

## Biographical Overview
Mullá Muḥammad Qá'iní, prominent Iranian Bahá'í. Born into a clerical family, he studied both the customary Islamic sciences and with the philosopher Ḥájí Mullá Hádí Sabzivárí (1797/8–1878). He became one of the foremost students of the eminent Shaykh Murtaḍá Ansárí (1801/2–64/5), and received an *ijáza* from him, giving Qá'iní the rank of [[mujtahid|mujtahid]] (see *'ulamá*) (lines 23348–23358).

He became a Bábí in the early 1850s and later a Bahá'í. He was imprisoned a number of times in Iran for his Bahá'í activities and eventually moved to [[ashkhabad|Ashkhabad]]. He died in Bukhara. He met [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] on several occasions and was the recipient of the *Lawḥ-i-Ḥikmat* (Tablet of Wisdom) (lines 23358–23366).

[[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] named him posthumously as a Hand of the Cause and [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] designated him as one of the Apostles of Bahá'u'lláh (lines 23367–23372).
