# Abner

**Abner son of Ner**, [[saul|Saul's]] cousin and the commander of his army, is the strong
man who keeps the house of Saul alive after Gilboa by installing Saul's surviving son
**Ish-bosheth** as king over the northern tribes — and who, by defecting to [[david|David]],
makes possible the union of Israel under David before being treacherously murdered by
[[joab|Joab]].

## Biographical Overview and Primary Appearances

- **Saul's general** — Abner commands Saul's forces and sits beside the king (1 Sam 20:25);
  David twice taunts him for failing to guard Saul (the night David takes Saul's spear and
  water-jug, 1 Sam 26).
- **Kingmaker** — After Saul's death Abner makes Ish-bosheth king at Mahanaim, sustaining a
  long civil war against David's house (2 Sam 2). At the pool of Gibeon his men and Joab's
  fight; in the rout Abner reluctantly kills Joab's brother **Asahel**, who would not stop
  pursuing him — incurring Joab's blood-vengeance.
- **Defection** — Quarreling with Ish-bosheth over Saul's concubine Rizpah, Abner resolves
  to transfer the whole kingdom to David, and negotiates terms (David requires the return of
  [[michal|Michal]] first). He swings the elders of Israel to David and departs in peace
  (2 Sam 3).
- **Murder** — Joab, behind David's back, recalls Abner to the gate of Hebron and stabs him
  in the belly, avenging Asahel (and removing a rival commander). David publicly disclaims
  the murder, curses Joab's house, and leads the mourning, composing a brief lament: "Should
  Abner die as a fool dies?" (2 Sam 3).

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Abner is the pivot on which the divided kingdom turns toward David: his defection delivers
the north, yet his murder threatens to taint David's accession with Saulide blood. The
narrative is careful to clear David of complicity — part of the book's recurring concern to
show David **profiting from the deaths of the house of Saul while dissociating himself from
each killing**.

## Alter's Reading

[[robert-alter|Alter]] ([[alter-the-david-story|*The David Story*]]) places Abner's murder
within the pattern of David's "vehement dissociation" from the convenient deaths that clear
his path, and within the blood-guilt motif that clings to [[joab|Joab]]: David's deathbed
charge to Solomon names Joab's killing of "Abner son of Ner and Amasa son of Jether" as the
unavenged blood that must not "go down in peace to Sheol" (1 Kings 2). The killing of Abner
"in peace," like that of Amasa, exemplifies for Alter the way the writer renders political
violence with unflinching moral clarity while leaving David's own culpability artfully
ambiguous.

## Reception

Jewish tradition debated Abner's guilt — praising his martial loyalty but faulting him for
propping up Ish-bosheth against the divinely chosen David, or for letting Saul pursue David;
his violent end is read as measure-for-measure justice. David's lament marks Abner as "a
prince and a great man... fallen this day in Israel" (2 Sam 3:38).
