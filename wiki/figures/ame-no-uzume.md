# Ame-no-Uzume

**Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto**, "the Heavenly-Alarming-Female," is the [[shinto|Shinto]] **goddess of dawn, mirth,
revelry, and sacred dance** — the kami whose **comic, ecstatic dance draws the sun-goddess out of hiding** and
restores light to the world. She is regarded as the patroness and mythic origin of **kagura** (Shinto sacred
dance) and of the *miko* (shrine dancers).

Her great moment is the **heavenly rock-cave** episode (see [[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]): when the offended sun
seals herself away and the world goes dark, the assembled kami need to lure her out. Uzume **overturns a tub
before the cave, stamps upon it resoundingly, and "becomes divinely possessed" — baring her breasts and pushing
down her skirt-band** in a wild, abandoned dance, until the **eight hundred myriad gods burst into a roar of
laughter** that shakes heaven. The puzzled Amaterasu, hearing such joy in her absence, opens the cave a crack
to ask why they rejoice — and is shown her own face in the **mirror** and drawn out, returning light to the
cosmos. Uzume's **mirth and uninhibited dance** are thus literally **world-saving**, and the scene is read as a
ritual of sun-return enacted in festival. She later confronts and wins over the imposing earth-kami
**Sarutahiko** at the **[[ninigi|descent of Ninigi]]**, and her line becomes the **Sarume** ("monkey-women")
ritual dancers of the court.
