# Michael the Archangel

## Biographical Overview
Michael (meaning "Who is like God?") is one of the chief celestial beings in the Abrahamic traditions. He is depicted as a warrior angel and the specific patron and protector of the nation of Israel. 

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Daniel 10 and 12**: Michael is described as "one of the chief princes" and the "great prince who protects your people," standing against the spiritual princes of Persia and Greece.
- **Jude 9**: References an apocryphal narrative (likely from the lost ending of the *Assumption of Moses*) where Michael disputes with the devil over the body of Moses. Rather than pronouncing a blasphemous judgment, Michael simply says, "The Lord rebuke you."
- **Revelation 12:7-9**: Leads the heavenly armies in eschatological battle against the dragon (Satan), casting him out of heaven.
- **Apocryphal Literature**: Frequently appears as a prominent figure in texts like *1 Enoch*, serving as an intercessor for humanity and a commander of God's forces.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In Jude, Michael is invoked as a model of appropriate deference to divine authority. Even as a powerful archangel engaged in direct cosmic conflict with the devil, Michael restrains himself from slanderous judgment, relying instead on the Lord's rebuke. This humility is contrasted sharply with the arrogance of the false teachers who "blaspheme the glorious ones."
