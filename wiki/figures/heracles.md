# Heracles (Hercules)

**Heracles** (Roman **Hercules**) is the greatest of the Greek heroes — a **mortal son of [[zeus|Zeus]]** (by
Alcmene) who, through suffering and superhuman labor, **won full divinity**, the supreme example of the
[[greek-hero-cult|hero who becomes a god]].

## The Hero's Career

Persecuted from birth by [[hera|Hera's]] jealousy, driven mad to kill his own children, he was set the **Twelve
Labors** in expiation (the Nemean lion, the Hydra, the Erymanthian boar, the Augean stables, Cerberus, the
apples of the Hesperides, etc.) — a career of monster-slaying and civilizing toil. At his death (poisoned by
the centaur's blood) he was burned on a pyre and **taken up to Olympus**, reconciled with Hera and made a god,
marrying Hebe.

## Cult and Significance

Worshipped both as a **hero** (at tombs) and as a **god** — a unique double status embodying the permeable
boundary between mortal and divine. Hugely popular at Rome as **Hercules**, patron of victory, commerce, and
strength. His apotheosis became a model for the [[imperial-cult|deification of emperors]].

## The Twelve Labors (in detail)

After [[hera|Hera]] struck him with madness so that he killed his own wife Megara and their children, the
oracle at Delphi sent Heracles to serve his cowardly cousin **King Eurystheus** of Mycenae for twelve years,
performing whatever labors he set — in expiation. The canonical **Twelve Labors** are:

1. **The Nemean Lion** — a beast with an impenetrable hide; Heracles strangled it and wore its skin as armor.
2. **The Lernaean Hydra** — a many-headed serpent that grew two heads for each one cut; with his nephew
   Iolaus cauterizing the stumps, he killed it and dipped his arrows in its deadly venom.
3. **The Ceryneian Hind** — [[artemis|Artemis's]] golden-horned deer; he pursued it for a year and caught it
   alive.
4. **The Erymanthian Boar** — captured alive and carried back (terrifying Eurystheus, who hid in a jar).
5. **The Augean Stables** — thirty years' filth of vast herds, cleaned in a single day by **diverting two
   rivers** through them.
6. **The Stymphalian Birds** — man-eating birds with bronze beaks, frightened up with [[hephaestus|
   Hephaestus's]] rattle (from [[athena|Athena]]) and shot down.
7. **The Cretan Bull** — the mad bull of Minos, captured and brought back.
8. **The Mares of Diomedes** — man-eating horses; he tamed them by feeding their cruel master to them.
9. **The Belt of Hippolyta** — the girdle of the Amazon queen (won, then lost when Hera stirred up war).
10. **The Cattle of Geryon** — the herds of a three-bodied giant in the far west, driven all the way back to
    Greece (en route he set up the **Pillars of Heracles** at Gibraltar).
11. **The Apples of the Hesperides** — guarded golden apples at the world's edge; Heracles held up the sky so
    **[[atlas|Atlas]]** would fetch them, then tricked the Titan into taking the burden back.
12. **Cerberus** — he descended **into the underworld** and brought up [[hades|Hades's]] three-headed hound
    alive, the supreme labor — a conquest of death itself.

## Death and Apotheosis

Long after, the centaur **Nessus**, dying from Heracles' poisoned arrow, tricked Heracles' wife **Deianira**
into keeping his blood as a "love charm." When she later smeared it on a robe to win back his love, the
**Hydra's venom** in it burned into his flesh in unbearable agony. Heracles built his own **pyre** on Mount
Oeta and was burned — but his divine part was carried up to Olympus, where, reconciled at last with
[[hera|Hera]], he was made a **god** and married Hebe.
