# Leo the Great

Pope (440–461), whose *Tome to Flavian* (449) gave the West's decisive contribution to the [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedonian settlement]], and who articulated the classic theology of the [[apostolic-succession|Roman primacy]]. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. XII, XV.

## Christology: The Tome

Leo's *Tome* (dispatched to Flavian of Constantinople, 13 June 449) sets out four theses:
1. The Person of the God-man is identical with the divine Word: "He Who became man in the form of a servant is He Who in the form of God created man."
2. Two natures co-exist without mixture, each retaining its properties (*salva proprietate utriusque naturae et substantiae*).
3. The natures are distinct principles of operation acting in concert: "Each form accomplishes in concert with the other what is appropriate to it, the Word performing what belongs to the Word, and the flesh carrying out what belongs to the flesh."
4. The [[communicatio-idiomatum|communicatio idiomatum]] follows from the oneness of Person: "the Son of God was crucified and buried, and also the Son of Man came down from heaven."

Three passages of the *Tome* alarmed the Illyrian and Palestinian delegates at [[chalcedonian-definition|Chalcedon]] (451) as seeming to divide Christ, and were ratified only after the Roman legates supplied a dossier of [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]] citations. Leo branded the [[eutyches|Eutychian]] Synod of Ephesus (449) the *Latrocinium* ("Robber Synod").

## The Roman Primacy

Kelly makes Leo the systematiser of the Western doctrine of papal authority, in four theses: (1) Christ conferred supreme authority on [[peter-apostle|Peter]] via the Gospel texts; (2) Peter was bishop of Rome, his *magisterium* perpetuated in his successors; (3) Peter is "mystically present" in the Roman see — the pope a kind of *Petrus redivivus*; (4) other bishops' authority is mediated through Peter/pope, who holds *plenitudo potestatis* over the whole Church.
