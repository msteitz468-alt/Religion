# Korah

## Biographical Overview
Korah was a Levite who led a major insurrection against the authority of Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. Supported by Dathan, Abiram, and 250 Israelite leaders, Korah challenged the exclusivity of the Aaronic priesthood, declaring that "all in the congregation are holy." 

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Numbers 16–17**: The narrative of the rebellion and its aftermath (censers, plague, budding rod). God vindicates Moses and Aaron when the earth opens and swallows Korah, Dathan, and Abiram alive, while fire consumes the 250 men offering incense.
- **Numbers 26:11**: An important genealogical note: "But the sons of Korah did not die." This explains the survival of his lineage.
- **1 Chronicles 6:31-38; 9:19**: Descendants of Korah are appointed by David over the service of song in the house of the LORD and serve as gatekeepers for the camp.
- **Psalms**: Eleven psalms (42–49, 84–85, 87–88) are attributed to the "Sons of Korah," a major guild of temple singers active through the first temple period and whose voices are prominent in Books Two and Three of the Psalter.
- **Jude 11**: Condemns false teachers who "perish in Korah's rebellion."

## Tradition-Specific Reception

### Ashley (NICOT Numbers)

[[timothy-ashley|Ashley]] reads chs. 16–17 as **legitimation of Aaron’s priesthood** after challenges to Moses in chs. 11–14:

- **Two rebellions, common cause:** Korah’s (Levite/priestly ambition—privilege for Levites or himself, not abolition of priesthood) and Dathan/Abiram’s (Reubenite civil revolt rehashing Egypt-as-paradise complaints). Common scholarly JE/Pg/Ps splits treated as artificial; uniting them shows different agendas made common cause against the order God ordained.
- **Kohathite slight?** Elizaphan of Uzziel (youngest Kohathite line) named leader in 3:30—perhaps Korah (Izharite) felt overlooked.
- **ʿēdâ of Korah** as parody alternate cultic community.
- **Unexpected climax:** novel death proves *these men spurned Yahweh*, not merely “Moses is vindicated.”
- **Aftermath:** censers holy → bronze altar covering; plague stopped only by Aaron’s priestly *kipper*; budding rod (thirteen rods including Levi); ch. 18 redefines who dies for encroachment; ch. 19 red cow answers mass corpse-pollution.
- **26:11:** sons of Korah did not die—postexilic Korahites and Korah psalms confirm survival; *nēs* as “warning sign” (unique OT sense).

### Judaism
The rebellion of Korah is remembered as the archetypal "dispute that is not for the sake of Heaven" (Pirkei Avot 5:17). Rabbinic literature expands on Korah's wealth and demagoguery. However, the survival of the Sons of Korah (who are said to have repented at the last moment) becomes a symbol of grace and the source of some of the most profound spiritual poetry in the Psalms.

### Christianity
Jude's use cements Korah as a type of the schismatic and heretic who rejects divine authority. The Korahite psalms, however, are deeply loved for their expressions of longing for God's presence (Ps 42) and love for Zion (Ps 84, 87).

### Kabbalah (the Zohar)
 The [[zohar|Zohar]] (1:17a–b, in the discussion of the second day of Creation) reads Korah's rebellion at the **Sod** level as a cosmic event — the paradigmatic case of **the left side rising against the right**. In the [[sefirot|sefirotic]] structure, Ḥesed (Lovingkindness) is the right side and Gevurah/Din (Judgment) the left; on the second day of Creation a "conflict" (*makhloqet*) blazed between them — the left side seeking to dominate — until the central pillar, Tif'eret, entered between them and mediated, harmonizing left into right. From the unresolved blaze of the left came Gehinnom (see [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]]).

- **Korah = the left refusing the center**: Korah embodies Gevurah/Din insisting on ruling alone, refusing to be harmonized by the mediating center. His was "a dispute not for the sake of Heaven" precisely because, unlike the creative left-right tension that the central pillar resolves, Korah's dispute sought separation, not unity.
- **Moses as the cosmic mediator**: [[moses|Moses]], who "contemplated the act of Creation" and understood how the central pillar reconciles left and right, attempted to mediate Korah's conflict as God had mediated the primordial one. Korah's refusal made him irreconcilable.
- **"Korah denied the act of Creation"**: His deepest sin, in the Zohar, is metaphysical: by tearing the left from the right he "denied the act of Creation" itself — the very structure by which the world is sustained through the balanced flow of the sefirot. The [[sitra-ahra|Other Side]] therefore attached itself to his blazing wrath.
- **Swallowed by the earth**: The earth (associated with Malkhut/[[shekhinah|Shekhinah]], the receptive lower world) opening to swallow him is read as the lower realm reclaiming the disordered force that refused its proper place in the divine harmony.

This Zoharic reading aligns the wiki's [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]] page (Korah as a case study in left-side imbalance) with the [[moses|Moses]] page (Moses as cosmic mediator who reaches Tif'eret).

## Theological and Narrative Significance
Korah serves as the biblical archetype for antiauthoritarian rebellion and schism. In Jude, Korah is the final figure in the triad of apostates (Cain, Balaam, Korah). His inclusion highlights the specific nature of the false teachers' sin: just as Korah rejected God's appointed mediators (Moses and Aaron), the intruders in Jude's church reject the apostolic authority and the lordship of Christ. His terrifying judgment (being swallowed by the earth) serves as a severe warning of the eschatological doom awaiting those who incite division. 

Simultaneously, the "Sons of Korah" represent the redemptive arc across the canon: the descendants of the great rebel become the great worshippers and gatekeepers of the sanctuary, composing psalms that long for the very presence their ancestor arrogantly demanded.
