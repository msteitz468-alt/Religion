# Confucius

**Confucius** (Kongzi, "Master Kong," 551–479 BCE) is the founding teacher of
[[confucianism|Confucianism]] and one of the most influential figures in human history — the moral
exemplar of East Asian civilization.

## Biographical Overview

Born in the state of Lu (modern Shandong) in the late Spring-and-Autumn period, of modest standing, he
became a teacher who took students regardless of class and held minor office. Frustrated in his ambition
to reform government from within, he spent years traveling among the states seeking a ruler who would
employ his Way (*[[dao|dao]]*), then returned to teach and (by tradition) edit the **Five Classics**. He
described himself as "a transmitter and not a maker," devoted to the wisdom of the ancient sage-kings.

## Teaching (as recorded)

The [[analects|Analects]] preserves his thought: **[[ren|ren]]** (humaneness) cultivated through
**[[li-confucian|li]]** (ritual propriety) and learning; the **junzi** (exemplary person); **filial
piety**; the **rectification of names**; and government by **moral example** rather than coercion.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[classical-confucianism|Classical Confucianism]]** — the Sage and Teacher, transmitter of the Way.
- **[[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]]** — read through the metaphysics of *li*/principle; venerated
  in state cult.
- **Imperial cult** — for two millennia honored with temples and sacrifices as the supreme teacher (the
  "Uncrowned King"), his descendants ennobled.
- **[[daoism|Daoist]] polemic** — in the Zhuangzi and the Laozi legend, Confucius is gently mocked or
  schooled by Daoist sages — a literary assertion of Daoism's priority.

## Historicity

A historical person, though the [[analects|Analects]] was compiled after his death and the layers of
legend (and his traditional editorship of the Classics) are debated. The reliability of the various
strata of the Analects is the main critical question.
