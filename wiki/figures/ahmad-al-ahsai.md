# Shaykh Ahmad al-Ahsá'í

*Populated from [[smith-concise-encyclopedia-bahai-faith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Biographical Overview

Shaykh Ahmad al-Ahsá'í (1753–1826) was the founder of the [[shaykhism|Shaykhi]] school of Shí'í Islam, which became the immediate intellectual precursor of the Babi religion. He was born in the Hasá region of eastern Arabia. He migrated to the Shí'í shrine cities in Iraq in the early 1790s, quickly gaining a reputation for piety and learning and attracting a growing following (lines 2579–2587).

In 1806 he moved to Iran, where he gained the support of many members of the Qajar family including the reigning monarch, Fath-'Alí Sháh. Around 1822 he was accused of heresy by Hájí Mullá Muhammad-Taqí Baraghání, one of the leading clerics in Qazvín. He returned to Iraq in 1822 but encountered such resentment from other clerics that after some years he determined to migrate to Mecca. He died en route and was buried at Medina (lines 2588–2601). He was succeeded as leader of the Shaykhi movement by his chief disciple, Sayyid [[kazim-rashti|Kázim Rashtí]].

## Teachings

Shaykh Ahmad's teachings were complex, and he often resorted to the Shí'í practice of pious dissimulation (*taqiyya*) to conceal more controversial ideas from opponents. He emphasized the mystical dimension of religious faith and claimed to unveil deeper esoteric truths. He believed he had a special relationship with the [[imams|Imams]], whom he encountered in visionary experiences and from whom he derived his authority (lines 2602–2615).

## Significance for Babi-Bahá'í History

The Shaykhi movement Shaykh Ahmad founded served as the milieu from which the Báb emerged. The Báb's earliest disciples were almost all Shaykhis, and Shaykh Ahmad's doctrines — particularly concerning the nature of the Imam, the resurrection, and the identity of the "Fourth Support" (*rukn-i-rábi'*) — laid the theological groundwork that the Báb would reinterpret and radicalize into an independent religion.
