# Tudi Gong (the Earth God)

**Tǔdì Gōng**, the **Earth God** or **Local God**, is the **tutelary deity of a specific patch of ground** — a
village, a street, a field, a building — and the **lowest-ranking yet most ubiquitous** god of
[[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]]. Depicted as a kindly, smiling **old man** with a white beard
and staff, often paired with his wife **Tǔdì Pó**, he is the friendly **neighborhood official** of the
**[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]** — the divine equivalent of a local clerk or village
headman, with jurisdiction over just his small territory.

Small **Tudi Gong shrines** stand everywhere — at field edges, shop entrances, under trees, by bridges — and he
is petitioned for the **everyday business of life**: good harvests, safe homes, success in trade, protection
from local ghosts (whom he supervises and keeps in order). Farmers and shopkeepers honor him on the 2nd and
16th of each lunar month. Because he reports up the hierarchy — to the **City God (Chenghuang)** above him, and
ultimately to the **[[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]** — he is also notified at funerals, since he must register
a death in his territory and escort the soul on its way. Modest, accessible, and benign, Tudi Gong embodies the
**immediate, local face** of the cosmic empire: every place has its own resident god.
