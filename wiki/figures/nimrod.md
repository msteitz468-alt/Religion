# Nimrod

A descendant of [[noah|Noah]] through Ham and Cush, described in Genesis as "a mighty one in the earth"
and "a mighty hunter before the Lord," whose kingdom "began" with **[[babylon|Babylon]]**. In the
interpretive tradition he is the builder of the Tower of Babel and the archetypal founder of worldly
empire.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 10:8–12** — Nimrod as the first "mighty" man and king; the beginning of his kingdom is
  Babel, Erech, and Accad in the land of Shinar; from there he goes to build Nineveh.
- **Genesis 11:1–9** (by tradition) — the building of the city and tower whose tongues God confounds.

## Augustine's Reading (City of God XVI)

[[augustine|Augustine]] treats Nimrod as the human founder of the **[[two-cities|earthly city]]** in its
paradigmatic form. He insists the Greek phrase be read "a hunter **against** the Lord" (not merely
"before"): "Hunter" means "deceiver, oppressor, and destroyer," and Nimrod "and his people therefore
erected this tower against the Lord, and so gave expression to their impious pride." His city is
**[[babylon|Babylon]]**, which "means Confusion" — the same pride that built the tower is punished in the
confusion of tongues, "so that man, who would not understand God … should be misunderstood when he
himself gave orders." Babylon, Nimrod's foundation, becomes for Augustine the standing type of the
earthly city, later doubled in [[rome|Rome]], "a second Babylon in the west."

## Theological Significance

Nimrod fixes the **earthly city's character**: domination (*libido dominandi*), pride reaching toward
heaven by its own power, and the resulting confusion. He is the counter-image to [[abraham|Abraham]],
called out of the same Mesopotamian world to found the line of the city of God.
