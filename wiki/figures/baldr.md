# Baldr

**Baldr** is the [[norse-religion|Norse]] god of **light, beauty, and innocence** — the most beloved and
radiant of the gods, son of [[odin|Odin]] and [[frigg|Frigg]]. His **death** is the great tragedy of Norse
myth and the **first event of the end**: the moment the doomed gods begin their slide toward
[[ragnarok|Ragnarök]].

## The Death of Baldr

Tormented by **dreams of his own death**, Baldr alarmed the gods. His mother **[[frigg|Frigg]]** then made
**every thing in creation swear an oath** never to harm him — fire, water, metal, stones, sickness, beasts —
overlooking only the **mistletoe**, too young to swear. Believing him invulnerable, the gods amused
themselves **hurling weapons at Baldr**, which all bounced off harmlessly. But **[[loki|Loki]]** discovered
the one exception, made a **mistletoe dart**, and put it in the hand of the **blind god Höðr**, guiding his
aim — and Baldr fell dead (see [[loki|Loki]]).

When the gods sent to **Hel** to ransom him, the death-goddess agreed to free Baldr **only if all things wept
for him**. All did — except one giantess (Loki in disguise), so **Baldr remained among the dead**. He is
fated to **return after Ragnarök**, reconciled with his unwitting killer Höðr, to rule the renewed and
peaceful world — the **hope on the far side of the end**.

## Significance

Baldr is the wiki's Norse **dying-and-returning god** — comparable to [[osiris|Osiris]], [[baldr|Baldr]]'s
death as the hinge of cosmic doom, and his survival of Ragnarök as a note of **redemption**. (The Christian
coloring of Baldr's "innocent death and return" in the late sources is much debated.)
