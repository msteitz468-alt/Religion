# Demeter (Ceres)

**Demeter** (Roman **Ceres**, from whom "cereal") is the goddess of **grain, agriculture, and the harvest**
— a daughter of [[cronus|Cronus]] and Rhea, and one of the most beloved of the gods.

## The Central Myth

Her story is the **abduction of her daughter [[persephone|Persephone]]** by [[hades|Hades]] (told in the
[[homeric-hymns|Homeric Hymn to Demeter]]): in grief Demeter withholds growth and the earth turns barren,
until a settlement returns Persephone for part of the year — the origin of the **seasons** (winter is
Demeter's mourning). The myth is the foundation charter of the **[[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian
Mysteries]]**, which Demeter establishes at Eleusis and which promise initiates a blessed afterlife.

## Cult and Roman Identification

Goddess of the Thesmophoria (a women's festival) and of Eleusis. As **Ceres** she was a major Roman
agricultural goddess, with the plebeian Cerealia and a triad with Liber and Libera.

## Myths in Detail

**The rape of Persephone.** As [[persephone|Persephone]] gathered flowers in a meadow, the earth split open
and **[[hades|Hades]]** burst forth in his chariot and seized her down to the underworld — with the secret
consent of her father [[zeus|Zeus]]. Hearing her cry, Demeter searched the whole earth for nine days and
nights with torches, neither eating nor bathing, until **[[helios|Helios]]** the all-seeing sun told her the
truth.

**The grief at Eleusis.** Disguised as an old woman, the grieving Demeter came to **Eleusis**, where she was
kindly received and made nurse to the king's son; she secretly tried to make the child immortal by laying him
in the fire by night, until the mother's scream broke the rite. Revealing herself, Demeter commanded that a
temple be built for her there — the seed of the **[[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]]**.

**The barren year and the bargain.** Still mourning, Demeter made the earth utterly **barren** — no seed
grew, no sacrifice rose to the gods — until Zeus relented and sent Hermes to bring Persephone back. But Hades
had given her **pomegranate seeds** to eat, binding her to the underworld; so the bargain was struck:
Persephone would spend part of each year below with Hades and part above with her mother. Demeter's joy at
the return, and her grief at the descent, are the myth of the **seasons** — the death of the seed in winter
and its return in spring — and in gratitude she taught humanity the arts of **agriculture** and the secret
rites that promise initiates a happy afterlife.
