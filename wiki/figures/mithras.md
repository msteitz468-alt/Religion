# Mithras

> **Figure page.** The god at the center of [[mithraism|the Roman Mysteries of Mithras]],
> developed (loosely) from the Iranian deity **Mithra** of the [[avesta|Avesta]]. The Iranian
> Mithra and the Roman Mithras are continuous in name but not simply identical: the Ostia team
> stress that "primitive" Avestan Mithra is "clearly naturalistic" and "far removed" from the
> Mithras of the Roman imperial cult.

## Iranian Background

In the [[avesta|Avesta]] (the *Mihir Yasht*), Mithra is a god of covenant, light, and the
pre-dawn sky, associated with truth, life-giving power, and martial vigor. Under
[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]] he was subordinated to [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda/Ormuzd]] yet
remained a major object of devotion — in Haywood's popular phrasing, exalted "to the right hand
of the awful Ormuzd." He is closely associated in the Iranian sphere with the goddess
[[anahita|Anahita]].

## The Roman Mithras

In the Roman mystery cult, Mithras appears above all in the **tauroctonos** — the stylized relief
of the god slaying a primeval bull "by pulling back its head and plunging a knife into its
throat" — placed on the axis of every [[mithraism|mithraeum]] as its cult focus. From the bull's
death new life arises; the image carries astral symbolism and the promise of the soul's ascent.
Mithras is repeatedly paired with **Sol** (the Sun): the two share the mythological feast that
the cult meal re-enacted, and the cult's late identity fused with **Sol Invictus**, "the
unconquered Sun." The Ostia team note the duality of Sol and Mithra was theologically undeveloped
— "if Mithraic theologians had been at work, a Nicaean battle could have arisen over the duality
of Sol and Mithra," but the cult, subordinating message to ritual, never reached that conflict.

## Significance and Reception

- **In the cult**: Mithras is savior, mediator, and exemplar; the highest grade
  ([[mithraism|Pater]]) enacted his role in the liturgy.
- **Versus Christ**: Mithras became the standard pagan comparandum for Christ — see
  [[christianity-mithraism-parallels|the Christianity–Mithraism comparison]]. Laeuchli's verdict:
  Mithras "had history, but it had no theological and communal drama which tore this history
  apart."
- **Afterlife**: Haywood claims fragments of Mithra-devotion survived into Islam (rebuilt "into
  Allah and into Mohammed") and, via [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]], into later dualist
  Christianity — recorded as his conjecture, not established fact.
