# Agni

**Agni** ("Fire," cognate with Latin *ignis*) is the **fire-god** and the second-most hymned deity of the
[[rig-veda|Rig Veda]] — the **divine priest** and the very heart of [[yajna|the Vedic sacrifice]]. As the
sacred fire on the altar, Agni is **uniquely present in every act of worship**, the god who is **physically
there** in every home and rite.

## The Mediator

Agni is the **mouth of the gods** and the **messenger between heaven and earth**: the offerings cast into him
are **carried up as smoke and flame to the gods**, and the gods come down to the sacrifice through him. He is
thus the indispensable **mediator** of all Vedic religion — "the priest of the gods and the god of the
priests." Born anew each day from the **fire-sticks** (and from the waters, the sun, and lightning), he is
called **"ever-young,"** present in the domestic hearth, the sacrificial altar, the funeral pyre, the sun, and
the lightning alike. His association with the sacrifice and the home kept Agni important in [[hinduism|Hindu]]
ritual long after [[indra|Indra]] faded — the Vedic fire-rites continue in Hindu weddings and rituals to this
day.
