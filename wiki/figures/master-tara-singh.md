# Master Tara Singh

The dominant figure in [[sikhs|Sikh]] politics from the 1920s to the early 1960s, leader of the
[[akali-movement|Shiromani Akali Dal]], and the chief Sikh voice through the Gurdwara Reform movement,
[[partition-of-punjab-1947|Partition]], and the campaign for the Punjabi Suba. Khushwant Singh's
*A History of the Sikhs* Vol. 2 traces his rise and his self-inflicted political fall.

## Biographical Overview

Born **24 June 1885** in the village of Harial (Rawalpindi district), the son of a Hindu of the Malhotra
caste, he "converted to Sikhism while at school." Educated at Khalsa College, Amritsar, he took a
teaching diploma and became a teacher at the Khalsa High School, Lyallpur — "the title 'master' has
attached to his name ever since." Singh notes him already as a young man organizing Khalsa College
student demonstrations against the 1907 colonization bill, "destined to become the dominant figure in
Sikh politics." He joined the [[akali-movement|Akali movement]] at its inception. He died on 22 November
1967.

## The Gurdwara Reform Movement

Tara Singh was arrested at **Guru ka Bagh** and again at **Jaito** (charged with "conspiracy to wage war
against the king"). When Lt.-Gov. Hailey split the Akali leadership with a conditional-release offer
during the passage of the **Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925**, Tara Singh and Baba Kharak Singh "considered the
conditional release derogatory to their self-respect and refused to give any undertaking," then ousted
the faction (Mehtab Singh's) that had accepted it. His faction "remained… dominant for the following
four decades." See [[akali-movement|the Akali movement]].

## Partition and the Sikh-Nation Claim

On **2 March 1947**, as the Khizr ministry fell, he unsheathed his *kirpan* before the Punjab Assembly in
[[lahore|Lahore]] shouting "Death to Pakistan." On Baisakhi 1947 he led 280 *jathedars* in an Akal Takht
oath to sacrifice their lives. He had declared, on **20 August 1944**, "for the first time that the Sikhs
were a separate nation," and before the Cabinet Mission (1946) argued for a united India but, failing
that, "a separate Sikh state with the right to federate either with India or Pakistan." His response to
the Cabinet Mission plan was "a wave of dejection, resentment and indignation." See
[[partition-of-punjab-1947|Partition of Punjab]].

## The Punjabi Suba and His Fall

Tara Singh led the post-Partition campaign for a Punjabi-speaking (and, Singh insists, Sikh-majority)
state. He denounced the 1953 States Reorganization Commission's rejection as "a decree of Sikh
annihilation." His decisive miscalculation came in **1961**: he began a fast-unto-death at the Golden
Temple on 15 August, but **called it off after 43 days** without achieving the Suba. Tried at the Akal
Takht for breaking his vow, he was sentenced to clean the congregation's shoes for five days. Singh's
verdict: "Master Tara Singh saved his life but killed his political career and dealt a grievous blow to
the cause of a Sikh-majority state." The SGPC and Akali Dal voted him out, and **Sant Fateh Singh**
succeeded him. His plea before the Akal Takht: "If I have committed a mistake, correct me. If I have done
the right thing, march with me. If I have acted treacherously, punish me." The Punjabi Suba he had fought
for was finally granted in 1966, after his eclipse.
