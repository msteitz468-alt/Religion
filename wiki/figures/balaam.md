# Balaam

## Biographical Overview
Balaam was a non-Israelite diviner or prophet from Pethor whom Balak, king of Moab, hired to curse the Israelites during their wilderness journey. Although God prevented Balaam from cursing Israel—forcing him to bless them instead, famously utilizing his talking donkey to restrain his madness—Balaam later advised the Midianites on how to seduce Israel into sexual immorality and idolatry (the incident of Baal-peor).

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Numbers 22-24**: The narrative of his hiring by Balak, the donkey incident, and the oracles of blessing.
- **Numbers 31:16**: Reveals that Balaam was responsible for counseling the Midianite women to seduce the Israelites, leading to a plague. He is subsequently killed in battle.
- **2 Peter 2:15-16**: Condemns false teachers who have "followed the way of Balaam, the son of Beor, who loved gain from wrongdoing."
- **Jude 11**: Part of the "woe triad," condemning those who "abandon themselves for the sake of gain to Balaam's error."
- **Revelation 2:14**: The church in Pergamum is warned against those who hold to the teaching of Balaam, who taught Balak to put a stumbling block before Israel, leading them to eat food sacrificed to idols and practice sexual immorality.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

### Ashley (NICOT Numbers) — final-form evangelical

[[timothy-ashley|Ashley]] densifies the cycle as the opening of the Moab section (22:2–24:25):

- **Character in chs. 22–24:** relatively **neutral**—not primarily a greed morality tale in the Numbers narrative itself; mercenary condemnation is a later reception layer (31:16; 2 Pet; Jude; Rev). Ashley works with *bārû* / *āšipu*-type seer categories without reducing Balaam to either pure prophet or pure villain.
- **Deir ʿAllā:** plaster texts naming Balaam son of Beor engaged cautiously—four parallels noted; not used to collapse the biblical portrait into the Transjordan inscription.
- **Donkey episode:** theological prefiguration of Balaam’s own opened eyes; seeing motif (*rāʾâ* and synonyms) unifies chs. 22–24 (Ashley citing Alter).
- **Oracles:** four major blessings; fourth turns toward curse on Moab and hostile powers; election irreversible by human manipulation of venues, fees, or ritual—“The Lord can strike a mighty blow with a crooked stick.”
- **Star/scepter (24:17):** royal ANE imagery; long messianic reception (Targum Onkelos king/anointed; Bar-Kochba; Matt 2; Rev 22:16) recorded as reception history—Ashley: “glints” rather than proof that the Numbers author intended full messianism.
- **24:25 → 31:** “returned to his place” tantalizes; final form expects readers to remember when reaching Balaam’s counsel and death in the Midian war.

### Cole (NAC)

[[dennis-cole|Cole]] emphasizes the **reversal** dynamic (curse → blessing) and messianic overtones of 24:17 within a seven-cycle Numbers structure.

### NT and later Christian

In Jude, 2 Peter, and Revelation, Balaam functions as a primary archetype for false teachers—spiritual authority corrupted by gain and antinomian leading of God’s people. In Jude’s triad (Cain, Balaam, Korah), Balaam is the mercenary center.

### Kabbalistic (Zohar)

Pritzker Vol. IX (Balaq): Balaam’s mouth bridled so *Koh* speaks blessing; *Yanuqa*; death as serpent-sword—Sod layer. See [[numbers|Numbers]].

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In the New Testament (specifically Jude, 2 Peter, and Revelation), Balaam functions as a primary archetype for false teachers. He represents the synthesis of spiritual authority corrupted by financial greed and the deliberate leading of God's people into antinomian behavior, particularly sexual immorality and idolatry. In Jude's rhetorical structure, Balaam forms the central pillar of the triad of rebellion (Cain, Balaam, Korah), signifying apostasy driven by mercenary motives. Ashley’s NICOT layer keeps the **Numbers cycle** and the **NT greed archetype** distinct as reception stages rather than collapsing them into one character sketch.
