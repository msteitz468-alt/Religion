# Joshua son of Jehozadak

Joshua (Hebrew *yᵉhôšūaʿ*; *yēšûaʿ* in Ezra–Nehemiah; LXX *Iēsous*) was the first
high priest of the restored post-exilic community, partner with
[[zerubbabel|Zerubbabel]] in rebuilding the second temple. He is not to be confused
with [[joshua|Joshua son of Nun]], the conqueror of Canaan. Coverage derives from
[[pieter-verhoef|Verhoef]]'s NICOT commentary on [[haggai|Haggai]].

## Biographical Overview

Joshua was son of Jehozadak, who had been deported at the Exile and whose father was
chief priest; Verhoef infers Joshua "was born during the Exile between 585 and 560
BCE" and was "a descendant of Zadok and of Aaron." He came to Jerusalem with the first
caravan under Cyrus, ca. 537 BCE, and is introduced in Haggai and Zechariah as **"high
priest," "the man responsible for ecclesiastical affairs in the postexilic
community."** He is named eleven times as "son of Jehozadak."

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Haggai 1** — addressed with Zerubbabel and roused to rebuild the temple.
- **Haggai 2:10–14** — the priestly ruling on defilement is sought from the priests
  under his charge.
- **Ezra 3:2** — named (before Zerubbabel) in the rebuilding of the altar, a religious
  act.
- **Zechariah 3** — Joshua's investiture vision; unlike in Haggai, he figures
  prominently in Zechariah's eschatology.

## Interpretive Notes (Verhoef)

- **Was Joshua "demoted" in Haggai?** Some scholars detect a derogatory attitude
  toward Joshua in Hag. 2:10–14 and 2:20–23. **Verhoef disagrees:** Joshua is named
  second after Zerubbabel because of the "transitory status of the high priest" at
  that time (Koole), but is introduced as high priest and appears *before* Zerubbabel
  in the religious act of Ezra 3:2. The request for a torah-ruling (2:11) implies no
  reproof — it presupposes the priests' teaching authority.
- **The title "high priest."** Beyse deems the title premature, holding (with Galling)
  that it was formally instituted only after the temple's completion.
- **Absence from the Zerubbabel oracle.** Verhoef notes Joshua "does not figure in
  Haggai's eschatological expectation," unlike in Zechariah; he attributes this to
  Joshua's being already covered by the restoration-of-worship theme, "Cult and
  kingdom [being] integral parts of the one perspective."
