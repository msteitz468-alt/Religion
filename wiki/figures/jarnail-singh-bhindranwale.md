# Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale

The militant Sikh revivalist preacher, head of the **Damdami Taksal** seminary, whose fortification of
the [[amritsar|Golden Temple complex]] precipitated [[operation-blue-star|Operation Blue Star]] (June
1984) and his own death. Khushwant Singh (*A History of the Sikhs* Vol. 2) presents a deliberately
double-edged portrait: a genuine terrorist and "Hindu baiter," yet a figure "built up" by Congress
politicians for their own ends. This is among the most contested figures in modern Sikh history; the
page records Singh's account without adjudicating.

## Biographical Overview

"Born in the village of Rode in Faridkote district in 1947, [he] was the youngest son in a family of
eight children." His father, "a poor Brar Jat peasant farmer, Joginder Singh," donated him at age seven
to the **Damdami Taksal** at Chowk Mehta; he "received little formal education apart from learning
passages of the Granth by rote." On the death of the incumbent Kartar Singh in a 1971 car accident, the
congregation elected Jarnail Singh as Kartar Singh's favourite disciple over Kartar Singh's own son
Amrik Singh; the seminary's heads carry the title "Bhindranwale."

## The Preacher

Singh stresses the genuine positive impact of his early ministry: a "return to the spartan traditions of
the Khalsa Panth," touring villages, baptizing thousands, and persuading "adults in their thousands [to]
abjure liquor, tobacco and drugs." "Men not only saved money they had earlier squandered in
self-indulgence, but now worked longer hours on their lands." Unusually, "Bhindranwale made no
distinction between higher and lower castes." Singh's vivid description: "tall, wiry, long-nosed, with
deep-set, suspicious eyes… a beard that flowed down to his navel and he added to his image by wearing
across his chest a bandolier charged with bullets," carrying "a silver arrow of the kind carried by
Guru Gobind Singh and Maharajah Ranjit Singh." His rhetoric mocked Hindus as "*dhotian wale*" and Mrs
Gandhi as "*Bahmani*." Singh: "He well understood that hate was a stronger passion than love"; his hate
list ran apostates → Sant Nirankaris → Hindus. The notorious taped remark on Hindu numbers: "Divide 66
crores, then each Sikh gets only 35 Hindus… How do you say you are weak?"

## Co-optation by Congress

Singh repeatedly argues Bhindranwale was first built up as a political pawn: "Among the earliest to
discover Bhindranwale's potential… was Giani Zail Singh, his arch rival Darbara Singh, and through them,
Mrs Gandhi's politically ambitious son, Sanjay Gandhi." Per Kuldip Nayar, Sanjay approved his use against
the Akalis — "We would give him… money off and on." The Communist leader Harkishen Singh Surjeet's
testimony: "Mrs Gandhi created Bhindranwale… No other explanation can be there." See
[[indira-gandhi|Indira Gandhi]].

## Escalation to Violence

- National emergence on **13 April 1978**, a clash with the **Sant Nirankaris** at Amritsar in which 17
  men (mostly Akhand Kirtani Jatha) were killed while Bhindranwale "stayed behind in the Golden Temple."
  See [[nirankari|Nirankari]].
- The murders of Baba Gurbachan Singh Nirankari (1980) and the press magnate Lala Jagat Narain (1981) —
  Bhindranwale named in the FIR, protected by Zail Singh and released; he "expressed enthusiastic approval
  of their slayings."
- Move to **Guru Nanak Nivas** in the Golden Temple complex (July 1982), then into the upper stories of
  the **Akal Takht**, which he fortified with arms (trained by the cashiered Maj. Gen. Shahbeg Singh).
- A "hit list" period of bombings, the murder of DIG A. S. Atwal at the temple gate (April 1983), and the
  October 1983 bus massacre of Hindu passengers — the strategy, Singh argues, of "terrorizing Punjabi
  Hindus into leaving the Punjab" to engineer a "de facto Khalistan." On [[khalistan|Khalistan]] itself
  he was equivocal: "I have definitely not opposed it. But at the moment I can't say I support it."

## Death and Legacy

His "bullet-ridden" body, with those of Amrik Singh and General Shahbeg Singh, was found in the basement
of the Akal Takht on the morning of **6 June 1984** after [[operation-blue-star|Operation Blue Star]]
(Singh refutes the government's claim he had "taken his own life"). Singh's assessment of the aftermath:
the army action "gave the movement for Khalistan its first martyr," and "India had to pay a very heavy
price for the miscalculation, the heaviest being the assassination of the miscalculator, Prime Minister
Indira Gandhi." His later judgment: "The Hindu baiter, Bhindranwale, has become a martyred hero of
lumpen sections of Sikh society."
