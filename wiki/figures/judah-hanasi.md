# Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi

## Biographical Overview
Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi (Judah the Prince) was the chief leader of the Jewish community in Judea under Roman rule during the late 2nd and early 3rd centuries CE. He was a direct descendant of Hillel the Elder. As the *Nasi* (President of the Sanhedrin), he possessed immense religious authority and considerable political wealth, reportedly maintaining a close relationship with the Roman Emperor "Antoninus." 

## Primary Source Appearances
- **The Mishna**: Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi's most significant contribution is the final redaction of the Mishna (c. 200 CE). Realizing that the Oral Law was in danger of being forgotten due to the persecutions and dispersions following the Bar Kokhba revolt, he undertook the monumental task of organizing, editing, and committing the oral traditions of the Tannaim to a definitive, structured text.
- **Talmudic Aggadah**: The Talmud refers to him simply as "Rebbi" (Our Teacher) or "Rabbenu Hakadosh" (Our Holy Teacher). Narratives often highlight his immense wealth combined with extraordinary personal piety and Torah scholarship, noting that "from the days of Moses until Rebbi, we have not seen Torah and greatness combined in one place" (Gittin 59a).

## Tradition-Specific Reception
**Judaism**: He is universally recognized as the savior of the Oral Law. By codifying the disputes and rulings of the earlier sages into the Six Orders of the Mishna, he provided the foundation upon which all subsequent Rabbinic Judaism was built (including both the Babylonian and Jerusalem Talmuds). His editorial decisions—such as whose opinion to record anonymously (which often determined the accepted halacha)—shaped Jewish practice permanently.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In the history of the Talmud's formation (as outlined by Unterman), Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi represents the culmination of the Tannaitic period. His redaction of the Mishna closed the era of the Tannaim and opened the era of the Amoraim, whose task became the analysis and elucidation of his text.
