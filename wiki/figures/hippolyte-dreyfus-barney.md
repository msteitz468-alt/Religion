# Hippolyte Dreyfus-Barney

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 11127–11150.*

## Biographical Overview

Prominent early French Bahá'í and scholar. An advocate at the Paris Court of Appeal, he became a Bahá'í in 1900 and soon mastered Persian and Arabic so as to be able to translate Bahá'í writings into French (lines 11129–11134). A stream of publications followed from 1904, such that by 1913 French readers had available a substantial portion of Bahá'u'lláh's major works (lines 11135–11138).

He published an introductory work, *Essai sur le béhaïsme* (1908), which remains in use (lines 11139–11141).

## Family and Collaboration

He married [[laura-dreyfus-barney|Laura Barney]] in 1911, and together they travelled widely to promote the Faith (lines 11141–11143). They were among the prominent Western Bahá'ís whom [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] consulted regarding the future of the Faith in 1922 (lines 11144–11147).

## Honours

Shoghi Effendi referred to Hippolyte as one of the [[disciples-of-abdu-l-baha|Disciples of 'Abdu'l-Bahá]] (lines 11147–11149).
