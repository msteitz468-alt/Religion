# Muhammad

**Muhammad ibn Abdallah** (c. 570 – 632 CE) is the prophet of [[islam|Islam]], believed by Muslims to
be the final messenger (*rasul*) of God and the **Seal of the Prophets** (*khatam al-nabiyyin*),
through whom the [[quran|Qur'an]] was revealed. He is at once the recipient of revelation and — through
his *sunna* (normative example) recorded in **hadith** — the second source of Islamic law and the most
imitated human being in the tradition. Islamic piety distinguishes sharply between the Qur'an (God's
verbatim word) and Muhammad's own words and deeds: he is venerated as a man, not divinized.

## Biographical Overview (Traditional / Sira)

The biographical portrait derives chiefly from the Qur'an, the *hadith*, and the *sira* literature
(notably Ibn Ishaq, via Ibn Hisham, and al-Waqidi) — sources composed generations after his death,
which historical-critical scholarship treats cautiously (see below).

- **Origins**: born in **Mecca** c. 570 into the **Banu Hashim** clan of the **Quraysh** —
  a clan of declining fortune, eclipsed by the Banu Umayya. Orphaned young (a posthumous child whose
  grandfather then died), he was raised by his uncle **Abu Talib** and excluded by custom from
  paternal inheritance.
- **Merchant and marriage**: he traded on Syrian caravan routes and entered the employ of the widow
  merchant **Khadija**, who proposed marriage to him (he c. 25, she c. 40). The marriage was
  monogamous until her death and central to his early life.
- **First revelation (610)**: while meditating in a cave on **Mount Hira** during Ramadan, he
  received the command *Iqra!* ("Recite!") and the first verses of Q 96 ("Recite in the name of your
  Lord who created…"), traditionally delivered by the angel **Gabriel (Jibril)**. **Khadija** became
  the first to believe — "the first Muslim" — followed by his young cousin **Ali**. A period of
  daunting silence (*fatra*) followed before revelations resumed.
- **Meccan mission (610–622)**: a decade of preaching strict monotheism (*tawhid*) and the coming
  Judgment against Quraysh polytheism, met with mounting hostility; the "Year of Sorrow" (c. 619) saw
  the deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib. To this period tradition assigns the **[[isra-and-miraj|Night Journey and Ascension]]** (*Isra* and *Mi'raj*, Q 17:1) — from Mecca to Jerusalem to the heavens and back in a
  single night — later memorialized by the [[jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]].
- **The Hijra (622)**: emigration to **Medina** (Yathrib), the event that begins the
  Islamic lunar calendar (AH) and turns the movement from a persecuted sect into a polity. The
  "Constitution of Medina" organized a community of believers alongside the city's tribes,
  including its Jewish clans.
- **Medinan period (622–632)**: revelations now longer, communal, legal, and military; armed
  conflict with Mecca (Badr 624, Uhud 625, the Trench 627), the expulsion/killing of Medinan Jewish
  groups, treaties (Hudaybiyya 628), and the bloodless conquest of **Mecca (630)**, with the cleansing
  of the **Ka'ba** of idols.
- **Death (632)**: he died in Medina after a final pilgrimage, leaving no surviving son and no
  undisputed succession arrangement — the seed of the [[sunni-islam|Sunni]]/[[shia-islam|Shia]] split.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **The Qur'an** addresses and describes him as warner, herald, and messenger (e.g. Q 17:105–6;
  33:40, the "Seal of the Prophets"); it insists he works no miracle except the Qur'an itself
  (its *i'jaz*, inimitability). The "asbab al-nuzul" (occasions of revelation) tie many passages to
  events in his life.
- **Hadith / [[sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]]** and the other collections preserve his *sunna* —
  the basis of law and the most detailed source for his biography.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

- **Seal of the Prophets**: the last and confirming prophet in the Abrahamic line, restoring the pure
  monotheism of **[[abraham|Abraham]]** (whom the Qur'an makes the builder, with Ishmael, of the
  Ka'ba) after the perceived corruptions of earlier communities. [[al-tabari|al-Tabari]] reads
  Muhammad as the successor and confirmation of Abraham.
- **The "beautiful model"** (*uswa hasana*, Q 33:21): his conduct is normative, generating the entire
  edifice of *sunna* and *fiqh*.
- **In Sufism**, his inner reality is elevated to the **Muhammadan Reality** (*al-haqiqa
  al-Muhammadiyya*) and identified with the [[al-insan-al-kamil|Perfect Man]] — see [[ibn-arabi|Ibn
  Arabi]]. Shi'ism reads the "light" verses as joining the Prophet and the Imams in a single divine
  light (see [[jafar-al-sadiq|Ja'far al-Sadiq]]).

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Source problem**: the earliest *sira* (Ibn Ishaq) postdates Muhammad by ~120 years and survives
  only in later recension; the hadith corpus was sifted for authenticity by Muslim scholars
  (*isnad* criticism) and is treated skeptically by much Western scholarship. The standard mid-century
  critical biography is **[[william-montgomery-watt|W. Montgomery Watt]]**'s *Muhammad at Mecca* /
  *Muhammad at Medina*, which reads his career in socio-economic terms while accepting his sincerity;
  revisionist scholars (Wansbrough, Crone & Cook's *Hagarism*) have questioned the standard narrative;
  most historians accept a core (a 7th-century Arabian prophet, the Qur'an, the move to Medina) while
  disputing details.
- **Western/Christian reception**: medieval Christendom cast him as impostor or heresiarch — the
  "pseudo-prophet" of **[[robert-of-ketton|Robert of Ketton]]**'s Latin Qur'an title; Dante placed
  him among the schismatics. This polemical portrait is itself a major theme of Qur'an reception
  history (see [[lawrence-quran-a-biography|Lawrence]]).
- **The "Satanic Verses"**: the disputed tradition that Muhammad briefly conceded the intercession of
  three Meccan goddesses before the verses were abrogated (Q 53; cf. 22:52) — accepted by some early
  Muslim historians, rejected by most later tradition.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

As the human locus of revelation, Muhammad anchors all Islamic exegesis: *tafsir bi'l-ma'thur*
proceeds from the Qur'an to *his* explanatory *sunna* ([[al-tabari|al-Tabari]]); the occasions-of-
revelation genre reconstructs his biography to fix meaning; Sufi and Shi'ite exegesis elevate his
inner reality. He is also the central figure of the textual-history debates that occupy modern
Quranic studies (the collection of the Qur'an after his death; see [[quran|the Qur'an]]).

## Funerary Practice and Death-Related Teaching (Sahih al-Bukhari Evidence)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 2, Book 23 (Al-Jana'iz) and Book 18. M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Preparing and Shrouding the Dead

Muhammad's instructions for washing the deceased are transmitted by Um Atiyya al-Ansariya (Nos. 344–353): wash an odd number of times with water and Sidr (lote-tree leaves); apply camphor at the last washing; begin from the right side and start with the parts washed in ablution; divide hair into three braids. Muhammad himself was shrouded in three white Yemenite cotton cloths with no shirt or turban (Aisha, Nos. 354, 361–363).

**Special case for the pilgrim in ihram** (Nos. 355–358): A man died at Arafat while in the state of ihram. Muhammad instructed: "Wash him with water and Sidr and shroud him in two pieces of cloth and neither perfume him nor cover his head, for Allah will resurrect him on the Day of Resurrection and he will be saying 'Labbaik.'" The ritual state at death is preserved into resurrection.

### The Funeral Prayer

Muhammad established four Takbirs as the structure of salat al-janaza (Nos. 337, 403–404, 417–418). Al-Fatiha is recited (No. 419). He prayed in absentia over An-Najashi (king of Ethiopia) on the day of his death, leading the companions in rows at the Musalla — establishing that funeral prayer may be offered over an absent deceased (No. 406). He also prayed at a grave after the fact when he discovered a person had been buried without his knowledge (Nos. 339, 407–408, 421, 424).

### Rules for Martyrs: Uhud

At Uhud the martyrs were NOT washed, NOT given funeral prayer, and buried with their blood (Nos. 427–431, 436). When two martyrs were placed together, Muhammad asked which knew more Qur'an and placed that one first: "I will be a witness on these on the Day of Resurrection."

### Muhammad's Own Grief at Deaths

A consistent pattern across Book 23: Muhammad wept at deaths and validated grief while prohibiting performative wailing (*niyaha*).

- **Son Ibrahim** (No. 390): Muhammad took Ibrahim in his arms as he died, kissed him, and wept. When asked if even he wept, he replied: "O Ibn 'Auf, this is mercy." He said: "The eyes are shedding tears and the heart is grieved, and we will not say except what pleases our Lord, O Ibrahim! Indeed we are grieved by your separation."
- **Sad bin Ubada** (No. 391): The Prophet wept; when all wept with him, he said: "Allah does not punish for shedding tears, nor for the grief of the heart but he punishes or bestows His Mercy because of this" — pointing to his tongue.
- **Deaths at Battle of Mu'ta** (No. 338): "The eyes of Allah's Apostle were full of tears" upon hearing of the deaths of Zaid, Jafar, and Ibn Rawaha.

**Prohibiting wailing**: Nos. 382, 384–385: "He who slaps his cheeks, tears his clothes and follows the ways and traditions of the Days of Ignorance is not one of us." **Patience as the standard**: "Verily, the patience is at the first stroke of a calamity" (No. 372, said to a weeping woman at a grave).

## Physical Description and Character (Bukhari, Book 56)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 4, Book 56 (Nos. 744–806). M. Muhsin Khan trans.*

### Physical Appearance

Multiple Companions describe Muhammad's appearance with consistent details:

- **Height**: neither very tall nor very short — medium (Nos. 744, 791, 795)
- **Complexion**: rosy/light brown (*azhar al-lawn*); specifically *not* white to the point of
  pallor, *not* dark (No. 747, 769)
- **Hair**: reached the earlobes or between the earlobes and shoulders; neither straight nor
  very curly (No. 769). At his death he had fewer than twenty white hairs on his head and in
  his beard (Nos. 744, 748)
- **Build**: broad-chested; fine body hair from chest to navel; no hair on the trunk (Nos. 779,
  795)
- **Hand**: the coolest, most pleasant-smelling hand of anything touched (No. 752)
- **Sweat**: smelled better than musk, even while sleeping; sweat collected in a jar by Umm
  Sulaim for perfume (Nos. 753, 754)
- **Face and forehead**: large forehead; long, arched eyebrows; a line between them (No. 770);
  gap between his front teeth (No. 795)

### The Five Names

Muhammad named five of his own names in prophetic speech (Book 56, No. 732):
1. **Muhammad** (the praised)
2. **Ahmad** (the most praised)
3. **al-Mahi** (the eraser — "by whom Allah will erase infidelity")
4. **al-Hashir** (the gatherer — "at whose feet the people will be gathered at the last hour")
5. **al-'Aqib** (the last — "after whom there is no prophet")

Quran 61:6 records Jesus foretelling the coming of "Ahmad" — cited as the Quranic
cross-reference to this fifth name.

### Character Traits

**Generosity**: "never said no to a request" (Abu Said al-Khudri, No. 762).
**Forbearance**: never struck a woman or a servant; never personally avenged himself on anyone
— only when Allah's ordinances were violated (Aisha, No. 770).
**The Last-Brick Similitude**: parable for his own role in prophethood (No. 735):
> "My example and the example of the prophets before me is that of a person who built a house
> nicely and beautifully except for a place of one brick. The people went around it and wondered
> at its beauty, but said: 'Would that this brick be put in its place!' So I am that brick and
> I am the last (end) of the prophets."

### Miracles Attested in Bukhari (Book 56)

Approximately 14 explicit miracles are narrated in this section:

1. **Food multiplication**: a small amount of dates feeds many (Nos. 780, 781)
2. **Water multiplication**: water flows from between his fingers (No. 779); water fills
   vessels from a small amount touched by his hands
3. **Healing by touch**: wiping the eye of Ali at Khaibar (No. 783)
4. **Split moon**: Q 54:1 "The moon was split" — the Companions saw it over Hira (No. 830)
5. **Prophetic speech understood by animals**: a camel complained to him (No. 776)
6. **Tree weeping**: the palm trunk he had leaned against wept when a new pulpit was built;
   he embraced it and it quieted (No. 783)
7. **Spring of water from beneath his fingers at Hudaibiyya**: No. 777
8. **Night illumination**: a stick lighted like a lamp for two Companions in the night
   (No. 793)
9. **Heraclius's letter episode**: recognition of prophethood by a foreign ruler
10. **Suraqa's horse**: hooves of pursuing Suraqa sank into the ground until he asked for
    peace (Book 57, No. 5 cross-reference)

### The 'Ashura Fast and Islam's Relation to Judaism

**No. 592** (Abu Musa): The Prophet found Jews fasting on 'Ashura; they said it was the day
Moses was saved from Pharaoh. The Prophet said, "We are more entitled to Moses than you," and
fasted, and told the Muslims to fast. (Compare: [[moses]] in Bukhari.)

## The Prophet's Death (Bukhari, Book 59)

*Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, Vol. 5, Book 59 (Al-Maghazi), Nos. 712–743.*

### The Poisoned Meat Narrative

The woman of Khaibar who offered the Prophet a poisoned roasted sheep is narrated (No. 713):
the foreleg spoke and warned him; the Prophet ate a morsel and detected the poison. He summoned
the woman; she confessed. One Companion (Bishr bin Al-Bara) who had eaten fully died. The
Prophet survived but the poison's effect is cited as contributing to his final illness: he told
Aisha in his last days, "I still feel the pain caused by the food I ate at Khaibar" (No. 713).

### Last Days and Death

The most extended death narrative in Bukhari (Nos. 715–730, narrated primarily by Aisha):

- **"Calamity of Thursday"** (No. 716–717): During his final illness, the Prophet asked for a
  writing material to write something that would prevent the community from going astray. Umar
  said, "The Prophet is seriously ill and you have the Qur'an; so the Book of Allah is
  sufficient for us." Arguments broke out; the Prophet told them to leave ("Leave me as I am").
  Shia and Sunni traditions differ sharply on the interpretation of this episode.
- **Final words**: "With the highest companion (*al-Rafiq al-A'la*)" — his last audible
  phrase, understood as a choice to leave the earthly world (No. 730).
- **Abu Bakr's announcement** (No. 333): "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, then Muhammad is dead,
  but whoever worshipped Allah, then Allah is Alive and shall never die" — reciting Q 3:144.
- **Estate**: he left only a white mule, weapons, and the charitable land (No. 741–743).
  Fatima's inheritance claim on this charitable land is the Fadak controversy — see
  [[fadak-inheritance]].

## Reception: Dante's *Inferno*

In [[inferno|Inferno]] 28, Dante places Muhammad (with Ali) among the **sowers of
discord and schism**, cloven and continually re-wounded. Kirkpatrick notes the
medieval — and historically false — construal behind this placement: Muhammad was
"thought to have been a Nestorian Christian before arriving at his own religious
vision," and so was classified as a Christian *schismatic* rather than the founder
of a separate religion. The wiki records this as a reception fact about the Latin
West's polemical imagination, not as a historical claim about the Prophet; cf. the
[[robert-of-ketton|Robert of Ketton]] "pseudo-prophet" tradition.
