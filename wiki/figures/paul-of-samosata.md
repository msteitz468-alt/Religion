# Paul of Samosata

Bishop of Antioch, deposed by a synod there in 268, and the most thoroughgoing exponent of [[adoptionism|adoptionist (Dynamic Monarchian)]] theology. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. V, VI.

## Teaching

Paul held a "nakedly unitarian" theology. He gave "the name of God to the Father Who created all things, that of Son to the mere man, and that of Spirit to the grace which indwelt the apostles," applying the title "Word" only to God's commandment and ordinance — not to a distinct subsistent Person. Of Christ he said "Mary did not bear the Word… what she bore was a man equal to us, but superior in all things as a result of holy spirit"; the Word's relation to Jesus was an "indwelling" or "participation," a "quality" (*poiotēs*) present in him. Kelly classes him as a "strict unitarian, denying any subsistence or personality to the Word."

## The Synod of 268 and *Homoousios*

The bishops who condemned Paul were Origenists who insisted the Word was a real *ousia*, "a real Person distinct from the Father." Notably, **the synod rejected the term *homoousios*** for the Son — Kelly suggests Paul may himself have used it to protest against the judges' conception of distinct *ousiai*. This rejection became an embarrassment at [[arian-controversy|Nicaea]] (325), when the same word was adopted as the badge of orthodoxy. See [[homoousion]].
