# Enoch

## Biographical Overview
Enoch is the seventh patriarch from Adam, the father of Methuselah and great-grandfather of Noah. In Genesis 5, his life is summarized with mysterious brevity: "Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him." This assumption into heaven without dying made Enoch a central figure for later Jewish mysticism and apocalyptic literature.

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Genesis 5:21-24**: The brief genealogical account of his translation to heaven.
- **1 Enoch**: A massive composite pseudepigraphal work in which Enoch is the visionary recipient of heavenly secrets, the scribe of righteousness, and the proclaimer of doom to the fallen angels (Watchers).
- **Jude 14-15**: Explicitly quotes 1 Enoch 1:9, introducing it as a prophecy by "Enoch, the seventh from Adam" concerning the coming judgment of the Lord with his holy myriads.
- **Hebrews 11:5**: Lists Enoch in the "Hall of Faith," stating he was taken up so that he should not see death because he had pleased God.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
In Second Temple Judaism and early Christianity, Enoch functions as the archetype of the apocalyptic visionary. Because he traversed the heavenly realms, he serves as the guarantor of cosmic secrets regarding judgment, the origins of evil (the Watchers), and the eschatological vindication of the righteous. In Jude, his prophecy is utilized as authoritative scripture to announce the doom of the false teachers. In 1 Peter 3:19, Christ's ascension and proclamation to the "spirits in prison" is modeled typologically on Enoch's mission to the fallen angels.
