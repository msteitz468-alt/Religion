# Isis

**Isis** (Egyptian *Aset*) is the great goddess of **magic, motherhood, healing, and protection** — the most
important goddess of [[egyptian-religion|Egypt]] and, in time, of the whole Roman world. Daughter of
[[geb|Geb]] and [[nut|Nut]], devoted wife of [[osiris|Osiris]], and mother of [[horus|Horus]].

## Myths in Detail

**The faithful wife and the restoration of Osiris.** Isis's central role is in the [[osiris|Osiris myth]]:
when Set murdered and scattered her husband, **Isis searched the world**, gathered the pieces, and by her
unrivaled magic **revived him** enough to conceive their son — the supreme act of devotion that made her the
patron of love, marriage, and resurrection.

**The hidden child Horus.** Pregnant and pursued by [[set|Set]], Isis hid in the **papyrus marshes of the
Delta** and there bore and raised the infant **[[horus|Horus]]** in secret, protecting him from Set's
malice and from the dangers of the swamp. In one famous episode the child Horus is **stung by a scorpion**,
and Isis's desperate cries halt the sun-barque of [[ra|Ra]] until [[thoth|Thoth]] comes down and gives her
the spells to heal him — establishing Isis as the great **healer and protector of children**, invoked in
countless magical spells.

**Mistress of the secret name.** It was Isis who, by guile, forced the aged [[ra|Ra]] to reveal his **secret
name** (see [[ra|Ra]]), gaining for herself and her son a share of the supreme creative power — "**Great of
Magic**," whose word commands even the greatest gods.

## The Universal Goddess

Isis's cult spread far beyond Egypt: in the Greco-Roman world she became a **savior-goddess of boundless
mercy** — "queen of heaven," mistress of fate, who promised her initiates rebirth and a blessed afterlife
(see [[cult-of-isis-roman|the Roman cult of Isis]]). Her image as the merciful mother enthroned with the
infant Horus on her lap is a famous comparandum for later Marian iconography (the Madonna and Child),
recorded without asserting dependence.
