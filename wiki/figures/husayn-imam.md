# Ḥusayn ibn 'Alí (Third Imám)

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 17545–17580 and 17833–17835.*

## Biographical Overview

Ḥusayn (626–680 CE) was the third of the Shí'í [[imams|Imáms]] (l. 17545–17547). He was the second son of 'Alí and Fáṭima (the Prophet Muḥammad's daughter). Having refused to pledge his allegiance to the Umayyad caliph Yazíd, Ḥusayn, with a small group of supporters, was confronted by Umayyad forces at [[karbala|Karbalá]]. All the fighting men were killed apart from Ḥusayn's son 'Alí (the future Fourth Imám), and the women and children were captured (l. 17548–17556).

## Significance in Shí'í Islam

Ḥusayn's death is regarded by Shí'ís as sacrificial [[martyrdom|martyrdom]] for the cause of truth against the forces of impiety (l. 17556–17558). It is annually commemorated with great emotional fervour during the holy month of Muharram and "continues to inspire Shí'ís to sacrificial action" (l. 17560–17564). He is expected to return following the advent of the Mahdí (l. 17564–17566).

## Reception in the Bahá'í Faith

[[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]] eulogized Ḥusayn, claimed to be his [[return|return]] and identified himself with him, and promised that God would soon "torment" those who had waged war against him — a prophecy Bahá'ís associate with the downfall of the Ottoman sultans and the Caliphate (l. 17566–17575). [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] referred to Ḥusayn's uniqueness and stated he had been endowed with a special "grace and power" among the Imáms (l. 17576–17579).

In the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Bahá'u'lláh cites the life of the Imám Ḥusayn as "a vivid example of the sufferings of God's chosen ones" (l. 18866–18868), and the example of Bahá'í [[letters-of-the-living|Letters of the Living]] dying at Ṭabarsí echoed Karbalá for Bábís.

[[mulla-husayn|Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í]] was regarded by some Bábís as the "return" of the Imám Ḥusayn (l. 17682–17683).
