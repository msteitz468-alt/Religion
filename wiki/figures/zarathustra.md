# Zarathustra (Zoroaster)

**Zarathustra** — Greek **Zoroaster** — is the prophet and founder of [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]],
known to history above all through his own hymns, the **[[gathas|Gathas]]**.

## Biographical Overview

Almost everything is uncertain. The **language** of the Gathas places him around **1200–1000 BCE** in
eastern Iran/Central Asia, though Greek and later Zoroastrian traditions give much later (and conflicting)
dates — "258 years before Alexander" is one influential but problematic reckoning. He was a **priest**
(*zaotar*) of the old Indo-Iranian religion who, by his own account, received a revelation of **Ahura
Mazda** and preached a reform: devotion to the one Wise Lord, the choice of **Asha** (Truth) over the
**Lie**, and moral responsibility. The Gathas show him meeting rejection before winning a royal patron
(Kavi Vishtaspa).

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]]** — the prophet who revealed the true worship of Ahura Mazda; later
  tradition surrounds his life with miracle and cosmic significance.
- **Greco-Roman** — "Zoroaster" became a legendary **magus**, sage, and supposed master of astrology and
  the occult (the *Magi* of Matthew 2 reflect this Persian priestly milieu).
- **Modern** — a touchstone for comparative religion and (loosely, via Nietzsche's *Thus Spoke
  Zarathustra*) for Western philosophy, though that work bears little relation to the historical prophet.

## Significance and Historicity

Zarathustra is among the earliest named individual religious founders, and arguably the first to frame
religion as a **cosmic moral choice** between good and evil with an eschatological resolution — the
intuition whose later diffusion is examined in
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]]. His historical existence is
generally accepted; almost every detail is debated.
