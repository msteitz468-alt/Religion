# Zarathustra (Zoroaster)

**Zarathustra** — Greek **Zoroaster** — is the prophet and founder of [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]],
known to history above all through his own hymns, the **[[gathas|Gathas]]**.

## Biographical Overview

Almost everything is uncertain. The **language** of the Gathas places him around **1200–1000 BCE** in
eastern Iran/Central Asia, though Greek and later Zoroastrian traditions give much later (and conflicting)
dates — "258 years before Alexander" is one influential but problematic reckoning. He was a **priest**
(*zaotar*) of the old Indo-Iranian religion who, by his own account, received a revelation of
**[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]** and preached a reform: devotion to the one Wise Lord, the choice of
**[[asha-druj|Asha]]** (Truth) over the **Lie**, and moral responsibility. The Gathas show him
meeting rejection before winning a royal patron (Kavi Vishtaspa).

Per [[boyce-zoroastrians-beliefs-and-practices|Boyce]], tradition holds that revelation came to
Zarathustra at **age thirty**, by a river at a spring festival, when the radiant being **Vohu
Manah** ("Good Purpose") led him into the presence of Ahura Mazda and the six
[[amesha-spentas|Holy Immortals]]. He is said to have been **married three times**, and (in
tradition) to have died violently in old age by a pagan priest's dagger. Boyce's reconstruction
casts him as a **reformer working from within** the old Ahuric priesthood — not inventing a religion
ex nihilo, but recasting inherited materials (asha, the Ahuras, the *yasna*) around the one good
God and a [[the-three-times|linear, bounded history]] ending in
[[frashokereti|Frashokereti]].

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]]** — the prophet who revealed the true worship of Ahura Mazda; later
  tradition surrounds his life with miracle and cosmic significance.
- **Greco-Roman** — "Zoroaster" became a legendary **magus**, sage, and supposed master of astrology and
  the occult (the *Magi* of Matthew 2 reflect this Persian priestly milieu).
- **Modern** — a touchstone for comparative religion and (loosely, via Nietzsche's *Thus Spoke
  Zarathustra*) for Western philosophy, though that work bears little relation to the historical prophet.

## Significance and Historicity

Zarathustra is among the earliest named individual religious founders, and arguably the first to frame
religion as a **cosmic moral choice** between good and evil with an eschatological resolution — the
intuition whose later diffusion is examined in
[[zoroastrian-influence-on-abrahamic-eschatology|the influence page]]. His historical existence is
generally accepted; almost every detail is debated.

## The *Manthran* and His Reception (Rose)

[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]] stresses that Zarathushtra is, in the
[[gathas|Gathas]], a **manthran** — a maker of inspired sacred utterance (*manthra*),
from the root *man* "to think" — not a "prophet" in the Near-Eastern sense, but one whose
poetic vision crystallizes "things as they are." Tellingly, **for most of the religion's
history the focus was not on Zarathushtra but on [[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]**, who
*generates* the manthras; there is **no mention of Zarathushtra in any Old Persian or
Sasanian royal inscription**, and no ancient iconography of him.

- **The name** is contested: in Old Avestan it is most plausibly "**owner of old
  camels**," though the Greek *Zoroastres* was read as "**star-diviner**" (*astrothutes*)
  and later, popularly, "He of the Golden Light."
- **Single author?** Most Zoroastrians and many philologists hold that one mind composed
  the Gathas; some philologists now treat Zarathushtra as the first to *"make heard"* the
  poems rather than their sole author, emphasizing the long **oral transmission**.
- **From manthran to mythic prophet**: in Middle Persian texts (Denkard Book 7, the
  [[bundahishn|Bundahishn]], the *Wizidagiha-i Zadspram*, the New Persian *Zardosht
  Nameh*) his life is magnified with pre-ordained birth, miracles, and the cure of
  Vishtaspa's horse — the hagiography that sustained lay devotion.
- **Modern reception**: Christian missionary attacks (John Wilson) on his credibility
  pushed nineteenth-century [[parsis|Parsis]] to reframe him as a "prophetic
  revolutionary"; modern icons derive from a Sasanian relief of **[[mitra|Mithra]]** at
  Taq-e Bostan; and Nietzsche's *Also Sprach Zarathustra* made the name a Western cipher
  (bearing little relation to the historical figure). Zoroastrians honor him as **Asho
  Zarathushtra** ("endowed with [[asha-druj|asha]]"), a paradigm to emulate — the stance
  declared in the **Fravarane** creed (Y 12).
