# Hera (Juno)

**Hera** (Roman **Juno**) is the **queen of the gods**, wife and sister of [[zeus|Zeus]], and goddess of
**marriage, women, and childbirth**. Daughter of [[cronus|Cronus]] and Rhea.

## Myths and Character

Famous for her **jealous wrath** against Zeus's lovers and their offspring — she persecutes Heracles,
Leto, Io, and Semele. Yet she is above all the dignified guardian of **lawful marriage** and the bond she
herself embodies. Mother of [[ares|Ares]], [[hephaestus|Hephaestus]], Hebe, and Eileithyia.

## Cult and Roman Identification

Major sanctuaries at Argos and Samos; a patron of cities. As **Juno** she is far more than Zeus's
consort: a great independent Roman goddess — **Juno Regina** (queen, of the Capitoline Triad with
[[zeus|Jupiter]] and [[athena|Minerva]]), **Juno Lucina** (childbirth), **Juno Moneta** (the warning
goddess, by whose temple Rome minted coins — hence "money"). Every woman had her protecting *iuno* as men
had a *genius*.

## Myths in Detail

**The sacred marriage.** [[zeus|Zeus]] wooed Hera (his own sister) by taking the form of a bedraggled
cuckoo, which she sheltered at her breast — whereupon he resumed his form. Their wedding was the divine model
of marriage; on it the earth produced the golden apples of the Hesperides, and the union was renewed each
year in her cult. But the marriage is stormy: Zeus's endless infidelities make Hera the goddess of
**jealous, vengeful wrath**, turned less on Zeus than on his lovers and their children.

**Her persecutions.** She hounded **Io**, whom Zeus turned into a heifer to hide her; Hera set the
hundred-eyed **Argus** to watch the cow, and when [[hermes|Hermes]] killed him, she sent a maddening gadfly
to drive Io across the world. She drove **Leto**, pregnant with [[apollo|Apollo]] and [[artemis|Artemis]],
from land to land, forbidding any place to receive her. She tricked **Semele** into asking Zeus to appear in
his full glory, which incinerated her (the unborn [[dionysus|Dionysus]] was saved). Above all she pursued
**[[heracles|Heracles]]**, Zeus's son by Alcmene: she sent serpents to his cradle, delayed his birth, and
later struck him with the madness that led to the **Twelve Labors** — "Heracles" ironically means "glory of
Hera."

**The Judgment of Paris and the binding of Zeus.** Hera, [[athena|Athena]], and [[aphrodite|Aphrodite]] each
claimed the golden **Apple of Discord** ("to the fairest"); the Trojan prince **Paris** judged Aphrodite the
winner (bribed with Helen), earning Hera's undying hatred of Troy — a divine engine of the
[[homer|Trojan War]]. Once Hera even led a revolt that **chained Zeus**, until the Hundred-Hander Briareus
freed him; in revenge Zeus hung her from Olympus with anvils tied to her feet.
