# The Kitchen God (Zào Jūn)

The **Kitchen God** (**Zào Jūn**, the "Stove Lord") is the most important **household deity** of
[[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]] — the resident **divine official of the family**, the lowest
but most intimate rank of the **[[celestial-bureaucracy|celestial bureaucracy]]**. His paper image sits above
the **stove**, the heart of the home, from where he **watches the household all year**, noting its members'
virtues and faults.

His defining role comes at the **end of the lunar year**: the Kitchen God **ascends to Heaven to report the
family's conduct to the [[jade-emperor|Jade Emperor]]**, who rewards or punishes the household accordingly. So,
a few days before the New Year, families perform the **"sending off" rite** — offering sweets and **smearing
his image's mouth with honey or sticky malt-sugar**, either to **sweeten his report** or to **glue his lips
shut** so he can say nothing bad — then **burn the image** (sometimes with a paper horse) to send him up in the
smoke, and paste a **new image** to welcome him back on New Year's Eve. A common folk legend makes him a man
who, having mistreated and abandoned his faithful wife, later met her again as a beggar, and in shame **threw
himself into the kitchen fire**, whereupon he was deified. The Kitchen God makes divine surveillance and the
reach of the heavenly bureaucracy **immediate and domestic** — Heaven keeps an officer in every kitchen.
