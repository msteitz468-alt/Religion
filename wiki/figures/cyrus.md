Cyrus II ("the Great"), founder of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, conqueror of Babylon (539
BCE), and — in the biblical presentation — the Gentile king whose decree (538 BCE) authorized
the Judean exiles to return and rebuild the Jerusalem temple. He is the only non-Israelite in
the Hebrew Bible called YHWH's **anointed** (*māšîaḥ*, Isa 45:1). He stands at the hinge of the
biblical story between exile and restoration, closing the [[1-2-chronicles|Hebrew canon]] and
opening [[ezra-nehemiah|Ezra–Nehemiah]].

## Biographical Overview

Cyrus is historically attested from Persian, Babylonian, and Greek sources (the Cyrus Cylinder,
the Nabonidus Chronicle, Herodotus, Xenophon's *Cyropaedia*). He united the Medes and Persians,
overthrew the Neo-Babylonian empire of Nabonidus in 539 BCE, and pursued a policy of repatriating
displaced peoples and restoring local cults — the general policy the biblical decree of return
particularizes for Judah. The biblical texts present his edict as the fulfillment of prophecy
(Jer 25/29's seventy years; Isaiah's naming of Cyrus). Scholars distinguish the **historical**
Cyrus (a Persian king following imperial custom, honoring Marduk in the Cyrus Cylinder) from the
**biblical** Cyrus (an instrument of YHWH), and the biblical decree's Hebrew form (Ezra 1;
2 Chr 36) from the Aramaic memorandum (Ezra 6) — recorded here as distinct evidence classes, not
harmonized in wiki voice.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **2 Chronicles 36:22–23**: the decree of return — the **final words of Chronicles and of the
  Hebrew Masoretic canon**. Cyrus, "stirred up" by YHWH, proclaims that YHWH "has appointed
  (*paqad*) me to build him a house in Jerusalem," and sends the people up with a benediction.
- **Ezra 1:1–4 / 6:3–5**: the decree repeated (overlapping 2 Chr 36:22–23 verbatim), and the
  Aramaic building authorization with the return of the temple vessels.
- **Isaiah 44:24–45:7**: Cyrus named ~150 years in advance (on the traditional reading) as
  YHWH's "shepherd" (44:28) who will say of Jerusalem "let it be built," and as YHWH's
  "anointed" (*māšîaḥ*, 45:1) whose right hand YHWH grasps — though "you do not know me" (45:4–5).
- **Daniel 1:21; 6:28; 10:1**: Cyrus as the horizon of the exile's end within the Daniel narrative.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Second Temple Judaism / the Chronicler**: Cyrus is the agent of the promised restoration, the
  Gentile monarch whose proclamation ends the exile in the [[davidic-covenant|Davidic]] hope's
  darkest hour. The overlap of 2 Chr 36:22–23 with Ezra 1:1–3 functions as a deliberate canonical
  cross-reference. See [[persians|Persians]] for the imperial reception.
- **Christianity**: Cyrus is read as a type of Christ (the "anointed" liberator who frees the
  captives and rebuilds the house of God) and as the classic proof-text that God rules the
  Gentile nations for the sake of his people (cf. Isa 45; Rom 11:29).
- **[[peter-leithart|Leithart]] (Brazos Chronicles, 2019) — "Cyrus as new David"**: Leithart's
  signature reading. The Chronicler's Judges-like cycle of faithfulness and apostasy leads the
  reader to expect a new David after the Saul-like [[josiah|Josiah]]; instead "David" arises from
  Persia. Leithart argues the Chronicler makes Cyrus speak *like David*: he "confesses Yahweh the
  God of heaven," claims to be **"appointed" (*paqad*) to build the house** — "language used for
  David's organization of temple personnel" (1 Chr 23–27) — and sends the exiles home with a
  benediction ("May YHWH his God be with him") echoing David's hopes for Solomon. On this reading
  the Davidic covenant **does not fail but transfers**: YHWH "hands authority to Gentiles,
  establishing an ecumenical imperial order that will last until the Messiah appears," leaving
  Israel "to be ruled by a new and doubtless very different King David." Leithart (following
  [[peter-leithart|Johnstone, as he cites him]]) further reads the decree as a **Jubilee /
  super-Sabbath proclamation**: it is phrased in the language of Lev 25 (the "proclamation," *qôl*,
  of liberty and return to ancestral property), issued — by the Chronicler's reckoning — in the
  fiftieth generation since Adam, so that Chronicles ends "not [with] lament but the joy of
  return" (Ps 126). This is attributed to Leithart as an interpretive position.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Cyrus concentrates the biblical claim that YHWH's sovereignty encompasses the Gentile empires:
the God of a defeated, exiled people commissions the emperor of the age — who does not know him —
to accomplish his purposes. As the sole Gentile "anointed," he sharpens the question of what
messianic anointing means (office and instrumentality vs. covenant sonship). Placed last in the
Hebrew canon, his decree turns the scriptures' ending away from the exile and toward an
"unfinished task" of rebuilding — read in [[peter-leithart|Leithart's]] terms as a "great
commission" to restore Israel's vocation as the sign of YHWH's kingship among the nations.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Isaiah's naming of Cyrus** is a principal locus of the composition debate over Isaiah:
  traditional/confessional readings take Isa 44–45 as genuine long-range predictive prophecy;
  historical-critical scholarship takes the Cyrus oracles as evidence for a sixth-century
  "Deutero-Isaiah." Recorded here as positions (see [[isaiah|Isaiah]]).
- **The decree(s) of Ezra 1 vs. Ezra 6**: the Hebrew proclamation and the Aramaic memorandum
  differ in form and detail; their authenticity and relationship are debated. The verbatim
  overlap of 2 Chr 36:22–23 with Ezra 1:1–3 bears on the Chronicles/Ezra–Nehemiah authorship
  question — [[peter-leithart|Leithart]] (with Japhet) treats the books as **separate works**
  cross-referencing each other rather than as one author's continuous composition. See
  [[1-2-chronicles|1–2 Chronicles]] and [[ezra-nehemiah|Ezra–Nehemiah]].

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Cyrus anchors the OT theology of the Gentile monarch as YHWH's instrument (developed backward
onto Huram of Tyre, the Queen of Sheba, and Pharaoh Neco in Chronicles, and forward into the
Danielic sequence of empires). In Leithart's reading he is the interpretive key to the whole
shape of Chronicles. Cross-links: [[1-2-chronicles|1–2 Chronicles]], [[ezra-nehemiah|Ezra–Nehemiah]],
