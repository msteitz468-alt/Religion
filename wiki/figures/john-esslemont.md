# John E. Esslemont

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 11927–11945.*

## Biographical Overview

Prominent British Bahá'í, best known for his book *Bahá'u'lláh and the New Era* (1923), which remains one of the most widely available introductions to the Bahá'í teachings (lines 11927–11932). He was Scottish.

He first encountered the Faith in 1914 and was a major figure in its early development in Britain (lines 11934–11936). He became a close friend and confidant of [[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]], who invited him to come to Haifa to serve as his English-language secretary in 1924 (lines 11937–11940).

Both [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]] and Shoghi Effendi supported his work (line 11933–11934).

## Posthumous Honours

He was posthumously designated a [[hands-of-the-cause|Hand of the Cause]], and was named as one of the [[disciples-of-abdu-l-baha|Disciples of 'Abdu'l-Bahá]] (lines 11941–11944).
