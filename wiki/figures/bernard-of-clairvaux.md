# Bernard of Clairvaux

Cistercian reformer, contemplative, and Marian mystic (1090–1153), who becomes
[[dante-alighieri|Dante]]'s **third and final guide** in the [[paradiso|Paradiso]]
(Cantos 31–33), succeeding [[beatrice|Beatrice]] for the approach to the
[[beatific-vision|final vision of God]].

## In the Divine Comedy

When Beatrice silently returns to her throne in the Celestial Rose, Dante turns to
find at his side an elder "robed like all in glory" who announces, "I am Bernardo,
her most faithful one" (31:100). Bernard surveys the order of the saints in the Rose
(Canto 32) — the Hebrew women forming the dividing wall between those who believed in
Christ to come and Christ come, the predestined infants saved by **grace alone**, the
thrones of Adam, Peter, Moses, John, Anne, and Lucy. He then prays the **Hymn to the
Virgin** ("Virgin and mother, daughter of your son… you are the point that truth
eternally / is fixed upon," 33:1–4) and her intercession opens the final vision to
Dante.

## Significance

Bernard embodies the movement, at the poem's summit, **from intellect to
contemplative love and Marian mediation**: reason (Virgil) gave way to revelation
(Beatrice), and theology now gives way to mystical contemplation. Kirkpatrick notes
that Dante pointedly **silences** Bernard's controversial history (his preaching of
the Second Crusade — in which [[cacciaguida|Cacciaguida]] died — and his polemic
against Abelard), drawing only on his role as the supreme intercessory devotee of the
Virgin. The structure of the close — Bernard's prayer (charity) → Mary's intercession
(hope) → the vision (faith) — recapitulates the three theological virtues earlier
examined.
