# Huitzilopochtli

**Huitzilopochtli** ("Hummingbird of the Left/South") is the **patron god of the Mexica (Aztecs)** — god of
the **sun and of war**, the deity who demanded the **human sacrifice** that fed the sun and kept the Fifth Sun
moving. His great temple crowned the **Templo Mayor** of Tenochtitlan; it was he who, by legend, led the
wandering Mexica to found their city where they saw the sign of **an eagle on a cactus devouring a serpent**
(still the emblem of Mexico).

## Myths in Detail — the Birth at Coatepec

His birth-myth is one of the most dramatic in Mesoamerica. The earth-goddess **[[coatlicue|Coatlicue]]** was
miraculously impregnated by a ball of feathers as she swept the temple on the mountain **Coatepec**. Her
daughter, the moon-goddess **Coyolxauhqui**, and her four hundred sons (the Centzon Huitznahua, the stars),
enraged at the dishonor, resolved to **kill their mother**. But as they attacked, **Huitzilopochtli sprang
fully-grown and fully-armed from Coatlicue's womb**, wielding the fire-serpent **Xiuhcoatl**; he **slew his
sister Coyolxauhqui**, dismembered her, and **hurled her body down the mountain** (her head becoming the moon),
and routed and scattered the four hundred (the stars). This is a cosmic drama re-enacted **every dawn**: the
sun (Huitzilopochtli) rising to drive off the moon and stars — and the reason the sun must be **fed with the
blood of sacrifice** to win that daily battle.
