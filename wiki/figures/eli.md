Eli, priest at the [[shiloh|Shiloh]] sanctuary in the closing generation before the monarchy, is
the figure through whom [[1-samuel|1 Samuel]] narrates the collapse of an old priestly house: he
receives the boy [[samuel|Samuel]] into the sanctuary, fails to restrain his own sons [[hophni|Hophni]] and
Phinehas, hears the oracle of his house's destruction from the child he raised, and dies falling
backward off his seat with a broken neck on the day the ark is captured.

**Disambiguation:** Eli's son Phinehas is *not* the [[phinehas|Phinehas son of Eleazar]], grandson
of [[aaron|Aaron]], who appears in Numbers and [[book-of-joshua|Joshua]]. The wiki keeps the two
strictly separate; only the Aaronide has a page.

## Biographical Overview

Eli presides over the sanctuary at Shiloh where the ark of the covenant is housed and where
Israelites come for the annual sacrifice. He is presented as personally devout but ineffectual as
a father and as a custodian of the cult: his sons' abuses proceed under his authority, and his
rebuke of them changes nothing. He is old, and by the end blind (1 Sam 4:15); the text gives his
age at death as ninety-eight and notes that he had judged Israel forty years. The Eli material is
the front half of the "story of Samuel" block ([[david-tsumura|Tsumura]]'s structural division,
1 Sam 1:1–7:17), within which the Ark Narrative is embedded.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **1 Sam 1**: Eli sits by the doorpost of the temple of YHWH at Shiloh, sees Hannah praying with
  her lips moving and no voice, and accuses her of drunkenness; on her answer he blesses her
  petition. She later brings the weaned child Samuel to him.
- **1 Sam 2:12–36**: Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas, "worthless men" who "did not know YHWH," take
  sacrificial meat with a three-pronged fork before the fat is burned, demand raw flesh under
  threat, and lie with the women serving at the entrance of the tent of meeting. Eli's rebuke
  fails. A man of God pronounces the oracle: the house of Eli is cut off, both sons will die on
  the same day as a sign, and YHWH will raise up a faithful priest.
- **1 Sam 3**: YHWH calls the boy Samuel three times in the night; Eli, on the third, instructs
  him how to answer. Samuel receives the oracle against Eli's house and reports it in the morning;
  Eli replies that it is YHWH, "let him do what seems good to him."
- **1 Sam 4**: Israel is defeated at Ebenezer; the ark is brought from Shiloh into the camp; the
  Philistines rout Israel a second time, kill thirty thousand, capture the ark, and kill Hophni
  and Phinehas. On hearing that the ark is taken, Eli falls backward off his seat beside the gate,
  breaks his neck, and dies. His daughter-in-law, wife of Phinehas, dies in childbirth and names
  the child Ichabod, "the glory has departed."
- **1 Kgs 2:27**: the removal of Abiathar from the priesthood is narrated as fulfilling "the word
  of YHWH which he had spoken concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh."

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Historical-philological / evangelical ([[david-tsumura|Tsumura]], NICOT)**: the Eli material
  belongs to the unified 1–2 Samuel narrative whose overture is the Song of Hannah. As recorded on
  [[1-samuel|the 1 Samuel page]], Tsumura reads the book's controlling pattern as divine reversal
  of human fortunes announced in 1 Sam 2:1-10; the fall of Eli's honored house is one of its
  primary instances — "honored → destroyed" — set against Hannah's "barren → mother of Samuel."
- **Jewish and Christian reception beyond this**: no source ingested in this wiki treats Eli's
  rabbinic or patristic reception at length. Nothing is recorded here that the collection does not
  support.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Eli's house functions in the narrative as the failed priestly order whose displacement clears the
way for prophet ([[samuel|Samuel]]) and eventually king. The "faithful priest" promise of 1 Sam
2:35 is picked up in [[1-2-kings|1 Kings 2:27]] as fulfilled in the fall of Abiathar and the
establishment of Zadok's line — a cross-book link the narrative itself makes. Eli's death at the
news of the ark's capture, not at the news of his sons' deaths, ties his fall to the fate of the
sanctuary rather than to family grief.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Text-critical**: 1–2 Samuel is the most textually difficult book in the Hebrew Bible, with
  Qumran Samuel scrolls diverging widely from MT (see [[1-samuel|1 Samuel — text]]). Tsumura's
  stated method is MT-first, resisting easy LXX/4QSam emendation; McCarter/Tov-style eclectic
  reconstruction is the competing approach. Recorded as positions.
- **Shiloh's destruction**: the biblical text does not narrate the destruction of the Shiloh
  sanctuary in 1 Sam 4, though Jeremiah 7:12-14 later invokes Shiloh's fate. Whether the sanctuary
  fell at this defeat is a question left open here; see [[shiloh|Shiloh]].
