# Joseph of Arimathea

In the canonical Gospels, **Joseph of Arimathea** is the man who asks Pilate for the body of
[[jesus-of-nazareth|Jesus]] and buries it in a rock-hewn tomb. He is the focus of the
[[burial-of-jesus|controversy over Jesus's burial]].

## Primary Source Appearances

Mark 15:43 introduces him as "a respected member of the council" who "asked for the body of
Jesus," wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a tomb, with Mary Magdalene and another Mary
watching. Matthew makes him "a rich man" and "a disciple"; Luke a "good and righteous" council
member who had dissented; John pairs him with Nicodemus and a lavish burial.

## Historicity ([[bart-ehrman|Ehrman]]'s reading)

Ehrman ([[ehrman-how-jesus-became-god|*How Jesus Became God*]]) treats Joseph as probably a
**later legendary development**. He is **absent from Paul** (the pre-Pauline creed of 1 Cor
15:4 names no one for the burial) and from the speech of Acts 13:29 (where unnamed "they" — the
hostile council collectively — take Jesus down and bury him). Across the Gospels the figure
grows: anonymous leaders → named Joseph (Mark) → Joseph + Nicodemus (John), fitting the
documented tendency to "name the nameless" (Metzger) and to "find good guys among bad guys"
(paralleling the progressive exoneration of [[pontius-pilate|Pilate]]). Internal tension: Mark
14:64 has the *whole* council, Joseph included, condemn Jesus, yet Joseph then buries him.
Ehrman judges a known, decent burial historically doubtful given Roman crucifixion practice and
Pilate's character, without endorsing Crossan's claim that the body was left for dogs.
