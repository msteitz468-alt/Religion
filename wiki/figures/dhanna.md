# Dhanna Bhagat

Dhanna Bhagat (c. 15th century) was a North Indian [[sant-tradition|Sant]] poet of Jat farmer (*jaat*) background whose compositions were included in the **[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]** as **Bhagat bāṇī**. His social identity as a Jat farmer is made explicit within the Granth itself, and his inclusion alongside the low-caste weaver [[kabir|Kabir]], calico-printer [[namdev|Namdev]], and cobbler [[ravidas|Ravidas]] reinforces the SGGS's structural anti-caste theology.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib (Raag Aasaa Bhagat Bāṇī, Angs 487–488)

Dhanna's compositions appear in the Bhagat bāṇī section of Raag Aasaa. His distinctive theological voice centers on **wandering through incarnations** and the **divine provision of grace for all creatures**, however humble — extending to worms under stones and turtles' babies separated from their mother.

### On incarnations and the Guru's wisdom

> "I wandered through countless incarnations, but mind, body and wealth never remain stable. Attached to, and stained by the poisons of sexual desire and greed, the mind has forgotten the jewel of the Lord." (L8756–8765, Ang 487)

> "The Guru has given the wealth of spiritual wisdom; practicing meditation, the mind becomes one with Him. Embracing loving devotional worship for the Lord, I have come to know peace; satisfied and satiated, I have been liberated." (L8797–8810, Ang 487)

> "Dhanna has obtained the Lord, the Sustainer of the World, as his wealth; meeting the humble Saints, he merges in the Lord." (L8824–8827, Angs 487–488)

### On divine providence for all creatures

> "The mother turtle is in the water, and her babies are out of the water. She has no wings to protect them, and no milk to feed them. The Perfect Lord, the embodiment of supreme bliss, the Fascinating Lord takes care of them." (L8921–8929, Ang 488)

> "The worm lies hidden under the stone — there is no way for him to escape. Says Dhanna, the Perfect Lord takes care of him. Fear not, O my soul." (L8932–8937, Ang 488)

## Validation by Mehl 5

Within the Bhagat bāṇī section, Guru Arjan (Mehl 5) provides a meta-commentary naming Dhanna specifically: "Hearing this, Dhanna the Jaat applied himself to devotional worship. The Lord of the Universe met him personally; Dhanna was so very blessed." (L8886–8891, Ang 488). The phrase "met him personally" (*partakh*) signals the highest form of divine encounter.
