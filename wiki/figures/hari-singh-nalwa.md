# Hari Singh Nalwa

The most celebrated general of [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]'s army and the Sikh kingdom's enforcer on the
Afghan frontier, whose death at the Battle of Jamrud (1837) became a turning point in the Sikh–Afghan wars.
This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1, Chs. 11, 14–16, 18**.

## Career (Khushwant Singh)

Picked out among Ranjit Singh's cavalry generals in the early reorganization of the army, Nalwa commanded
the advance column at the battle of Naushera / Pir Sabak (1823) and in many other campaigns. As the
"ruthless administrator" of the Peshawar frontier he took savage reprisals on villages
harbouring raiders: "Within a few months a petrified peace descended on the tribal land and the name of
Nalwa became a terror among the Pathans" (Singh, ch. 18). He built a chain of forts guarding the Khyber,
notably Shabkadar and Jamrud.

## Death at Jamrud (30 April 1837)

At the Battle of Jamrud, Nalwa was mortally wounded by Mohammed Akbar Khan (son of Dost Mohammed) in a sweep
on his advance column. He took four wounds — two sabre cuts, an arrow he pulled out himself, and finally a
gunshot — and ordered his officers to conceal his death "until the enemy had been driven beyond the mouth of
the Khyber," dying inside Jamrud fort (Singh, ch. 18, fn.9). The Afghans left some 11,000 dead on the field
but, Singh writes, "For the Afghans the killing of Hari Singh Nalwa turned the defeat at Jamrud into a
victory"; his ghost was said to haunt the defiles, "spreading terror among the people."
