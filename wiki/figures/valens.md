## Biographical Overview

Valens was the eastern Roman emperor from 364 until his death at the Battle of Adrianople in 378. He succeeded to the eastern half of the empire when his brother Valentinian I took the western half. He was killed in the catastrophic Roman defeat against Gothic forces crossing the Danube: "The Emperor Valens was among those killed". The significance of Adrianople for the [[arian-controversy|Arian controversy]], per Ayres, "stems from the death of Valens and the rise of [[theodosius-i|Theodosius]]".

## Religious Policy

Valens pursued a consistently Homoian ecclesiastical policy in the east. "The antipathy of Valens and the imperial government to pro-Nicenes made it difficult to act openly against Homoians". He systematically exiled pro-Nicene bishops:

- **Meletius of Antioch**: exiled 365–366 (returned during Procopius' revolt) and again 371–378.
- **Eusebius of Samosata**: "driven into exile in Thrace in 374 for his pro-Nicene sympathies".
- **Barsai of Edessa**: exiled 371, an event [[ephrem-the-syrian|Ephrem the Syrian]] "experienced at first hand".

In the empire's final crisis, Valens may have relaxed his own position in 378 as "the threat from the Goths became clear".

## Relationship with Basil of Caesarea

The encounter between Valens and [[basil-of-caesarea|Basil of Caesarea]] in 372 — when Valens travelled to Caesarea for the feast of the Epiphany — became a celebrated exemplum of episcopal resistance. Ayres notes it is better read as showing "Valens' pragmatism and the complexity of 'party' allegiance in these years". Basil gave Valens communion; Valens in turn allowed Basil to retain his see and "was even entrusted with the ecclesiastical reorganization of Armenia".

Valens also divided the province of Cappadocia into two, weakening Basil's jurisdiction. Ayres notes: "Despite what Basil presents as the scheming of an Arian emperor, we do not know if this administrative move had anything to do with opposition to Basil", citing Raymond Van Dam's argument that the arrangement may reflect a degree of imperial trust.

## Role in the Controversy's Resolution

Valens' death ended the Homoian ascendancy in the east. "Just as the victories of Constantius after 350–3 created the conditions for the rise of the Homoians, now the rise to power of a new emperor enabled the victory of the pro-Nicene cause". The succession crisis following Adrianople opened space for all parties — pro-Nicene, Heterousian, and Homoiousian — to reorganize openly. Gratian's post-Adrianople decree permitting the return of exiled bishops ended Valens' effective policy.

## Reception

[[gregory-of-nazianzus|Gregory Nazianzen]]'s *Oration 43* presented the Basil–Valens encounter as an "archetypal encounter between heroic bishop and worldly authority", a presentation Ayres nuances as shaped by later hagiographical purposes.
