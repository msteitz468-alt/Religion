Hophni, elder-named of the two sons of [[eli|Eli]] the priest at [[shiloh|Shiloh]], is in
[[1-samuel|1 Samuel]] one half of a pair the text almost never separates. He and his brother
Phinehas are introduced as "worthless men" who "did not know YHWH," they seize sacrificial meat by
force and lie with the women serving at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and they die together
on the day the ark of the covenant is captured — the sign given in advance by the man of God that
the house of Eli is finished.

**Disambiguation (important).** Hophni's brother Phinehas is **not** the
[[phinehas|Phinehas son of Eleazar]], grandson of [[aaron|Aaron]], of [[numbers|Numbers]] 25 and
[[book-of-joshua|Joshua]] 22. The two are different persons who share a name; the wiki keeps them
strictly separate and only the Aaronide has his own page. Because the text names Hophni and
Phinehas jointly in nearly every occurrence, this page treats the brother-pair together under
Hophni's title.

## Biographical Overview

Hophni and Phinehas are the sons of Eli, who presides over the sanctuary at Shiloh where the ark
is housed and where Israelites come for the annual sacrifice. They hold priestly office there
under their father's authority. The narrative's charge against them is twofold and specific.

**Contempt for the offering.** Their custom is that while the meat is boiling the priest's servant
comes with a three-pronged fork, thrusts it into the pan or kettle, and takes whatever it brings
up. Worse, before the fat is burned the servant demands raw flesh for roasting, and when the
worshipper protests that the fat must be burned first, the answer is: give it now, or I will take
it by force. "Thus the sin of the young men was very great in the sight of YHWH, for they treated
the offering of YHWH with contempt" (1 Sam 2:12-17).

**The women at the entrance of the tent.** The text states that they lay with the women who served
at the entrance of the tent of meeting (2:22). Eli, hearing of all that his sons were doing, asks
why they do such things and warns that when a man sins against YHWH there is no one to intercede
for him — but "they would not listen to the voice of their father, for it was the will of YHWH to
put them to death" (2:25). The rebuke changes nothing.

A man of God comes to Eli with the sentence: the house of Eli is cut off, no one in it will reach
old age, and **the death of both sons on the same day** is given as the sign; YHWH will raise up a
faithful priest in their place (2:27-36). The boy [[samuel|Samuel]] then receives the same oracle
in the night, and reports it to Eli, who answers, "It is YHWH; let him do what seems good to him"
(3:11-18).

**Death at Aphek.** Israel is defeated by the [[philistines|Philistines]] at Ebenezer. The elders
send to Shiloh for the ark of the covenant, and Hophni and Phinehas come with it into the camp.
The Philistines rout Israel a second time, thirty thousand foot soldiers fall, the ark is
captured, and "the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died" (4:1-11). A Benjaminite runner
brings the news to Shiloh; Eli, ninety-eight and blind, sitting by the gate watching for the ark,
falls backward off his seat at word of the ark's capture, breaks his neck, and dies. Phinehas's
widow, in labor, dies bearing a son whom she names Ichabod — "the glory has departed from Israel"
— naming the ark's loss, and her husband's and father-in-law's deaths, together (4:12-22).

## Primary Source Appearances

- **1 Sam 1:3** — first notice: "the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of YHWH"
  at Shiloh.
- **1 Sam 2:12-17** — the fork, the demand for raw flesh before the fat is burned, the threat of
  force, and the verdict that the sin was very great.
- **1 Sam 2:22-25** — the women at the entrance of the tent of meeting; Eli's failed rebuke.
- **1 Sam 2:27-36** — the man of God's oracle: the house of Eli cut off, both sons dead on one day
  as the sign, a faithful priest raised up.
- **1 Sam 3:11-14** — Samuel's night oracle against Eli's house "because his sons were blaspheming
  God and he did not restrain them."
- **1 Sam 4:1-22** — Aphek/Ebenezer; the ark brought into the camp; the rout; the deaths of Hophni
  and Phinehas; Eli's fall and death; the birth and naming of Ichabod.
- **1 Sam 14:3** — Ahijah, of the house of Eli, is identified as "son of Ahitub, Ichabod's
  brother, son of Phinehas, son of Eli," carrying the line forward.
- **1 Kgs 2:27** — the removal of Abiathar is narrated as fulfilling the word spoken "concerning
  the house of Eli in Shiloh."

## Tradition-Specific Reception

Reception material specific to Hophni and Phinehas in this collection is **thin**; no rabbinic,
patristic, or Islamic reception source on these figures has been ingested.

- **Historical-philological / evangelical ([[david-tsumura|Tsumura]], NICOT 1 Samuel)**, as
  recorded on [[eli|the Eli page]] and [[1-samuel|the 1 Samuel page]]: the Eli material belongs to
  the unified 1–2 Samuel narrative whose overture is the Song of Hannah, and the book's controlling
  pattern is the **divine reversal of human fortunes** announced in 1 Sam 2:1-10. The fall of Eli's
  honored priestly house is one of its primary instances — "honored → destroyed" — set against
  Hannah's "barren → mother of Samuel." Recorded as Tsumura's position.
- The Ark Narrative in which the brothers die is treated by Tsumura, as recorded on the source
  page, as **embedded** within the Story of Samuel (1 Sam 1:1–7:17) rather than as an independent
  document — a structural claim, recorded as his.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Hophni and Phinehas are the mechanism by which 1 Samuel discredits an entire priestly order rather
than two individuals. The charges are cultic and sexual, and both are located precisely at the
sanctuary: the offering and the entrance of the tent. Their deaths are narrated not as tragedy but
as the fulfillment of an announced sign, and the sign is verified in the same chapter that reports
the ark's capture — so that the collapse of the house and the departure of the glory are one
event. The promise of a "faithful priest who will do according to what is in my heart" (2:35) is
picked up in [[1-2-kings|1 Kings 2:27]] as fulfilled when Abiathar falls and Zadok's line stands,
a cross-book link the narrative itself makes. The displacement of the priestly house also clears
narrative space for the prophet ([[samuel|Samuel]]) and, in due course, the king.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Text-critical.** 1–2 Samuel is the most textually difficult book in the Hebrew Bible, with the
  Qumran Samuel scrolls diverging widely from MT. [[david-tsumura|Tsumura]]'s stated method is
  MT-first, resisting easy LXX/4QSam emendation; McCarter/Tov-style eclectic reconstruction is the
  competing approach. Recorded as positions, not adjudicated. Some witnesses at 1 Sam 2:22 lack
  the clause about the women at the entrance of the tent; the wiki records the MT reading as the
  received text and the variant as part of the same broader text-critical dispute, without
  resolving it.
- **The Ark Narrative.** Whether 1 Sam 4:1–7:1 was an independent composition later inserted is a
  standing critical question; Tsumura's recorded position treats it as embedded in the Samuel
  story. Positions, not verdict.
- **Shiloh's destruction.** 1 Sam 4 does not narrate the destruction of the Shiloh sanctuary,
  though Jer 7:12-14 later invokes Shiloh's fate. Left open here; see [[shiloh|Shiloh]].
- **The name Hophni.** Frequently discussed as Egyptian in origin, as is Phinehas; the wiki records
  this only as a commonly noted observation and cites no ingested source establishing it.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

The brothers anchor commentary discussion of priestly corruption and cultic abuse, of the
Zadokite–Abiatharide succession question, of the relation of the Ark Narrative to its Samuel
frame, and of the reversal theology the wiki records from Tsumura. In homiletic use they are the
standing example of hereditary office without vocation, and of a father who reproves without
restraining. Cross-links: [[eli|Eli]], [[samuel|Samuel]], [[hannah|Hannah]], [[shiloh|Shiloh]],
