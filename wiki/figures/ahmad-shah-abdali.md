# Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)

Founder of the Afghan Durrani empire and the great external antagonist of the eighteenth-century Sikhs,
whose nine invasions of the Punjab between 1747 and 1769 destroyed Mughal authority there and — in
[[khushwant-singh-history-of-the-sikhs|Khushwant Singh]]'s reading — inadvertently created the power
vacuum the [[misls|misls]] filled. This page draws on **Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Vol. 1,
Chs. 7–9**.

## Background (Khushwant Singh)

"Ahmed Shah Abdali was an Afghan of the Saddozai clan. He was Nadir Shah's most trusted general, and on
his master's assassination took possession of much of the treasure looted from India, including the
famous Koh-i-noor. He became ruler of Afghanistan under the title of Durrānī (from *dur-i-daurāṉ*, the
best of pearls)" (Singh, *History of the Sikhs* Vol. 1, fn.1, p. ~1209 cache). After Nadir Shah's murder
(June 1747) he claimed the right to conquer India as successor; he died at Kandahar on 23 October 1772.

## The Nine Invasions and the Sikhs

Singh frames Abdali through a paradox: "Abdali was the bitterest antagonist of the Sikhs and paradoxically
their greatest benefactor. His repeated incursions destroyed Mughal administration in the Punjab and at
Panipat he dealt a crippling blow to Maratha pretensions in the north. Thus he created a power vacuum in
the Punjab which was filled by the Sikhs" (Singh, ch. 8). He never subdued them: "They were everywhere and
yet elusive… Fighting the Sikhs was like trying to catch the wind in a net."

Key episodes across the nine invasions (Singh, Chs. 7–9):
- **4th invasion (1757):** after Sikh bands pillaged his Delhi plunder, Abdali sacked [[amritsar|Amritsar]] —
  "The Harimandir was blown up and the sacred pool filled with the entrails of slaughtered cows" — and
  assigned the Punjab to his son Taimur. The martyr [[deep-singh|Baba Deep Singh]] died avenging the
  desecration.
- **5th invasion — Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761):** the Marathas were crushed; Singh quotes the
  judgment that "the battle of Panipat… was really won by the Sikhs," who harassed Abdali's retreat and
  freed some two thousand Hindu women being taken to Afghan harems.
- **6th invasion — the Vaḍā Ghallūghārā (5 February 1762):** see [[ghallughara|the Ghallugharas]]; Abdali
  caught a Sikh column at Kup, most of the ~30,000 being non-combatants, and again blew up the Harimandir.
- **7th invasion (1764):** the Harimandir was destroyed a third time, defended to the death by thirty Sikhs
  under Gurbaksh Singh. The Afghan eyewitness Nur Mohammed grudgingly praised Sikh valour: "If you wish to
  learn the art of war, come face to face with them in battle.… They came like the lion and fled like the
  fox."
- **8th invasion (1766):** Abdali spared the Harimandir for the first time; Muslim citizens of Lahore
  petitioned to be governed by the Sikh sardar Lehna Singh — a shift Singh calls decisive for
  [[ranjit-singh|Ranjit Singh]]'s later success.
- **9th invasion (1769):** "like a spent bullet," reaching no further than the Jhelum.
