# Athena (Minerva)

**Athena** (Roman **Minerva**) is the goddess of **wisdom, skilled craft, and strategic warfare** — the
patron of cities and civilization, a virgin goddess (Parthenos) born, fully armed, **from the head of
[[zeus|Zeus]]**.

## Myths and Character

Goddess of *practical intelligence* (*mētis*) — as distinct from the brute violence of [[ares|Ares]] — she
champions heroes who win by skill and cunning (Odysseus, Perseus, Heracles). She won the patronage of
**Athens** by giving the **olive tree**, defeating [[poseidon|Poseidon]]; the **Parthenon** is her temple.
Inventor of the loom, the bridle, and the ship; bearer of the **aegis** and the **Gorgon's head**; companion
of the owl.

## Cult and Roman Identification

The civic goddess par excellence (the Panathenaia). As **Minerva** she joined [[zeus|Jupiter]] and
[[hera|Juno]] in the **Capitoline Triad** and was patron of craftsmen and the arts.

## Myths in Detail

**Birth from the head of Zeus.** [[zeus|Zeus]] lay with the Titaness **Metis** ("Cunning"), but a prophecy
warned that her child would surpass him — so he **swallowed Metis** whole. In time a splitting headache
seized him; [[hephaestus|Hephaestus]] cleaved his skull with an axe, and **Athena sprang out fully grown and
armed**, with a war-cry. Born of pure intellect from her father's head, she is motherless wisdom itself, and
Zeus's favorite, alone trusted with the aegis and the thunderbolt.

**Arachne.** The mortal weaver **Arachne** boasted she surpassed Athena at the loom and challenged the
goddess. Athena wove the gods in glory; Arachne wove the gods' shameful deceptions — and her work was
flawless. Enraged (whether at the insult or the truth), Athena struck her, and Arachne hanged herself in
despair; Athena turned her into a **spider**, forever weaving.

**Patron of heroes.** Goddess of *practical* skill, Athena guides the heroes who win by intelligence: she
helps **Perseus** slay Medusa (and bears the **Gorgon's head** on her aegis thereafter), aids **Heracles**
in his Labors, stands by **Diomedes** and **Achilles** at Troy, and is the constant protector of clever
**Odysseus** through the [[homer|Odyssey]]. Bitter at the [[hera|Judgment of Paris]], she fought fiercely for
the Greeks, and the fall of Troy turned on her image (the **Palladium**) and the **wooden horse**, devised
under her inspiration.
