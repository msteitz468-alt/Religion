# The Dagda

**The Dagda** — *An Dagda*, "the Good God" (good not morally but as **good-at-everything**) — is the
**father-figure of the [[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]]**, a god of **abundance, fertility, magic,
wisdom, and the cycle of life and death**. His epithets, *Eochaid Ollathair* ("All-Father") and *Ruad
Rofhessa* ("Lord of Great Knowledge"), mark him as a chief and sage of the gods, often paired with
[[lugh|Lugh]] and [[nuada|Nuada]] in the rule of the divine race.

## Attributes and Myths in Detail

The Dagda is defined by his **three great treasures**, each a paradox of plenty and power:

- the **cauldron of plenty (the Coire Ansic)**, one of the four talismans the Tuatha Dé brought to Ireland,
  **from which no company ever went away unsatisfied** — inexhaustible food, an emblem of the god's
  bottomless generosity (and an ancestor of the cauldron-of-rebirth and, distantly, the Grail);
- the **great club or staff**, so huge it must be dragged on wheels, that **kills nine men with one end and
  restores the dead to life with the other** — the god holding death and resurrection in a single tool;
- the **harp, Uaithne**, which he plays to make the **seasons turn in order** and to summon the moods of
  sorrow, laughter, and sleep from the host; when the Fomorians steal it, the Dagda calls it across the air
  and it leaps to his hand, killing nine of them on the way.

In **[[gods-and-fighting-men|Gods and Fighting Men]]** he is comic as well as cosmic: before the Second Battle
of Mag Tuired the Fomorians, mocking his appetite, force him to **eat a vast porridge** of eighty gallons of
milk, meal, fat, whole sheep and pigs poured into a hole in the ground — which he scrapes up with a
ladle "big enough for a man and woman to lie in" — and waddles off comically swollen, yet undefeated. He
unites at **Samhain** with the **[[morrigan|Morrígan]]** as she straddles a river, a sacred coupling that
secures victory and the land's fortune. He owns **Brú na Bóinne (Newgrange)**, the great mound, until his son
**Aengus** tricks him out of it.

## Significance

The Dagda is the Celtic **father-god of plenty** — earthy, immense, faintly grotesque, but holding the deepest
powers: **food that never runs out, the club of death and life, and the harp that orders time**. He is the
genial counterpart to the warlike Morrígan and the bright Lugh.
