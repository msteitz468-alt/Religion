# Padmasambhava

**Padmasambhava** ("the Lotus-Born"), also **Padmākara** and, devotionally, **Guru Rinpoche**
("Precious Master"), is the 8th-century Indian tantric master revered by the [[nyingma|Nyingma]]
as the **"second Buddha"** who established [[vajrayana|Vajrayana]] Buddhism in Tibet. He is the
attributed author of the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]] cycle and the source of the
entire [[terma|treasure]] tradition.

## Biographical Overview

Tradition makes him a master from **Oḍḍiyāna** (a region to the north-west of India). With the
abbot **Śāntarakṣita** and under **King Trisong Detsen**, he is held to have formally established
the Dharma in Tibet during the royal dynastic period. The accounts are hagiographic and
semi-legendary; the wiki records the traditional portrait, not a critical biography.

## Primary Source Appearances

In the [[bardo-thodol|Tibetan Book of the Dead]] he is the **revealer and attributed composer**
of the cycle: the colophons of Chapters 2, 4, 7 and others name "Padmākara, the preceptor of
Oḍḍiyāna" as author, and the framing narrative has him teach it to Trisong Detsen and Prince
Mutri Tsenpo and then conceal it. Chapter 13 (the masked drama) carries his colophon: "I,
Padmākara, having actually seen the hells, composed this."

## Tradition-Specific Reception

For the **[[nyingma|Nyingma]]** he is the central founder-figure and the fountainhead of the
*[[terma|gter-ma]]* revelations: through his consort **Yeshe Tsogyal** and other disciples he is
said to have concealed treasure-teachings at power-places across Tibet, to be recovered by future
**treasure-finders** (*gter-ston*) — among them [[karma-lingpa|Karma Lingpa]], discoverer of the
Book of the Dead. He is invoked across the cycle (e.g. as a refuge in the masked drama, dwelling
in his celestial palace of **Lotus Light** in Oḍḍiyāna) and is one of the buddha-forms suggested
for [[phowa|consciousness transference]] into the nirmāṇakāya.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

A historical Padmasambhava of the 8th century is plausible, but the figure of the sources is
heavily mythologised; the *terma* system itself — texts revealed centuries later but attributed to
him — has been a point of dispute between the Nyingma and the New Translation schools.
