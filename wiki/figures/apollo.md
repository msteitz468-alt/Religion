# Apollo

**Apollo** (the rare god whose name is the **same in Greek and Latin**) is among the greatest Olympians —
god of **prophecy, light/the sun, music and poetry, healing, archery, and (paradoxically) plague**. Son of
[[zeus|Zeus]] and the Titaness **Leto**, twin of [[artemis|Artemis]], born on **Delos**.

## Myths and Powers

The ideal of youthful male beauty and order. He slew the serpent **Python** at **Delphi** and founded there
the most important **oracle** of the Greek world (his priestess the Pythia). Patron of the **Muses**
(*Musagetes*) and the lyre; his arrows bring sudden death and plague (the [[homer|Iliad]] opens with his
plague on the Greeks). Father of [[asclepius|Asclepius]] the healer. The myths of **Daphne** (the laurel)
and of Hyacinthus belong to him.

## Cult and Roman Identification

Delphi and Delos; the Pythian Games. The Romans adopted **Apollo** directly (one of the few Greek gods taken
without renaming); **Augustus** made him his patron deity.

## Myths in Detail

**Birth on Delos.** [[hera|Hera]], jealous of [[zeus|Zeus's]] love **Leto**, forbade any land to give her
refuge to give birth. Only the barren floating island of **Delos** dared receive her (promised it would
become rich with Apollo's shrine); there, clinging to a palm tree, Leto bore [[artemis|Artemis]] first, then,
after nine days' labor, **Apollo**. The island blazed with gold at his birth.

**The slaying of Python and the Delphic oracle.** Still young, Apollo went to **Delphi** (Pytho) and slew
the great serpent **Python** that guarded the place (and had harried his mother) — claiming the site for his
own **oracle**, the most authoritative in the Greek world, where his priestess the **Pythia** spoke his
prophecies. He founded the Pythian Games in commemoration and underwent purification for the killing.

**Daphne, and the god's loves and griefs.** Apollo mocked [[eros|Eros]], who in revenge shot him with a
golden arrow of love for the nymph **Daphne** and shot her with a leaden arrow of refusal. As Apollo pursued,
Daphne prayed to escape and was changed into the **laurel** — which became Apollo's sacred tree and crown.
His loves often end in loss: he accidentally killed his beloved **Hyacinthus** with a discus (from whose
blood sprang the flower); **Coronis**, mother of [[asclepius|Asclepius]], betrayed him; and he cursed the
prophetess **Cassandra** — who refused him — so that her true prophecies would never be believed.

**Plague and music.** The bright god of order and healing is also the bringer of sudden death: the
[[homer|Iliad]] opens with Apollo's arrows of **plague** raining on the Greek camp. Patron of the **Muses**
and the lyre, he embodies harmony, prophecy, and the deadly precision of the bow alike.
