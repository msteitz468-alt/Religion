# Mary Sue Hubbard

**Mary Sue Hubbard** (née Whipp; 1931–2002) was [[l-ron-hubbard|L. Ron Hubbard]]'s third wife and the founding head of the **[[guardian-office|Guardian's Office]]** — the intelligence and legal arm of the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology]] she built into a global covert operations network. She was the highest-ranking woman in the movement and second only to Hubbard in formal authority until her displacement in the early 1980s by [[david-miscavige|David Miscavige]].

## Biographical Overview

Mary Sue Whipp married Hubbard in **1952** while he was still technically married to Sara Northrup (the marriage to Sara was annulled). She became his most trusted operational deputy and bore him four children: Diana, Quentin, Suzette, and Arthur.

She was named **first Guardian of Scientology** on **March 1, 1966** — a title Hubbard declared she held "for life." The Guardian's Office (GO), which she directed, was described by Atack as a "private CIA": at its height it employed up to 5,000 operatives engaged in intelligence-gathering, infiltration, and covert operations against critics and government agencies.

## Operation Snow White and Conviction

Mary Sue co-authored or oversaw the **Snow White Program** — the systematic infiltration of the IRS, FBI, Interpol, and other government agencies. The program was initiated by **Guardian Order 732, April 1973** (authored by Hubbard personally, per Atack and Armstrong's testimony, despite the Church's claim it was an independent GO initiative).

When the **FBI raided** Scientology offices on **July 7, 1977** (134 agents; 28,000 documents seized), Mary Sue was among those indicted. She was convicted on Operation Snow White charges and **sentenced to five years** (of which she served approximately one year), along with GO executive Jane Kember.

## Abandonment by Hubbard

After the FBI raids, Hubbard went into increasingly deep hiding. Mary Sue last saw him in **August 1979**. He did not stand by her during the trial or sentencing, did not visit her during her imprisonment, and made no public statement. From her perspective and that of observers including Atack and Wright, Hubbard effectively abandoned his wife to bear responsibility for policies he had directed.

## Displacement by Miscavige

While she was serving her sentence (1983), **David Miscavige** — then consolidating power through the **Commodore's Messenger Organization (CMO)** — confronted and dismantled the Guardian's Office's leadership network. Miscavige reportedly told Mary Sue she had been **"disfellowshipped"** while Hubbard was still alive. According to Atack and Wright's accounts, Mary Sue **openly cursed Miscavige** in response. The GO was formally dissolved and replaced by the **Office of Special Affairs (OSA)**, under Miscavige's control.

Her authority as Controller was stripped; the office she had held "for life" per Hubbard's own designation was abolished.

## Later Life

Mary Sue lived quietly after her release from prison. She died in 2002, after surviving Hubbard by sixteen years. Hubbard's estate and the Church's assets she had helped build flowed through channels she no longer controlled.

## Theological and Institutional Significance

Mary Sue Hubbard represents the founding nexus of Scientology's covert organizational culture. The Guardian's Office she built institutionalized the principles of **Fair Game** and "acceptable truth" as operational realities rather than merely policy statements. Her displacement — by a man much younger than herself, wielding the same organizational tools the GO had invented — is one of the central ironies of Scientology's institutional history.

## Controversies

The question of how much operational initiative Mary Sue had versus how much was directed by Hubbard is central to the **Snow White** controversy. Atack argues persuasively that Hubbard authored GO 732 and that Mary Sue and the GO were implementing his directives. The Church's defense at trial characterized her as an independent actor; former GO members and Armstrong's testimony characterize her as executing Hubbard's program.
