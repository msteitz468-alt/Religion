# Tychicus

## Biographical Overview
Tychicus was an Asian Christian and a close companion of the Apostle Paul. He is mentioned five times in the New Testament, functioning primarily as a trusted messenger and envoy for Paul during his imprisonments. He traveled with Paul from Macedonia to Asia (Acts 20:4) and was later entrusted with delivering letters and providing pastoral updates to churches in Asia Minor.

## Primary Source Appearances
- **Acts 20:4**: Mentioned as an "Asian" accompanying Paul on his return journey through Macedonia to Jerusalem, likely serving as a representative of the Asian churches delivering the collection for the Jerusalem poor.
- **Ephesians 6:21-22**: Paul sends Tychicus, calling him a "beloved brother and faithful minister in the Lord," to inform the Ephesian church about Paul's status and to "encourage your hearts." This establishes Tychicus as the vital narrative link between the imprisoned apostle and the congregation.
- **Colossians 4:7-9**: In language nearly identical to Ephesians, Tychicus is sent to Colossae alongside Onesimus for the same purpose—to deliver news and encourage the church. 
- **2 Timothy 4:12**: Paul notes that he has sent Tychicus to Ephesus.
- **Titus 3:12**: Paul mentions that he plans to send either Artemas or Tychicus to Titus in Crete.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
Tychicus models the role of the apostolic envoy. While Paul was imprisoned and physically separated from the churches he founded, figures like Tychicus were essential for maintaining the relational and theological unity of the early Christian network. In Ephesians and Colossians, Tychicus's mission is explicitly pastoral ("to encourage your hearts"), demonstrating that the delivery of these profound theological letters was accompanied by a personal, comforting presence from Paul's trusted inner circle.
