# Persephone (Proserpina)

**Persephone** (Roman **Proserpina**), also called **Kore** ("the Maiden"), is the daughter of
[[zeus|Zeus]] and [[demeter|Demeter]] and the **queen of the underworld** — a goddess of two worlds, of
**spring growth** above and of **the dead** below.

## The Central Myth

[[hades|Hades]] **abducts** her while she gathers flowers; her grieving mother [[demeter|Demeter]] makes the
earth barren until Zeus intervenes — but because Persephone has eaten **pomegranate seeds** in the
underworld, she must spend part of each year there. Her annual **descent and return** is the myth of the
**seasons** (and of the seed that dies in the ground to rise again), and the heart of the
[[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]]. With [[hades|Hades]] she reigns over the dead and in
[[orphism|Orphic]] belief judges and purifies souls.

## Myths in Detail

**The pomegranate.** The detail that fixes her fate: in the underworld, [[hades|Hades]] gave Persephone
**pomegranate seeds** to eat (six, or four, or one) — and to eat the food of the dead is to belong to the
dead. So when [[demeter|Demeter]] won her release, Persephone could not be wholly free: for each seed she must
return one month (or one-third of the year) to the underworld. Her **descent and return** is the
turning of the seasons (see [[demeter|Demeter]]).

**Dread queen of the dead.** Above ground she is **Kore**, the spring maiden of flowers; below, she is the
stern **Queen of the Underworld** beside Hades, who judges and receives the dead. The living invoked her name
in awe. When **Orpheus** descended to win back his wife Eurydice, it was Persephone, moved by his song, who
persuaded Hades to release her (on the fatal condition Orpheus not look back). When **Theseus and Pirithous**
came to abduct her, she had them trapped in the Chairs of Forgetfulness. She quarreled with [[aphrodite|
Aphrodite]] over the beautiful **Adonis**, and [[zeus|Zeus]] ruled he should spend part of the year with
each — a second seasonal myth echoing her own.

**Mistress of souls.** In the [[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]] and in [[orphism|Orphic]] belief,
Persephone is the goddess who holds the keys to the afterlife — she purifies and releases the souls of the
initiated, and the Orphic gold tablets are addressed to her as the queen who grants the blessed dead their
reward.
