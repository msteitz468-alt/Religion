# Odin

**Odin** (Óðinn; Anglo-Saxon **Woden**, whence "Wednesday") is the **chief god of the [[norse-religion|Norse]]
pantheon** — the **All-Father**, god of **war and death, wisdom and poetry, magic and kingship**. Complex and
unsettling, he is a god of **knowledge won at terrible cost** and of victory **given and treacherously
withdrawn** — the patron of kings, warriors, poets, and sorcerers.

## Myths in Detail — Wisdom at a Price

Odin's defining trait is his **relentless, self-sacrificing pursuit of wisdom**:

- **The eye at Mímir's well.** To drink from the **Well of Mímir**, the source of wisdom beneath
  [[yggdrasil|Yggdrasil]], Odin **gave one of his eyes** — he is forever **one-eyed**, having traded sight
  for insight.
- **The runes.** To win the **runes** (letters of power and magic), Odin **hanged himself on Yggdrasil**,
  pierced by his own spear, for **nine nights**, "given to myself, myself," neither eating nor drinking,
  until he seized the runes screaming from the depths (Hávamál) — a striking self-sacrifice, god to himself
  on the tree.
- **The mead of poetry.** Odin won the **mead of poetry** (which makes whoever drinks it a poet or scholar)
  by seducing the giantess Gunnlöð, drinking it in three draughts, and escaping in **eagle form** — so
  poetry is "Odin's gift."

## God of War and Death

Odin chooses who falls in battle (he is treacherous — granting victory then withdrawing it), and through his
**Valkyries** gathers the brave slain into **[[valhalla|Valhalla]]** — not out of mercy but to muster an army
for **[[ragnarok|Ragnarök]]**, where he knows he is **fated to be devoured by the wolf [[fenrir|Fenrir]]**.
He travels the worlds in disguise (a one-eyed wanderer in a broad hat), accompanied by his ravens **Huginn
and Muninn** ("Thought" and "Memory"), his wolves, and his eight-legged horse **Sleipnir**. He practiced even
the "unmanly" magic of **[[seidr|seiðr]]** in his hunger for power and foreknowledge.
