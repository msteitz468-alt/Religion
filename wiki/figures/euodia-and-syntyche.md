# Euodia and Syntyche

**Euodia** and **Syntyche** are two women in the church at [[philippi|Philippi]] whom Paul addresses by name in Phil 4:2–3, urging them to “be of the same mind in the Lord.” In [[g-walter-hansen|Hansen]]’s PNTC they illustrate the letter’s unity crisis: real leaders whose conflict threatens the gospel-worthy life of the colony-church.

## Biographical Overview (as Hansen reads the text)

Hansen treats both as **significant co-workers** who “contended” (*synēthlēsan*) with Paul in the gospel — athletic/military imagery of shared struggle, not minor helpers. Their names are “in the book of life.” The appeal is personal and public: Paul asks a “true companion” (*gnēsie syzyge*, “true yokefellow”) to help them reconcile.

Hansen’s intro notes that women leaders (Lydia, Euodia, Syntyche) fit Philippi’s cultural pattern more readily than some other cities.

## Primary Source Appearance

- **Phil 4:2–3 only** (no other NT narrative). Context: final appeals after the citizenship/Savior climax (3:20–21) and the call to stand firm (4:1).

## Hansen’s interpretive notes

- **Conflict:** not trivial personality clash only — a **power struggle** among leaders that the letter’s humility/unity program (2:1–5; Christ hymn) is meant to address.
- **True yokefellow:** Hansen surveys options (Timothy, Epaphroditus, Silas, Lydia, Luke, a man named Syzygos, the church collectively, even a wife tradition) without forcing a single identification; the metaphor draws on Greco-Roman “yoke of friendship” pairs.
- **Prescript link:** overseers and deacons greeted in 1:1 partly as those who must ensure instructions are followed and conflicts (including 4:2) resolved.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

They personalize the letter’s central demand: *phronein* the same thing in the Lord (cf. 2:2, 5; 3:15; 4:2). Gospel partnership ([[partnership|*koinōnia*]]) includes women who labored in the mission and who must embody the mind of Christ toward each other.
