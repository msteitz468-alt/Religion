# Tsukuyomi

**Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto** is the **moon-god** of [[shinto|Shinto]] — the second of the "three precious children"
born from **[[izanagi-and-izanami|Izanagi]]'s** [[harae|purification]] (from his **right eye**, as
**[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** the sun came from the left and **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]** the storm from the nose).
Izanagi assigns Tsukuyomi the rule of the **realms of night** (or the sea, in variants), making him the
counterpart of his radiant sister the sun.

Tsukuyomi is a strikingly **shadowy and little-developed** deity — Shinto's chief myth about him explains the
**separation of day and night**. In the **[[nihongi|Nihongi]]** version, Amaterasu sends Tsukuyomi down to
attend the food-goddess ** Uke-mochi**; when she produces food for a feast by **disgorging it from her mouth,
nose, and rectum**, Tsukuyomi is so **disgusted that he kills her** — from whose corpse the grains, cattle, and
silkworms spring (an agricultural origin-myth). Amaterasu, appalled at his violence, declares she will **never
look upon him again** — and so **the sun and the moon dwell apart, one by day and one by night**. (The
[[kojiki|Kojiki]] tells a similar killing of the food-goddess Ō-getsu-hime by Susanoo.) Tsukuyomi receives
relatively little cult, the moon being far less central to Shinto than the imperial sun.
