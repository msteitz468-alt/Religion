# Ibrāhím George Kheiralla

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 20145–20189.*

## Biographical Overview

Ibrāhím George Kheiralla (1849–1929) was a pioneer Bahá'í teacher in the United States. Of Syrian Christian background, he became a Bahá'í in Egypt in 1889/90 and moved to Chicago in 1893 (l. 20148–20150). In 1894 he began a successful spiritual healing practice while simultaneously teaching about the Bahá'í Faith (l. 20150–20154).

## Role in American Bahá'í Expansion

His teaching work developed into a systematic endeavour to spread the Bahá'í teachings in America. By 1898, approximately 700 Americans had become Bahá'ís through his efforts (l. 20156–20159). In 1898–99 he made the pilgrimage to Akká to meet [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]], who honoured him greatly, calling him "'Bahá's Peter'" and the "'Conqueror of America'", and having him assist in laying the foundation stone of the [[shrine-of-the-bab|Shrine of the Báb]] (l. 20161–20167).

## Break with 'Abdu'l-Bahá

On his return to the United States (May 1899) difficulties arose, primarily because Kheiralla wished to be acknowledged as supreme leader of the American Bahá'ís (l. 20172–20176). Failing to gain 'Abdu'l-Bahá's support, he transferred his allegiance to [[covenant-breakers|Muḥammad-'Alí]] (1900) (l. 20177–20180). A temporary schism resulted, with a small and declining minority — termed "Behaists" — supporting Kheiralla. Subsequent efforts to attract new followers were largely unavailing (l. 20181–20186).
