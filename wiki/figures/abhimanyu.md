Abhimanyu is the son of [[arjuna|Arjuna]] and Subhadra (Krishna's sister), and is therefore [[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]]'s nephew. In [[c-rajagopalachari|Rajagopalachari]]'s [[mahabharata|Mahābhārata]] retelling, he appears first in early battle chapters as a prodigious young warrior, and becomes the focus of tragedy in Chapter LXXX when he is killed inside the chakravyuha (lotus formation) on the thirteenth day of battle.

## Biographical Overview

Abhimanyu's age is characterized as adolescent at the time of the Kurukshetra war: "Abhimanyu, son of Arjuna and Subhadra, was still adolescent, but had already won recognition as a mighty man-at-arms even as the equal of his father and uncle in battle." (Ch. LXXIX) He self-identifies as "Krishna's nephew and son of Arjuna" — traits he treats as guarantees against fear (Ch. LXXIX).

His skill in arms was learned from his father Arjuna "and from Vasudeva, his uncle" (Ch. LXXIX). He knew how to penetrate the lotus array formation because Arjuna had taught him, but crucially Arjuna had not yet taught him how to fight his way out — a gap that becomes fatal (Ch. LXXIX).

He was already betrothed or married to Uttara (daughter of Virata) — Draupadi mentions "Subhadra's son Abhimanyu" leading her children in battle, and Arjuna's lament at Abhimanyu's death includes "what solace can be given to Uttara." (Chs. LV, LXXXI)

## Primary Source Appearances

**First Day of Battle (Ch. LXIV):** Abhimanyu entered combat against Bhishma himself, and "the gods came to watch the combat" between oldest and youngest warriors. His flag displayed a golden karnikara tree. He hit Kritavarma once, Salya five times, and Bhishma nine times. He brought down Bhishma's palm-tree flag. The Kaurava warriors admired him: "Indeed, a worthy son to Dhananjaya!" Bhishma too was filled with great joy at the young hero's valour but "unwillingly, he had to use his full strength against the boy." (Ch. LXIV)

**Third Day (Ch. LXVI):** Sakuni attacked Satyaki with a large force. Satyaki's chariot was smashed; "he had to scramble up Abhimanyu's chariot and thereafter both fought from the same chariot." Together they destroyed Sakuni's forces. (Ch. LXVI)

**Fourth Day (Ch. LXVII):** Five Kaurava warriors — Aswatthama, Bhurisravas, Salya, Chitrasena, and the son of Chala — surrounded Abhimanyu. "The warrior fought like a lion opposing five elephants." When Arjuna saw the combined attack on his son, he joined with a wrathful lion-roar. Abhimanyu saw Dhrishtadyumna in trouble and sent a shower of arrows on Salya, putting him in danger; when Duryodhana and brothers rushed to Salya's help, Bhimasena arrived. (Ch. LXVII)

**Sixth Day (Ch. LXIX):** Abhimanyu came with a force of twelve chariots to support Bhimasena and Dhrishtadyumna, who were in serious difficulty. "Dhrishtadyumna was greatly relieved when he saw this." Drupada's son then got into Abhimanyu's car. (Ch. LXIX)

**Seventh Day (Ch. LXX):** Three of Duryodhana's brothers attacked Abhimanyu "who inflicted a heavy defeat on them but spared their lives, because Bhima had sworn to kill them." Bhishma then attacked Abhimanyu; Arjuna directed his chariot toward Bhishma. (Ch. LXX)

**Ninth Day (Ch. LXXII):** There was "a great fight between Abhimanyu and Alambasa. Abhimanyu demonstrated that his valour was no less than his illustrious father's. Alambasa had to flee on foot to save himself." (Ch. LXXII)

**Under Drona's command (Ch. LXXV):** Dronacharya fought Abhimanyu among others — Satyaki, Bhima, Arjuna, Dhrishtadyumna, Drupada, and Kasiraja — and defeated them on various occasions. (Ch. LXXV)

**Thirteenth Day — Entry into the Lotus Formation (Ch. LXXIX):** When Arjuna was again drawn south to fight the samsaptakas, Drona rearranged in lotus (padma / chakravyuha) formation and attacked Yudhishthira. None of the Pandava commanders could break it. Yudhishthira called Abhimanyu and said: "Dear son, Dronacharya is harrying our army greatly. Arjuna is absent and, if we should be defeated in his absence, he will be grieved beyond measure. No one among us has been able to break Drona's array. You know you can do it and no one else."

Abhimanyu acknowledged he knew how to enter but not how to exit: "I have been instructed by my father how to penetrate this formation and can certainly do so. But if, after forcing my way, it should unfortunately become necessary for me to come out, I shall be at a loss what to do, being as yet uninstructed in the art of extrication."

Yudhishthira promised that all the Pandava warriors would follow immediately in his wake. Bhimasena pledged to enter right behind him. Abhimanyu, "impelled by his own gallant nature, undertook the adventure," saying: "I shall please my great father and uncle. Let my valour be staked on this."

His charioteer urged caution; Abhimanyu replied: "Friend, I am Krishna's nephew and son of Arjuna, am I not? Which other has that advantage? Fear dare not approach me! These enemies here have not a sixteenth part of my strength. Drive fast towards Drona's division."

As his golden chariot with beautiful young horses approached, the Kaurava soldiers exclaimed in fear: "Here is one greater in valour than Arjuna." Like a young lion, Abhimanyu breached the formation. But Jayadratha, king of the Sindhus, closed the breach before the other Pandavas could follow — isolating Abhimanyu inside. (Ch. LXXIX)

**Thirteenth Day — Death in the Lotus Formation (Ch. LXXX):** Surrounded but undaunted, Abhimanyu slew Kaurava warriors in great numbers. Duryodhana's son Lakshmana charged him; Abhimanyu "pierced Lakshmana. The handsome youth, with beautiful nose and eyebrows and hair, lay dead on the field and the Kaurava soldiers were filled with grief."

Six great warriors then closed on him simultaneously: Drona, Kripa, Karna, Aswatthama, Brihatbala, and Kritavarma. Acting on Drona's advice, Karna "shot from behind" — breaking Abhimanyu's bow. His horses and charioteer were also killed, disabling him. He stood on foot with sword and shield, "filling the warriors around with amazement." Drona broke his sword with an arrow; Karna tore his shield to pieces.

Taking up a chariot wheel, Abhimanyu "whirling it like a discus, stood up facing all the enemies that surrounded him." The poet says the dust from the wheel "enhanced the natural beauty of the young hero." He "fought fiercely like a second Vishnu with the discus." But the chariot wheel was shattered. The son of Duhsasana then "closed with him in mortal combat. Both fell down together but Duhsasana's son rose again and, while Abhimanyu was struggling to his feet, struck him with his mace and killed him."

The Kaurava army danced around his dead body; "all good men in the army were grieved and tears rolled from their eyes. Even the birds of prey, that circled overhead making noises seemed to cry 'Not thus!' 'Not thus!'" Yuyutsu, Dhritarashtra's own son, threw away his weapon in disgust and left the field, crying: "This is ignoble." (Ch. LXXX)

**Aftermath (Ch. LXXXI):** Dronacharya's "eyes were filled with tears of affectionate admiration" during the battle and he exclaimed to Kripa: "Was there ever a fighter to equal this boy Abhimanyu?" (Ch. LXXIX).

Arjuna's return and discovery are deeply affecting. He had a premonition approaching camp: "How is it Abhimanyu does not run out to meet us today as usual?" His question to the silent camp confirmed his fear: "I did not teach him how to make his way out of that formation. Has he been slain indeed?" He fell in a swoon on hearing the full story, then rose and swore to kill Jayadratha before sunset the next day.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Abhimanyu's death is the moral turning point of the war in Rajagopalachari's narrative. He is explicitly depicted as a victim of *adharma*: a crowd of redoubtable veteran warriors killed an isolated adolescent in a coordinated, unchivalrous manner. Sanjaya says to Dhritarashtra: "your people danced round his dead body like savage hunters exulting over their prey." Yuyutsu's revulsion — a Kaurava prince condemning the act — underlines how completely it violates the code of honorable war both sides had sworn.

His death also provides the specific motivation for Arjuna's vow against Jayadratha, which drives the dramatic arc of the fourteenth day.

Rajagopalachari frames his courage through lineage: "I am Krishna's nephew and son of Arjuna, am I not? Which other has that advantage?" His self-understanding is inseparable from whose son he is. The narrative also stresses that his skill was genuinely his own: "the skill in the use of arms which he had learnt from his illustrious father and from Vasudeva, his uncle." He was not merely riding on his father's reputation but had become in fact "even the equal of his father and uncle in battle."

The chariot wheel episode — Abhimanyu wielding a wheel as Vishnu wields the discus — carries obvious typological resonance. Rajagopalachari calls it "like a second Vishnu with the discus," connecting the boy-warrior to divine archetype.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Within this range of [[rajagopalachari-mahabharata|Rajagopalachari's retelling]], no commentary tradition beyond the text itself is discussed. The emotional power of the scene is allowed to speak directly. Rajagopalachari's prose is notably restrained and allows Sanjaya's lament and the reactions of figures like Yuyutsu to carry the moral judgment.
