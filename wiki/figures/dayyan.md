# Dayyán

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. Claims grounded in lines 10366–10389.*

## Biographical Overview

Bábí title (*Dayyán* = 'Judge') of Mullá (or Mírzá) Asadu'lláh Khú'í (d. 1856) of Khuy in western Azerbaiján (lines 10366–10369). He converted to Bábism during the period of [[the-bab|the Báb]]'s exile in the province.

He soon gained the Báb's praise for a learned apologia he wrote for the new faith. He was unusual for the range of his learning, which included knowledge of Syriac and Hebrew. He was the recipient of the Báb's *Lawh-i-Hurúfát* ('Tablet of the Letters') (lines 10370–10376).

## Post-Báb Period

In the confused period of the early 1850s, following the Báb's execution, he was one of those who laid claim to Bábí leadership and gained a following (lines 10377–10380).

He was finally murdered in Baghdad, seemingly at the instigation of [[subh-i-azal|Subh-i-Azal]], who had denounced him as the 'father of iniquities' (lines 10380–10388).
