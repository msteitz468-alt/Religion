# Yama

**Yama** is the **lord of the dead** in [[vedic-religion|Vedic religion]] — and, distinctively, the **first
human being ever to die**, who thereby "found the path" to the next world and became its king. The son of
[[surya|Vivasvat]] (the sun), Yama and his sister-twin **Yami** were the first mortals; because Yama was the
first to cross over into death, he rules the realm of the departed ancestors (the *pitrs*), a comparatively
**bright and blessed** realm in the early Rig Veda (the fathers feast with Yama under a tree), darkening into
a god of judgment and punishment in later [[hinduism|Hinduism]]. His Iranian cousin is **Yima** (the first
king of Zoroastrian myth) — another clear Indo-Iranian inheritance. The young Nachiketas's dialogue with Yama
on the secret of death in the **Katha [[upanishads|Upanishad]]** is one of the great Indian meditations on
mortality.

## The Iranian Yima (Jamshid)

In [[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]] myth the same Indo-Iranian figure becomes **Yima**,
**Yima Xshaeta** ("the radiant," whence Middle Persian **Jamshid**) — but the narrative
turns from *first to die* toward *first king*. Per
[[rose-zoroastrianism-an-introduction|Rose]]:

- In **Videvdad 2**, Yima is the first mortal to converse with [[ahura-mazda|Ahura
  Mazda]]; declining to *promote* the religion (*daena*), he instead accepts the charge to
  **protect all living things**, ruling a **golden age** without heat, cold, old age, or
  death, thrice enlarging the earth as it fills. Warned of a catastrophic winter, he
  builds the underground **vara** — an enclosure stocked with the best of humans,
  animals, and plants, lit by its own light — to preserve life (an ark-like motif).
- He is the **"good shepherd"** (Vd 2.2) who glows with the [[xwarenah|*xwarenah*]]
  (divine glory). When a "lie enters his mind" (the Zamyad Yasht), the glory **flies away
  as a bird** and his reign collapses toward the *daevas* — the loss of legitimacy that
  the [[xwarenah|xwarenah]] concept dramatizes.
- His regenerative role **prefigures the [[saoshyant|Saoshyant]]**: later eschatology
  (e.g. the [[bundahishn|Bundahishn]]) has the people of the *vara* repopulate the world,
  and Jamshid is bound to **Nav Ruz** (New Year), the festival that itself anticipates
  the world "made wonderful." Rose notes that **[[persians|Cyrus]]** as "good shepherd"
  (Isa. 44.28) may have coalesced with this archetype of the just ruler.
