# King Benjamin

**King Benjamin** is the Nephite king whose temple discourse (Mosiah 2–5) is one of the [[book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]]'s central covenant texts.

## Biographical Overview (textual)

- Serves his people with his own hands; recounts that he has not sought gold or taxed them for luxury (Mosiah 2).
- From a tower, delivers a speech: service to fellow beings is service to God; unprofitable servants; the **natural man** is an enemy to God unless yields to the Holy Spirit; atoning blood of Christ; beggar ethic (Mosiah 3–4).
- People cry for mercy; take upon themselves the **name of Christ**; enter a covenant; become the children of Christ (Mosiah 5–6).
- Names his son Mosiah as successor; dies.

## Theological Significance

- Classic LDS proof-text locus for covenant belonging and the natural man (Mosiah 3:19).
- Links royal leadership to self-emptying service rather than priestcraft.
