# Pier della Vigna

Chancellor and chief minister of Emperor Frederick II, and a founder of Italian
literary language (c. 1200–1249); in [[inferno|Inferno]] 13 he is a soul among the
**suicides**, transformed into a bleeding thorn-tree in the trackless wood of the
seventh circle.

## In the Divine Comedy

Fallen from favour through a court scandal (though, Kirkpatrick notes, Dante allows
him innocent of treachery to Frederick), Pier killed himself by beating his head
against his prison wall. When Dante, prompted by Virgil, snaps a twig, the tree
bleeds and speaks (the episode parallels Virgil's own Polydorus in *Aeneid* 3). Pier
insists on his fidelity — "I swear… I never broke my faith / with my own lord, so
worthy of all honour" — and explains the suicides' contrapasso: having violently
cast off their bodies, at the Last Judgment they alone will not reclaim them, but
hang each soul's flesh on its own thorn-tree. Kirkpatrick draws the parallel to
Dante himself: politically disgraced and lost in a "dark wood," yet refusing Pier's
despairing example — in life and in poetic temper alike.

## Significance

Pier's episode is a meditation on despair as the sin against hope, and on the
self-wounding of a man who made language his craft (his speech is famously knotted
and rhetorical). The thorn-tree imagery faintly evokes the Crown of Thorns and the
Atonement that could have made sense of his suffering.
