# Lugh

**Lugh** is the great **light-, skill-, and kingship-god** of [[celtic-religion|Celtic religion]] — the most
**pan-Celtic** of all the gods, attested across the Continent as **Lugus** (his name preserved in **Lyon /
Lugdunum**, "fort of Lugus"), in Ireland as **Lugh Lámhfhada** ("of the Long Arm"), and in Wales as **Lleu
Llaw Gyffes**. The Romans equated **Lugus with Mercury**, the god of every art. His festival,
**Lughnasadh** (1 August), one of the four Celtic quarter-days, is named for him.

## The many-skilled god — Myths in Detail

### Lugh comes to Tara

In **[[gods-and-fighting-men|Gods and Fighting Men]]**, Lugh is born of two worlds: his father is **Cian** of
the **[[the-tuatha-de-danann|Tuatha Dé Danann]]**, and his mother is **Ethniu (Eithne)**, daughter of the
**Fomorian** giant **[[balor|Balor]]**. The most famous scene is his **arrival at the court of Tara**. The
doorkeeper bars him: no one enters unless he has an art none within already possesses. Lugh offers himself in
turn as **wright, smith, champion, harper, poet, historian, sorcerer, physician, cup-bearer, brazier** — and
to each the doorkeeper answers that the king already has such a man. Then Lugh asks: *"But have you one man who
can do **all** these things?"* — and so he is admitted as **Samildánach**, "the many-skilled," and given
command. He is the **master of every craft at once** — the genius of civilization itself.

### Lugh and the death of Balor

The Tuatha Dé groan under the tribute and tyranny of the **Fomorians**, the chaotic, monstrous powers of sea
and blight. Their champion is **[[balor|Balor]] of the Evil Eye** — a giant whose single great eye, when its
heavy lid is hauled open by his servants, **withers armies with a glance**. A prophecy has foretold that Balor
will be slain by his own **grandson**; he has imprisoned his daughter Ethniu in a tower to prevent it — but
Lugh is that grandson.

At the climactic **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**, the two hosts meet. As Balor's deadly eye is being levered
open, **Lugh casts a sling-stone** (or in some tellings a spear) that strikes the eye **clean through the back
of his skull**, so that its blighting gaze falls upon the **Fomorians themselves** and destroys them. With
Balor's death the Fomorian power is broken and the gods are free. The episode is the Irish **[[the-combat-myth|combat
myth]]**: the bright, ordered, skilled god overthrowing the one-eyed power of chaos and blight (cf.
the slaying of [[tiamat|Tiamat]], [[vritra|Vritra]], the Midgard Serpent).

### Lugh the father; Lleu in Wales

Lugh is the otherworldly **father of the hero Cú Chulainn**, whom he comes to aid on the battlefield. In
Wales his reflex **Lleu Llaw Gyffes** ("the fair one of the skilful hand") is the hero of the **Fourth Branch**
of the [[mabinogion|Mabinogion]]: cursed by his mother **Arianrhod** never to have a name, arms, or a human
wife, he is named and armed by trickery and given the conjured flower-bride **Blodeuwedd**, who betrays him.
He can be killed only under near-impossible conditions; struck, he flies up as an **eagle**, and is found and
restored by **Gwydion** — a death-and-return that marks his old divine nature.

## Significance

Lugh fuses **light, sovereignty, and total skill** — not a narrow sun-god but the patron of every human craft
and of rightful kingship. His killing of Balor makes him the Celtic embodiment of **order defeating chaos**,
and Lughnasadh ties him to the **harvest** and the first-fruits.
