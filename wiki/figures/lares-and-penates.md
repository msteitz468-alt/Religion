# The Lares and Penates

The **Lares** and **Penates** are the distinctively **Roman household gods** — the intimate, protective
spirits at the center of [[roman-household-religion|Roman domestic religion]], with no real Greek equivalent.

## The Two Powers

- **The Lares** — guardian spirits of the **household and the family line**, closely tied to the **ancestral
  dead** and to place. The *Lar familiaris* protected the home; the *Lares Compitales* guarded crossroads and
  neighborhoods. They were honored daily at the home shrine (*lararium*).
- **The Penates** — gods of the **storehouse (*penus*)** and the family's provision and prosperity. The
  **Penates of the Roman state** (the *Penates Publici*) were, by legend, the very gods **Aeneas carried from
  burning Troy** ([[virgil-aeneid|the *Aeneid*]]) to found the Roman line — making household piety and
  national destiny one.

## Significance

The Lares and Penates embody ancient religion as **family, ancestral, and place-bound practice** — the daily,
universal piety of ordinary Romans beneath the great state cult, and a prime comparandum for ancestor and
household religion worldwide.
