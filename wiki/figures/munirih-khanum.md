# Munírih Khánum (1847–1938)

*All content from Peter Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (Oneworld, 2000), lines 23198–23223.*

## Biographical Overview
Wife of [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]]. Originally named Fáṭimih, the name Munírih ('Illumined') was given to her by [[bahaullah|Bahá'u'lláh]]. Her father was Mírzá Muḥammad-'Alí Nahrí, a member of a prominent Iṣfáhání merchant family and uncle of the [[king-and-beloved-of-martyrs|King and Beloved of Martyrs]]. According to the Bahá'í account, her parents had been childless until given a portion of the Báb's food to eat (lines 23198–23211).

Widowed shortly after her first marriage to one of her cousins, she was chosen to marry 'Abdu'l-Bahá by Bahá'u'lláh, who arranged for her to be brought to [[akka|Akká]]. The marriage took place on 8 March 1873. Four children (daughters) survived childhood. She was buried in the Monument Garden close to the Shrine of the Báb (see [[haifa|Haifa]]) (lines 23211–23222).
