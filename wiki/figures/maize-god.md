# The Maize God

The **Maize God** is among the most important deities of [[mesoamerican-religion|Mesoamerica]], for **maize
(corn) was the staff of life and the very substance of humanity** — in the [[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]], the gods
make true human beings out of **maize dough**. As a beautiful, eternally youthful god, he personifies the
crop and the **cycle of its death and rebirth**: he is cut down at harvest and **reborn from the earth** each
year — a classic **dying-and-reviving vegetation god**.

## Maya and Aztec Forms

In Maya thought the maize god is closely tied to the **[[hero-twins|Hero Twins]]**: their **father, Hun
Hunahpu** (the maize lord), was killed in [[xibalba|Xibalba]] and is **resurrected** through his sons, who
recover and revive him from the underworld — the maize sprouting again from the ground where it was buried.
The Aztecs honored maize gods including **Centeotl** and the maize-maiden **Xilonen/Chicomecoatl**. Because
human flesh itself was held to be made of maize, the Maize God stands at the center of Mesoamerican life: the
crop is divine, humanity is its child, and the harvest is a sacred death-and-renewal.
