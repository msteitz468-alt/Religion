# Hephaestus (Vulcan)

**Hephaestus** (Roman **Vulcan**) is the god of **fire, the forge, and metalworking** — the divine smith and
craftsman, the only Olympian who is **lame** and **laborious**.

## Myths and Character

Son of [[hera|Hera]] (in some versions parthenogenetically); cast from Olympus and crippled. The master
artificer who forges the gods' palaces, [[zeus|Zeus's]] thunderbolts, Achilles' shield, and the first woman
**Pandora**. Married to [[aphrodite|Aphrodite]], who betrays him with [[ares|Ares]] — he traps the lovers in
an unbreakable net and exposes them to the gods' laughter. His workshop lies beneath volcanoes (Etna).

## Cult and Roman Identification

Patron of smiths and artisans (Athens's Hephaisteia). As **Vulcan**, an ancient and somewhat fearsome Roman
fire-god, honored at the Vulcanalia to ward off destructive fire — the source of the word "volcano."

## Myths in Detail

**Cast from Olympus.** Accounts differ on his lameness: in one, [[hera|Hera]], ashamed of a weakly or
deformed son, **flung him from Olympus** into the sea, where the sea-goddesses Thetis and Eurynome reared him
for nine years in a cave (he repaid them with beautiful craft); in another, he took Hera's side in a quarrel
and [[zeus|Zeus]] hurled him from heaven — he fell a whole day and landed on Lemnos, crippled.

**Revenge on his mother — the golden throne.** To avenge his casting-out, Hephaestus sent Hera a gift of a
splendid **golden throne**; when she sat in it, hidden, unbreakable bonds seized her, and **no god could free
her**. Only Hephaestus could release her, and he refused to return to Olympus — until [[dionysus|Dionysus]]
got him drunk and led him back on a donkey in a merry procession, and he freed Hera in exchange for
[[aphrodite|Aphrodite's]] hand (and a place among the gods).

**The divine craftsman.** From his volcanic forge come the marvels of the gods: [[zeus|Zeus's]] **aegis** and
thunderbolts, **Achilles' shield** (a whole cosmos worked in metal), the **chains** that trapped Ares and
Aphrodite, the first woman **Pandora** (molded at Zeus's command), Helios's chariot, the bronze giant Talos,
and self-moving golden handmaidens who attend him. The smith is the gods' indispensable maker, his ugliness
and toil the price of his genius.
