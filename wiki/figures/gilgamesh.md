# Gilgamesh

**Gilgamesh** is the legendary **king-hero of Uruk** — **two-thirds god, one-third man** — and the
protagonist of the [[epic-of-gilgamesh|Epic of Gilgamesh]], the great Mesopotamian meditation on
**mortality**. A historical king of Uruk (c. 2700 BCE) probably underlies the legend.

## The Hero's Story

A magnificent but oppressive king, Gilgamesh is matched by the wild man **Enkidu**, created by the gods to be
his equal; after a great wrestling-match they become **inseparable friends** and slay the monster **Humbaba**
and the **Bull of Heaven** (sent by [[ishtar|Ishtar]]). When the gods take **Enkidu's life** in punishment,
Gilgamesh is shattered by grief and terror of death, and **wanders the earth seeking immortality** — reaching
the flood-survivor **Utnapishtim**, only to learn that he cannot escape death (he fails the test of staying
awake, and a **serpent steals** the plant of rejuvenation). He returns to Uruk wiser, finding such immortality
as humans can have in his **deeds and the walls of his city**. (See [[epic-of-gilgamesh|the epic]] for the
full narrative, including the Flood.) After death, Gilgamesh was honored as a **judge and god of the
underworld**.

## Significance

Gilgamesh's quest is one of literature's first and greatest treatments of **human mortality and the search
for meaning** — and its embedded **Flood** story is a famous parallel to [[genesis|Noah]] (see
[[genesis-ancient-near-east|the ANE comparison]]).
