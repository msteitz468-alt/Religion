# Izanagi and Izanami

**Izanagi** ("the Male-Who-Invites") and **Izanami** ("the Female-Who-Invites") are the **creator-couple** of
[[shinto|Shinto]] — the divine brother and sister who **generate the islands of Japan and the kami**, and whose
story turns on the **origin of death and pollution**. Theirs is the foundational narrative of the
[[kojiki|Kojiki]]'s age of the gods.

## Myths in Detail

### The making of the land

The earlier kami command Izanagi and Izanami to "make, consolidate, and give birth to" the drifting land.
Standing on the **Floating Bridge of Heaven**, they **thrust down the jeweled spear Ame-no-nuboko** and stir
the brine; the drops that fall from its tip congeal into the **first island, Onogoro**. Descending there, they
erect a pillar and a hall, and **circle the pillar from opposite sides to wed**. At their first union
**Izanami (the woman) speaks first** — and the child, the malformed **"leech-child" (Hiruko)**, is set adrift
as a failure; the kami declare it wrong for the **female to have spoken first**. Reversing the order so
**Izanagi speaks first**, they then successfully give birth to the **eight great islands of Japan** and to a
host of **kami** — of sea, wind, mountains, trees, and fields.

### Izanami's death and Yomi

Bearing the **fire-kami [[kagutsuchi|Kagutsuchi]]**, **Izanami is fatally burned** and dies — the **first
death** — passing to **Yomi**, the polluted land of the dead. Grief-stricken Izanagi beheads the fire-child
(more kami spring from its blood and body) and **descends to Yomi to bring Izanami back**. She bids him wait
and **not look at her**; but, impatient, he lights a tooth of his comb as a torch and sees her **rotting
corpse, maggot-ridden and crawling with the eight thunder-kami**. Horrified, he **flees**; the enraged Izanami
sends the hags and thunders and a host of Yomi's warriors after him, and at last pursues him herself. Izanagi
escapes through the pass between the living and the dead and **blocks it with a great boulder**. Across it the
two **pronounce their divorce**: Izanami vows to **kill a thousand people a day**, Izanagi to **give life to
fifteen hundred** — and so, the myth says, **death and birth enter the world** in fixed proportion.

### Purification and the great kami

Defiled by his contact with the dead, **Izanagi [[harae|purifies]] himself by bathing** — and from this washing
the supreme kami are born: **[[amaterasu|Amaterasu]]** from his left eye, **[[tsukuyomi|Tsukuyomi]]** from his
right, **[[susanoo|Susanoo]]** from his nose. **Purification is creative**, and the sun, moon, and storm come
from it.

## Significance

Izanagi and Izanami give Shinto its **cosmogony** (generation, not creation-from-nothing), its account of the
**origin of death and of pollution (kegare)**, and the charter of **[[harae|purification]]** — the act that,
washing away the defilement of death, brings forth the greatest gods. Their tale also encodes a patriarchal
ritual norm (the male must speak first).
