# Krishna

**Krishna** is among the most beloved and theologically central figures of [[hinduism|Hinduism]] — the
eighth **avatar** (descent) of Vishnu and, for [[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vaishnava]] traditions, the
supreme God in fully personal form.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **[[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]]** — as Arjuna's charioteer, Krishna delivers the Gita's teaching and
  reveals his cosmic form (ch. 11); the source of his role as supreme teacher.
- **Mahabharata** — the master statesman and divine guide of the Pandavas.
- **Bhagavata Purana / Harivamsa** — the cycle of his birth, the child who steals butter, the
  flute-playing cowherd of Vrindavan, and his love-play (*rasa-lila*) with the *gopis* — the heart of
  Krishna *bhakti*.

## Theological Significance

Krishna embodies the Hindu doctrine of **avatara** — God descending in form "whenever dharma declines"
(Gita 4.7–8) — and anchors the **bhakti** path of loving devotion. In the Vedanta debate his Gita
self-disclosures are claimed by every school: [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] hears non-dual identity,
[[vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Vishishtadvaita]] and [[dvaita-vedanta|Dvaita]] hear a personal God really
distinct from the devotee.

## Reception and Controversies

The relation of the philosopher-statesman Krishna of the Mahabharata to the pastoral, erotic Krishna of
the Puranas is debated (one figure or a fusion?). His historicity is treated as legendary. In modern
devotion (and globally via ISKCON) he is worshipped as the supreme personality of Godhead.
