Zedekiah, born Mattaniah, was the last king of Judah — a son of [[josiah|Josiah]] installed on the
throne by [[nebuchadnezzar|Nebuchadnezzar]] after the deportation of his nephew Jehoiachin. He
rebelled against Babylon, endured an eighteen-month siege of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]], was captured
in flight, saw his sons slaughtered before his eyes, was blinded, and was carried in bronze
fetters to [[babylon|Babylon]]. With him the Davidic monarchy in Judah ends.

## Biographical Overview

After Jehoiachin surrendered to [[nebuchadnezzar|Nebuchadnezzar]] and was deported with the
officials, the craftsmen and smiths, and the temple's gold vessels, the Babylonians installed his
uncle Mattaniah as vassal king and changed his name to Zedekiah — the renaming itself an assertion
of Babylonian sovereignty. He reigned eleven years and is judged in Kings, like Jehoiakim before
him, as having done what was evil in the sight of YHWH.

Zedekiah rebelled against Babylon. [[nebuchadnezzar|Nebuchadnezzar]] came against Jerusalem with
his whole army and built siegeworks around it. The siege lasted about eighteen months, until the
famine in the city broke it: the wall was breached, and the army fled by night through the gate
between the two walls. Zedekiah was overtaken in the plains near [[jericho|Jericho]], his army
scattering from him, and was brought to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. There sentence was passed. **His
sons were slaughtered before his eyes; then his eyes were put out**, and he was bound in bronze
fetters and taken to [[babylon|Babylon]] — the last thing he saw being the killing of his heirs
(2 Kgs 25:1–7).

What followed completed the destruction: Nebuzaradan burned the temple, the palace, and every
great house, broke down the walls of Jerusalem, and cut up the bronze pillars and the bronze sea
for their metal; the chief priests and sixty men of the land were executed at Riblah. Gedaliah was
appointed governor at Mizpah, assassinated by Ishmael of the royal line, and the remnant fled to
Egypt.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **2 Kings 24:17–25:21**: the installation and renaming; the rebellion; the siege, famine, and
  breach; the flight and capture near [[jericho|Jericho]]; the killing of his sons, his blinding,
  and his deportation; the burning of the temple and the dismantling of the city.
- **2 Chronicles 36:11–21**: the parallel account. The Chronicler adds that Zedekiah did not
  humble himself before the prophet Jeremiah, that he broke his oath sworn by God to
  Nebuchadnezzar, that the leaders and priests were exceedingly unfaithful (*maʿal*) following all
  the abominations of the nations, and that the exile lasted until the land had enjoyed its
  sabbaths.
- **[[ezekiel-prophet|Ezekiel]]**: the prophet's oracles are dated from Jehoiachin's exile and
  address the final years of Judah under Zedekiah, including the sign-acts of the siege and the
  covenant-breaking of the king.
- **Jeremiah**: Jeremiah's ministry runs through Zedekiah's reign; the king repeatedly consults
  him while refusing his counsel.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[iain-provan|Provan]] (NIBC Kings, as recorded on [[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]])**: Zedekiah's
  reign falls in Provan's "endgame" block, in which three deportations dismantle Solomonic glory.
  Provan's hope in Kings is placed not in Zedekiah but in **Jehoiachin** at Babylon's table — the
  "slender thread" by which, in the wiki's summary of his reading, "grace may triumph over law"
  (cf. 2 Sam 7:15–16). Zedekiah is thus the terminus of the judgment line, not of the promise.
- **[[peter-leithart|Leithart]] (BTCB Chronicles, as recorded on
  [[1-2-chronicles|1–2 Chronicles]])**: Chronicles ends not with Jehoiachin's ration but with
  [[cyrus|Cyrus]]'s decree; and Leithart's *maʿal* chain — the sacrilege term running from Achan
  through Saul, Asa, Uzziah, Ahaz and [[manasseh|Manasseh]] — terminates at the exile itself
  (2 Chr 36:14), the verse describing Zedekiah's generation.

No Jewish, Islamic, or patristic reception of Zedekiah has been ingested into this wiki; none is
asserted here.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Zedekiah's mutilation is the narrative image of the end of the monarchy: the Davidic king is
deprived of heirs and of sight and led away in chains from the city he could not hold. Kings makes
this the terminus of the judgment announced under [[manasseh|Manasseh]] and unaverted by
[[josiah|Josiah]]'s reform; Chronicles frames it as the exhaustion of the land's sabbaths and the
prelude to return. The break is dynastic and cultic at once — no king, no temple, no city wall.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Correlation with Babylonian records**: the campaigns of [[nebuchadnezzar|Nebuchadnezzar]]
  against Judah are among the more externally corroborated events of the monarchy; this wiki has
  not ingested a source treating the correlation in detail, so no specific synchronism is asserted
  here.
- **586 or 587 BCE**: the absolute date of Jerusalem's fall is disputed on chronological grounds;
  recorded as an open question.
- **Kings vs Chronicles emphasis**: Kings ends with Jehoiachin's release and rations in Babylon;
  Chronicles ends with Cyrus's decree. The wiki's [[1-2-kings|1–2 Kings]] page records both
  endings "without harmonization," and the difference bears directly on how each book construes
  what survives Zedekiah.
- **Zedekiah's agency**: whether he is portrayed as a weak king overborne by his officials or as
  a covenant-breaker judged for oath-breaking depends on which account and which prophetic corpus
  is read; both portrayals are present in the primary sources.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Zedekiah anchors the exilic problem that shapes much later Jewish and Christian reading: how the
[[davidic-covenant|Davidic covenant]] can stand when the last Davidic king is blinded and
childless in Babylon. Within this wiki's ingested sources, that question is answered differently
by Provan (Jehoiachin as the thread of grace) and by Leithart's Chronicles (Cyrus as the
unexpected successor to the royal role) — recorded as two readings, not merged.
