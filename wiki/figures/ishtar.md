# Ishtar (Inanna)

**Inanna** (Sumerian; Akkadian **Ishtar**) is the most important goddess of [[mesopotamian-religion|
Mesopotamia]] — the paradoxical deity of **both love/sex and war**, "**Queen of Heaven**," and the planet
**Venus** (the morning and evening star). Passionate, ambitious, and dangerous, patroness of **Uruk**.

## Myths in Detail — the Descent to the Underworld

Inanna's greatest myth is her **Descent**: the Queen of Heaven resolved to enter the **Great Below**, the
realm of her sister **[[ereshkigal|Ereshkigal]]**, queen of the dead. To pass its **seven gates**, she had to
surrender at each one a garment and an emblem of her power — crown, jewels, rod, robe — until she stood in the
underworld **naked and stripped of all might**. There the judges fixed her with the **"eye of death,"** and
Ereshkigal hung her **corpse on a hook** to rot.

After three days and nights, her faithful minister **Ninshubur** appealed to the gods. [[enlil|Enlil]] and
[[anu|Anu]] refused, but the wise **[[enki|Enki]]** fashioned two sefless creatures who slipped into the
underworld, won Ereshkigal's gratitude, and revived Inanna with the **food and water of life**. But the law
of the underworld is absolute: **no one leaves without providing a substitute.** Demons followed Inanna up to
seize one. She spared those who had mourned her — but found her husband **Dumuzi (Tammuz)** seated on his
throne in fine robes, **not mourning at all**. In fury she handed **Dumuzi** to the demons. His devoted sister
**Geshtinanna** offered to share his fate, so that each spends **half the year in the underworld** — the
**dying-and-returning vegetation god** whose descent and return mark the seasons (the wilting and greening of
the land).

## The Bull of Heaven

In the [[epic-of-gilgamesh|Epic of Gilgamesh]], Ishtar propositions the hero; when he scornfully rejects her
(reciting how she destroyed her former lovers), she demands the **Bull of Heaven** from [[anu|Anu]] to ravage
Uruk — which Gilgamesh and Enkidu slay, an offense for which the gods condemn Enkidu to death.
