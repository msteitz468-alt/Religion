# Ishmael

**Ishmael** (Hebrew *Yishma'el*, "God hears"), the firstborn son of [[abraham|Abraham]] by **Hagar**, Sarah's Egyptian servant, is the progenitor of the Ishmaelites and, in Islamic tradition, the ancestor of the northern Arabs and of the line leading to Muhammad. In Genesis he is circumcised at thirteen, blessed to become "a great nation" of twelve princes, yet set outside the covenant, which passes to [[isaac|Isaac]]. His status — Abraham's son but not the covenant heir — makes him a pivotal figure in the divergence of the Abrahamic traditions, and the focus of one of their sharpest disagreements (the identity of the near-sacrificed son).

---

## Biographical Overview

- **Birth to Hagar** (Gen 16): the barren [[sarah|Sarah]] gives Hagar to Abraham; pregnant Hagar flees Sarah's harshness; the angel at the spring promises a son, **Ishmael**, "a wild ass of a man, his hand against everyone."
- **Circumcision** (Gen 17): at thirteen, Ishmael is circumcised with Abraham and the household; God blesses him to father **twelve princes** and a great nation, while establishing the covenant with Isaac.
- **The expulsion** (Gen 21): after Isaac's weaning, Sarah demands Hagar and Ishmael be sent away; in the wilderness, God hears the boy's cry, opens a well, and renews the promise.
- **Later notices** (Gen 25): his twelve sons; his presence with Isaac at Abraham's burial; his death at 137.

---

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 16, 17, 21, 25** — birth, circumcision, expulsion, descendants.
- **Galatians 4:21–31** — Paul's allegory: **Hagar/Ishmael = Sinai, slavery, the present Jerusalem**, "born according to the flesh," contrasted with Sarah/Isaac, "born through promise."
- **Qur'an** — **Ismaʿil**, a prophet and patient one (Q 19:54–55; 21:85); with Ibrahim he raises the foundations of the Kaaba (Q 2:125–129); in much (not all) Islamic tradition he is the son offered in the near-sacrifice (Q 37:99–113).

---

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Jewish**: the son outside the covenant; rabbinic tradition is ambivalent — sometimes casting Ishmael as wild or idolatrous (the "mocking" of Gen 21:9), sometimes crediting his repentance and honoring his role at Abraham's burial. "Ishmaelites" becomes a designation for Arab peoples and, in the medieval period, for Islam.
- **Christian**: through **Galatians 4**, Ishmael becomes the allegorical type of the "flesh," the old covenant of Sinai, and slavery — over against the child of promise. This typology shaped centuries of supersessionist reading.
- **Islamic**: Ismaʿil is a revered prophet, the father of the Arabs and ancestor of Muhammad, co-founder of the Kaaba with Ibrahim, and (in the dominant tradition) the son of the sacrifice — making him central to Islamic identity and to the Hajj (the running between Safa and Marwa recalls Hagar's search for water).
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)** — see below.

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar)

The Zohar (Lekh Lekha) reads Ishmael through the lens of **circumcision and grade**:

- **Circumcision without *peri'ah***: Ishmael was circumcised (Gen 17), so he bears a relation to the covenant of [[circumcision|Yesod]] — but, in the Kabbalistic reading, an *incomplete* one. The full covenant (with the uncovering, *peri'ah*, instituted later for Israel) belongs to [[isaac|Isaac]] and his line; Ishmael's circumcision attaches him to a lower, outer grade rather than to the inner holiness. This is the mystical correlate of his being "a great nation" yet not the covenant heir.
- **Son of the servant / the outer side**: as the son of Hagar the Egyptian, Ishmael is associated with the more external grades — close enough to Abraham ([[abraham|Ḥesed]]) to be blessed and circumcised, but not the channel of the holy seed, which runs through Sarah's son.
- The Ishmael/Isaac and Hagar/Sarah polarities feed the Zohar's broader mapping of **holy line vs. outer/other side**, paralleling (without simply equating to) the [[esau|Esau]]/Jacob split.

### Pritzker Vol. II reingest anchors (2026-07-18)

Locus-anchored densification from Pritzker *Zohar* II body (Matt English).

**Abram-phase lower engendering.** In the Zohar body (1:90a–90b), “‘Abram does not engender; Abraham does engender.’… as long as he was Abram, he engendered below; once… Abraham and entered the covenant, he engendered above.” Ishmael is the named instance of that lower birth: “Look, he engendered Ishmael while he was still Abram!” Va-Yera: “Ishmael issued from him, since Abraham was then uncircumcised—being situated below, not yet perfected by the sign of the holy covenant”; once circumcised, Isaac issues as “holy seed… fire from water” (1:109b–110a). Annunciation contrast: “Behold, a son!—not Ishmael; this is a son who honors his father” (1:103a).

**Metsaḥeq / expulsion.** After Isaac’s birth, “as long as Ishmael remained in Abraham’s house he did not attain a name… the son of Hagar the Egyptian” — gold vs. scoria (1:118b). Abraham says “his son”; Sarah says “son of Hagar.” R. Shim’on praises Sarah: “Seeing him *metsaḥeq*, reveling, in idolatry… not the son of Abraham… reverting to the pattern of his mother” (1:118b–119a). No share of faith / inheritance in this world or the coming world; God consents. “Through Isaac seed will be named for you—not through Ishmael” (1:119a). Hagar *va-teta* = strayed into delusion/idolatry; God hears the *boy*, not her voice; “there where he is” = under age twenty of the higher court (1:119a).

**End-time note.** In the Zohar body (1:119a), within R. Shim’on’s messianic chronology: “The Ishmaelites are destined… to incite all nations… to attack Jerusalem.”

**Toledot / Ḥayyei Sarah asides.** Generations contrast: Ishmael’s twelve princes vs. Isaac’s two sons, with Jacob surpassing (1:135a). “Years of Ishmael’s life endured” because “he returned to God” (1:124a–124b).

### Hamilton (NICOT) reception (2026-07-18)

Attributed to [[victor-hamilton|Victor P. Hamilton]] ([[hamilton-genesis-1-17-nicot|Genesis 1–17]], [[hamilton-genesis-18-50-nicot|18–50]]).

- **Birth oracle (16):** name *Yishmaʿel* explained with Yahweh attentive to humiliation (// 1 Sam 1:20 pattern of El-name + tetragrammaton etymology). Destiny: “wild ass of the steppe” (derogatory desert nomad); hand against every man; *weʿal-penê* as defiance. Born “for Abram” not “for them” — Sarai absent from naming unit.
- **Akedah continuity:** ch. 22 thematically continuous with dismissal of Hagar/Ishmael (21:8–21) — both loss of a son, early-morning departure, question whether God can protect the endangered child.
- **Wilderness (21):** only if one follows LXX does Abraham place a teenage Ishmael on Hagar’s back. Parallel eye-opening: God opens Hagar’s eyes to the well as Abraham lifts eyes to the ram.
- **Death/tôlĕdôt (25):** structure of 25:1–11 // 25:12–18; twelve chieftains parallel twelve Aramean (22), Edomite (36), and Israelite tribes. Verb “blessed” used of Isaac (v. 11), not of Ishmael — yet twelve offspring illustrate what constitutes blessing. Longevity 137; outlives Abraham ~48 years. Isaac and Ishmael jointly bury Abraham (possible reconciliation // Esau–Jacob at Isaac’s funeral).
- **Gal 4:** Hamilton’s full treatment after Gen 21: Hagar/Ishmael = flesh / Sinai / present Jerusalem; not a successive old-vs-new covenant scheme.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Ishmael is the great **"what might have been"** of the covenant narrative — Abraham's beloved firstborn ("O that Ishmael might live before you!", Gen 17:18), genuinely blessed, yet not the chosen line. He embodies the painful selectivity of election: God hears and provides for Ishmael (his very name and the wilderness rescue turn on God's *hearing*), even as the covenant passes to another. Across the three traditions he becomes the hinge on which their genealogical and theological self-understandings diverge.

---

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- The **identity of the son of the near-sacrifice** (Isaac in Genesis; predominantly Ishmael in Islamic tradition) is a standing Jewish/Christian–Islamic dispute (see [[isaac|Isaac]] and [[akedah-interpretation|The Akedah]]).
- The **Ishmaelites = Arabs** equation is a traditional genealogical construction, not a critical-historical datum.
- The **expulsion of Hagar and Ishmael** is a major focus of feminist, womanist (Hagar as the abused outsider), and interreligious readings.
