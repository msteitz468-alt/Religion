# Adam

The first human being in the Genesis creation narratives — progenitor of humanity, the first to transgress a divine command, and across all three Abrahamic traditions the bearer of immense theological weight: as the image of God, as the source of mortality and (in Christian theology) of sin, and in [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] as the microcosm whose body maps the divine [[sefirot|sefirotic]] structure and whose first sin was a cosmic catastrophe. This page focuses on the figure's portrayal across traditions, with particular attention to the Zoharic reading introduced to the wiki.

**A terminological caution**: distinguish **Adam ha-Rishon** (the biblical first man, the subject of this page) from **[[sefirot|Adam Qadmon]]** (the "Primordial Human" — the divine anthropos whose body is the configuration of the ten sefirot, a cosmological structure, not the Eden figure). The two are related by the doctrine that the earthly Adam was created "in the image" of the supernal/divine anthropos, but they are not identical.

---

## Biographical Overview (Textual)

The Genesis account is itself layered:
- **Genesis 1:26–28** (the Priestly creation account): humankind (*adam*, collective) created male and female together, in the image of God (*b'tselem Elohim*), blessed to be fruitful and to rule the earth
- **Genesis 2:4b–25** (the Eden narrative): YHWH Elohim forms *ha-adam* from the dust of the ground (*adamah*), breathes life into him, places him in the Garden of Eden to till and keep it, forbids the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and forms the woman from his side
- **Genesis 3**: the transgression — the serpent, the eating of the fruit, the opening of the eyes, the expulsion from Eden, the curses (mortality, painful toil, painful childbirth)
- **Genesis 4–5**: father of Cain, Abel, and Seth; lives 930 years and dies

The very name *adam* puns on *adamah* ("ground/earth") and *dam* ("blood"), and functions as both a proper name and the collective noun "humankind" — an ambiguity that every tradition exploits.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

### Jewish (rabbinic)
Rabbinic Midrash develops Adam extensively: his cosmic size (filling the world from end to end before being diminished after the sin), his radiant garments of light (*or*, later exchanged for garments of skin, *or* spelled differently — a famous wordplay), Adam's repentance, and the legend of **Lilith** as his first wife before Eve (rooted in the two creation accounts). Crucially, rabbinic Judaism does **not** develop a doctrine of inherited original sin; Adam's sin brought mortality but each person remains responsible for their own sin. The *yetser ha-ra* (evil inclination) is a feature of human nature, not a transmitted guilt.

### Christian
Paul makes Adam the typological hinge of salvation history (see [[paul|Paul]]):
- **Romans 5:12–21**: "sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin" — Adam as the figure (*typos*) of the one to come; Christ as the second Adam who reverses Adam's trespass
- **1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49**: "as in Adam all die, so in Christ shall all be made alive"; Adam the "man of dust," Christ the "life-giving spirit" and "man of heaven"
Augustine systematized this into the doctrine of **original sin** — Adam's guilt and corrupted nature transmitted to all his descendants — which became central to Western Christian theology. See [[original-sin|Original Sin]] (if present) and [[fall-of-man|the Fall]].

### Islamic
In the Qur'an, Adam (آدم) is the first prophet and God's vicegerent (*khalifa*) on earth (Q 2:30–39). God teaches Adam "the names of all things," demonstrating human superiority over the angels, whom God commands to prostrate before Adam — all do except Iblis (Satan). Adam and his wife are tempted by Iblis and eat from the tree, but in the Qur'anic account Adam **repents and is forgiven** (Q 2:37); there is no doctrine of inherited original sin, and the lapse occurs in the Garden before the descent to earth. Adam's dignity as prophet is preserved.

### Kabbalistic (the Zohar)
The Zohar reads Adam at the **Sod** level as both microcosm and cosmic actor:
- **Adam as the image of the sefirot**: the human being was created in the image of the supernal *Adam Qadmon*; the limbs and faculties of Adam correspond to the ten sefirot (see [[sefirot|Ten Sefirot]] — Adam Qadmon). Adam is the microcosm in whom the entire divine structure is reflected, which is the deep meaning of *b'tselem Elohim*
- **The garments of light**: before the sin, Adam was clothed in supernal light and could see "from one end of the world to the other"; he beheld by the light of the first day (the primordial light, *or*, later hidden away for the righteous)
- **The first sin as cosmic divorce**: the Zohar's most distinctive reading. Adam's sin was, in its inner meaning, a **separation of the sefirot** — specifically a sundering of the divine union. By "eating from the tree" Adam separated the Tree of Knowledge ([[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] / Malkhut) from the Tree of Life (Tif'eret), worshipping or grasping the Shekhinah in isolation from her divine source. This act introduced division into the Godhead as experienced below, empowered the [[sitra-ahra|Sitra Aḥra]], drew the [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] toward exile, and brought death into the world. The expulsion from Eden is simultaneously the Shekhinah's estrangement
- **Adam and the serpent**: the serpent is the instrument of the Sitra Aḥra (Samael riding the serpent, with Lilith); the sin injected the "filth" (*zuhama*) of the serpent into Eve and into humanity, a defilement removed from Israel only at Sinai (and renewed in each generation)
- **Cosmic repair**: subsequent sacred history — the patriarchs, Sinai, the commandments, and finally the Messiah — is the long *tikkun* (repair) of Adam's primordial rupture, the gradual restoration of the divine union he sundered

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Adam is the pivot on which each tradition's anthropology turns:
- **Image of God**: the ground of human dignity and, in Kabbalah, of the human being as a microcosm of the divine structure (the basis of theurgy — what a human does in the body affects the divine body)
- **Origin of death**: universally, Adam's sin brings mortality
- **Original sin**: a specifically (Western) Christian development; absent in Judaism and Islam
- **Cosmic divorce / the breaking of unity**: the specifically Kabbalistic development — Adam's sin as an event *within* God

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- **Literal vs. symbolic Adam**: critical scholarship reads the Genesis Adam narratives as etiological myth (explaining mortality, toil, the human condition) drawing on ANE parallels; traditional readings range from strict historicity to allegory (Philo already read Adam allegorically as Mind/*nous*). See [[philo-of-alexandria|Philo of Alexandria]], whose allegorical Adam (mind) and Eve (sense-perception) prefigure later allegorizing.
- **One Adam or collective humanity**: the dual creation accounts and the noun/name ambiguity generate perennial debate (the androgynous primal Adam of Midrash; the "two Adams" of Paul).
- **Inherited sin**: a live inter-religious dividing line — Christianity affirms it, Judaism and Islam reject it.

---

## Influence on Commentary Traditions

Adam grounds Pauline typology (the Adam–Christ structure of Romans and 1 Corinthians), Augustinian original-sin theology, Philonic and Origenist allegory (Adam as mind/soul), rabbinic Midrash on the primal human, and the entire Kabbalistic doctrine of microcosm and cosmic repair. Few figures generate so much theology from so few verses.
