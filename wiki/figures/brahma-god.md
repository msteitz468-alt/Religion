# Brahmā

*Shell page — to be populated from ongoing ingests.*

**Brahmā** (not to be confused with *Brahman*, the impersonal absolute of Advaita philosophy) is the Creator god of Hinduism, one of the three members of the **Trimurti** alongside [[vishnu|Viṣṇu]] (Preserver) and Śiva (Destroyer). He is called "Grandfather" (*Pitāmaha*) and is the progenitor of gods, sages, and — in the epic texts — also of demons through various lineages. In the [[ramayana|Rāmāyaṇa]], Brahmā's role is primarily as the source of boons granted to powerful beings: Rāvaṇa's boon of invulnerability to gods was granted by Brahmā, setting the conditions for Viṣṇu's incarnation as [[rama|Rāma]]. Brahmā also appears as the one who prompted [[valmiki|Vālmīki]] to compose the Rāmāyaṇa. Despite his centrality in epic cosmology, Brahmā receives little active cult worship in contemporary Hinduism — a paradox explained in myth by a curse.

**Note on naming:** *Brahmā* (masculine, with long final ā) is the Creator god. *Brahman* (neuter, often written without the long ā) is the impersonal cosmic principle (Absolute) in Upanishadic and Vedantic philosophy. *Brahmin* (*Brāhmaṇa*) is the priestly caste. These are three distinct concepts despite their etymological relationship.
