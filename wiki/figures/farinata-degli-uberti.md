# Farinata degli Uberti

Leader of the Florentine [[guelphs-and-ghibellines|Ghibellines]] (d. 1264), met by
[[dante-alighieri|Dante]] among the heretics (Epicureans) in [[inferno|Inferno]] 10.
One of the "dominant personalities" Kirkpatrick names as drawing nineteenth-century
readers to the poem.

## In the Divine Comedy

Farinata rises from his burning tomb "from the waist up… as though he held all Hell
in high disdain" (10:34–36), interrupting Dante to ask his ancestry — and, learning
Dante's forebears were Guelphs whom he twice scattered, trades political barbs even
in damnation. His patriotism is the moral knot of the canto: after the Ghibelline
victory at **Montaperti** (1260), when allies wished to raze Florence, "I was
alone… and boldly stood to speak in her defence" (10:91–93). Yet he displays the
damned's peculiar **myopia** — able to see the distant future but blind to the
present — and so cannot answer whether his line will return. In the same tomb lies
Cavalcante de' Cavalcanti, father of Dante's friend Guido, who collapses when Dante
uses a past-tense verb that he takes to mean Guido is dead.

## Significance

Farinata embodies the tragic grandeur Dante grants even to the damned: a heretic by
doctrine, yet a figure of civic courage and noble bearing. The encounter measures
the cost of Florentine faction and stages Dante's recurring tension between
admiration for a soul's virtues and judgment of its sin.
