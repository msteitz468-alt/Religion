# Aisha bint Abi Bakr

**Aisha bint Abi Bakr** was the daughter of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (the first caliph after [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s death) and a wife of the Prophet. She is designated **Umm al-Mu'minin** (Mother of the Faithful) in Islamic tradition. She is one of the most prolific transmitters of hadith in the entire Sunni corpus — in [[sahih-al-bukhari]] alone she appears as narrator in at least 35 hadiths within Book 26 (Hajj) and extensively across Books 1–50.

**Coverage note**: This page is drafted primarily from Sahih al-Bukhari, Vols. 1–3 (Books 1–50). Her roles in the Farewell Pilgrimage, ritual law, and the early revelation narratives are well documented here. Biographical details — her marriage to the Prophet, the slander incident (*hadith al-ifk*), the First Civil War (*fitna*), and the Battle of the Camel — require other sources not yet ingested.

---

## Biographical Overview

The biographical details visible in the current source range concern primarily: (1) her role as transmitter of private prophetic practice, and (2) her participation in the Farewell Pilgrimage (Hajjat al-Wada').

- **Parentage**: daughter of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, the Prophet's closest companion and first caliph.
- **Epithet**: *Umm al-Mu'minin* (Mother of the Faithful) — the honorific given to all the Prophet's wives by Qur'an 33:6.
- **Role as narrator**: Her proximity to the Prophet in private life made her the authoritative source for traditions about his personal habits, private prayers, and domestic arrangements. The formula "Aisha said" or "the wife of the Prophet" introduces her testimony throughout Bukhari.

## The Revelation Narrative (Vol. 1, Book 1)

Aisha transmits the foundational account of the Prophet's first revelation at the cave of Hira (Vol. 1, Book 1, No. 3). She reports:

- The angel came to Muhammad and commanded him to read; he replied "I do not know how to read." The angel squeezed him three times and then recited Qur'an 96:1–3.
- Muhammad returned trembling; Aisha reports him saying "Cover me! Cover me!" (*zammiluni*).
- She describes accompanying him to her cousin **Waraqa ibn Nawfal** (a Christian convert, reader of the Hebrew Gospel), who identified the being as "the same one who kept the secrets (*al-namus*)" sent to Moses.
- When revelation paused (*fatra*), the Prophet was troubled; Aisha's account implies ongoing anxiety during the interval.

This account makes Aisha the primary transmitter of the most important event in Islamic history — the first revelation — despite not having been present at the time (she narrates it from Muhammad). The narrative is foundational to the entire Sunni understanding of prophethood.

## Role in the Farewell Pilgrimage (Vol. 2, Book 26)

Aisha participated in the Prophet's last Hajj and her testimony provides the most detailed single witness record of the Farewell Pilgrimage in Bukhari.

### Menstrual Status and Its Legal Significance

The most frequently recurring theme in Aisha's Hajj hadiths concerns her menses during the Farewell Pilgrimage (Nos. 627, 631–632, 633, 702, 712, 713, 815). She arrived in Mecca while menstruating and could not perform Tawaf of the Ka'ba or Sa'y between Safa and Marwa. The Prophet told her: "Undo and comb your head hair, and assume Ihram for Hajj (only) and leave the Umra." She performed all other Hajj ceremonies and was then sent with her brother Abdur-Rahman to Tan'im (outside the sanctuary) to assume Ihram for Umra as compensation: "This Umra is instead of your missed one." This became a legal precedent: menstruating women perform all Hajj ceremonies except Tawaf of the Ka'ba until they are clean (No. 712).

### Key Testimony on Hajj Practices

- **Talbiya formula** (No. 622): Aisha confirmed the exact wording of the Prophet's Talbiya: "Labbaika Allahumma Labbaik, Labbaika la sharika Laka labbaik, Inna-l-hamda wan-ni'mata Laka walmu'lk, La sharika Laka."
- **Scent application** (No. 612): "I used to scent Allah's Apostle when he wanted to assume Ihram and also on finishing Ihram before the Tawaf round the Ka'ba." She also saw "the glitter of the scent in the parting of the hair of the Prophet while he was Muhrim" (No. 611).
- **Garlands for the Hadi** (Nos. 753–762): "I twisted with my own hands the garlands for the Budn of the Prophet." The Prophet sent the Hadi (sacrificial animals) ahead while remaining in Medina as a non-Muhrim; Aisha asserted this did not make things forbidden for him — a contested legal point against Ibn Abbas's position (No. 757).
- **Three types of Hajj** (No. 633): Aisha confirmed that the community performed three types: Umra only, both Hajj and Umra, and Hajj only. "Allah's Apostle assumed Ihram for Hajj."
- **The Ka'ba and Ibrahim's foundations** (Nos. 653–656): Aisha transmitted the Prophet's statement that the Quraysh had shortened the Ka'ba from Ibrahim's original foundations for lack of funds, and that he would have rebuilt on those foundations had the people not recently converted. Ibn Umar endorses this account.
- **The round wall (al-Hijr)** (No. 654): The Prophet confirmed to Aisha that al-Hijr is part of the Ka'ba.
- **Safa and Marwa** (No. 706): Aisha corrected her nephew Urwa's misinterpretation of Qur'an 2:158. He thought the verse made Sa'y optional; she explained it was revealed for Ansar who had assumed Ihram for an idol. "Surely, Allah's Apostle set the tradition of Tawaf between Safa and Marwa, so nobody is allowed to omit the Tawaf between them."

## Zakat Transmissions (Vol. 2, Book 24)

- A woman came to Aisha with two daughters; Aisha had only one date and gave it to her. The Prophet said this act would shield the woman from Hell-Fire (No. 499).
- The Prophet told his wives that "Whoever has the longest hand" (i.e., most charitable) would be first to follow him in death; this referred to Zainab bint Jahsh (No. 501).
- Multiple hadiths (Nos. 506, 518, 520–521): when a woman gives from her husband's food in charity without wasting it, she and her husband both receive equal reward.
- Aisha purchased Barira (a slave-girl) to manumit her. Meat given to Barira as charity was not charity for the Prophet's household: "It is an object of charity for Barira but a gift for us" (No. 570).

## Hermeneutical Role

Aisha in these hadiths frequently functions as an authoritative corrector and clarifier of prophetic practice:
- Corrected Urwa's misreading of Qur'an 2:158 on Safa and Marwa (No. 706).
- Contested Ibn Abbas's position that garlanding the Hadi made things forbidden for the Prophet (No. 757).
- Provided the authoritative account of the Ka'ba's Abrahamic foundations (Nos. 653–656).
- Transmitted the first revelation narrative (Vol. 1, Book 1, No. 3).

Her testimony in Book 26 is repeatedly introduced with "Aisha said" or "the wife of the Prophet" — a formula signaling her unique authority as both eyewitness and intimate companion of the Prophet.
