# Louis Gregory

*Populated from [[peter-smith|Smith, A Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000)]]. All claims are from that source unless marked otherwise.*

## Biographical Overview

Louis Gregory was a prominent African-American Bahá'í. Born of slave parents in South Carolina, he later graduated from Howard University with a degree in law. He became a Bahá'í in Washington DC in 1909 (lines 15480–15481).

## Role in Racial Justice

Already an advocate of racial equality before becoming a Bahá'í, Gregory encountered some initial opposition in the local Bahá'í community. He was supported by [[abdu-l-baha|'Abdu'l-Bahá]], who during his visit to Washington DC (1912) deliberately seated Gregory next to him at a formal luncheon in clear disregard of social convention (lines 15483–15490).

'Abdu'l-Bahá subsequently encouraged him to marry Louisa Mathew (1866–1956), a white Englishwoman — this being the first Black-white marriage in the American Bahá'í community, an act requiring considerable courage given the social conditions of the time (lines 15492–15501).

Gregory undertook extensive travels to promote the Faith, particularly in the southern states. He played a leading role in the advocacy of racial equality by the Bahá'ís, and for a number of years was the only Black Bahá'í on the American national spiritual assembly (lines 15503–15513).

## Post-Mortem Honors

[[shoghi-effendi|Shoghi Effendi]] posthumously named him a **Hand of the Cause of God** (line 15514). Gregory is thus one of the Hands named posthumously (named 5 August 1951, per the list at line 16082).

## Significance

Louis Gregory is an emblematic figure in the Bahá'í commitment to racial unity. His life and marriage demonstrated in practice the Bahá'í principle of the oneness of humanity against the prevailing racial segregation of early 20th-century America. His posthumous elevation to Hand of the Cause represents the Faith's highest recognition of his contributions.
