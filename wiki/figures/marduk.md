# Marduk

**Marduk** is the **king of the gods of [[babylonian-religion|Babylon]]** — the young champion who defeated
chaos and created the world, raised to supremacy as Babylon itself rose to dominate Mesopotamia. Son of
[[enki|Ea]], patron of Babylon, his title is **Bel**, "Lord."

## Myths in Detail — the Champion of Order

Marduk's charter is the **[[enuma-elish|Enuma Elish]]**: when the chaos-dragon **[[tiamat|Tiamat]]** and her
monster-army threatened the gods and none dared face her, the young Marduk agreed to fight **on condition that
he be made supreme king**, his decree irrevocable. The gods enthroned him; armed with the four winds, a net,
a bow, and a storm-chariot, he **netted Tiamat, drove the winds into her, and split her in two**, forming the
**sky and earth** from her body and ordering the stars, moon, and sun. He set the gods in their stations, had
Kingu slain to make **humanity** from his blood, built **Babylon**, and received the **fifty names** that sum
up all divine power. So Marduk is the model of the **divine king who imposes cosmic order (ma'at-like) on
chaos** — and his elevation over the older [[enlil|Enlil]] mirrors Babylon's political rise.

## Significance

The Enuma Elish was recited at Babylon's **New Year (Akitu)** festival, where the king's rule was renewed
before Marduk and creation re-enacted. Marduk's victory-creation pattern (order over the chaos-waters) is a
central ANE comparandum for the imagery of [[genesis|Genesis 1]] and the biblical "sea-dragon" motifs
(Leviathan, Rahab). The Assyrians replaced him with their own **[[ashur|Ashur]]**.
