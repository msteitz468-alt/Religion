# The Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama)

**Siddhartha Gautama**, the historical Buddha ("Awakened One") and founder of [[buddhism|Buddhism]].

## Biographical Overview

Traditional accounts: a prince of the Shakya clan (hence **Shakyamuni**) in the Himalayan foothills,
sheltered from suffering, who at 29 encountered the "four sights" (an old man, a sick man, a corpse, an
ascetic), renounced his palace life, practiced extreme asceticism, abandoned it for the Middle Way, and
attained **awakening** (*bodhi*) under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya. He then taught the
[[four-noble-truths|Dharma]] for ~45 years and died (*parinirvana*) at ~80. Dating is disputed
(traditional c. 563–483 BCE vs. a revised c. 480–400 BCE).

## Primary Source Appearances

- **[[tipitaka|Pali Canon]]** — the discourses (*suttas*) present him as a human teacher who, by his
  own effort, woke to the nature of reality; the closest to a historical portrait.
- **The Mahayana sutras** ([[lotus-sutra|Lotus]], etc.) — present a cosmic, trans-historical Buddha
  whose earthly life is itself *upaya* (skillful means), one of countless buddhas.

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **[[theravada|Theravada]]** — a supremely awakened *human* teacher and the model arhat; not a god,
  not to be prayed to for salvation.
- **[[mahayana|Mahayana]]** — the *trikaya* (three-body) doctrine: the historical Buddha is the
  "transformation body" of an eternal buddha-reality; devotion to many buddhas and bodhisattvas.
- **[[vajrayana|Vajrayana]]** — the Buddha as accessible through tantric deity yoga and guru lineage.

## Theological/Doctrinal Significance

His awakening defines the goal (liberation by insight, not by a creator's grace); his teaching of
[[anatta|non-self]] and [[four-noble-truths|the Four Noble Truths]] founds a **non-theistic**
soteriology — the wiki's first founder figure who is explicitly *not* a prophet of, or mediator to, a
God.

## Historicity and Controversies

His historical existence is widely accepted; the biography is heavily legendary, and the dates,
birthplace specifics, and the historical kernel of the awakening narrative are debated. The
trans-historical Mahayana Buddha vs. the human Buddha of the Pali suttas is itself a tradition-dividing
portrait.

## Dasgupta's Account (Vol. I, Ch. V)

[[dasgupta-history-of-indian-philosophy|Dasgupta]] places the Buddha's birth "in or about 560 BCE" in the
**Lumbinī Grove near Kapilavastu** (the Nepal terai), son of **Suddhodana**, a prince of the **Śākya**
clan, and Queen **Mahāmāyā**. The legend of the four predictive sights ("a decrepit old man, a diseased
man, a dead man, and a monk") frames the **Great Renunciation** at age 29; he travelled to **Rājagṛha** and
then **Uruvelā**, where with five ascetics he carried austerity to the point of collapse before concluding
"the truth was not to be won by the way of extreme asceticism." After supreme enlightenment he taught for
**45 years**, dying at over 80 by passing through the stages of *dhyāna* into nirvāṇa
(*Mahāparinibbāna-suttanta*). Dasgupta frames the Buddha's originality against the philosophical deadlock he
inherited — Upaniṣadic absolutism, [[carvaka|Cārvāka]] materialism, and Ājīvaka fatalism — onto which "he
erected a new superstructure of thought on altogether original lines."
