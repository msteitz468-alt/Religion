# Khadíjih Bagum

*All claims from Smith, Concise Encyclopedia of the Bahá'í Faith (2000), lines 20074–20102.*

## Biographical Overview

Khadíjih Bagum (d. 1882) was the Báb's second cousin (her father was an uncle of the Báb's mother) and childhood playmate (l. 20075–20076). She became his first wife in August 1842 (l. 20080) and gave birth to a son, Aḥmad, who died in infancy in 1843 (l. 20080–20083). According to her own later account she recognized her husband's religious station before his "declaration" of mission to [[mulla-husayn|Mullá Ḥusayn Bushrú'í]] in 1844 (l. 20083–20089).

After the execution of the Báb and his uncle (both 1850) she went to live with her sister Zahrá's family, while her mother-in-law moved to [[karbala|Karbalá]] (l. 20090–20093). Khadíjih subsequently became a Bahá'í (l. 20094). She and her maid — "a devoted African slave-woman named Fiddán, who had served the family since the time of Khadíjih's marriage" — both died on 15 September 1882 and were buried in the shrine of Sháh-Chirágh in Shíráz (l. 20095–20100).
