# Hermes (Mercury)

**Hermes** (Roman **Mercury**) is the **messenger of the gods** and the most versatile Olympian — god of
**travel, boundaries, trade, eloquence, thieves, and luck**, and the **guide of souls** (*psychopompos*) to
the underworld. Son of [[zeus|Zeus]] and the nymph Maia.

## Myths and Character

The [[homeric-hymns|Homeric Hymn to Hermes]] tells how, on his first day, the infant Hermes **invented the
lyre** (from a tortoise shell) and **stole [[apollo|Apollo's]] cattle** — the patron of cleverness, theft,
and exchange from birth. He wears winged sandals and the *petasos* hat and bears the **caduceus** (herald's
staff). He alone moves freely between Olympus, earth, and Hades.

## Cult and Roman Identification and Afterlife

Worshipped at boundary-markers (herms) and by merchants and travelers. As **Mercury** (from *merx*,
"merchandise") he was the god of commerce and profit. In late antiquity Hermes was fused with the Egyptian
Thoth as **Hermes Trismegistus**, patron of alchemy and the Hermetic writings (cf. [[gnosticism|Gnostic]]
and [[nag-hammadi-library|Hermetic]] texts).

## Myths in Detail

**The most precocious day in myth.** Born at dawn in a cave to [[zeus|Zeus]] and the nymph Maia, the infant
Hermes climbed from his cradle that very morning. He found a **tortoise**, scooped out its shell, and strung
it into the first **lyre**. By afternoon he had slipped out and **stolen fifty cattle from [[apollo|Apollo]]**,
driving them backwards to confuse their tracks and inventing fire and sacrifice along the way — then slipped
back into his cradle feigning innocence. When Apollo, by his prophetic art, traced the theft, the two
brothers came before Zeus, who laughed and bade them make peace. Hermes gave Apollo the **lyre** (which Apollo
loved ever after), and Apollo gave him the cattle and the golden herald's staff, the **caduceus** — sealing
Hermes as god of exchange, cleverness, and reconciliation.

**Slayer of Argus; guide of souls.** Zeus sent Hermes to free **Io** by lulling and killing the hundred-eyed
[[hera|Argus]] (whence his title *Argeiphontes*); Hera set the eyes in the peacock's tail. As **Psychopompos**
he alone passes freely between the worlds, leading the souls of the dead down to Hades — and leading
**[[persephone|Persephone]]** back up to her mother. He aids heroes (giving Perseus winged sandals and the
adamantine sword, guiding King Priam safely through the Greek camp in the [[homer|Iliad]], and helping
Odysseus against Circe with the herb *moly*).
