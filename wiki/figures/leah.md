# Leah

**Leah** (Hebrew *Le'ah*), the elder daughter of Laban and the first (substituted) wife of [[jacob|Jacob]], is the matriarch of **fruitfulness and hidden grace**. Unloved in favor of her sister [[rachel|Rachel]], she is "opened" by God and bears **six of the twelve tribes** — including [[judah|Judah]] (the royal line) and Levi (the priestly line) — plus Dinah. She is buried with Jacob in the cave of Machpelah (where Rachel is not). In the [[zohar|Zohar]] Leah is the **upper, concealed world** — Binah, the supernal Mother (Jubilee) — the hidden depth that Jacob attains only through his love of the revealed Rachel.

---

## Biographical Overview

- **The substitution** (Gen 29:21–26): Laban gives Leah to Jacob in place of Rachel on the wedding night ("in the morning, behold, it was Leah"); Jacob then works a further seven years for Rachel.
- **"Hated," yet fruitful** (Gen 29:31–35): "YHWH saw that Leah was hated, and he opened her womb"; she bears **Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah**, naming them out of her longing to be loved.
- **The birth-rivalry** (Gen 30): through her servant Zilpah and in her own right she bears further sons (Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun) and a daughter, **Dinah**.
- **Death and burial** (Gen 49:31): Jacob notes that Leah is buried in the **cave of Machpelah** with Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, and Rebekah — and there Jacob himself is laid.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 29–31; 34; 49:31** — the marriage, the births, Dinah, the burial note.
- **Ruth 4:11** — "like Rachel and **Leah**, who together built up the house of Israel."

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Jewish**: *Le'ah Imenu*; the mother of half the tribes, including the bearers of priesthood (Levi) and kingship ([[judah|Judah]]) — so that the **enduring institutions of Israel descend from the unloved wife**. Midrash reads her "weak eyes" (Gen 29:17) tenderly (worn from weeping at the prospect of marrying Esau) and praises her gratitude at Judah's birth ("this time I will praise YHWH") as the first to give thanks beyond her portion. She, not Rachel, lies beside Jacob in Machpelah.
- **Christian**: traditionally read (e.g., Augustine) as a figure in the allegory of the active vs. contemplative life (Leah = active/laborious life, Rachel = contemplative), and as ancestress of the priestly and royal (hence messianic) lines.
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)** — see below.

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar)

In the Zohar (Va-Yetse), Leah and [[rachel|Rachel]] are the **two worlds**, upper and lower:

- **Leah = the upper, concealed world (Jubilee; *Hu*, "He")** — identified with **Binah**, the supernal Mother, "constantly concealed," "none of whose matters are revealed." This is why "Leah was hated" can be said of so virtuous a matriarch: not that Jacob despised her, but that "**all her actions were concealed from Jacob**" — the upper world is hidden by nature. Her six sons issue from the realm of Jubilee/Binah, the source from which blessings flow down.
- **"Through the revealed, one reaches the concealed"**: Jacob openly cleaves to the revealed [[rachel|Rachel]] (Malkhut, *Attah*/"You"), but in doing so he attains Leah (Binah, *Hu*/"He"). The Zohar reads "*he lay with her in the night, Hu* ('He')" (Gen 30:16) as a cipher for Leah = the concealed supernal world. The two sisters together are the two faces of the divine feminine — supernal Mother (Binah) and immanent Bride (Shekhinah/Malkhut).
- That Leah (Binah, the concealed) is buried with Jacob in **Machpelah**, while Rachel (Malkhut, the exiled lower Shekhinah) lies on the road, encodes this polarity in the geography of the tombs.

### Pritzker II reingest anchors (Va-Yetse)

Material below is grounded in Zohar II (Pritzker Vol. II), Parashat Va-Yetse.

**Concealed / Jubilee / *Hu* (Va-Yetse, 1:153b–154b).** In the Zohar (Pritzker II, 1:153b–154a), Leah’s coupling is not open until morning (*In the morning, here she was Leah!*). The first seven years Jacob serves are read as concealed Jubilee (mistaken for Sabbatical); the second seven as revealed Sabbatical — crowning with two worlds. Higher world, Jubilee, is called *Hu*, He, “since all its matters are concealed… *He lay with her in the night, Hu*” (1:154b). Jacob “did not cleave to the concealed, rather to the revealed… *He cleaves to his wife*.”

**“Hated” = unrevealed (Va-Yetse, 1:154b).** In the Zohar (Pritzker II, 1:154b): “*YHVH saw that Leah was hated*… Jubilee is always a concealed world, none of whose matters are revealed; so all its actions were concealed from Jacob.” The moral problem (children of a “hated” woman normally unvirtuous; mother/sister pattern) is resolved by the concealment reading, not by moral disdain.

**Six directions; joyous mother (Va-Yetse, 1:153b–155a).** Leah’s six sons (plus Dinah) emerge through the mystery of Jubilee — “six directions above her”; Issachar and Zebulun complete the six. In the Zohar, Ps 113:9 (*joyous mother of children*) maps Leah to Jubilee — freedom and joy of worlds, the higher world. Judah from her line completes the Throne: “the throne was incomplete until she gave birth to Judah… *Va-ta’amod*, She stood” (1:155a).

**Stork / ḥasidah; Lebanon birds (Va-Yetse, 1:163a).** In the Zohar (Pritzker II, 1:163a), Laban’s two daughters are read with Lebanon/bird typology: the elder (Leah) as higher world — “Since She is *ḥasidah*, *Ḥesed* issues from Her”; home in the junipers as six heads/sons.

**Death unmentioned; burial concealed (Va-Yetse, 1:158a).** In the Zohar (Pritzker II, 1:158a), when Benjamin is born Rachel dies and the lower world assumes the house; “Leah does not die then — house belongs to lower world.” Higher-world matters are concealed: Leah’s death is unmentioned, unlike Rachel’s. Burial polarity: Leah in the Cave of Machpelah (concealed); Rachel on the road (revealed).

**Mandrake hire re-read as modesty (Va-Yetse, 1:156b–157a).** In the Zohar, Leah’s interception of Jacob is framed as speaking outside, not before her sister; the tent opens to the road; Holy Spirit is roused because tribes would issue from her — not mere impudence.

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Leah carries one of Genesis's quiet reversals: the **unloved, substituted wife becomes the mother of priesthood and monarchy** — Levi and Judah, the Temple and the throne, and so the messianic line itself. Her naming-speeches trace a movement from craving her husband's love to praising God beyond her need. The Zohar elevates this hiddenness into metaphysics: Leah is the concealed supernal world (Binah), the inexhaustible source that is reached only through the love of the revealed.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- Not independently attested; ancestral-saga horizon; the two-sisters/twelve-tribes structure is widely read as encoding tribal-confederation history.
- Leah's "weak eyes" (*rakkot*, Gen 29:17) is a translation crux ("weak," "tender," "lovely").
