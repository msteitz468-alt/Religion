# Lisa McPherson

**Lisa McPherson** (1959–1995) was a public [[scientology|Scientology]] parishioner who died on **December 5, 1995**, while in the care of the [[church-of-scientology|Church of Scientology's]] **Flag Land Base** in Clearwater, Florida. Her death became one of the most significant legal and public-relations crises in the Church's history.

## Biographical Overview

McPherson was a long-term Scientologist and a financially significant parishioner at Flag. According to [[wright-going-clear|Lawrence Wright]] (*Going Clear*, not yet fully ingested for this specific account) and other sources, she was undergoing the Introspection Rundown — a Hubbard-prescribed process for handling psychotic breaks — under the direct supervision of the Flag Land Base and, according to **Marc Headley**, under [[david-miscavige|David Miscavige]]'s personal oversight.

## Headley's Account (*Blown for Good*, 2009)

Headley places Lisa McPherson in close proximity to Miscavige's personal attention in 1995 — the year she died:

- She was at Flag under what Headley describes as Miscavige's **personal supervision** — a statement indicating she was a VIP parishioner receiving direct attention from RTC/Gold management
- After her **death in December 1995**, Headley describes a "hit and run" mentality adopted by those responsible: rooms at the Fort Harrison Hotel were cleaned out quickly; RTC representatives who had been assigned to supervise her care were **reassigned to dishwashing duty** — a form of punishment that simultaneously removed them from their previous posts
- Headley does not provide clinical detail about the circumstances of her death but treats it as a known internal fact at Gold Base — something staff were aware of as a serious liability event for the Church

## Legal and Public Context

(From background knowledge, not from the ingested Headley source specifically): McPherson died from a pulmonary embolism after a period of isolation and minimal care. Criminal charges were initially filed against the Church of Scientology of Flag Service Organization; the charges were later dropped. Civil litigation brought by McPherson's estate reached a confidential settlement in 2004. The case became a flagship example for critics of the Church's handling of members in mental health crises.

The Introspection Rundown under which she was held is a Hubbard-written procedure that prohibits all communication with the person under treatment. Critics argue this prevented McPherson from receiving appropriate medical care; the Church disputes this characterization.

## Theological Significance

McPherson's case intersects with a central controversy in Scientology: the prohibition on psychiatry and conventional mental health treatment, and the Church's insistence on handling all mental health conditions through auditing and Hubbard's prescribed procedures. See [[scientology-religion-or-commercial-enterprise|Religion or commercial enterprise?]] and [[fair-game|Fair Game]] for the broader context in which the Church responded to public scrutiny of her death.
