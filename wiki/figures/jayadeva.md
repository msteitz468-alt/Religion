# Jayadeva (Jai Dayv)

Jayadeva (c. 1150–1200) was a Sanskrit poet of Bengal and the author of the celebrated *Gīta Govinda* — an erotic-mystical poem in Sanskrit depicting the love of Radha and Krishna. He is the sole classical Sanskrit composer among the Bhagats in the **[[guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]]**, where his composition appears under Raag Goojaree as **Bhagat bāṇī**. His inclusion bridges classical Brahminical Vaishnava devotion into the Sikh scriptural corpus.

## In the Guru Granth Sahib (Raag Goojaree, Ang 527)

Jayadeva's single composition in the SGGS (Raag Goojaree, Padas Of Jai Dayv Jee, Fourth House) is composed in Sanskrit-influenced elevated language, in contrast to the vernacular Punjabi and Brajbhasha of the other Bhagats. Its content focuses on the primordial, transcendent Lord rather than on the Radha-Krishna love narrative of the *Gīta Govinda*:

> "In the very beginning, was the Primal Lord, unrivalled, the Lover of Truth and other virtues. He is absolutely wonderful, transcending creation; remembering Him, all are emancipated." (L8071–8084, Ang 527)

> "Dwell only upon the beauteous Name of the Lord, the embodiment of ambrosial nectar and reality. Remembering Him in meditation, the fear of birth, old age and death will not trouble you." (L8084–8096, Ang 527)

> "Worship the immaculate Lord, in thought, word and deed. What is the good of practicing Yoga, giving feasts and charity, and practicing penance?" (L8133–8140, Ang 527) — The critique of external religious practice echoes the other Bhagats' anti-formalism, though expressed in elevated Sanskrit register.

> "Meditate on the Lord of the Universe, the Lord of the Universe, O man; He is the source of all the spiritual powers of the Siddhas. Jai Dayv has openly come to Him; He is the salvation of all, in the past, present and future." (L8148–8156, Ang 527)

## Theological and Canonical Significance

Jayadeva's inclusion in the SGGS is notable on multiple counts:
- He is a **Brahmin** — in contrast to the low-caste weavers, cobblers, printers, and farmers among the other Bhagats
- He composed in **Sanskrit** — the elite literary language, in contrast to the vernaculars of the other Bhagats
- He is associated with **Vaishnava bhakti of the erotic-mystical (śṛṅgāra) type** through the *Gīta Govinda* — yet his SGGS composition makes no reference to the Radha-Krishna register

His inclusion demonstrates that the Sikh Gurus' criterion for Bhagat selection was **inner devotional attainment** rather than caste or linguistic register — a Brahmin composing in Sanskrit could be included alongside low-caste vernacular poets.
