# Rachel

**Rachel** (Hebrew *Raḥel*, "ewe"), the beloved wife of [[jacob|Jacob]] and mother of [[joseph|Joseph]] and Benjamin, is the matriarch of **love, longing, and the lower world**. Jacob serves fourteen years for her; she is long barren while her sister [[leah|Leah]] bears sons; she dies giving birth to Benjamin on the road near Bethlehem and is buried there (not in the cave of Machpelah) — becoming, in Jeremiah's vision, the mother who "weeps for her children" in exile. In the [[zohar|Zohar]] Rachel is the **lower, revealed world** — the visible [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] (Malkhut) — the partner whom Jacob (Tif'eret) openly desires and cleaves to.

---

## Biographical Overview

- **The well and the betrothal** (Gen 29): Jacob meets Rachel at the well, loves her, and agrees to serve Laban seven years for her — "they seemed but a few days because of his love."
- **The deception and the double marriage** (Gen 29:21–30): Laban substitutes [[leah|Leah]]; Jacob then serves another seven years for Rachel; she remains his beloved but is barren.
- **The birth of the tribes** (Gen 30): in the rivalry with Leah, Rachel gives Bilhah as a surrogate (Dan, Naphtali); finally bears **[[joseph|Joseph]]**.
- **The household gods** (Gen 31): fleeing Laban, Rachel steals and conceals his *teraphim*.
- **Her death** (Gen 35:16–20): dies bearing **Benjamin** near Ephrath/Bethlehem; buried on the road, her tomb a lasting landmark.

## Primary Source Appearances

- **Genesis 29–35** — the love story, the rivalry, the births, her death.
- **Ruth 4:11** — the Bethlehem elders bless the bride "like Rachel and Leah, who together built up the house of Israel."
- **Jeremiah 31:15** — "Rachel weeping for her children… because they are no more" — the matriarch mourning the exiles.
- **Matthew 2:18** — Jeremiah's Rachel oracle applied to the massacre of the infants at Bethlehem.

---

## Tradition-Specific Reception

- **Jewish**: the beloved matriarch; her roadside tomb (*Kever Raḥel*) near Bethlehem is a major pilgrimage site. Midrash makes her the supreme **intercessor**: when the patriarchs and Moses fail to move God to mercy for the exiles, Rachel's plea — recalling how she yielded her place to Leah — succeeds (*Lamentations Rabbah*), and God promises the return "for your sake." Her weeping (Jer 31) is the archetype of maternal intercession for Israel.
- **Christian**: the Jeremiah oracle is read in Matthew 2 as fulfilled in the slaughter of the innocents; Rachel becomes a figure of the Church/mothers mourning and of hope beyond grief.
- **Kabbalistic (the Zohar)** — see below.

---

## Kabbalistic Reception (the Zohar)

The Zohar (Va-Yetse) sets Rachel and [[leah|Leah]] in a precise sefirotic polarity — the **two worlds**:

- **Rachel = the lower, revealed world (Sabbatical; *Attah*, "You")** — the visible [[shekhinah|Shekhinah]] / Malkhut. She is the world that is "the beginning of all, to climb its rungs," the rung a person first reaches and openly cleaves to. Jacob's open, passionate love for Rachel is the soul's love for the revealed Shekhinah.
- **Leah = the upper, concealed world (Jubilee; *Hu*, "He")** — Binah, the hidden supernal Mother (see [[leah|Leah]]). "Through the revealed, one reaches the concealed": Jacob serves for Rachel (the revealed) but thereby attains Leah (the concealed). "Jacob, aspiring, did not cleave to the concealed but to the revealed — this is the mystery: *he cleaves to his wife* (Gen 2:24)."
- **Her death and burial on the road**: that Rachel is buried "on the way" (not in Machpelah) is read as the lower Shekhinah's accompanying of Israel into exile — she stands by the road to weep for and intercede for her children as they pass into captivity, the mystical ground of the Jeremiah 31 oracle.

---

## Theological and Narrative Significance

Rachel embodies the pathos of the covenant: the beloved who suffers barrenness, bears the favored son, and dies young — the matriarch of **love and loss** whose tomb marks the road of exile and whose tears become the paradigm of intercession and the promise of return. In the Zohar this is raised to cosmology: Rachel *is* the lower Shekhinah, the divine Presence who goes into exile with Israel and weeps until the restoration.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies

- Not independently attested; ancestral-saga horizon. The **location of Rachel's tomb** (near Bethlehem vs. in Benjamin, per 1 Sam 10:2) is an old textual-geographical crux.
- The **rivalry of Rachel and Leah** and the surrogate births raise questions read variously through feminist, social-historical, and theological lenses.
