## Biographical Overview

Photinus was bishop of Sirmium and a figure associated with the "Marcellan" theological trajectory. In this range of *Nicaea and Its Legacy*, Ayres gives him only a brief introduction: "Photinus of Sirmium we will discuss in later chapters" (*Nicaea and Its Legacy*, cache l. 1292–1293). His fuller treatment falls outside the range assigned to this extraction agent (book lines 2252–4295). The notes below are restricted to what Ayres actually states in this range.

## Role in the Fourth-Century Controversy (per Ayres)

Within the range covered here, Ayres identifies Photinus as a figure who "can be linked with" the Marcellan trajectory and as belonging to a "continuing body of supporters of Marcellus himself well into the 370s" (l. 1291–1292). He appears alongside "a continuing body of Marcellans writing to Athanasius in the early 370s" as evidence that the Marcellan trajectory persisted beyond [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus of Ancyra]]'s own lifetime (l. 1292–1294). The primary doctrinal tendency Photinus inherited from Marcellus was the insistence that discussion of Word and Spirit must "manifest the constancy and eternity of the divine unity" and a suspicion of "terminologies which speak of generation and of subordinate degrees of existence" (l. 1351–1354).

## Theological Position / Trajectory

Ayres's characterization of Photinus within this range is only by association with Marcellus's theology, which is described as the third of four "theological trajectories" — "theologians of the undivided Monad." The characteristic emphasis of this trajectory is:

> the insistence that discussion of Word and Spirit must manifest the constancy and eternity of the divine unity. Terminologies which speak of generation and of subordinate degrees of existence of Word and Spirit are taken to be breaches of that unity. (l. 1351–1354)

Photinus's specific positions, condemnations, and the details of his teaching are reserved for later chapters outside this extraction range. **Readers should treat this page as a stub pending extraction from later chapters of Ayres's work.**

## Reception and Later Labelling

Photinus was condemned — Ayres mentions this association without yet providing detail in this range. His connection to Marcellus made him a target for the same eastern anti-Marcellan polemic that drove opposition to Marcellus.
