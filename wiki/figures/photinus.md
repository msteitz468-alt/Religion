# Photinus of Sirmium

Photinus was bishop of Sirmium and a pupil of [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus of Ancyra]],
associated in Lewis Ayres's [[nicaea-and-its-legacy|*Nicaea and Its Legacy*]] (2004) with the
**"Marcellan"** theological trajectory — the "theologians of the undivided Monad," the third of
Ayres's four early-fourth-century trajectories.

## Role in the Fourth-Century Controversy (per Ayres)

Ayres treats Photinus as evidence that the Marcellan trajectory persisted well beyond Marcellus's
own lifetime: he is linked with "a continuing body of supporters of Marcellus" still writing to
[[athanasius|Athanasius]] in the early 370s. The trajectory's characteristic emphasis was

> the insistence that discussion of Word and Spirit must manifest the constancy and eternity of
> the divine unity. Terminologies which speak of generation and of subordinate degrees of
> existence of Word and Spirit are taken to be breaches of that unity.

Because of this association with Marcellus, Photinus became a target of the same Eastern
anti-Marcellan polemic — reflected in the explicit anti-Marcellan/anti-Photinian clauses of the
mid-century creeds (the Dedication and Fourth Antiochene creeds; see [[arian-controversy]]).

## Reception and Later Labelling

In the wider tradition (cf. the [[marcellus-of-ancyra|Marcellus]] page, drawn from J.N.D. Kelly)
Photinus is remembered for pushing Marcellus's "undivided Monad" theology toward an
[[adoptionism|adoptionist]] Christology, for which he was repeatedly condemned. Ayres reserves
the detail of Photinus's own positions for chapters beyond the trajectory survey treated here.
