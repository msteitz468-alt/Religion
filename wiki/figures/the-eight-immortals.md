# The Eight Immortals (Bā Xiān)

The **Eight Immortals** (**Bā Xiān**) are a band of **Daoist transcendents** — humans who attained
**immortality (xiān)** through cultivation, alchemy, or the gift of the gods — and one of the most popular and
auspicious motifs in [[chinese-folk-religion|Chinese folk religion]] and art. Half-divine, often comic, fond of
wine and wandering, they represent the **Daoist ideal of the deathless adept** made warm and human, and each
patronizes a different aspect of life. They belong to the orbit of **[[xi-wangmu|the Queen Mother of the
West]]**, at whose **Peach Festival** the peaches of immortality are served.

## The eight and their emblems

- **Lǚ Dòngbīn** — the leader, a scholar-swordsman who carries a **demon-slaying sword** and a fly-whisk;
  patron of scholars and barbers.
- **Lǐ Tiěguǎi** ("Iron-crutch Li") — a lame beggar with an iron crutch and a **gourd** of medicine whose smoke
  is his wandering spirit; patron of the sick. (His soul, leaving his body to travel, returned to find the body
  burned, so he entered the corpse of a lame beggar.)
- **Zhōnglí Quán** — a stout, bare-bellied sage with a **fan** that revives the dead.
- **Zhāng Guǒlǎo** — an ancient who rides a **white mule backwards** and folds it like paper when not in use.
- **Hé Xiāngū** — the one **woman**, holding a **lotus**; a maiden who achieved immortality by eating a magic
  peach (or mother-of-pearl).
- **Lán Cǎihé** — an ambiguous, youthful figure with a **flower-basket**; patron of florists.
- **Hán Xiāngzǐ** — a flute-playing youth, patron of musicians.
- **Cáo Guójiù** — a court official in robes holding **jade castanets / a court tablet**; patron of actors and
  nobility.

## Myths and meaning

Their most famous adventure is **"crossing the sea"** — *bā xiān guò hǎi* — in which, instead of riding a
cloud, each crosses the ocean by the **magic power of his or her own emblem**, giving the proverb *"the Eight
Immortals cross the sea, each showing their own talents"* (i.e. everyone has their own way). As a group they
embody **longevity, good fortune, and the variety of the human condition** — old and young, male and female,
rich and poor, noble and beggar — and so appear endlessly on birthday gifts, porcelain, and temple carving as
emblems of blessing.
