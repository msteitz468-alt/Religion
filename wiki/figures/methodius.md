# Methodius of Olympus

A bishop and theologian (martyred c. 311), best known as the first systematic critic of [[origen]] — especially on the resurrection — and as a transitional figure between third-century speculation and fourth-century orthodoxy. Drawn from [[kelly-early-christian-doctrines|J.N.D. Kelly]], chs. VI, VII, XVII.

## Christology and Anthropology

Methodius held a Word-flesh ([[logos-sarx-logos-anthropos|Logos-sarx]]) Christology: only the Word and his flesh are compounded in the God-man, "the body in a miraculous way became the receptacle of the Logos." On the [[original-sin|Fall]] he read Genesis literally: the first man was created immortal, with a body like ours even before sinning, and fell through sensual desire fanned by the Devil. He retained a weakened form of [[recapitulation]] — Christ as new Adam — but, Kelly notes, "almost completely overlooks the positive significance of Christ's death," stressing instead that the Lord's humanity "disclosed the resurrection of the flesh."

## Critique of Origen on the Resurrection

Methodius originated the classic anti-Origenist argument (later taken up by Eustathius of Antioch and Epiphanius). Against Origen's view that only the bodily "form" (*eidos*) survives, he objected that "there is no real resurrection since what is raised is not the body," dismissing Origen's "seminal reason" as "no more than a mould quite external to the body, like the tube through which water passes." His own view: Christ was raised "in exactly the same body as He bore upon the cross," and the resurrection body will be materially identical with the present body, restored to its pre-Fall impassibility and glory. See [[bodily-resurrection]].
