## Biographical Overview
Jehoiakim (originally Eliakim) was a son of Josiah, installed as king of Judah by Pharaoh Necho II of Egypt after the deposition of his brother Jehoahaz. He reigned for eleven years, heavily taxing his people to pay tribute to Egypt and engaging in oppressive building projects. When Babylonian power eclipsed Egyptian power after the Battle of Carchemish (605 BCE), Jehoiakim became a vassal to Nebuchadnezzar, but rebelled after three years. He died during or just before the resulting Babylonian siege of Jerusalem in 598 BCE.

## Primary Source Appearances
- **2 Kings 23-24 / 2 Chronicles 36**: Describe his installation by Egypt, his heavy taxation, his switch of vassalage to Babylon, his rebellion, and his death.
- **Jeremiah 22**: A scathing denunciation of Jehoiakim for his covetousness, injustice, and shedding of innocent blood, prophesying that he would receive the "burial of a donkey."
- **Jeremiah 26**: Jehoiakim executes the prophet Uriah for prophesying against the city and seeks to kill Jeremiah.
- **Jeremiah 36**: Jehoiakim shows his absolute contempt for the word of the LORD by cutting up and burning Jeremiah's scroll, piece by piece, as it is read to him in his winter apartment.

## Tradition-Specific Reception
- **Judaism**: Characterized as one of the quintessential wicked kings of Judah. Rabbinic tradition heavily criticizes him for his idolatry, arrogance, and murder of prophets, viewing him as having forfeited his portion in the world to come.
- **Christianity**: Viewed as the archetype of a hard-hearted ruler who actively seeks to destroy God's revelation.

## Theological and Narrative Significance
Jehoiakim represents the definitive rejection of the prophetic word and the reversal of his father Josiah's reforms. His burning of Jeremiah's scroll in 604 BCE is identified by commentators as the theological "point of no return" for Judah. While Josiah tore his clothes upon hearing the Book of the Law (2 Kings 22), Jehoiakim cuts up the prophetic scroll and throws it in the fire without a shred of fear or repentance.

## Historicity and Interpretive Controversies
His reign is well-documented in both biblical and ANE sources. A point of interpretive debate is the exact nature of his death and burial. 2 Kings 24:6 says he "rested with his ancestors," a standard formula, but Jeremiah 22:18-19 prophesied he would be dragged away and thrown outside the gates of Jerusalem like a dead donkey. It is generally thought his body was discarded during the siege.

## Influence on Commentary Traditions
Commentators like Hetty Lalleman stress that the burning of the scroll (Jer 36) marks the decisive shift in the Book of Jeremiah, after which judgment becomes inescapable. John Goldingay contrasts Jehoiakim's brazen defiance with the weakness of Zedekiah, making Jehoiakim the primary antagonist of Jeremiah's early ministry.
